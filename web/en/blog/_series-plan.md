## Naming: why not "the Septad"?

The primary document's *Septad* is exactly **seven** interacting pathophysiologies (MCAS, hEDS/hypermobility, POTS/dysautonomia, autoimmunity, chronic infection, small fiber neuropathy, GI dysmotility). This blog series is **informed by** the Septad but covers a little more, so calling it "the Septad" would be wrong:

- **+1 topic:** *fibromyalgia-pattern pain* (a symptom cluster every reader meets; not one of the formal seven).
- **+1 capstone:** *"chained together"* — the synthesis of how the amplifiers integrate (not itself a condition).
- **+ MCAS expands to 3 articles** (overview / treatment / failure), not one.

So: **9 topics, and the total exceeds 9 articles.** The name is **"the conditions that travel with ME/CFS"** (co-occurring conditions), and "Septad" is referenced only as the paper's tighter 7-condition core that this series builds on.

## Goal and positioning

ME/CFS rarely travels alone. The primary document identifies a cluster of seven interrelated conditions — the **Septad** — that co-occur and feed one another, and this series starts from that idea and extends it to the fibromyalgia-pain pattern. It explains one condition at a time in plain language, and always draws the distinction this series is built around:

> **A co-occurring condition ON ITS OWN** (MCAS is the disease; treat the cells) vs
> **the same condition ON TOP OF ME/CFS** (usually a downstream amplifier; treat the amplifier, not the cause).

This distinction changes the treatment goal, the expected results, and — crucially — what a treatment *failure* means. Every article carries it.

## The fixed article template (calibrated from the MCAS mini-series)

1. **Patient vignette** (2nd person, sensory, immediate) — the "4 p.m. again" style opening.

6. **What it means if the treatment doesn't work** — the honest section.
7. **Falsifiable predictions** and **what you can actually do**.
8. Closing cross-links to the next article + reference to `@Loth2026mecfs`.

## — Topic 2: POTS and the heart that races when you stand → Done ✅

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Patient hook** | "You're lying down. Horizontal, calm, and your heart is at 95. You check your pulse because it feels wrong — a quiet room, a rested body, and your heart is running like you just climbed stairs. Stand up, and it climbs past 120 before you've walked anywhere." |
| **Normal trigger** | Standing → blood pools in legs → baroreflex → brief compensatory tachycardia |
| **ME/CFS mechanism** | Reduced blood volume + endothelial dysfunction + possible α-adrenergic autoantibodies → exaggerated tachycardia on standing; orthostatic intolerance correlates with MCAS (mast mediators dilate vessels and drop BP) [@Rohrhofer2025mecfsmast] |
| **By-itself vs with-ME/CFS** | POTS-alone: treat flow/blood volume; POTS-on-ME/CFS: one amplifier of the energy/immune disorder — fixing the heart rate does not fix the crash |
| **Key doc lines** | ch11 cardiovascular; ch14d-cross-disease.typ:901; hyp-carotid-mast-oi |
| **Falsifiable prediction** | If supine norepinephrine is elevated and HR normalises with α-blockade, peripheral sympathetic excess is confirmed (see primary doc) |
| **Can pair with** | MCAS, SFN, hypermobility |

## Cross-series notes

- **Distinction discipline.** Every entry keeps "condition-on-its-own vs condition-on-top-of-ME/CFS" explicit, per the new primary-document subsection "MCAS Without ME/CFS vs ME/CFS With MCAS" (ch08 sec-08) — the same framing applies by analogy to every Septad member.
- **Amplifier over cause.** The consistent finding across the Septad is downstream amplification; the largest honest correction this series offers readers is that fixing one amplifier rarely fixes the core energy/immune defect, and a treatment failure says nothing against the underlying illness.
- **Evidence honesty.** Each topic labels three tiers — confirmed / promising hypothesis / individual experience — and does not turn hypotheses (e.g., autoantibodies, matrix-stiffness) into recommendations.
- **Citation source.** All claims trace to the primary document and its bib (`web/bib/*.bib`). Before writing each article, extract the specific `ch*`/`*.typ` anchor lines and confirm each citation key resolves.
