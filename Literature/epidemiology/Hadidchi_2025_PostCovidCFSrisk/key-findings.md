# Key Findings: Hadidchi 2025 — CFS/ME Risk After COVID-19

## Main Findings
1. **Sustained elevated risk:** SARS-CoV-2 infection raises new-onset ME/CFS risk that persists up to 4 years post-infection. HR 1.46–1.56 vs controls.
2. **Risk independent of severity:** Non-hospitalized COVID-19 patients had slightly higher HR (1.56) than hospitalized (1.46), showing ME/CFS risk is not proportional to acute illness severity.
3. **Acute biomarkers not predictive:** Ferritin, D-dimer, LDH, CRP during acute infection did NOT predict who would develop CFS/ME. This is a critical null result distinguishing acute-phase from post-acute biomarker patterns.
4. **Re-infection not additive:** Multiple COVID-19 infections were not associated with increased CFS/ME risk beyond the first infection.
5. **Vaccination finding:** COVID-19 vaccination in early rollout was associated with increased CFS/ME risk (requires replication; confounded by era).

## Clinical Implications
- Post-COVID monitoring for ME/CFS should extend for years, not just months
- Acute-phase biomarkers are not useful screening tools for future ME/CFS risk
- Female sex, pre-existing autoimmune disease, and liver disease are risk modifiers

## Limitations
- Retrospective, single health system (Montefiore, Bronx, NY)
- ICD-code-based CFS/ME diagnosis — underascertainment likely
- Vaccination finding confounded by era (early rollout = most vulnerable first)
- No ME/CFS-specific clinical assessment (no PEM, orthostatic intolerance, etc.)
