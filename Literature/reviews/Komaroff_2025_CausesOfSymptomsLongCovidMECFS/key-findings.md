# Key Findings: Komaroff & Dantzer 2025 — Symptom Causes & Persistence

## Main Findings

1. **Shared pathophysiology:** Long COVID and ME/CFS share underlying biological abnormalities: autoantibodies against neural targets, endothelial dysfunction, acquired mitochondrial dysfunction, pro-inflammatory gut microbiome (plus autonomic dysfunction, hypometabolism, redox imbalance, reduced NK function, complement activation, mast cell activation, cellular senescence). [Section: "Can the underlying abnormalities directly produce symptoms?"]

2. **Novel symptom-generating pathway — sickness behavior + torpor:** Symptoms may be generated by evolutionarily conserved responses to vital threats, mediated by recently-discovered dedicated neural circuits:
   - Area postrema → brainstem: suppresses eating, drinking, movement (sickness behavior)
   - Organum vasculosum → ventromedial preoptic area: appetite suppression, warmth seeking, fever
   - Median preoptic nucleus → torpor: hypometabolic state, reduced ATP generation
   These circuits are activated by neuroinflammation (microglia/astrocytes).

3. **Neuroinflammation as hub:** triggered from inside (active/reactivated CNS infection, brain injury) or outside the brain (humoral: circumventricular organs, porous BBB, active transport of IL-1/IL-6/TNF-α; neural: vagal afferents → NTS).

4. **Torpor-like metabolic adaptation:** reduced ATP generation and hypometabolism framed as an adaptive AMPK/mTOR-driven switch, not just damage.

5. **Persistence (chronicity) mechanisms:** persistent infectious agents/nucleic acids/antigens; residual tissue injury needing repair-inflammation; defective resolution of inflammation; herpesvirus reactivation; gut dysbiosis/leaky gut; autoimmunity (mixed evidence); defective neural-circuit "switch"; mutually reinforcing vicious cycles.

6. **Genetic vulnerability:** 3 long COVID GWAS loci (HLA-DQA1-DQB, ABO, BPTF-KPAN2-C17orf58) with MR causal roles in long COVID AND ME/CFS; WGS deep-learning → 115 ME/CFS risk genes (CNS + immune implicated).

7. **Treatment implication:** target neuroinflammation directly; traditional anti-inflammatories unlikely to work; need novel anti-inflammatory/inflammasome therapies + better imaging of smoldering inflammation.

## Clinical Implications

- Supports ME/CFS and long COVID as objective biological illnesses (shared abnormalities), countering the "no biological basis" view.
- Provides a treatment target (neuroinflammation-driven neural-circuit symptom generation) distinct from targeting single peripheral abnormalities.
- Reinforcement for the sickness-behavior framework already present in the paper.

## Limitations

- Rodent-derived neural circuit model; human relevance indirect.
- Neural-circuit/torpor chronic engagement in ME/CFS/long COVID is speculative.
- Review — aggregates primary evidence; null/negative findings (e.g., Omdal 2026 no peripheral cytokine/inflammation biomarker association) constrain the "systemic inflammation → symptoms" arm and must be reconciled (neuroinflammation-specific path; localized/imaging rather than serum markers).
