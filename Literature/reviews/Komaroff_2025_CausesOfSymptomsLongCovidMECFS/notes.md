**Authors:** Komaroff AL, Dantzer R
**Journal:** Cell Reports Medicine, 2025;6(8):102259 (Epub 2025 Jul 30)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.xcrm.2025.102259
**PMID:** 40744021 | **PMCID:** PMC12432373

## Abstract (verbatim)

Debilitating symptoms for many years can follow acute COVID-19 ("long COVID"), myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), and various post-acute infection syndromes (PAISs). Together, long COVID and ME/CFS affect 60–400 million individuals, globally. Many similar underlying biological abnormalities have been identified in both conditions including autoantibodies against neural targets, endothelial dysfunction, acquired mitochondrial dysfunction, and a pro-inflammatory gut microbiome. Each of these abnormalities may directly cause some of the symptoms. In addition, the symptoms also may be caused by ancient, evolutionarily conserved symptomatic and metabolic responses to vital threats—sickness behavior and torpor—responses mediated by specific, recently discovered neural circuits. These neural circuits constitute a symptom-generating pathway, activated by neuroinflammation, which may be targeted by therapeutics to quell neuroinflammation. Many factors cause the symptoms to become chronic, including persistent infectious agents (and/or their nucleic acids and antigens) and the fact that many of the underlying biological abnormalities reinforce each other, creating ongoing physiological vicious cycles.

## Key Points

- Long COVID and ME/CFS together affect 60–400 million people globally (varies by definition); long COVID alone estimated to cost society several trillion dollars over 5–10 years; ME/CFS affects up to 3.1 million in the US.
- Shared underlying abnormalities: autoantibodies against neural targets, endothelial dysfunction, acquired mitochondrial dysfunction, pro-inflammatory gut microbiome; also autonomic dysfunction, reduced CBF, hypometabolic state, redox imbalance, reduced NK function, complement activation, mast cell activation, cellular senescence, tryptophan/serotonin/kynurenine abnormalities.
- Central thesis: symptoms may be generated by two ancient evolutionary responses to vital threats — **sickness behavior** and **torpor** — mediated by recently-discovered dedicated neural circuits.
- Named neural circuits: (1) area postrema → brainstem (reduces eating/drinking/movement); (2) organum vasculosum → ventromedial preoptic area (appetite suppression, warmth seeking, fever, autonomic); (3) median preoptic nucleus → torpor/hypometabolism.
- Neuroinflammation (microglia/astrocytes) triggers these circuits; triggered from inside the brain (active/reactivated infection, brain injury) or from outside (humoral: circumventricular organs, porous BBB, active cytokine transport; neural: vagal afferents → nucleus tractus solitarius).
- Metabolic switch: AMPK/mTOR; torpor-like reduced ATP generation proposed as adaptive energy-conservation, not merely damage.
- Chronicity drivers: persistent infectious agents (nucleic acids/antigens), residual tissue injury/repair inflammation, defective resolution of inflammation, herpesvirus reactivation, gut dysbiosis/leaky gut, autoimmunity (mixed evidence), defective neural-circuit "switch," and self-reinforcing vicious cycles (mitochondrial injury ↔ inflammation; redox ↔ energy; redox ↔ endothelial; endothelial+NK+neuroinflammation; spike-fibrin pro-inflammatory epitope).
- Genetic vulnerability: long COVID GWAS loci (HLA-DQA1-DQB, ABO, BPTF-KPAN2-C17orf58) with Mendelian randomization causal roles in both long COVID and ME/CFS; WGS+deep-learning 115 risk genes.
- Treatment: target neuroinflammation directly; traditional anti-inflammatories "unlikely to be effective"; novel therapies (biologics, inflammasome inhibitors) and better smoldering-inflammation imaging needed.

## Five questions the review addresses

1. Can the underlying abnormalities each directly produce symptoms? — Partly yes; many can directly.
2. Could sickness symptoms explain some behavioral changes? — Yes; major pathway.
3. Might torpor contribute to the metabolic changes? — Yes; torpor-like hypometabolic/ATP-reduction analogue.
4. How does the brain direct the changes? — Dedicated, recently-discovered neural circuits.
5. Why do symptoms become chronic? — Persistent agents/antigens, tissue injury, defective resolution, reactivation, dysbiosis, autoimmunity, defective switch, vicious cycles.

## Caveats / Limitations

- Review, not primary data — inherits quality of primary sources.
- Sickness-behavior/torpor neural circuits identified in **rodents**; human evidence is indirect (LPS fMRI studies), so application to chronic human ME/CFS/long COVID is inferential.
- The neural-circuit + torpor model is a hypothesis about chronic engagement of an adaptive circuit; not directly tested in ME/CFS/long COVID.
- The review openly acknowledges mixed evidence on autoimmunity and that vaccine/antiviral reduction of long COVID "requires much more evidence."
