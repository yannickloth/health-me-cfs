# Wilshire 2018 — Key Findings

Paper: Wilshire CE, Kindlon T, Courtney R, Matthees A, Tuller D, Geraghty K, Levin B.
Rethinking the treatment of chronic fatigue syndrome—a reanalysis and evaluation of
findings from a recent major trial of graded exercise and CBT. BMC Psychology 2018;6:6.
DOI: 10.1186/s40359-018-0218-3

## Reanalysis (protocol-specified procedures)

- On the protocol's primary outcome (overall improvement), CBT/GET did NOT significantly
  outperform Control after multiple-comparison correction.
- Recovery rates: consistently low; not significantly different across treatment groups.
- Significant secondary effects were almost entirely on self-report measures.
- Self-report effects did not endure beyond two years.
- Conclusions attribute modest effects to participant reporting bias.

## Document-captured figures

- Recovery rate on original thresholds: 22% → 7%, not significantly different from control.
- Step-count actigraphy: no significant improvement in any group.
- Six-minute walk improvement: clinically trivial (~20 m).
- Long-term follow-up: all groups converged — no durable benefit.

## Significance for ME/CFS

Foundational critique of the GET/CBT (PACE) paradigm. Supports GET-avoidance guidance and
the harms/evidence-weakness framing for behavioral treatments. Already integrated — see
`notes.md` for citation locations.
