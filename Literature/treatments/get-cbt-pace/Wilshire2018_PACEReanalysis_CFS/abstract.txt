Title: Rethinking the treatment of chronic fatigue syndrome—a reanalysis and evaluation of findings from a recent major trial of graded exercise and CBT
Authors: Carolyn E. Wilshire, Tom Kindlon, Robert Courtney, Alem Matthees, David Tuller, Keith Geraghty, Bruce Levin
Journal: BMC Psychology (2018) 6:6
DOI: 10.1186/s40359-018-0218-3
PMID: 29562932
PMCID: PMC5863477
Type: Research article (reanalysis)

Background: The PACE trial was a well-powered randomised trial examining graded exercise
therapy (GET) and cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) for chronic fatigue syndrome. Reports
concluded both treatments were moderately effective, each leading to recovery in over a fifth
of patients. The reported analyses did not consistently follow the published protocol.

Methods: Results based on the original protocol-specified procedures, approximated using data
from a Freedom of Information request.

Results: On the original protocol-specified primary outcome (overall improvement rates) there
was a significant effect of treatment group. However, CBT/GET groups did not significantly
outperform the Control group after correcting for the number of comparisons in the protocol.
Recovery rates were consistently low and not significantly different across groups. On secondary
measures, significant effects were almost entirely confined to self-report measures and did not
endure beyond two years.

Conclusions: Serious concerns about the robustness of claims of CBT/GET efficacy. Modest
self-report effects do not exceed what could be accounted for by participant reporting biases.

Keywords: Chronic fatigue syndrome, Myalgic encephalomyelitis, Graded exercise therapy,
Cognitive behavioral therapy
