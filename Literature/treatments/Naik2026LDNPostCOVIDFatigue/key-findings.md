# Key Findings — Naik 2026 (LDN for Post-COVID Fatigue, RCT)

## Main Findings
1. **LDN does not beat placebo for fatigue in post-COVID fatigue syndrome.** At 16 weeks, FSS mean 4.2 (LDN) vs 4.4 (placebo); adjusted mean difference −0.76 (95% CI −4.18 to 2.67). No statistically significant difference.
2. **No secondary-outcome benefit.** Pain VAS, PQSymp-12, daily step count, and EQ-5D-5L utility all null. This is a *consistent* null across patient-reported symptoms, an objective activity measure (step count), and quality of life — not a single-endpoint miss.
3. **LDN is well tolerated.** Grade ≥3 AEs 6.2% (LDN) vs 3.8% (placebo); any AE 79.0% vs 83.5%. Safety does not distinguish LDN from placebo.
4. **No responder subgroup identified.** Prespecified subgroup analyses (sex, comorbidity burden, age, illness duration, baseline severity, infection ascertainment) found no significant effect modification.

## Clinical Implications
- The strongest prior LDN evidence in post-viral fatigue was uncontrolled (Polo 2019 n=218, 73.9% response; O'Kelly 2022; Tamariz 2024 HR 5.04; Isman 2024). This RCT is the first placebo-controlled test, and it is **null**.
- Off-label LDN prescribing for post-COVID fatigue now has a well-powered negative RCT to weigh against the observational optimism.
- The result does NOT rule out a responder subtype (e.g., TRPM3-impaired, high baseline inflammation), but no such subtype emerged from the prespecified subgroups.

## Limitations
- Preprint — not yet peer-reviewed.
- PCFS is post-SARS-CoV-2 ME/CFS-like illness, not classic ME/CFS (generalizability discount applies).
- Wide confidence interval on the primary outcome (−4.18 to 2.67) — the null is consistent with a possible small effect, though the point estimate is near zero.
- Modest adherence asymmetry (77.9% vs 86.8% ≥80% dose), though per-protocol analyses were also null.

## Integration Points
### Part 3 (treatments/pharmacological) — ch16 supplements / LDN
- **Could support:** downgrading the LDN evidence base from "moderate (observational)" to "negative RCT in post-viral fatigue."
- **Cites as:** clinical-finding (null RCT) — the first placebo-controlled LDN result in a post-viral-fatigue population.

### ch14d (cross-disease / Long COVID parallels)
- **Could support:** post-COVID fatigue ≠ ME/CFS treatment-response equivalence; a treatment that fails in PCFS may still be untested in classic ME/CFS.

### ch30 sec-12 / ch33 (pharmacodiagnostic compendium, LDN entry)
- **Could support:** updating the LDN entry with the null RCT result and the "observational signal did not replicate under placebo control" caveat.
