# Key Findings: ADHD and Late-Life Cognitive Impairment (Bauer-Negrini 2026)

## Main Findings
1. **ADHD → incident MCI/dementia:** HR 4.60 (95% CI 3.99–5.29, P<.001) in All of Us cohort (n=187,341, ≥50 y, mean follow-up 10.26 y).
2. **Dose-response genetic evidence:** ADHD PRS highest-vs-lowest quintile HR 1.35 (1.25–1.46, P<.001) — convergent clinical + genetic signal.
3. **Confounder-robust (partially):** attenuated but persistent after somatic comorbidity (HR 4.04), neuropsychiatric comorbidity (3.19), full adjustment incl. education/utilization (3.08); 1:2 matched cohort HR 2.26.
4. **Reverse causation:** association non-significant at ≥9-year lag (HR 1.28, P=.07).
5. **Worse outcomes among incident cases with ADHD:** suboptimal mental health OR 1.69, QoL OR 1.66, social satisfaction OR 2.39, +20% healthcare utilization.
6. **Ancestry-dependent:** significant in European (HR 1.38) and African (HR 1.29) ancestry strata.

## Clinical Implications
- ADHD is a lifelong exposure raising late-life dementia/MCI risk → targeted monitoring and integrated care.
- ADHD PRS adds independent (reverse-causation-immune) risk information beyond EHR diagnosis.

## Limitations
- Observational; residual confounding not excluded.
- EHR ascertainment bias (differential healthcare contact/recognition).
- Long dementia prodrome → reverse causation not fully excluded.
- PRS reflects shared liability/pleiotropy, not proven direct causality.
