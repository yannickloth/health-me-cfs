# Key Findings — Autism First-Impression / Thin-Slice Stigma (Sasson 2017 anchor)

## Anchor core effect (Sasson 2017)
1. TD observers form less favorable first impressions of autistic adults/children from thin slices of real-world behavior.
2. Reduced trait ratings co-occur with reduced intention to pursue social interaction (the stigmatizing behavioral component).
3. Effects: large (η² 0.47–0.55), robust within seconds, stable over increased exposure, persist child + adult age.
4. Bias disappears for transcript-only content → "style, not substance" drives negative impressions.

## Meta-analytic replication (Wanigasekera 2025)
5. 21 articles, 221 effects: less favorable impressions of autistic people across all modalities EXCEPT speech transcript; moderate-to-large effects.
6. Most pronounced for interpersonal attraction and social/communication presentation.
7. Observer autism knowledge and quality of contact moderate impressions (perceiver-locus).

## In-group / null-of-bias evidence (DeBrabander 2019; Morrison 2020)
8. Autistic raters SHARE the trait-inference tendency but do NOT show reduced social interest in autistic targets → trait judgment dissociates from willingness-to-interact.
9. Autistic–autistic real-world dyads show more favorable rapport/outcomes than autistic–TD dyads → social difficulty is co-produced by neurotype mismatch (relational model).

## Perceiver-bias locus (Morrison 2019)
10. Rater characteristics (autism knowledge, contact, personality) drive more first-impression variability than target characteristics.

## Disclosure reduces bias — but condition-specific (Norris 2024; Parisi 2026)
11. Disclosing an autism diagnosis improves employment-interview ratings (Norris 2024).
12. Disclosing a SCHIZOPHRENIA diagnosis WORSENS ratings (stigma); mislabeling as autism IMPROVES them (Parisi 2026) → disclosure effects are not universal.

## Mechanism papers (Foster 2025; Edey 2016; Jones 2024; Grossman 2015)
13. Reduced/atypical facial affect drives negative first impressions (Foster 2025) — the specific presentational signal.
14. Mind-reading difficulty in cross-neurotype interaction is bidirectional ("double mind-blindness", Edey 2016).
15. Double-empathy problem operationalized: NT observers both detect and demonstrate reduced empathy (Jones 2024).
16. Brief-exposure awkwardness judgments establish the paradigm (Grossman 2015, precursor).

## Transdiagnostic comparators ("style" drives impressions in other conditions)
17. Parkinson's facial masking + abnormal movement → negative first impressions (Hemmesch 2014).
18. Schizophrenia reduced facial expression → negative first impressions (Parisi 2026).
19. Thin-slice judgments operate in the CLINICAL context and can bias clinical decisions (Slepian/Ambady 2014).

## Research gaps (documented, NOT padded)
- Zero direct studies of "first impressions of ME/CFS patients" or "thin-slice clinician judgment of fatigue patients".
- Zero dedicated "autism + ME/CFS comorbidity stigma" studies. (Quadt 2026 and the integrated ND-burnout content address neurodivergence prevalence/consequences in chronic fatigue, NOT first-impression stigma — out of scope, already integrated.)
- The ME/CFS application is mechanism transfer by analogy, not direct cohort evidence → cap any ME/CFS-specific claim at speculation level.
