## What this topic integrates
The autism social-perception / first-impression / thin-slice stigma literature, anchored on Sasson 2017. Two framing purposes:
(a) **ME/CFS relevance** — how first-impression formation from thin slices of presentational "style" (flat affect, slowed movement, reduced expressivity, gaze) — independent of the medical "substance" the patient reports — maps onto why ME/CFS patients (invisible illness) face clinician/peer dismissal.
(b) **Autism-domain rigor** — the fully-developed autism first-impression/stigma literature developed with ME/CFS-grade evidentiary standards, cross-referenced where autism and ME/CFS genuinely intersect (co-occurring autism + ME/CFS = compounded first-impression bias).

## Evidence-strength guidance for Phase 3
- Anchor + meta-analysis are strong (discounted ~0.60–0.64) but GENERAL-POPULATION perceivers of ANOTHER condition → weight 0.75, analogy only.
- **No ME/CFS-specific first-impression paper exists** (documented gap). Therefore NO ME/CFS causal claim may exceed `#speculation`. Do NOT write `#hypothesis-box`/`#fhypothesis` for a ME/CFS-specific first-impression mechanism.
- The transdiagnostic "style (facial affect/flat affect) drives negative impressions" claim (Sasson + Foster + Hemmesch + Parisi + Wanigasekera) is the most defensible cross-condition claim.
- Disclosure-reduces-bias is CONDITION-SPECIFIC (Norris helps autism; Parisi shows schizophrenia disclosure hurts) — frame as open nuance, not a universal intervention.

## Key risk / over-reach guard
ME/CFS patients with co-occurring autism face a plausible DOUBLE first-impression bias, but this is hypothesis (H3), not established. Do not present comorbid-autism + ME/CFS compounded stigma as demonstrated fact — no dedicated study exists.
