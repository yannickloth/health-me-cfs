# Sasson et al. 2017 — Neurotypical Peers Less Willing to Interact Based on Thin Slice Judgments (ANCHOR)

**Journal:** Scientific Reports, 2017, Vol. 7, p. 40700
**DOI:** 10.1038/srep40700

## Purpose in the ME/CFS paper
Anchor for integrating the autism first-impression/thin-slice stigma literature. The paper's mechanism — first impressions from thin slices of presentational "style" (facial affect, prosody, movement, gaze) drive reduced willingness to interact, independent of conversational substance — is the model for why invisible-illness (ME/CFS) patients face clinician/peer first-impression dismissal. Also developed as fully-rigorous autism-domain knowledge per the dual-framing integration intent.

## Labs / cohort-overlap
- UT Dallas (Sasson, Faso) + Indiana U (Kennedy, Nugent) + Emerson College (Grossman, Lovell).
- Same lab lineage as: Morrison2019, Morrison2020, DeBrabander2019, Foster2025, Jones2024, Efthimiou2025 (Sasson senior/co-author), Grossman2015 (precursor). Discount these when counting independent replications.
- Independent confirmation of the core effect: Wanigasekera2025 (WA meta-analysis), Boucher2023 (Simon Fraser).

## Key design
- 3 studies; TD observers rate thin-slice video of autistic adults/children vs controls.
- Trait judgments (awkwardness, likeability, attractiveness, assertiveness, trustworthiness, intelligence) + intentions to socially engage.
- Manipulation: full video vs audio-only vs transcript-only.

## Headline effects
- Less favorable first impressions of autistic vs control targets; reduced social-interaction intentions.
- Robust within seconds; no change with increased exposure; persists child + adult.
- Bias disappears for transcript-only content → style, not substance, drives negative impressions.

## ME/CFS mapping (mechanism transfer, by analogy — NOT direct ME/CFS evidence)
- Flat affect / slowed movement / reduced expressivity of a severe/bedbound or chronic-fatigue patient = the "style" analog.
- The medical "substance" the patient reports (symptom history) is discounted relative to how the patient presents — a mechanism for diagnostic dismissal.
- Relational framing: impression is co-produced by perceiver bias, not a deficit solely in the patient → intervention targets perceiver knowledge/contact, not patient social-skills correction.
