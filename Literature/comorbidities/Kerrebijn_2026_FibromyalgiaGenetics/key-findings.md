# Key Findings: Kerrebijn 2026 — Fibromyalgia Genetics

## Main Findings
1. **Fibromyalgia is a CNS/nociplastic disorder:** Heritability exclusively enriched in brain tissues and neural cell types (dentate gyrus, enteric neurons, interneurons, cortical/striatal projection neurons). Establishes fibromyalgia as a central nervous system disorder genetically.
2. **26 risk loci identified** (multi-ancestry GWAS, 2,563,755 individuals). Strongest: HTT coding variant (inframe glutamic acid deletion, exon 58), OR=1.09.
3. **Neural gene prioritization:** HTT, GPR52 (HTT regulator), DCC, DRD2/NCAM1, MDGA2, CELF4 — all neural roles.
4. **Pervasive genetic correlations:** rg > 0.7 with low back pain, PTSD, IBS, joint pain, hypermobility, myalgia; strong with depression, somatoform, dissociative, migraine.
5. **Not primarily autoimmune:** No significant MHC signal, no immune/glial heritability enrichment, modest autoimmune rg; stronger with atypical subtypes (seronegative RA, T2-low asthma).
6. **No sex difference in genetic architecture** (inter-sex rg = 1.03) despite 87.7% female cases.
7. **Modest observed heritability:** 10.4%; PRS AUC 0.59 European, risk quintile OR range 0.63-1.5.

## Clinical Implications
- Fibromyalgia co-occurring with ME/CFS likely shares a partially shared, centrally mediated genetic vulnerability (CNS/nociplastic) rather than a peripheral autoimmune driver.
- Shared genetic architecture with IBS, low back pain, PTSD supports a transdiagnostic central sensitization model spanning chronic overlapping pain conditions.
- GPR52 and CELF4 are genetically supported molecular targets; GPR52 is already an investigational HD drug target (repurposing opportunity).
- Lack of sex-specific genetic architecture suggests the female predominance reflects nongenetic factors or diagnostic bias, not sex-specific risk variants.

## Limitations
- Mostly European sample (90%), limiting generalizability and PRS transferability to non-European ancestries.
- ICD-10 code-based case definition may include/exclude individuals not meeting full syndromic criteria.
- Single published meta-analysis; not yet fully externally replicated locus-by-locus (partial overlap with independent Bright 2026).
- Cell-type enrichment limited by overlapping gene expression across related cell types.
- Healthy participant bias in biobank cohorts.
- Genetic correlation with autoimmune disorders subject to potential diagnostic misclassification.
