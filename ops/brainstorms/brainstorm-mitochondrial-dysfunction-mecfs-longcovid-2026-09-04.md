## Category 1 — Novel Hypotheses

**1.1** *The brain-energy deficit is a recovery-timing, not a resting-trait, biomarker — a state-coupled "energy-credit" hypothesis.* Weber-Fahr's resting ATP/PCr reduction is small and centered on cingulate cortex; its diagnostic power may live in its *responsiveness*, not its baseline. Hypothesis: the brain shows a delayed/reduced ATP/PCr repletion trajectory after a standardized cognitive or exertional challenge — analogous to, but separable from, the peripheral 2-day CPET — and this recovery slope, not the resting value, tracks cognition and PEM severity. Evidence: resting ³¹P reduction @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine (moderate n, controlled); ACC ATP/PCr–cognition correlation @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine; peripheral 2-day CPET precedent (already integrated). Cert: 0.32. Falsifiable: in a challenge protocol (cognitive load or submaximal exercise) with serial ³¹P-MRSI, ME/CFS patients show a slower ATP/PCr recovery slope than VO₂max- and effort-matched controls, and this slope predicts next-day PEM symptom severity; a normal recovery slope in patients falsifies the state-coupling claim and demotes the finding to a resting trait. Consequence: brain-energy imaging could become a *provocation* test for PEM — reading recovery after a task, not a static scan — mirroring how the 2-day CPET works for muscle. *origin: brainstorm.*

**1.2** *Cingulate-centric ATP/PCr reduction is the imaging correlate of interoceptive/central-fatigue processing cost, not a generic "brain tiredness."* Weber-Fahr localizes the deficit to cingulate cortex — the hub of interoception, effort valuation, and salience. Hypothesis: the low-energy region is not merely the most-vulnerable (highest-demand) tissue but the specific locus whose under-supply raises the perceived cost of effort, coupling bioenergetic state to the central-fatigability symptom. Evidence: deficit centered on cingulate @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine; ACC ATP/PCr–cognition link @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine; cingulate role in effort-cost valuation (already-integrated fatigue-PEM relationship). Cert: 0.25. Falsifiable: region-of-interest analysis shows ATP/PCr reduction is strongest in cingulate even after normalizing for regional metabolic demand, and the reduction correlates specifically with effort-aversion/interoceptive scales more than with generic fatigue scales; a purely demand-driven (uniform-with-vulnerability) pattern falsifies the special-locus claim. Consequence: brain-energy and the "everything feels costly" symptom may be the same object — measuring one readout of the other. *origin: brainstorm.*

**1.3** *Glycolysis upregulation is an ATP-budget response to a mitochondrial ceiling (OxPhos caps), and its magnitude in plasma is the metabolomic shadow of a metabolic-safe-mode already in effect.* Germain's exertion-driven glycolysis/gluconeogenesis upregulation is read as "mitochondrial stress" by the authors. Refined hypothesis: the proteomic switch is a *compensatory, load-sensing* response — the cell is defaulting to glycolysis because the OxPhos ceiling (supercomplex/WASF3/PDH-adjacent pathology) cannot meet the demand spike; the plasma proteome then reports *attempted compensation*, whose failure (protein-abundance decoupling from VO₂max/AT @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation) is the PEM correlate. Evidence: glycolysis/gluconeogenesis up + decoupling from VO₂max/AT @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation; sedentary-control arm isolates this from deconditioning @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation; existing metabolic-safe-mode/muscle-trap cascade. Cert: 0.30. Falsifiable: serial metabolomics after CPET show glycolytic intermediates (lactate, pyruvate, alanine) rise and remain elevated in the 24-h PEM window in proportion to the drop in a mitochondrial-function marker, and this ratio is absent in VO₂max-matched sedentary controls; a purely substrate-driven (fed vs fasted) explanation falsifies the ceiling-response claim. Consequence: the blood test after a standard exercise challenge could expose a mitochondrial "speed limit" that resting labs never see. *origin: brainstorm.*

**1.4** *Fragile mitophagy is a "damage-without-clearance" PEM endotype whose defining feature is lysosomal-inefficiency-mediated accumulation of injured mitochondria, not baseline ATP shortfall.* Groysman names the endotype but offers no mechanism by which it is measured or discriminated. Formal hypothesis: in this subtype, exertion generates mitochondrial injury (ROS/DAMPs) at a normal or modest rate, but lysosomal/mitophagic *completion* is rate-limiting, so injured mitochondria accumulate and emit danger signals that sustain the PEM-recovery failure and slow repletion — making PEM severity track injury-to-clearance mismatch rather than resting ATP. Evidence: recovery-failure framing @Groysman2026fragileMitophagy (concept only); cross-species OXPHOS suppression with persistent stress response @Tasoula2026multiomicsLCmito; existing mitophagy/biogenesis-turnover cascade. Cert: 0.20 (concept is unmeasured; certainty reflects plausibility of the formalized distinction). Falsifiable: patients typed as fragile-mitophagy show, on serial post-exertion sampling, elevated markers of *uncleared* damaged mitochondria (e.g., mitophagy-adaptor load, oxidized mtDNA, Parkin/LC3 flux imbalance) that correlate with PEM duration *independently* of resting respiratory capacity; if PEM tracks baseline ATP deficit rather than clearance markers, the "ATP-deficit" model wins and fragile-mitophagy collapses into the existing cascade. Consequence: PEM may be two mechanistically different failures — not enough energy versus failure to clean up after exertion — which need different treatments. *origin: brainstorm.*

**1.5** *Lysosomal-completion inefficiency (fragile mitophagy) links the peripheral muscle defect and the TRPM3/mito-Ca²⁺ immune defect through a shared Ca²⁺-sensitive mitophagic step.* Magawa shows reduced mitochondrial Ca²⁺ influx via TRPM3 in NK cells. Independent hypothesis-bridge: mitochondrial Ca²⁺ is required for efficient mitophagic/lysosomal turnover (mitochondrial Ca²⁺ gates fusion, mPTP, and autophagosome targeting). If a Ca²⁺-influx deficit generalizes beyond NK cells, it could produce both the immune phenotype (Magawa) and an impaired-clearance phenotype (Groysman) from one upstream Ca²⁺-handling lesion — unifying two currently disconnected new findings. Evidence: reduced TRPM3-linked mito-Ca²⁺ influx @Magawa2026TRPM3NKmitoCa (single lab); fragile-mitophagy concept @Groysman2026fragileMitophagy. Cert: 0.18 (two weak/single-lab inputs; the generalization from NK cells to myocytes/neurons is untested). Falsifiable: in myocytes and neurons, pharmacologically blocking mitochondrial Ca²⁺ uniporter (MCU) influx reproduces the fragile-mitophagy clearance signature (impaired mitophagic completion) seen in ME/CFS immune cells; if mito-Ca²⁺ blockade does not impair clearance, the shared-step claim fails. Consequence: a defect in one ion channel's calcium delivery could explain both immune exhaustion and slow post-exertion muscle recovery as one disease mechanism. *origin: brainstorm.*

**1.6** *Persistent OXPHOS suppression is a tissue-hierarchy phenomenon — muscle is the most vulnerable sink and blood the least informative readout of it.* Tasoula finds muscle most suppressed across species to ≥12 months. Hypothesis: the mitochondrial deficit is graded by tissue oxidative dependence and turnover (muscle > other tissues > immune/blood), so blood-based mitochondrial readouts systematically underestimate the tissue-level disease and may be why some blood assays null. Evidence: muscle-most-affected across species/tissues @Tasoula2026multiomicsLCmito; MT-RNR1/2 lowered expression @Keele2026mapMECFSmito; tissue-level findings corpus. Cert: 0.30. Falsifiable: paired blood-PBMC and muscle-biopsy mitochondrial measures in the same patients show the muscle defect exceeds the blood defect by a fixed gradient, and interventions that rescue blood OXPHOS do not rescue muscle unless they reach the tissue; a flat (equal) tissue gradient falsifies the hierarchy. Consequence: relying on blood to measure a muscle-centered energy disease could keep researchers blind to the true target organ. *origin: brainstorm.*

## Category 2 — Research Directions

**2.1** *Highest-priority design: in-vivo ³¹P-MRSI brain-energy provocation protocol (brain "2-day test"), with cognitive-load and submaximal-exercise arms, VO₂max- and effort-matched controls.* Extend Weber-Fahr's resting finding into the temporal domain that PEM lives in. Design: n≥25 ME/CFS, n≥25 recovered-post-COVID, n≥25 sedentary healthy, and — to kill the deconditioning objection before it lands — a VO₂max-matched subgroup. Serial whole-brain ³¹P-MRSI at baseline, immediately post-challenge, and at 24 h and 48 h, plus regional (cingulate/ACC) ATP/PCr and a validated cognitive battery. This is the direct translation of Idea 1.1 and is the single highest-value experiment because it attaches a mechanistically-specific, non-self-report readout to the defining symptom (PEM). Evidence: resting deficit + cognition link @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine; deconditioning-control logic @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation; 2-day CPET precedent. Cert: 0.40 (design feasibility/correctness — that it is the necessary next test, not that it will be positive). Consequence: a non-invasive brain scan taken across a day of rest and a task could, for the first time, image the actual energy slump patients describe after exertion. *origin: brainstorm.*

**2.2** *Dual-compartment PEM study: simultaneous brain ³¹P-MRSI and muscle ³¹P/¹³C-MRS with serial plasma proteomics over one standardized 2-day protocol.* Germain (plasma) and Weber-Fahr (brain) have never been run on the same patients in one window. A design that co-collects brain-energy recovery, muscle metabolic recovery (PCr resynthesis, OxPhos rate), and the exertion proteome (glycolysis/gluconeogenesis switch @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation) across a single 2-day CPET would let the field ask whether the brain and muscle deficits are the same process at different sites, or independent lesions — the load-bearing question for whether "mitochondrial dysfunction" is one disease or several. Evidence: brain readout @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine; exertion proteome @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation; 2-day CPET corpus. Cert: 0.38. Consequence: one well-instrumented day could reveal whether brain fog and muscle crash share an energy mechanism or are two separate failures needing two treatments. *origin: brainstorm.*

**2.3** *Cross-replicate the tissue-hierarchy claim (Idea 1.6) with paired blood/muscle/brain measures and a prospective recovery trajectory.* Tasoula is a secondary re-analysis with disclosed COI and no prospective cohort. The direction is to convert it into a de-novo, prospective, multi-tissue study (PBMC + muscle biopsy + ³¹P-brain-MRS) at baseline and 12 months, in a single well-phenotyped cohort, to test persistence and the muscle>blood gradient with primary data rather than re-analysis. Evidence: Tasoula re-analysis @Tasoula2026multiomicsLCmito; supporting MT-RNR @Keele2026mapMECFSmito. Cert: 0.35 (feasibility of a first primary multi-tissue study). Consequence: a prospective multi-tissue study would tell patients whether the energy defect persists, remits, or worsens over a year — and which tissue to monitor. *origin: brainstorm.*

**2.4** *Lysosomal-mitophagic flux study to operationalize fragile-mitophagy (Idea 1.4) — the gap between the hypothesis and any data.* Groysman provides zero measurements. The decisive direction: quantify mitophagic *flux* (autophagosome formation vs lysosomal degradation) — not just static markers — in patients before and serially after a standardized exertion, using lysosomal-inhibitor pulse (chloroquine/bafilomycin clamp) ex vivo or in accessible cells, to determine whether accumulation is a production problem or a clearance problem. Design needs a pre-specified a priori definition of the fragile-mitophagy endotype so the subtype can be detected, not merely named. Evidence: concept @Groysman2026fragileMitophagy; cross-tissue OXPHOS + stress response @Tasoula2026multiomicsLCmito. Cert: 0.30. Consequence: before "fragile mitophagy" can be a subtype, someone must measure whether the bottleneck is too much damage or too little cleanup — this study does exactly that. *origin: brainstorm.*

**2.5** *Deconditioning-decoupled biomarker discovery protocol: leverage Germain's VO₂max-matching architecture to build a PEM biomarker panel that survives the sedentary-control test.* The field's recurring failure is the deconditioning confound. Germain's contribution is architectural: match on VO₂max/activity AND use a sedentary arm. The direction is to turn that architecture into a reusable discovery pipeline that selects protein/metabolite PEM markers that discriminate ME/CFS from both healthy-active AND healthy-sedentary controls across the 2-day window — yielding markers with the deconditioning objection already answered. Evidence: sedentary-controlled 2×CPET proteomics @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation; protein-abundance decoupling from VO₂max/AT @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation. Cert: 0.35. Consequence: a biomarker panel validated against sedentary-but-healthy people would end the "it's just deconditioning" argument that has shadowed every PEM blood finding. *origin: brainstorm.*

## Category 3 — Drug/Medication Ideas (research-stage targets only)

**3.1** *Autophagy/mitophagy-activating agents (e.g., rapamycin-class, spermidine) targeted specifically to a prospective fragile-mitophagy endotype, contingent on Idea 2.4.* If clearance — not ATP production — is the bottleneck in the fragile-mitophagy subtype, then pro-clearance pharmacology becomes mechanistically indicated *only in that subtype*, and would be predicted to fail (or harm via over-autophagy) in the ATP-deficit subtype. This is a subtype-stratified, mechanism-first design, not a generic "boost mitophagy" claim. Evidence: concept @Groysman2026fragileMitophagy; existing mitophagy/biogenesis corpus. Cert: 0.15. Consequence: a drug that clears damaged cell parts would be tried only in patients whose PEM is a cleanup failure, not an energy shortage — so it is not wasted on the wrong patients. *origin: brainstorm.*

**3.2** *Mitochondrial-Ca²⁺-targeted probe (e.g., MCU/mito-Ca²⁺ modulators) to test whether restoring mitochondrial Ca²⁺ influx rescues the TRPM3-linked immune phenotype.* Magawa identifies reduced mito-Ca²⁺ influx as the immune-cell phenotype. A targeted probe (not a clinical recommendation) in the ex-vivo NK-cell model that raises mitochondrial Ca²⁺ uptake would test whether the deficit is upstream-causal or downstream-epiphenomenal. Evidence: reduced TRPM3-linked mito-Ca²⁺ influx @Magawa2026TRPM3NKmitoCa; TRPM3 ion-channel corpus. Cert: 0.16 (probe-only; single-lab phenotype). Consequence: proves whether the calcium-to-mitochondria delivery problem actually causes the immune-cell weakness, or is a bystander sign. *origin: brainstorm.*

## Category 7 — Mathematical Model Extensions

**7.1** *A two-compartment (brain/muscle) coupled recovery-ODE extension of the existing metabolic model, adding a state variable for ATP/PCr recovery time-constant per compartment.* The existing ch50 models treat a systemic energy pool. Weber-Fahr (brain) and 2-day CPET/muscle-MRS (periphery) suggest two semi-independent compartments with distinct repletion kinetics. Extension: a model with separate brain and muscle ATP/PCr pools, each with its own damage-rate and recovery-time-constant, coupled through a shared circulating exhaustion factor — parameterizable from the dual-compartment study (Idea 2.2) to ask whether PEM is best explained by a common factor or by parallel tissue lesions. Evidence: brain ATP/PCr @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine; exertion proteome @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation; ch50 model corpus. Cert: 0.22 (model plausibility). Falsifiable: fitted against dual-compartment data, the model predicts that perturbing one compartment's recovery constant (e.g., muscle via a one-off task) changes the other compartment's recovery only if the shared-factor term is nonzero; if the data fit better with independent compartments (shared term ≈ 0), the single-disease hypothesis is rejected. Consequence: the math would decide whether "brain fog" and "muscle crash" are two organs failing together or one energy failure rippling between them. *origin: brainstorm.*

**7.2** *An injury-clearance (fragile-mitophagy) ODE: track injured-mitochondrial mass Mᵢ, production k·damage, and clearance rate λ(Mᵢ, lysosomal-capacity); PEM recovery time diverges as clearance saturates.* Formalize Groysman: with first-order damage and a saturating (Michaelis–Menten) lysosomal clearance, the model shows a threshold above which injured mass accumulates and recovery time diverges — reproducing "each crash leaves residual damage." This converts a named-but-unmeasured concept into a parameterized model whose key free parameter (lysosomal capacity) is directly measurable (Idea 2.4). Evidence: concept @Groysman2026fragileMitophagy; muscle-most-affected OXPHOS suppression @Tasoula2026multiomicsLCmito. Cert: 0.18. Falsifiable: the model predicts a specific relationship between repeated-exertion interval and residual (non-recovering) mitochondrial injury; if a subject's serial measures show full recovery between spaced exertions with no cumulative drift regardless of lysosomal-capacity proxy, the divergence prediction fails. Consequence: a simple equation could capture why some patients worsen with each crash — and flag the safe retry interval before damage accumulates. *origin: brainstorm.*

**7.3** *A "ceiling-cap" respiratory term added to the OxPhos rate equation, so glycolysis upregulation is modeled as load-dependent compensation rather than fixed flux.* Existing models let OxPhos flux scale with demand. Germain's decoupling (protein abundance stops tracking VO₂max/AT) suggests a load-dependent ceiling. Extension: model OxPhos as demand-driven up to a hard cap C(t), with the shortfall forcing glycolysis and a detectable by-product signature (the plasma proteome). Fitting the ceiling C and its post-exertion dynamics against 2×CPET proteomics would quantify the "mitochondrial speed limit" and its recovery time. Evidence: glycolysis up + decoupling from VO₂max/AT @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation; OxPhos/supercomplex corpus. Cert: 0.22. Falsifiable: if C is a fixed tissue property, the glycolytic switch fraction should be a deterministic function of demand; observing demand-independent glycolytic variability across matched exertions falsifies a pure ceiling model and requires an additional (e.g., signaling) term. Consequence: the model turns a confusing blood pattern into a measurable number — the ceiling — that could track disease severity and treatment response. *origin: brainstorm.*

## Category 8 — Cross-Disease Bridges

**8.1** *Cross-disease ³¹P-MRS brain-energy bridge: does the cingulate ATP/PCr deficit generalize to non-SARS triggers (post-EBV/other ME/CFS, chemotherapy-related fatigue, MS fatigue, depression-fatigue)?* Weber-Fahr is SARS-specific. The bridge hypothesis is that cingulate high-energy-phosphate depression is a shared final-pathway readout of central fatigue across etiologies — if so it is a *state biomarker of the fatigue phenotype* rather than a COVID-specific finding, and would generalize to non-Long-COVID ME/CFS (which Weber-Fahr's subgroup hints at) and other fatiguing conditions. Evidence: reduced ATP/PCr @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine; subgroup meeting ME/CFS criteria @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine. Cert: 0.25. Falsifiable: if post-EBV ME/CFS and non-COVID fatiguing conditions show the same cingulate ATP/PCr depression while non-fatigued recovered controls do not, the shared-pathway claim is supported; a COVID-only signature falsifies it. Consequence: one brain-energy readout could serve fatigue across many diseases, pointing toward shared energy-restoring treatments. *origin: brainstorm.*

**8.2** *Ryback-null bridge: what the serum-transfer failure implies for other proposed humoral-transferable defects (antibody/IgG and EV-mediated) across post-infectious syndromes.* The Ryback null specifically fails serum-transfer to *myoblasts in vitro*. Cross-disease implication: any hypothesis relying on a freely-circulating serum factor damaging a distant *healthy quiescent target cell in vitro* should be treated with the same suspicion — including antibody/IgG-mediates-energy claims and EV cargo claims — while tissue-resident, activity-dependent, or CNS-compartmentalized mechanisms (which the null does not touch) remain viable. Evidence: null @Ryback2026myoblastNull; serum-transfer corpus (biofabrication/Mughal, IgG-energy). Cert: 0.40 (that the null should lower prior on the whole class of *serum-to-quiescent-cell in-vitro transfer* claims, not on tissue mechanisms). Falsifiable: other in-vitro serum/plasma-transfer mitochondrial assays on quiescent target cells will similarly fail to replicate at scale, while co-culture, tissue, or activity-primed assays succeed — localizing the transferable-defect layer precisely. Consequence: researchers would stop chasing a hypothetical blood-borne toxin that damages resting cells and redirect to mechanisms that need living tissue or activity to appear. *origin: brainstorm.*

**8.3** *Mitochondrial-Ca²⁺ (TRPM3/MCU) bridge across NK-immune, neuronal, and muscle compartments — is defective mitochondrial Ca²⁺ handling a generalizable post-infectious lesion?* Magawa's NK finding, Groysman's clearance concept, and the muscle/ischemic-Ca²⁺ cascade all implicate mitochondrial Ca²⁺ handling. Cross-disease bridge: if mito-Ca²⁺ gating is impaired across cell types after infectious triggers, it would unify immune dysfunction, CNS energy failure, and muscle recovery-failure under one ion-handling theme and point to a shared druggable node. Evidence: NK mito-Ca²⁺ influx deficit @Magawa2026TRPM3NKmitoCa; concept @Groysman2026fragileMitophagy; existing AIM-ischemic mito-Ca²⁺ cascade. Cert: 0.18 (generalization untested). Falsifiable: measuring the same mitochondrial-Ca²⁺-influx deficit in neurons and myocytes of the same patients (or post-infectious animal models) would generalize the lesion; a tissue-restricted pattern falsifies a systemic Ca²⁺-handling model. Consequence: if calcium delivery to the cell's power plants is broken everywhere, one ion-channel target could treat immune, brain, and muscle symptoms together. *origin: brainstorm.*

## Category 9 — Diagnostic / Biomarker Ideas

**9.1** *³¹P-MRSI cingulate/ACC ATP/PCr as an objective neurocognitive-fatigue biomarker and (in provocation form) a PEM endophenotype discriminator.* Direct translation of the highest-certainty new finding: resting ACC ATP/PCr already correlates with cognition @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine. A candidate biomarker is a brain-energy index (regional ATP/PCr, normalized to a reference region) tested for (a) discriminating ME/CFS/Long-COVID fatigue from recovered and healthy, (b) tracking cognitive performance, and (c) — in provocation form (Idea 2.1) — separating PEM endotypes by recovery slope. This is non-invasive, objective, and CNS-relevant, targeting the symptom (brain fog) most resistant to objective measurement. Evidence: deficit + cognition correlation @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine; ME/CFS subgroup similarity @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine. Cert: 0.35. Falsifiable: the index must achieve pre-specified discrimination AUC and cognitive correlation in an independent cohort, and must improve over self-report alone in a net-reclassification analysis; failure on either falsifies its diagnostic value. Consequence: brain fog — the symptom patients can least describe — could get a numeric, repeatable imaging score. *origin: brainstorm.*

**9.2** *A deconditioning-validated exertion-response biomarker panel (protein/metabolite change-score over the 2-day window) that discriminates ME/CFS from sedentary-but-healthy.* Most candidate biomarkers fail the "is it just deconditioning?" test. The design (Idea 2.5) produces a *change-score panel* — exertion-induced protein/metabolite shifts validated against both active and sedentary controls — rather than resting levels. Germain's decoupling of protein abundance from VO₂max/AT @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation is itself a candidate diagnostic signature: an exertion response that does NOT track fitness is disease-specific and deconditioning-proof by construction. Evidence: sedentary-controlled 2×CPET proteomics + decoupling @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation. Cert: 0.32. Falsifiable: the panel must separate ME/CFS from sedentary controls on the 2-day change-score with pre-specified AUC, and its discrimination must persist after covarying for VO₂max/activity objectively measured; if sedentary controls show the same exercise response, the marker is deconditioning, not disease. Consequence: a blood test that stays abnormal only in truly-ill people — not in healthy people who are merely unfit — would be the strongest answer yet to the deconditioning dismissal. *origin: brainstorm.*

**9.3** *An "abundance-decoupling index" (ADI): the mismatch between exertion-driven protein change and fitness-driven expectation, computed per patient, as a numeric metabolic-maladaptation score.* Operationalize Germain's key observation into a per-patient scalar: for each exertion-responsive protein, compute actual change vs change predicted from the subject's VO₂max/AT trajectory; the residual (decoupling) is summed into an ADI. High ADI = metabolic response decoupled from fitness = the maladaptation signal. This converts a group-level finding into a personalized, interpretable number usable for severity tracking and treatment response. Evidence: protein-abundance decoupling from VO₂max/AT @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation. Cert: 0.28. Falsifiable: ADI must correlate with PEM severity and clinical trajectory better than any single protein or resting level, and must be reproducible across two independent 2-day sessions (test–retest); non-reproducibility or no severity link falsifies its use. Consequence: a single "decoupling score" could summarize in one number how disconnected a patient's energy machinery is from their actual fitness. *origin: brainstorm.*

**9.4** *MT-RNR1/2 expression as a low-cost screening/deep-phenotyping axis within existing cohorts (DecodeME/mapMECFS), not as a standalone diagnostic.* Keele is in-silico supporting only, but its value is cheap: RNA-measured mitochondrial-gene (MT-RNR1/2) expression in already-sequenced cohorts could be cross-checked against severity, duration, and subtype cheaply before any expensive imaging/proteomics is justified, providing a coarse, scalable first-pass enrichment signal. Evidence: lower MT-RNR1/2 expression in two studies @Keele2026mapMECFSmito. Cert: 0.15 (supporting only; no mechanism). Falsifiable: MT-RNR1/2 expression must correlate with an independent mitochondrial or clinical measure in prospective samples, or it is noise; no correlation falsifies its screening value. Consequence: researchers could mine data they already have to pick which patients most need the expensive brain-energy or proteomic tests. *origin: brainstorm.*

## Category 10 — Reasons This Mechanism May NOT Be Relevant / Generalize

**10.1** *The in-vivo brain ATP/PCr reduction may be a Long-COVID-restricted or cognition-linked epiphenomenon, not a core ME/CFS energy lesion.* Weber-Fahr is SARS-Long-COVID (with an ME/CFS-criteria subgroup) and low-moderate n. The finding could be (a) a correlate of cognitive load/depression/medication rather than an energy-generating deficit, (b) specific to the post-COVID trajectory rather than general ME/CFS, or (c) an MRSI artifact (partial-volume, T1/T2 effects, PCr normalization masking ATP changes). If ATP/PCr reduction is downstream of reduced neural activity (a consequence, not a cause of fatigue), then targeting it therapeutically would be irrelevant. Evidence: single low-moderate-n study @WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine; cognition correlation could be reverse-causal. Cert: 0.45 (that the generalizability-to-whole-ME/CFS and causality-to-fatigue are both unestablished). Falsifiable: the deficit must replicate in non-COVID ME/CFS, persist after controlling for cognitive effort/depression, and precede or predict fatigue onset (not merely co-occur) for it to be causal; a COVID-only or co-occurrence-only pattern bounds its relevance. Consequence: a striking scan could turn out to show tired brains acting tired rather than a root energy failure — more work is needed before it is read as the mechanism. *origin: brainstorm.*

**10.2** *The exertion-proteome and brain findings may largely recapitulate ordinary deconditioning/effort despite the sedentary-control arm.* Even with a sedentary arm, residual confounds remain: sedentary *healthy* controls are not matched on the autonomic, immune, or activity-rest *cycling* patterns of ME/CFS (patients oscillate between crashes and low activity), and effort-exerted or perception-of-effort differences across a maximal CPET can drive metabolic/proteomic differences without a mitochondrial cause. Germain reduces but does not eliminate the axis. Evidence: sedentary arm present but not matched on illness-specific activity-cycling @Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation; VO₂max/AT decoupling could reflect effort differences. Cert: 0.35 (that deconditioning/effort still explains part of the signal). Falsifiable: if bed-rest and illness-cycling-matched controls reproduce the "maladaptation" proteome and the brain-energy recovery pattern, the disease-specificity claim weakens; failure of matched controls to reproduce it falsifies this concern. Consequence: even the best-designed comparison cannot fully erase the question of whether patients' findings reflect their illness or their enforced inactivity — the control design must be pushed further. *origin: brainstorm.*

**10.3** *Persistent OXPHOS suppression to ≥12 months may reflect residual immune/virologic activity or general post-acute illness rather than a stable mitochondrial disease.* Tasoula's cross-species/tissue suppression could be a shadow of ongoing immune activation (which also consumes and suppresses OXPHOS) rather than intrinsic mitochondrial dysfunction, and re-analysis design + COI limit confidence. Evidence: sustained suppression + immune activation together @Tasoula2026multiomicsLCmito; secondary re-analysis @Tasoula2026multiomicsLCmito. Cert: 0.35. Falsifiable: if OXPHOS suppression resolves when immune-activation markers normalize (in patients and animals), it is a downstream shadow of immunity, not a persistent intrinsic defect; persistence of suppression independent of immune markers supports an intrinsic-lesion reading. Consequence: what looks like a fixed mitochondrial disease might be a long tail of immune activity wearing the same costume. *origin: brainstorm.*

## Category 11 — Null Hypothesis Assessment

**11.1** *Null: ME/CFS serum factors do not transfer a mitochondrial defect to healthy cells in vitro — the serum-cell-transfer layer of the field is refuted for this readout.* Ryback's pre-registered, large (>1,700 Seahorse runs) null @Ryback2026myoblastNull shows no maximal-respiratory-capacity difference between ME/CFS and control sera on healthy myoblasts. As a *negative*, its quality is high (pre-registered, large, independent of the original lab). The null it supports is narrow but real: healthy *myoblasts* exposed to ME/CFS *serum* show no transferable mitochondrial defect. This refutes the specific "freely-circulating serum factor damages resting healthy muscle mitochondria in vitro" claim (the layer the original Fluge myoblast work rested on) but does NOT touch tissue-resident, in-vivo, activity-dependent, or CNS/immune-compartment mechanisms (Germain, Weber-Fahr, Charlton bed-rest, muscle-biopsy WASF3/PDH). Cert: 0.55 (that this specific in-vitro layer is null; NOT that mitochondrial dysfunction in vivo is null). Falsifiable: the null is overturned only if an independent large pre-registered replication finds a difference under matched conditions; if it remains null, the field must stop citing the Fluge myoblast serum line as evidence for a circulating mitotoxic factor. Consequence: one prominent story — that ME/CFS blood carries something that damages healthy muscle cells in a dish — looks like it does not survive careful retesting, though the disease's energy problems in living tissue remain real. *origin: brainstorm.*

**11.2** *Null: brain-energy and exertion-proteome differences, once deconditioning/effort/immune are fully controlled, are small or absent — the "core mitochondrial energy crisis" is over-weighted relative to central/immune drivers.* Weber-Fahr (brain) and Germain (proteome) both show group differences, but neither has been tested against the full confound stack (Idea 10.2, 10.3). The honest null for this cycle: after VO₂max-cycling matching, acute-rest control, immune-activity covariate, and reverse-causality checks, the brain-ATP and proteome effects shrink to non-significant or small, indicating the mitochondrial-energy-deficit framing captures a real but minor component of the syndrome relative to CNS-autonomic and immune regulation. Cert: 0.30 (that this over-weighting null is plausible, given these are correlational, moderate-signal, recently-collected findings). Falsifiable: the null is rejected only if the brain-ATP and proteome effects survive the full confound stack with pre-specified effect sizes in independent cohorts; if they shrink to null, the "energy-crisis-as-primary" framing needs de-weighting. Consequence: the field could discover it has over-attributed ME/CFS to a raw energy shortage when the true lever is upstream control systems. *origin: brainstorm.*

## Category 12 — Evidence-Quality Concerns

**12.1** *Three of the seven new items are in-silico, secondary re-analysis, or single-lab, and two carry disclosed conflict or authorship/cohort flags — the "new reinforcement" is thinner than the count suggests.* Of the seven additions, only Weber-Fahr (controlled primary, top-tier) and Germain (controlled primary, sedentary arm) are strong primaries; Tasoula is a secondary re-analysis with disclosed COI; Keele is in-silico at ~0.14; Magawa is single-lab small-n (n=10) on the already-heavily-corpus'd TRPM3/Marshall-Gradisnik line; Groysman has zero data; Ryback is a null. Weighting risk: the convergent "mitochondrial dysfunction is reinforced" narrative could overstate certainty because the *strongest-quality* items (Ryback null) cut against a specific serum layer, while several supportive items are weak-tier. Cert: 0.75 (that over-aggregation is a real risk). Consequence: counting papers can make the evidence look stronger than the quality of most individual papers justifies. *origin: brainstorm; evidence-hygiene concern.*

**12.2** *Cohort/site non-independence inflates apparent replication.* Germain 2025 shares the Cornell (Hanson/Giloteaux) site/biobank with existing corpus Germain 2017/2020/2022; Keele uses DecodeME/mapMECFS shared biobanks; Magawa is the same Griffith TRPM3 lab as a large existing corpus block. These are not independent confirmations — they are the same cohorts/labs re-sampled, which inflates the perceived weight of reinforcement across the cycle. Cert: 0.70 (that non-independence is real and materially reduces the added information). Consequence: some of this "new" mitochondrial evidence comes from the same patient groups and labs already in the paper, so it reinforces less than it appears to. *origin: brainstorm; provenance concern.*

**12.3** *The brain-MRS and proteomic findings are group-level, correlational, moderate-n, and not yet tied to a disease-specific molecular cause — they cannot yet distinguish disease effect from state, treatment, or measurement confound.* Weber-Fahr (n=27 vs 23) and Germain (132 subjects) are moderate-n; neither randomizes or longitudinally follows the pre-to-post-disease transition; neither rules out medication, cognitive state, or MRSI methodological confounds; Germain is cross-sectional-ish within the Cornell cohort. Risk: these become "next biomarker" hype before independent, pre-registered, longitudinal replication with full confound control. Cert: 0.65 (that they are preliminary as biomarkers/causal evidence). Consequence: striking group scans and proteomes still need independent, bigger, blinded replication before they can support any diagnostic or causal claim. *origin: brainstorm; standard evidence-quality concern.*
