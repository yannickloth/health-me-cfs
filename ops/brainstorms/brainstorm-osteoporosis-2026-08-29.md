## Category 1: Novel Hypotheses

### 1.1 Bone loss in ME/CFS follows a *severity-gradient*, not a flat "all patients at risk" — and circulating turnover markers (sclerostin/CTX/P1NP) can detect the unloading pathway before any BMD change
**Mechanism:** H1's disuse chain (mechanical unloading → osteocyte mechanosensing loss → sclerostin↑ → RANKL/OPG imbalance → resorption) predicts a **dose–response**: mild/ambulatory ME/CFS (intermittent loading) should show minimal or no bone loss, whereas severe/bedbound patients (sustained unloading, Rolvien2022 precedent) should show measurable decline. This makes the hypothesis *more* precise and testable than a flat prevalence claim — and it implies bone-turnover markers (serum sclerostin, CTX, P1NP) change on weeks–months, well before BMD drops (months–years). If true, the moderate–severe/bedbound subset (not all ME/CFS) is the only group needing bone surveillance, and the paper's "risk" claim should be restricted to that subset.
**Evidence:** Rolvien2022 (0.39, unloading→sclerostin→resorption); Qin2013 (0.19, turnover axis); Hart2021 (0.22, deconditioning). No direct severity-stratified ME/CFS BMD data (research gap).
**Certainty:** 0.30 — the disuse dose–response is well-established in bedrest/immobilization; its severity-stratified instantiation in ME/CFS is unmeasured.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Across a severity-stratified ME/CFS cohort, serum sclerostin and CTX will rise and P1NP will fall monotonically from mild→severe, and will correlate with objective immobility (e.g. step count, recumbent hours) and with BMD decline on follow-up, independent of age/sex/vitamin D.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If true, only severely affected, largely bedbound patients — not all ME/CFS patients — are the ones needing bone-density checks, which spares most patients unnecessary testing.

### 1.2 Chronic low-grade inflammation — not just mechanical unloading — may suppress bone formation and shift RANKL/OPG toward resorption, a *competing or additive* novel mechanism to H1
**Mechanism:** ME/CFS shows chronic inflammatory/immune dysregulation. Inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α) drive RANKL↑ and suppress osteoblast activity, causing bone loss independently of loading. If a subset of ME/CFS patients is inflammatory-driven, bone loss could occur even in *ambulatory* patients with normal activity — the opposite prediction to hypothesis 1.1. This matters because it (a) competes with the pure-disuse reading of H1, and (b) would predict bone risk in a group 1.1 says is low-risk. The two mechanisms are distinguishable by turnover-marker profile (disuse→high sclerostin; inflammation→high RANKL/low P1NP with elevated inflammatory cytokines).
**Evidence:** Rolvien2022 (0.39, disuse axis); Qin2013 (0.19, RANKL/OPG axis); ME/CFS inflammatory-cytokine literature (indirect, no bone-specific data). No ME/CFS bone-turnover or cytokine-bone study (research gap).
**Certainty:** 0.20 — inflammation→bone loss is established in other conditions; the ME/CFS inflammatory-bone link is speculative and unmeasured.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a severity-stratified cohort, a subgroup of ambulatory ME/CFS patients will show elevated CTX and suppressed P1NP with high IL-6/TNF-α but *normal* sclerostin and normal objective immobility — indicating an inflammatory rather than disuse driver.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If true, some patients with near-normal activity could still lose bone — so activity level alone would not tell a patient their bone risk.

## Category 2: Research Directions

### 2.1 Severity-stratified cross-sectional BMD + bone-turnover-marker study in ME/CFS — directly fills the H1 gap
**Design:** A cross-sectional study across three strata (mild/ambulatory; moderate; severe/bedbound, plus very severe/bedbound) measuring DXA (hip/spine), serum sclerostin, CTX, P1NP, 25(OH)D, calcium, plus an objective immobility measure (step count, recumbent hours). Endpoint: BMD Z-scores and turnover markers across strata, adjusted for age/sex/BMI/medication. This is the single most decisive test of H1 and of idea 1.1/1.2, and directly resolves the "no direct ME/CFS BMD study" research gap.
**Evidence:** Rolvien2022 (0.39); Qin2013 (0.19); Hart2021 (0.22) — all provide the mechanistic frame but are non-ME/CFS; Earl2017 (0.80) shows vitamin D can be measured and is not deficient overall.
**Certainty:** 0.50 for feasibility (DXA + serum markers + activity monitoring are routine, non-invasive); 0.30 for finding a severity-monotonic BMD/turnover gradient.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If H1/disuse holds, moderate–severe and bedbound strata will show lower hip/spine BMD Z-scores and higher CTX/lower P1NP than mild/ambulatory, with sclerostin tracking immobility; if it fails, BMD will be uniform across strata.
**Non-specialist consequence:** This one non-invasive study would tell ME/CFS patients (and doctors) which — if any — patients actually lose bone, instead of assuming risk from bedrest in other diseases.
**Tier:** 1
`origin: brainstorm`

### 2.2 Re-analysis of Earl2017 stratified by ME/CFS severity — does the "no vitamin D deficiency" finding hold in the bedbound subgroup?
**Design:** Earl2017 (BMJ Open, 92 CFS/ME vs 94 controls) reported *higher* 25(OH)D in patients driven by supplements and no fatigue–vitD association, but the cohort was **not severity-stratified**. A subgroup re-analysis (or new cohort) restricted to moderate–severe/bedbound patients tests whether the "no deficiency" conclusion generalizes to the very subgroup where housebound UV-limited deficiency (H2) is mechanistically expected. Endpoint: 25(OH)D and deficiency prevalence in the severe/bedbound stratum vs ambulatory patients and controls.
**Evidence:** Earl2017 (0.80, direct cohort but non-stratified — its severity gap is the core limitation); H2 mechanism (housebound → low sun exposure).
**Certainty:** 0.45 for feasibility (re-analysis of existing data or a small severe-subset cohort); 0.30 for finding deficiency concentrated in the severe stratum.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If H2 holds, the severe/bedbound stratum will show lower 25(OH)D and higher deficiency prevalence than ambulatory ME/CFS and controls, even though the pooled cohort does not; if H2 is fully wrong, deficiency prevalence will be equal across strata.
**Non-specialist consequence:** It would settle whether the sickest, most housebound patients are the one group that does need vitamin D attention, despite the reassuring pooled finding.
**Tier:** 1

## Category 10: Reasons This Mechanism May NOT Be Relevant to ME/CFS  *(MANDATORY)*

### 10.1 ME/CFS unloading is *partial and intermittent*, not the continuous immobilization that drives disuse bone loss — the threshold may never be crossed
**Mechanism relevance risk:** The bedrest/immobilization precedent (Rolvien2022) involves continuous, near-total unloading (spaceflight, spinal-cord injury, strict bed rest). ME/CFS moderate–severe patients, while reduced, typically retain some weight-bearing (transfers, toileting, brief ambulation). Bone's mechanosensing responds to *low* but non-zero load, and disuse bone loss is dose-dependent. If ME/CFS unloading stays above the bone-loss threshold, H1's mechanism would be quantitatively true (unloading→sclerostin) but clinically negligible — no measurable BMD loss. This is the strongest reason the osteoporosis-risk claim may fail despite a correct mechanism.
**Evidence:** Rolvien2022 (0.39, continuous-unloading basis of the precedent); Hart2021 (0.22, deconditioning spectrum); Qin2013 (0.19, muscle-load dependence).
**Certainty:** 0.45 that partial/intermittent ME/CFS unloading does not reach the continuous-unloading bone-loss threshold.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If ME/CFS BMD is preserved across all severities (strata do not diverge in idea 2.1), and sclerostin does not track immobility, then H1's threshold is not crossed in ME/CFS and the disuse mechanism is mechanistically present but clinically irrelevant.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A patient with reduced activity but who still moves around each day might lose no meaningful bone at all — the worry could be unfounded for all but the most bedbound.
**Tier:** 1

### 10.2 Even if disuse bone loss occurs, ME/CFS patients may face *low fracture risk* and the interventions carry harm — so the osteoporosis angle may be net-irrelevant to clinical care
**Mechanism relevance risk:** Bone loss only matters clinically when it leads to fractures. ME/CFS patients have low falls risk (reduced activity, few high-impact exposures) and a relatively short exposure window vs decades of post-menopausal bone loss. Moreover, the interventions are harm-laden: bisphosphonates→atypical femur fracture (Shane2014), calcium-only→MI (Bolland2010), anabolic side-effects overlapping ME/CFS (TaylorSaag2019), and bedbound administration constraints (Arjunan2023). If the absolute fracture risk is low and the treatments are risky, the osteoporosis topic in ME/CFS may contribute *more iatrogenic risk than benefit* — the mechanism (H1) may be real yet the clinical consequence and treatment net negative.
**Evidence:** Shane2014 (0.40, AFF); Bolland2010 (0.36, calcium MI); TaylorSaag2019 (0.29, anabolic overlap); Arjunan2023 (0.24, administration constraints).
**Certainty:** 0.40 that any disuse bone loss in ME/CFS does not translate into clinically significant fracture risk, so treatment is net-harmful.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In severe/bedbound ME/CFS cohorts (or analogous bedbound populations), osteoporosis-treatment initiation will show no fracture-risk reduction that offsets the AFF/MI/anabolic AE risk; fracture rates in untreated patients will be low.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Even a real small bone-density drop might not need treating — because for these patients the pills carry more risk than the fractures they prevent.

## Category 11: Null Hypothesis Assessment — "What if osteoporosis/bone health has no role?"  *(MANDATORY)*

### 11.1 Null: Disuse bone loss in ME/CFS is real but clinically negligible — H1–H4 all fail to change management
**Assessment:** If H1's threshold is not crossed (10.1) and bone loss does not raise fracture risk (10.2), then the entire osteoporosis-risk edifice collapses to: no measurable BMD loss (null H1), no deficiency beyond general population (null H2/H3 — consistent with Earl2017's pooled higher vitamin D), and no achievable exercise that matters (null H4). Under this null, the bone-health topic in ME/CFS reduces to a `#limitation`/`#practical-warning` noting the *hypothetical* risk and the *concrete* harm of premature supplementation — with no causal claim and no surveillance mandate. The Earl2017 pooled finding already points partly in this direction.
**Evidence:** Earl2017 (0.80, no deficiency, no fatigue association); Rolvien2022 (0.39) only partially transfers given partial unloading (10.1).
**Certainty:** 0.40 that the osteoporosis-risk claim is clinically irrelevant even if the disuse mechanism is physiologically present.
**Falsifiable prediction:** No severity-stratified BMD study will show a fracture-relevant BMD deficit in ME/CFS, and no treatment will show a net benefit over its harms.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The honest bottom line could be "bone loss is a theoretical worry here, not a real one — don't take bone drugs or high-dose supplements without a proven deficiency."
**Tier:** 1
`origin: brainstorm`

### 11.2 Null: The vitamin-D/calcium *deficiency* premise is wrong in ME/CFS — Earl2017 generalizes, and the bone-health framing shifts from "treat deficiency" to "avoid iatrogenic harm"
**Assessment:** Earl2017 (0.80) directly contradicts H2 and the MEA deficiency premise: higher, not lower, 25(OH)D and no fatigue association, in a non-severity-stratified cohort. If this generalizes (idea 2.2 finds no bedbound-deficiency pocket), then the two nutrient-deficiency hypotheses (H2, H3) are refuted. The residual defensible content is *harm avoidance*: the main risk in the bone-health space is not untreated deficiency but over-treatment (calcium→MI, bisphosphonate→AFF, anabolic side-effect overlap). The paper's "bone health" section then correctly de-emphasizes routine supplementation and emphasizes not prescribing without indication.
**Evidence:** Earl2017 (0.80); Shane2014 (0.40); Bolland2010 (0.36); TaylorSaag2019 (0.29).
**Certainty:** 0.55 that the deficiency premise (H2/H3) is wrong for the general ME/CFS population, leaving harm-avoidance as the main bone-health content.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A severity-stratified re-analysis (idea 2.2) will find no excess vitamin-D deficiency in ME/CFS (including bedbound) vs controls; calcium status will not be depressed.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Patients would be told "your vitamin D and calcium are probably fine — the bigger risk is over-supplementing, not deficiency."

## Category 12: Evidence Quality Concerns  *(MANDATORY)*

### 12.1 Earl2017 — the only direct ME/CFS study — is not severity-stratified, so its reassuring "no deficiency" result may not generalize to the sickest patients
**Assessment:** Earl2017 (0.80) is the single direct anchor and drives the contradiction of H2. But it pooled mild-to-severe ME/CFS and was supplement-exposed (higher 25(OH)D was supplement-driven). A bedbound, UV-limited, possibly lower-supplement subgroup could be deficient and be masked by pooled averages. This is a selection/generalizability concern: the very subgroup where H2 predicts deficiency (severe/housebound) may be underrepresented or averaged away. Until stratified, the "no deficiency" claim should be read as population-level, not universal.
**Evidence:** Earl2017 (0.80, non-stratified design); H2 mechanism; research gap (no severity-stratified vitamin D study).
**Certainty:** 0.50 that the pooled Earl2017 result underrepresents or masks deficiency in the severe/bedbound stratum.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A severity-stratified vitamin-D analysis will show lower 25(OH)D and higher deficiency in the severe stratum than the pooled Earl2017 estimate suggests.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The reassuring "ME/CFS patients are not vitamin-D deficient" finding may simply not apply to the most housebound patients.
**Tier:** 1
`origin: brainstorm`

### 12.2 Every mechanistic anchor is non-ME/CFS and mostly animal/review — external validity to ME/CFS is unestablished, so the 0.27–0.39 discounted certainties cannot bear mechanistic weight
**Assessment:** The entire mechanistic chain rests on Rolvien2022 (clinical review of immobilization, 0.39), Hart2021 (narrative review, 0.22), and Qin2013 (SCI *rat* model, 0.19). None is an ME/CFS population, none measures ME/CFS-specific unloading magnitude, and the bedrest precedent assumes continuous immobilization that ME/CFS may not reach (10.1). Qin2013's translation is doubly discounted (animal, non-ME/CFS). These anchors establish that *some* unloading causes bone loss, not that *ME/CFS unloading* does. All mechanistic weight above is therefore speculation by construction, and H1's 0.40 is generous.
**Evidence:** Rolvien2022 (0.39); Hart2021 (0.22); Qin2013 (0.19); zero direct ME/CFS BMD/OP/calcium studies (research gap).
**Certainty:** 0.90 that no current evidence establishes an ME/CFS-specific bone-loss claim beyond low-certainty speculation.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A severity-stratified ME/CFS BMD/turnover study will either establish or refute the disuse bridge; until then all bone-loss claims are unverified extrapolation from other populations.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Everything said about osteoporosis risk in ME/CFS is borrowed from bedrest and animal studies, not measured in ME/CFS patients themselves.
**Tier:** 1
