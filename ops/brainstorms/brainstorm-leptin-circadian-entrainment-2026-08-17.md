## Scope note

The mechanism under scrutiny: metabolic signalling (leptin) can modulate circadian re-entrainment speed via a DMH LepR→SCN circuit (Tang 2023, Faber 2021), is reciprocally gated by the clock (Osorio-Mendoza 2025), and can be engaged therapeutically by encapsulated leptin-producing cells (Fleury 2026). The ME/CFS-specific data are cautionary: elevated circulating leptin (Domingo 2024) vs. null at baseline (Cleare 2001); light, not metabolic signalling, is the dominant circadian lever (Cambras 2026); melatonin/phototherapy gave no CFS symptom benefit (Williams 2002).

Every idea below carries `origin: brainstorm`. Certainties are the generator's provisional self-assessment (0.0–1.0) and are independently reassessed in Phase 5.

## Category 1 — Novel Hypotheses

### 1a. Leptin-resistance entrainment deficit — elevated leptin desensitizes the metabolic input to the SCN, leaving ME/CFS entrainment dependent on a light signal that is itself unstable

**Mechanistic rationale.** Chronic elevation of a hormone classically desensitizes its receptor signalling (leptin resistance, documented in obesity via SOCS3/PTP1B feedback on LepR). If ME/CFS patients have chronically elevated circulating leptin (Domingo 2024), their DMH LepR neurons — the very cells that relay metabolic state to the SCN and shift clock phase (Tang 2023; Faber 2021) — may be desensitized. The consequence is a *specific* entrainment deficit: the metabolic arm of circadian reset is blunted, so the SCN must rely on light alone. In ME/CFS, light exposure is already unstable/abnormal (Cambras 2026). The hypothesis unifies two otherwise-separate findings: elevated leptin AND light-dominant circadian instability are not competing explanations — the former *causes* over-dependence on the latter, and the latter is broken.

**Evidence link.** Tang 2023 (DMH LepR→SCN entrainment circuit, discounted 0.38); Faber 2021 (DMH LepR diurnal coupling, 0.35); Domingo 2024 (elevated leptin, 0.65); Cambras 2026 (light-dominant instability, 0.65). Leptin-resistance biology is established in obesity but untested in ME/CFS central circuits.

**Certainty: 0.15.** Leptin resistance is real; its localization to the DMH LepR→SCN circuit in ME/CFS is entirely untested.

**Falsifiable prediction.** ME/CFS patients with the highest plasma leptin show the *least* phase-shift response to a controlled light pulse (or the poorest re-entrainment after a schedule shift) relative to low-leptin patients — i.e., a negative correlation between circulating leptin and central entrainment responsiveness that is NOT explained by BMI. Refuted if phase-shift magnitude is uncorrelated with leptin after BMI control.

**Non-specialist consequence.** If true, a patient's body clock would be "deaf" to one of the two signals that normally reset it — food/metabolic state — forcing it to lean entirely on light, which ME/CFS patients already process abnormally; this would reframe sleep-wake instability as a two-signal failure, not a single light problem. (Origin: brainstorm)

### 1b. Circadian-metabolic vicious cycle — the bidirectional coupling means mis-timed leptin both reflects and perpetuates circadian disruption

**Mechanistic rationale.** The coupling is bidirectional, not one-way: the SCN clock gates when and how strongly leptin acts (Osorio-Mendoza 2025), while leptin signals back to shift the SCN (Tang 2023; Faber 2021). This creates the conditions for a self-reinforcing loop: a destabilized clock mis-times the daily leptin surge → mis-timed metabolic signalling feeds a wrong-phase signal back to the SCN → further phase instability. Once entered, the loop is hard to break from either side alone, which would explain why correcting a single input (light, or melatonin, per Williams 2002) fails to stabilize ME/CFS circadian rhythms.

**Evidence link.** Osorio-Mendoza 2025 (clock-gated leptin, 0.35); Tang 2023 (leptin→SCN, 0.38); Williams 2002 (single-input intervention null, already in bib); Thomas 2026 framework (neuroendocrine timing dynamics, already in bib).

**Certainty: 0.12.** The loop is mechanistically coherent but no ME/CFS study has measured both sides in the same cohort, let alone causally.

**Falsifiable prediction.** In a longitudinal ME/CFS cohort, within-patient changes in circadian phase instability temporally co-occur with (and cross-predict) changes in the diurnal timing of the leptin peak, such that each predicts the other at the next timepoint (cross-lagged correlation) — refuted if phase instability and leptin timing vary independently over time.

**Non-specialist consequence.** If the loop is real, ME/CFS circadian problems would be a two-sided feedback cycle rather than a one-way "broken clock," which would explain why single fixes (light box, melatonin) don't hold — and would point to interventions that break the loop rather than push one lever. (Origin: brainstorm)

### 1c. Elevated leptin in ME/CFS is an immune epiphenomenon, not a functional circadian signal — the Fleury lever is therefore irrelevant to the ME/CFS circadian defect

**Mechanistic rationale.** Leptin is pleiotropic: it is both an adiposity/energy signal and a pro-inflammatory cytokine (structurally an IL-6-family cytokine, produced by adipose tissue and immune cells). ME/CFS is characterized by low-grade immune activation and metabolic dysregulation. The elevated circulating leptin in ME/CFS (Domingo 2024) may therefore be a downstream marker of adipose/immune activation — an *output* of the disease state — rather than a functional input to the SCN. If the elevated leptin is epiphenomenal, then the Fleury 2026 finding (raise leptin → accelerate entrainment) has no application to ME/CFS: adding more of a marker that is not doing circadian work cannot reset the clock, and the "leptin factory" would push a non-causal biomarker further in the wrong direction.

**Evidence link.** Domingo 2024 (elevated, 0.65); Cleare 2001 (baseline null, 0.70 — suggests any elevation is modest and state-dependent); Broderick 2013 (leptin as immune-metabolic marker in GWI, already in bib).

**Certainty: 0.25.** The epiphenomenon reading is the most parsimonious account of the null-vs-elevated discrepancy and is consistent with leptin's known immune role.

**Falsifiable prediction.** After adjusting Domingo-style cohort data for adiposity (BMI/body-fat %), sex, and immune-activation markers (CRP/IL-6), the group difference in circulating leptin attenuates to non-significance — i.e., the elevation is fully explained by confounders, not by disease-specific circadian biology. Refuted if a robust leptin elevation survives full confounder adjustment.

**Non-specialist consequence.** If leptin is just a bystander signal, then studying or targeting it as a circadian treatment is a dead end for ME/CFS — the real question would shift to whatever *drives* the elevation (adiposity, immune activation), which is a different research target entirely. (Origin: brainstorm)

---

### 1d. Timing/amplitude hypothesis — ME/CFS has a normal *mean* but abnormal *circadian phase/amplitude* of leptin, reconciling the Cleare null with the Domingo elevation

**Mechanistic rationale.** A single plasma sample cannot detect a difference in the daily *rhythm* of a hormone — only its level at that moment. If ME/CFS patients have a phase-shifted or amplitude-damped diurnal leptin rhythm (leptin peaks later, or has a blunted day-night excursion) while the 24-hour *mean* is normal, then two single-timepoint studies would be expected to produce *opposite* results purely as a sampling artifact: Cleare 2001 (drawn near the control's peak or the patient's trough) finds no difference; Domingo 2024 (drawn at a different clock time) finds elevation. The hypothesis dissolves the apparent contradiction between the only two human measurements and reframes the question from "is leptin high?" to "is leptin's *timing* wrong?"

**Evidence link.** Cleare 2001 (null, 0.70) vs. Domingo 2024 (elevated, 0.65) — the internal contradiction flagged in Phase 2; Osorio-Mendoza 2025 (clock gates leptin signalling, 0.35); Thomas 2026 framework (timing-structured hormone measurement needed, already in bib).

**Certainty: 0.18.** The sampling-artifact mechanism is well-established in chronobiology; whether ME/CFS actually shows an abnormal leptin rhythm is unmeasured.

**Falsifiable prediction.** A 24-hour serial (e.g., hourly or 2-hourly) plasma-leptin profile in ME/CFS vs. matched controls shows a significant difference in acrophase (peak timing) and/or amplitude but no significant difference in 24-hour mean — refuted if the mean differs while phase and amplitude are indistinguishable from controls.

**Non-specialist consequence.** If this is right, the two published human studies don't actually disagree — they just sampled a moving target at different times — and the correct next measurement is a full 24-hour profile, not another single blood draw, which would immediately settle a decade-old contradiction. (Origin: brainstorm)

## Category 2 — Research Directions

### 2a. 24-hour serial plasma-leptin profile in ME/CFS vs. matched controls

**Mechanistic rationale / gap.** Directly tests idea 1d (timing/amplitude) and 1b (vicious cycle). The field currently has only two single-timepoint human measurements that disagree (Cleare 2001 null; Domingo 2024 elevated). A diurnal profile under controlled light/meal conditions is the minimal experiment that can resolve mean-vs-phase-vs-amplitude, and can simultaneously relate the leptin rhythm to actigraphic circadian phase (building on Cambras 2026's actigraphy infrastructure).

**Evidence link.** Cleare 2001; Domingo 2024; Cambras 2026 (actigraphy protocol reusable); Thomas 2026 (timing-structured framework).

**Certainty: 0.30.** Feasible with existing methods; high informational value per patient; the open question is whether the effect size (if any) justifies the sampling burden in a severely ill population.

**Falsifiable prediction.** The profile either (a) confirms a phase/amplitude difference with preserved mean, or (b) shows no difference in any parameter — both are informative and falsifiable outcomes that eliminate competing hypotheses.

**Non-specialist consequence.** A single 24-hour study would settle whether the "leptin question" in ME/CFS is even worth pursuing, saving years of argument over two contradictory single measurements. (Origin: brainstorm)

### 2b. Confounder-adjusted re-analysis of existing leptin data (BMI, sex, adiposity, immune markers)

**Mechanistic rationale / gap.** Leptin is strongly correlated with fat mass and is higher in females — and ME/CFS is female-predominant and frequently associated with altered body composition. Neither Cleare 2001 nor Domingo 2024 (as summarized) reports a full adjustment for BMI/body-fat %, sex, and inflammatory markers. A re-analysis (or new matched case-control design with these covariates measured) is the cheapest decisive test of idea 1c (epiphenomenon vs. disease-specific signal). This is a measurement-validity study dressed as a research direction: it converts the elevation finding from "suggestive" to "robust" or "confounded."

**Evidence link.** Domingo 2024 (0.65); Cleare 2001 (0.70); Broderick 2013 (leptin as immune-metabolic marker, GWI).

**Certainty: 0.35.** High feasibility; the covariate structure of the original studies is the only open question (whether raw data are available for re-analysis).

**Falsifiable prediction.** A fully-adjusted model either retains a disease-associated leptin signal (supporting a non-epiphenomenal role) or attenuates it to null (supporting the confound reading) — a clean binary outcome.

**Non-specialist consequence.** This is the "check whether the finding survives basic statistics" step — if the elevated-leptin result vanishes once body-fat and sex are accounted for, a whole research thread (and any hope of a leptin-based treatment) would be quietly closed. (Origin: brainstorm)

---

### 2c. Central leptin-sensitivity proxy in ME/CFS — test the leptin-resistance entrainment deficit (idea 1a) without invasive CNS sampling

**Mechanistic rationale / gap.** Idea 1a posits central LepR desensitization in the DMH→SCN circuit. This cannot be directly biopsied in humans, but can be proxied: (i) the soluble-leptin-receptor / total-leptin ratio (a peripheral index of leptin sensitivity used in obesity research), and/or (ii) functional MRI of the DMH during a standardized metabolic challenge, and/or (iii) the physiological response to a controlled meal (postprandial hormone dynamics). A peripheral proxy that correlates with a functional DMH readout would be a step toward testing whether ME/CFS patients are centrally leptin-resistant.

**Evidence link.** Tang 2023 (DMH LepR circuit, 0.38); Faber 2021 (0.35); Domingo 2024 (0.65); obesity leptin-sensitivity assay literature (transferable, not in bib).

**Certainty: 0.20.** The proxies are established in obesity but unvalidated for ME/CFS central circuits; DMH fMRI during metabolic challenge is methodologically novel and technically demanding.

**Falsifiable prediction.** If ME/CFS patients are centrally leptin-resistant, the soluble-LepR:leptin ratio (or DMH fMRI response to a metabolic challenge) will show a resistance signature that correlates with both elevated circulating leptin AND poorer circadian phase stability — refuted if the proxy is normal despite elevated leptin and phase instability.

**Non-specialist consequence.** This would give researchers a blood-test shortcut for a "leptin-deaf" brain-clock — because you can't biopsy the hypothalamus, a validated proxy is the only practical way to test whether the metabolic reset signal is broken in ME/CFS. (Origin: brainstorm)

## Category 10 — Reasons this mechanism may NOT be relevant to ME/CFS

### 10a. Light dominance — the human circadian system is far more light-driven than metabolic, and ME/CFS's own data say light is the lever

**Critical assessment.** The Fleury 2026 effect was demonstrated in mice (nocturnal) and macaques, where metabolic signals are relatively potent zeitgebers. Humans have a strongly light-dominant circadian system; metabolic/food-entrainment signals are weak modulators, not primary zeitgebers, in people. This is directly corroborated *within the ME/CFS literature itself*: Cambras 2026 shows that light-exposure patterns — not metabolic intervention — are the dominant environmental circadian modulator in ME/CFS, associated with concrete downstream biomarkers (VCAM-1, triglycerides, serotonin). If light is already the dominant and primary lever in this population, the marginal contribution of a metabolic (leptin) lever is likely negligible. The mechanism may be real in animals and still nearly irrelevant in humans with ME/CFS.

**Evidence link.** Cambras 2026 (0.65); Fleury 2026 (0.28, animal); Faber 2021 (0.35, mouse).

**Certainty: 0.35.** The light-dominance point is well-established in human chronobiology and directly supported by the ME/CFS-specific Cambras 2026 data.

**Falsifiable prediction.** In an ME/CFS cohort, the variance in circadian phase instability explained by a timed metabolic challenge (leptin/meal manipulation) is a small fraction of the variance explained by light-exposure timing — i.e., metabolic input contributes negligible independent predictive power. Refuted if metabolic input explains a substantial, independent share of phase variance.

**Non-specialist consequence.** If light is doing almost all the work, then pursuing a metabolic/leptin lever for ME/CFS sleep-wake problems is chasing a weak handle when a stronger, better-documented one (light) is already on the table. (Origin: brainstorm)

---

### 10b. Downstream-of-SCN pathology — the failure is not at entrainment, so no entrainment lever (light, melatonin, or leptin) can fix it

**Critical assessment.** The Fleury mechanism accelerates *entrainment* — the process of aligning the SCN clock to an external schedule. But the strongest cautionary datum is Williams 2002: direct circadian interventions (melatonin, phototherapy) that *do* target entrainment produced no CFS symptom benefit. If the ME/CFS circadian problem is downstream of the clock itself (in SCN output pathways, sleep-effector systems, or autonomic/hormonal output coupling), then improving entrainment speed is irrelevant — you can align a clock perfectly and still have the downstream machinery fail. Under this reading, the whole metabolic-entrainment literature, however mechanistically elegant, addresses the wrong node.

**Evidence link.** Williams 2002 (no benefit, already in bib); McCarthy 2022 (circadian disruption in ME/CFS, already in bib); Fleury 2026 (0.28, entrainment-speed endpoint only).

**Certainty: 0.25.** Consistent with the null treatment responses, but the localization of the ME/CFS circadian defect (input vs. output) is itself unmeasured.

**Falsifiable prediction.** ME/CFS patients show normal SCN entrainment (normal melatonin/DLMO phase-locking to light) but abnormal downstream circadian outputs (body-temperature rhythm, cortisol, sleep architecture) — i.e., the defect localizes post-SCN. Refuted if ME/CFS patients show abnormal phase-locking/entrainment itself.

**Non-specialist consequence.** If the clock sets correctly but the machinery it drives is broken, then "fixing the clock" — by light, melatonin, or any new leptin trick — won't help symptoms, and research should shift to whatever is downstream of the clock. (Origin: brainstorm)

---

### 10c. Direction-of-effect opposition — the Fleury lever raises a hormone ME/CFS already has too much of

**Critical assessment.** Fleury 2026's intervention is *additive*: it raises circulating leptin to accelerate entrainment. ME/CFS patients already show *elevated* circulating leptin (Domingo 2024). So the plausible therapeutic direction is exactly opposite to what Fleury demonstrated — and if central LepR signalling is already desensitized by chronic elevation (idea 1a), adding more leptin is not merely futile but could deepen desensitization. The "leptin factory" is thus not a neutral technology with unclear ME/CFS application; its direction of effect is actively misaligned with the ME/CFS-observed state.

**Evidence link.** Domingo 2024 (0.65, elevated); Fleury 2026 (0.28, additive mechanism); Cleare 2001 (0.70, null baseline).

**Certainty: 0.30.** The direction-of-effect conflict is a logical consequence of the two papers taken together, independent of any deeper mechanistic claim.

**Falsifiable prediction.** Any translational attempt predicated on *raising* leptin to aid ME/CFS entrainment would be expected to fail or worsen; the falsifiable version is that ME/CFS patients with the highest baseline leptin show the *least* benefit (or harm) from any leptin-elevating stimulus — refuted if higher baseline leptin predicts better entrainment response.

**Non-specialist consequence.** This is the single most important caution for any reader: the headline technology *adds* a hormone that ME/CFS patients already have in excess, so the exciting animal result points the wrong way for this disease and should not be read as a treatment lead. (Origin: brainstorm)

## Category 11 — Null Hypothesis Assessment

### 11a. Null default — if the leptin-circadian mechanism has no role in ME/CFS, no existing ME/CFS claim is threatened

**Null assessment.** If the mechanism is irrelevant, the downstream consequences are minimal and asymmetric: the Fleury/Tang/Faber/Osorio-Mendoza papers remain valid *animal* basic science with zero ME/CFS translation; the elevated-leptin finding (Domingo 2024) remains interpretable as a metabolic/immune biomarker with no circadian meaning; the Cleare 2001 null remains the correct baseline reading. Critically, *no existing ME/CFS paper claim requires revision*, because no ME/CFS study has ever claimed a functional leptin-circadian entrainment mechanism — the whole construct is being *imported* into ME/CFS from animal work, not exported from ME/CFS data. The null is therefore cheap: accepting it costs nothing already in the document.

**Evidence link.** All five mechanism papers are animal (discounted 0.28–0.38); the two ME/CFS-specific papers are null/cautionary (0.65–0.70).

**Certainty: 0.40.** This is the default reading given the evidence distribution — the asymmetry (animal-mechanism vs. null/cautionary human data) makes "no role" the cheap hypothesis.

**Falsifiable prediction.** The null would be rejected by the same decisive experiment as idea 2a: a 24-hour profile showing a disease-specific, confounder-independent abnormality in leptin's circadian timing/amplitude that correlates with phase instability.

**Non-specialist consequence.** If the mechanism turns out to have no role in ME/CFS, the field loses nothing — no established ME/CFS finding is wrong, and the animal studies remain valid in their own domain; the cost of being wrong here is near zero, which is why caution (not enthusiasm) is the correct posture. (Origin: brainstorm)

---

### 11b. Reconciliation requirement — the null hypothesis must explain the Domingo-vs-Cleare discrepancy without invoking a mechanism

**Null assessment.** A null hypothesis is only complete if it accounts for the *apparent* positive signal. Here the positive-looking datum is Domingo 2024 (elevated leptin). The null must therefore explain that elevation by non-circadian means — the most plausible being (i) small-pilot sampling variance (n=31), (ii) failure to control BMI/sex/adiposity, and/or (iii) a single-timepoint artifact (idea 1d). If the null holds, the correct synthesis of the two human studies is: "after confounder control and time-of-day accounting, circulating leptin is not robustly abnormal in ME/CFS" — and the two studies are reconciled as sampling/confound differences, not biological contradiction. This is a *claim the document must be able to defend* if it integrates the null reading.

**Evidence link.** Cleare 2001 (0.70, null); Domingo 2024 (0.65, elevated, small pilot); idea 2b (confounder re-analysis) as the confirming experiment.

**Certainty: 0.30.** The reconciliation is plausible but not yet demonstrated; it is a hypothesis about why the two studies differ, not an observed fact.

**Falsifiable prediction.** The null-reconciliation is falsified if a confounder-adjusted 24-hour profile still shows a robust, time-of-day-independent leptin elevation in ME/CFS (i.e., if Domingo 2024's result survives scrutiny).

**Non-specialist consequence.** Before anyone can claim "ME/CFS has abnormal leptin," the burden is to explain why the *other* careful study found nothing — and the null answer (confounders + timing) is the simplest, most likely resolution until proven otherwise. (Origin: brainstorm)

## Category 12 — Evidence Quality Concerns

### 12a. Translation gap and endpoint mismatch — the trigger study cannot support any ME/CFS inference

**Evidence-quality concern.** Fleury 2026 has three compounding limitations not fully captured by its 0.28 discounted certainty: (i) **species** — mice are nocturnal and cynomolgus macaques, however "diurnal," are not humans; the potency of a metabolic zeitgeber in these species does not transfer to the strongly light-dominant human clock (see 10a); (ii) **endpoint** — the outcome is *entrainment speed* (time to re-align after a schedule shift), not any clinical symptom, so even a perfect human replication would say nothing about fatigue, unrefreshing sleep, or PEM; (iii) **absence of a diseased model** — the animals were healthy, so the study cannot speak to how the lever behaves in a state (like ME/CFS) where leptin is already elevated and the circadian system already disrupted. These are not mere "preclinical" caveats; they sever the chain from the headline finding to any ME/CFS claim.

**Evidence link.** Fleury 2026 (0.28, primary); Cambras 2026 (0.65, human light-dominance); Williams 2002 (no symptom benefit from direct circadian intervention).

**Certainty: 0.40.** These limitations are factual and inherent to the study design, not speculative.

**Falsifiable prediction.** A human study measuring *symptom* outcomes (not entrainment speed) after a metabolic-circadian intervention would be the only design capable of overturning this concern; the concern is falsified by such a study showing symptom benefit, not by any further animal work.

**Non-specialist consequence.** The exciting animal result measures "how fast the body clock resets," not "how the patient feels" — and it was done in healthy animals, so it cannot tell us anything about a disease where the clock and the hormone are already abnormal. (Origin: brainstorm)

---

### 12b. Human-data measurement validity — single-timepoint sampling and small, under-controlled cohorts cannot resolve the central question

**Evidence-quality concern.** The two human measurements that anchor the ME/CFS side of this topic share weaknesses that certainty scores alone understate: (i) **single timepoint** — both Cleare 2001 and Domingo 2024 (as summarized) measured leptin once, which is structurally blind to phase/amplitude (idea 1d); (ii) **small n** — 32 and 31 patients, respectively, giving wide confidence intervals that can hide a modest true difference or produce a spurious one; (iii) **assay era** — Cleare 2001 used 2001-vintage assays with higher measurement error than modern platforms; (iv) **confounder control** — BMI, sex (leptin is strongly female-higher and ME/CFS is female-predominant), and inflammatory state are not established as fully adjusted in the summary; (v) **generalisability** — Domingo 2024 is a cross-sectional pilot mixing ME/CFS and long-COVID groups. Collectively these mean the human evidence cannot distinguish "no difference" from "a real but modest/phase-specific difference" — which is exactly the question the whole topic hinges on.

**Evidence link.** Cleare 2001 (0.70); Domingo 2024 (0.65); idea 2b (confounder re-analysis) and 2a (24-hour profile) as remedies.

**Certainty: 0.35.** These are standard measurement-validity limitations, identifiable from the summaries; the precise covariate structure of the original papers would need verification.

**Falsifiable prediction.** The concern is operationally falsified by a prospective, sex-matched, BMI-controlled 24-hour serial study with modern assays — if such a study shows a robust disease signal, the single-timepoint/small-n concerns are moot; if it shows none, they were the explanation.

**Non-specialist consequence.** The two human studies are each a single snapshot of a moving target in small, imperfectly matched groups — so "is leptin abnormal in ME/CFS" is currently unanswerable from the existing data, and any confident-sounding conclusion on either side is premature. (Origin: brainstorm)

## Strongest takeaways

1. **The therapeutic premise is actively misaligned** (1c + 10c): the Fleury lever *adds* a hormone ME/CFS already has elevated (Domingo 2024), so the mechanism — even if real — points the wrong way. This is the single most important caution for the document.
2. **The two human measurements probably don't contradict** (1d + 12b + 2a): they are single snapshots of a moving target in small, under-controlled cohorts; a 24-hour serial profile is the decisive, feasible experiment.
3. **The cheap-null asymmetry** (11a): accepting "no role" costs the document nothing — no existing ME/CFS claim depends on this mechanism — so caution is the correct integration posture, exactly as Phase 2 pre-fired WEAK-EVIDENCE.
