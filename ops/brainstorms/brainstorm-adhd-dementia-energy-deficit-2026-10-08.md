## 1. Novel Hypotheses

### 1.1 Cumulative exposure duration discriminates developmental from acquired energy deficit (the "integral" hypothesis)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The single most important conceptual gap in the forward extension is *exposure duration*. ADHD is a developmental, lifelong energy-deficit state present from childhood; by age 50 it has accumulated ~4–5 decades of marginal cerebral energy reserve, plausibly the "progressive reserve erosion" the document already speculates about (@spec:progressive-reserve-erosion). ME/CFS is an *acquired* deficit with median onset in the 20s–40s, and its course is variable (some recover, some plateau, some decline). If neurodegeneration is a function of the time-integrated energy deficit — ∫(demand − production)dt over decades — then a developmental deficit and an adult-onset deficit are not equivalent exposures even if their instantaneous magnitude is identical.

**Novel hypothesis.** Dementia risk from an energy deficit scales with cumulative lifetime exposure (magnitude × duration), not with current deficit magnitude alone. This predicts ADHD (long exposure) >> ME/CFS (shorter, variable exposure), and predicts a dose–response of ME/CFS dementia risk with disease duration and with onset age (earlier onset = longer exposure = higher risk), conditional on the deficit not fully reversing.

**Certainty:** 0.35

**Falsifiable prediction.** In any future ME/CFS-to-dementia cohort, dementia hazard rises monotonically with ME/CFS duration and is higher for early-onset than late-onset cases matched for current age; equivalently, no elevated dementia risk among short-duration or recovered ME/CFS patients. Falsified if ME/CFS dementia risk is flat across duration, or if recovered patients retain full elevated risk (which would imply a fixed "damage imprint" rather than cumulative erosion).

**Non-specialist consequence:** If duration is what matters, then a person who developed ME/CFS in their 20s has a longer potential exposure than one who developed it at 60, and the question "how long have you been sick" becomes the key variable for any future cognitive-risk estimate.

### 1.2 Exertion-triggered vs resting hypometabolism as the discriminator (PEM is not a dementia signature)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The Alzheimer-side of the energy-deficit bridge is *resting* hypometabolism: FDG-PET shows decades-early, sustained reductions in cerebral glucose metabolism at rest in at-risk individuals (@Mosconi2013GlucoseAD), framed as a self-reinforcing vicious cycle spanning glucose transport, glycolysis and mitochondria (@Cunnane2011BrainFuelAD). ADHD is likewise a *resting* inefficiency (reduced global glucose metabolism; @sec:brain-energy-cross-disease). ME/CFS's signature energy failure is different in kind: post-exertional malaise is *episodic and exertion-triggered* — energy delivery collapses transiently after load, then (partially) restores at rest. A system that intermittently undershoots demand but returns to near-baseline between events is mechanistically different from one that is *constitutively* hypometabolic.

**Novel hypothesis.** Only *sustained resting* cerebral energy deficit — not intermittent, exertion-triggered deficit — seeds the mitochondrial cascade that leads to neurodegeneration (@Swerdlow2014MitoCascadeAD). Because ME/CFS's deficit is episodic, it should not reproduce the ADHD/AD dementia signal even if both are correctly labeled "energy deficit."

**Certainty:** 0.30

**Falsifiable prediction.** Resting-state FDG-PET or MRS PCr:ATP in ME/CFS patients *between* crashes will be nearer to normal than the resting hypometabolism seen in prodromal AD or ADHD; and the magnitude of resting (not post-exertional) hypometabolism — if any — is what predicts later cognitive decline. Falsified if ME/CFS resting hypometabolism is as deep and as regionally AD-like as prodromal AD, yet no dementia ensues (which would decouple hypometabolism from degeneration).

**Non-specialist consequence:** If only the "always-on" kind of brain energy shortfall predicts dementia, then ME/CFS's "crash-after-effort" pattern may be a different, less dangerous problem than the steady shortfall of ADHD or early Alzheimer's — a distinction that could reassure patients.

### 1.3 Trajectory specificity: the ADHD→dementia route is dopaminergic/Lewy-body, not amyloid — and so would any ME/CFS route

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The strongest mechanistic clue in the cohort literature is not the headline HR but the *subtype* split: the 15-year prospective cohort found ADHD preferentially precedes Lewy-body dementia (dementia HR 3.33; LBD HR 54.5) over an amyloid-Alzheimer trajectory (@Golimstok2024ADHDLewyBody). The document's own PFC work (ch07 sec-28) casts ADHD as a *catecholamine/prefrontal* energy bottleneck. A dopaminergic/bioenergetic pathway to degeneration is a different disease than amyloid-driven AD. This matters enormously for ME/CFS: if any energy-deficit route exists, it would plausibly be this same catecholamine-sensitive one, not the amyloid cascade.

**Novel hypothesis.** The energy-deficit→neurodegeneration bridge, if real, runs through a dopaminergic/synuclein (Lewy-body) trajectory, not an amyloid trajectory. A testable consequence: ME/CFS and ADHD patients, if they carry any elevated degeneration risk, should show prodromal markers of synucleinopathy (REM-sleep behavior disorder, α-synuclein seeding) rather than prodromal amyloid markers.

**Certainty:** 0.28

**Falsifiable prediction.** α-synuclein seed-amplification assay and REM-sleep-behavior-disorder prevalence are elevated in older ADHD (and would-be ME/CFS) patients relative to amyloid-PET positivity; conversely, if ADHD/ME/CFS patients show normal synuclein markers, the LBD route is not general and the trajectory-specificity claim fails.

**Non-specialist consequence:** If ADHD-linked dementia is really a Parkinson's-like disease rather than Alzheimer's, then the relevant early-warning signs for at-risk people would be sleep disturbance and movement changes, not the memory tests we currently screen with.

### 1.4 "No idle state" hypothesis: inability to down-regulate brain energy demand as the shared neurodevelopmental→degeneration engine

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** ADHD is classically characterized by default-mode-network dysregulation and an inability to enter a low-demand resting state — the brain "idles" poorly. ME/CFS shares a related signature: unrefreshing sleep, disrupted local-sleep dynamics, and evidence of elevated resting default-mode activity (the document's sec-27/sec-28 and the orexin/sleep work). A brain that cannot enter a genuine low-demand state accumulates metabolic load even at rest, which is precisely the condition the mitochondrial cascade requires to drive decades-long ROS-mediated damage (@Swerdlow2014MitoCascadeAD).

**Novel hypothesis.** The shared driver is not "low reserve" per se but "no idle": an inability to down-regulate whole-brain energy demand during rest converts a marginal reserve into a continuous oxidative stressor. ADHD and ME/CFS would then share a degeneration risk only to the extent that each *fails to idle*; ME/CFS patients who preserve restorative sleep and down-regulation would be protected regardless of their daytime deficit.

**Certainty:** 0.22

**Falsifiable prediction.** Quantitative EEG/MEG measures of resting-state down-regulation (e.g., default-mode deactivation, sleep slow-wave density, glymphatic clearance markers) predict later cognitive trajectory in ADHD and ME/CFS better than daytime hypometabolism magnitude; patients with intact slow-wave sleep show no decline despite daytime fatigue. Falsified if sleep/down-regulation quality does not stratify any cognitive or degeneration readout.

**Non-specialist consequence:** If the real risk factor is a brain that never rests, then improving deep sleep might matter more for long-term brain health than any daytime energy supplement — a low-tech, accessible lever.

## 2. Research Directions

### 2.1 The decisive study: longitudinal ME/CFS cognitive + resting cerebral energetics into the dementia-relevant age range

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The open question (@oq:mecfs-energy-deficit-dementia-risk) exists *only* because no ME/CFS cohort has been followed into late life with both cognitive and metabolic readouts. The AD field has the template: resting FDG-PET/MRS hypometabolism that precedes decline (@Mosconi2013GlucoseAD @Cunnane2011BrainFuelAD). The missing link is applying that template to ME/CFS.

**Design.** A cohort of ME/CFS patients (IOM criteria) aged ≥50, with baseline + biennial resting FDG-PET (or MRS PCr:ATP) and computerized cognitive battery, compared to (a) age-matched healthy controls and (b) an ADHD-only group, tracked for dementia incidence over 10–15 years. The three-arm design is what discriminates the developmental-vs-acquired deficit question directly rather than by inference.

**Certainty:** 0.55 (design certainty; the hypothesis it tests is low-certainty)

**Falsifiable prediction.** Resting hypometabolism magnitude at baseline predicts cognitive slope in the ADHD arm but is near-absent in the ME/CFS arm (per 1.2); a null (no ME/CFS dementia despite follow-up) is itself the decisive negative result. Falsified (for the bridge) if ME/CFS shows both resting hypometabolism and no dementia, which would break the hypometabolism→degeneration inference.

**Non-specialist consequence:** One well-powered study following middle-aged ME/CFS patients into old age with a brain-energy scan every few years would answer — for the first time — whether ME/CFS quietly shortens brain health, instead of the question remaining permanently open.

### 2.2 Mendelian randomization: ADHD liability → dementia (and separately, a CFS/ME GWAS instrument if it reaches sufficient power)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The PRS finding (@BauerNegrini2026ADHDdementia, quintile HR 1.35) is quasi-causal but not a formal Mendelian-randomization (MR) test; the document's own falsifiability clause for `@hyp:adhd-dementia-risk` names MR as the decisive test. MR is cheap, uses existing summary statistics, and is immune to reverse causation and most ascertainment bias.

**Design.** Two-sample MR: ADHD GWAS instrument → dementia/MCI outcome GWAS; sensitivity to horizontal pleiotropy (Egger, weighted median). Secondary: a bidirectional arm to test reverse causation (dementia liability → ADHD diagnosis). A third arm — CFS/ME instrument → dementia — is currently underpowered (the largest ME/CFS GWAS explains little variance), but should be logged as a design ready to run once a larger ME/CFS GWAS exists.

**Certainty:** 0.50

**Falsifiable prediction.** A null ADHD→dementia MR would directly falsify `@hyp:adhd-dementia-risk` per its stated criterion; a null reverse-direction MR would rule out the prodromal-misdiagnosis confound; a null CFS→dementia MR (when powered) would bound the acquired-deficit extension.

**Non-specialist consequence:** This is a near-free, statistics-only test that could convert the "ADHD raises dementia risk" claim from association into cause — or deflate it — using data that already exist, before anyone funds a decades-long study.

### 2.3 Metabolic-mediation reanalysis of the existing ADHD→dementia cohorts (diabetes, obesity, sleep-disordered breathing)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** Fluegge & Fluegge reported that removing the metabolic signal (diabetes) removes the ADHD→AD association (@Fluegge2018ADHDmetabolic, PMID 28811268) — the strongest single indirect support for metabolic mediation. This has never been systematically tested across the larger, more recent cohorts (@Dobrosavljevic2021ADHDdementia @Rast2025NDCneurodegeneration @BauerNegrini2026ADHDdementia). If the association survives full cardiometabolic adjustment, the "energy deficit" is something more specific than shared metabolic syndrome; if it attenuates to null, the bridge collapses into a vascular/metabolic story.

**Design.** Re-analyze All of Us and the Swedish register data, stratifying ADHD→dementia by diabetes status, BMI, hypertension, and sleep-disordered breathing, with a formal interaction test. The critical contrast: does ADHD raise dementia risk in *metabolically healthy* ADHD adults, or only in those with comorbid metabolic syndrome?

**Certainty:** 0.45

**Falsifiable prediction.** The ADHD→dementia hazard survives in the metabolically healthy stratum if the energy-deficit mechanism is primary; it fully attenuates in that stratum if diabetes/metabolic syndrome is the true mediator. Falsified for the bridge if attenuation is complete.

**Non-specialist consequence:** If the ADHD-dementia link is really driven by diabetes and metabolic syndrome, then the actionable target shifts from "brain energy" to controlling blood sugar and metabolic health — for ADHD and ME/CFS patients alike.

### 2.4 Cross-condition resting hypometabolism atlas (ADHD vs ME/CFS vs prodromal-AD vs LBD) with regional mapping

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The document's sec-27 establishes ME/CFS hypometabolism with a specific regional profile (brainstem/cerebellar), ADHD with a prefrontal profile (@sec:brain-energy-cross-disease), prodromal AD with a temporoparietal profile (@Mosconi2013GlucoseAD), and LBD with an occipital/catecholaminergic profile. ## 3. Drug / Medication Ideas

### 3.1 Stimulant-exposed ADHD as a natural experiment (do NOT extrapolate to ME/CFS treatment)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The Israel cohort found stimulant-treated adults showed *no* dementia-risk increase, arguing against a medication-cause reading and hinting that pharmacologically augmenting dopamine does not itself accelerate degeneration (@Levine2023ADHDdementia). This is the cleanest available "intervention" data point, but it is about ADHD patients taking stimulants for ADHD — it is *not* evidence that stimulants are safe or protective for ME/CFS cognitive fatigue, and no such recommendation follows.

**Idea (research-stage pharmaco-epidemiology, not a treatment claim).** Extend the stimulant natural experiment: in ADHD cohorts, test whether stimulant *dose-duration* modifies dementia trajectory (protective/neutral/aggravating), and — separately and cautiously — record dementia outcomes in any ME/CFS patients who used stimulants for fatigue, purely as an observational safety readout. The mechanistic payload is whether sustained catecholamine augmentation changes the trajectory of a dopaminergic-degeneration route (1.3).

**Certainty:** 0.25

**Falsifiable prediction.** Stimulant dose-duration shows no gradient on dementia risk in ADHD (supporting a metabolic-not-catecholaminergic primary mechanism); any protective signal would instead implicate the dopaminergic deficiency itself. Falsified (as neutral) if higher cumulative stimulant exposure raises dementia risk.

**Non-specialist consequence:** This changes nothing about how ME/CFS is treated today; it only tells researchers whether the ADHD-dementia link is a drug effect or something deeper — a question that can be answered from existing records without any new prescription.

## 4. Supplement / Nutraceutical Ideas

### 4.1 Ketone-body fuel bypass as a *mechanism probe* (not a ME/CFS treatment recommendation)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** Cunnane's framework identifies brain glucose hypometabolism as a rate-limiting step in prodromal AD, and the known metabolic workaround is supplying ketones, which neurons oxidize independent of the failing glucose pathway (@Cunnane2011BrainFuelAD, PMID 21035308). Because the energy-deficit bridge is exactly a glucose-hypometabolism claim, ketones are the substrate-level experiment that can falsify or support it — in ADHD first, where the deficit is established.

**Idea (mechanism-testing, not treatment).** Acute/longitudinal ketone-ester or MCT administration in ADHD adults with MRS or FDG-PET readout of cerebral energy state: if a substrate bypass reverses the ADHD hypometabolism signature and alters cognitive trajectory, the deficit is a *fuel-supply* problem and the bridge is mechanistically real. This does not imply a ketogenic recommendation for ME/CFS, whose deficit is exertion-triggered (1.2) and where substrate supply is unproven.

**Certainty:** 0.30

**Falsifiable prediction.** Ketone administration partially normalizes the ADHD resting-hypometabolism signature; no normalization argues the deficit is demand-side or mitochondrial, not substrate-supply. Falsified (as a useful probe) if ketones normalize metabolism but do nothing to cognition or trajectory.

**Non-specialist consequence:** A dietary-fuel experiment could show whether the ADHD (and by extension ME/CFS) brain's energy problem is about running out of fuel or about the engine itself — which determines whether "feed the brain" is even a meaningful strategy.

## 5. Non-Pharmacological Interventions

### 5.1 Lifelong energy accounting and reserve preservation as a *prevention* framing (ADHD, cautiously extendable)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** If cumulative exposure drives degeneration (1.1) and "no idle" drives it further (1.4), then the non-pharmacological lever is *reducing the lifetime integral of demand-over-capacity* — the same pacing/reserve logic the document already applies to ME/CFS onset (@hyp:metabolic-reserve-predisposition), but pointed forward at late-life cognition.

**Idea (prevention-stage, not a ME/CFS treatment).** For ADHD adults — the group with the *demonstrated* risk — a low-cost intervention of structured cognitive pacing, sleep-prioritization, and metabolic-risk control (targeting the 2.3 mediation) is a plausible dementia-delay strategy worth piloting. **Non-specialist consequence:** For adults with ADHD, the practical takeaway is that controlling sleep and metabolic health may protect future brain function — while for ME/CFS no new lifestyle mandate follows from this paper.

## 6. Combinations + Access

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** If `@hyp:adhd-dementia-risk` (0.60) is real, ADHD adults constitute a newly identifiable high-risk group where early cognitive screening could be rational — the consequence the document already names. ## 7. Mathematical Model Extensions (ODE / DAG)

### 7.1 Two-timescale reserve model: fast headroom + slow cumulative damage integral

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** Architecture C already models instantaneous headroom R(t) = J_production − J_demand (@sec:architecture-c-metabolic-reserve). It has no *slow* variable for accumulated damage, which is what "progressive reserve erosion" (@spec:progressive-reserve-erosion) and the dementia outcome require. The natural extension is a two-timescale system.

**Model.** Add a slow damage state D(t) accumulating only when demand exceeds production: dD/dt = k·max(0, J_demand − J_production) − λ·D (repair), with dementia threshold D_crit. The forward consequence: a lifelong small deficit (ADHD, δ≈0.08 constant over 50 years) yields a large cumulative D; an intermittent large deficit (ME/CFS PEM episodes) yields a *smaller* integral if it fully repairs between episodes. This single equation reproduces the developmental-vs-acquired asymmetry of 1.1 as a formal, quantitative prediction rather than a verbal one.

**Certainty:** 0.30 (structural; uncalibrated)

**Falsifiable prediction.** Fitting the model to observed ADHD dementia HR (~4) and to a null ME/CFS outcome constrains the repair rate λ; if the ADHD data require near-zero repair while the ME/CFS null requires near-complete repair, the model cannot jointly fit both without an additional term (e.g., resting-vs-exertional distinction from 1.2) — forcing a more specific mechanism.

**Non-specialist consequence:** Turning the "erosion" idea into a simple equation with two timescales lets researchers predict, rather than guess, how much more dementia risk a lifelong shortfall should carry than an adult-onset one.

### 7.2 Causal DAG extension separating developmental vs acquired deficit paths to dementia

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The current DAG work links energy deficit → ME/CFS onset. The forward extension needs a DAG with *dementia* as an outcome and the two deficit types as distinct exposures with distinct upstream nodes.

**Model.** Construct a DAG with: Developmental energy deficit (ADHD) → [lifelong hypometabolism → mitochondrial cascade → Lewy-body/amyloid degeneration] → dementia; and Acquired energy deficit (ME/CFS) → [exertion-triggered PEM, resting preservation] → dementia, with shared nodes (mitochondria, dopamine) but different arrow weights. The formal payoff is identifying the *adjustment set* that would identify the acquired-deficit effect: the DAG shows whether adjusting for duration, resting-hypometabolism, and metabolic syndrome suffices, or whether an unmeasured confounder (shared genetics) blocks any observational estimate.

**Certainty:** 0.35

**Falsifiable prediction.** The DAG's back-door identification is testable: if no valid adjustment set exists without an unmeasured confounder (e.g., a shared pleiotropic locus), then no observational ME/CFS study can answer the question and only MR (2.2) or the longitudinal design (2.1) can. Falsified if the DAG yields a clean observational adjustment set that data can satisfy.

**Non-specialist consequence:** The DAG tells researchers up front whether the ME/CFS-dementia question is even *answerable* from records — or whether they must wait for genetics or a long study — saving wasted observational effort.

## 8. Cross-Disease Bridges

### 8.1 Type-2 diabetes as the cleanest *acquired* energy-deficit comparator to bound ME/CFS risk

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** Fluegge's finding — diabetes carries the ADHD→AD signal (@Fluegge2018ADHDmetabolic) — reveals that type-2 diabetes (T2D) is the best-established *acquired* metabolic deficit linked to dementia. T2D has a known, modest, well-quantified dementia excess (roughly HR 1.5–2). If ME/CFS is "an acquired brain-energy deficit," T2D is the empirical floor/ceiling comparator: an acquired metabolic deficit that demonstrably raises dementia risk, with decades of longitudinal data.

**Bridge.** Use the T2D→dementia literature as the template for what an acquired energy-deficit→dementia effect looks like in magnitude, latency, and mechanism (vascular + metabolic), and treat it as the bound for any ME/CFS estimate: ME/CFS dementia risk, if any, should be of T2D's order or lower, not ADHD's (developmental) order. This converts the unanswerable "is ME/CFS like ADHD" into the answerable "is ME/CFS like T2D."

**Certainty:** 0.35

**Falsifiable prediction.** If a future ME/CFS dementia estimate exceeds the T2D bound (HR > ~2), the acquired-deficit reading is wrong and something ME/CFS-specific (neuroinflammation, vascular) is additive. Falsified if ME/CFS risk matches T2D's, supporting the shared acquired-metabolic-deficit mechanism.

**Non-specialist consequence:** Comparing ME/CFS to type-2 diabetes — a common, well-understood condition — gives a realistic ceiling for how worried anyone should be, replacing an unanswerable analogy with a concrete benchmark.

### 8.2 Autism as the discriminator control the field has been missing

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The energy-deficit bridge explicitly groups ADHD and autism (@spec:energy-deficit-neurodegeneration-bridge), and both are established "energy-deficit" neurodevelopmental conditions (Frye 2024 ASD mitochondrial meta; ASD resting hypermetabolism in early development, hypometabolism later). But autism→dementia data are essentially absent. This is a *leverage point*: ASD and ADHD share the energy-deficit premise but differ on the catecholamine axis (ADHD is dopaminergic/noradrenergic; ASD less so). Testing ASD→dementia disambiguates *energy deficit per se* from *catecholamine deficiency*.

**Bridge.** If ASD also shows elevated dementia risk, the shared-energy-deficit mechanism is supported and broadened. If ASD shows *no* dementia risk despite an energy deficit, then the ADHD→dementia link is specific to the dopaminergic/catecholamine route (1.3) — and the "energy deficit" framing is insufficient.

**Certainty:** 0.30

**Falsifiable prediction.** A register study of ASD→dementia (Sweden, All of Us) will show either (a) elevated risk supporting shared-energy, or (b) null risk implicating catecholamine-specificity. Either outcome is informative; the *null* outcome is the one that most sharply revises the current bridge's grouping of ADHD and autism.

**Non-specialist consequence:** Looking at whether autism carries the same dementia risk as ADHD is the fastest way to tell whether the culprit is "brain energy shortage" in general, or something more specific about the dopamine system.

### 8.3 The hypometabolism-vicious-cycle bridge: ME/CFS as a possibly-reversible "prodromal-hypometabolism" state

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** Cunnane frames AD hypometabolism as a *vicious cycle* whose initiating step is unresolved (@Cunnane2011BrainFuelAD), and the document's sec-27 documents ME/CFS hypometabolism. The provocative bridge is that both conditions sit on the same "hypometabolism → dysfunction → more hypometabolism" loop, but ME/CFS sits on the *reversible* portion (no proteinopathy yet) while AD sits on the *irreversible* portion (proteinopathy locked in). This reframes ME/CFS not as "at risk for dementia" but as "a transient excursion into the same metabolic loop AD permanently occupies."

**Bridge.** ME/CFS hypometabolism is a model system for the *upstream, pre-proteinopathy* stage of the AD vicious cycle; studying how ME/CFS hypometabolism reverses (in those who recover) or persists (in those who don't) illuminates what tips the loop from reversible to irreversible — relevant to AD without claiming ME/CFS becomes AD.

**Certainty:** 0.25

**Falsifiable prediction.** Recovered ME/CFS patients show normalized resting brain metabolism, proving the loop is reversible pre-proteinopathy; if hypometabolism persists in recoverers, the loop is not simply reversible and the analogy weakens. Falsified if ME/CFS hypometabolism reversal does not track clinical recovery.

**Non-specialist consequence:** ME/CFS might serve as a "living laboratory" for the earliest, still-reversible stage of the brain-energy failure seen in Alzheimer's — useful for research on both conditions even if ME/CFS itself never becomes dementia.

## 9. Diagnostic / Biomarker Ideas

### 9.1 Resting cerebral energetics (MRS PCr:ATP or FDG-PET) as a "reserve depth" biomarker for late-life decline

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The AD field already treats resting hypometabolism as a decades-early signature (@Mosconi2013GlucoseAD). The natural translation is a single resting brain-energy readout that grades *reserve depth* and predicts which energy-deficit individuals (ADHD today; ME/CFS hypothetically) will decline.

**Biomarker.** Resting MRS PCr:ATP (or FDG-PET normalized uptake) measured once at midlife in ADHD (and later ME/CFS) adults as a continuous "reserve depth" score; test whether it predicts cognitive slope and incident MCI/dementia independent of amyloid. This is the biomarker form of hypotheses 1.1/1.2 and the endpoint that 2.1 would collect.

**Certainty:** 0.30

**Falsifiable prediction.** Baseline resting cerebral energetics predicts later cognitive decline in ADHD independently of amyloid-PET status; if it predicts nothing once amyloid is controlled, the energy-deficit-first reading fails.

**Non-specialist consequence:** A single midlife brain-energy scan could, if this works, flag which ADHD (or eventually ME/CFS) patients are worth monitoring — turning a vague worry into a number.

### 9.2 α-synuclein seed-amplification assay + RBD screening as the LBD-trajectory early marker

### 9.3 Cumulative mitochondrial damage readout (mtDNA mutation burden / PBMC spare capacity) as the "erosion" biomarker

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The reserve-erosion model predicts accumulated mitochondrial damage as the intermediate between lifetime energy deficit and degeneration (@spec:progressive-reserve-erosion). ## 10. Why This Mechanism May NOT Be Relevant to ME/CFS (critical)

### 10.1 Acquired vs developmental: duration and reversibility are not equivalent exposures

**Alternative explanation.** The ADHD deficit is developmental and lifelong — ~50 years of continuous marginal reserve by age 50. The ME/CFS deficit is acquired in adulthood, often partially reversible, and in a subset of patients substantially recovers. A decades-long constant shortfall and an adult-onset, possibly-plateauing shortfall are different biological exposures; nothing in the ADHD→dementia data licenses extending a lifelong-deficit effect to an acquired one. The document itself flags this asymmetry in @oq:mecfs-energy-deficit-dementia-risk.

**Certainty:** 0.55

**Falsifiable prediction.** A ME/CFS cohort followed to late life shows no elevated dementia risk after matching on duration-of-deficit — i.e., the ADHD effect does not transfer. Falsified if short-duration ME/CFS still confers risk comparable to ADHD's.

**Non-specialist consequence:** The most likely reason ME/CFS would *not* lead to dementia is that its energy problem starts in adulthood and can improve — unlike ADHD's, which has been present since childhood.

### 10.2 Exertion-triggered (PEM) vs resting hypometabolism: different energy pathologies

**Alternative explanation.** The dementia-relevant energy deficit is a *resting* hypometabolism that is present continuously and seeds a mitochondrial cascade (@Mosconi2013GlucoseAD @Swerdlow2014MitoCascadeAD). ME/CFS's signature is post-exertional malaise — episodic, load-triggered energy failure with relative preservation at rest. An intermittently-failing system that restores between events does not accumulate the same continuous oxidative stress that the mitochondrial cascade requires. "Energy deficit" is a label covering two mechanistically distinct states.

**Certainty:** 0.45

**Falsifiable prediction.** Resting cerebral energetics in ME/CFS patients between crashes are near-normal (and near-normal resting metabolism coexists with profound PEM), so no resting-hypometabolism-driven degeneration ensues. Falsified if ME/CFS resting metabolism is persistently AD-like yet dementia does not follow.

**Non-specialist consequence:** ME/CFS's "crash after effort, recover with rest" pattern may simply be a different kind of energy problem from the constant shortfall that precedes dementia — a reassuring distinction if it holds.

### 10.3 Confounded and partially reverse-causal association: prodromal decline mislabeled as ADHD

**Alternative explanation.** Late-life ADHD diagnosis in EHR is unusual; a person first "diagnosed" with ADHD at 50–60 may be experiencing prodromal cognitive/attentional change, inflating the association through reverse causation. The driving paper's own sensitivity analysis — the association becoming non-significant with a ≥9-year lag — is direct evidence that part of the signal is prodromal. Stimulant medication and psychiatric comorbidity (the Sweden attenuation 2.92→1.62, @Dobrosavljevic2021ADHDdementia) add residual confounding. If the ADHD→dementia link is largely confounded, there is no clean "energy-deficit→dementia" mechanism to transfer to ME/CFS at all.

**Certainty:** 0.40

**Falsifiable prediction.** The association attenuates substantially under full stimulant/psychiatric/cardiometabolic control and under lagged analyses, and MR (2.2) returns null. Falsified (for this alternative) if the effect survives MR and full adjustment intact.

**Non-specialist consequence:** The "ADHD causes dementia" story may itself be partly an artifact of people in early cognitive decline being labeled ADHD — meaning there may be no genuine energy-deficit effect to worry about for anyone.

## 11. Null Hypothesis Assessment + Revisions Required (critical)

### 11.1 Null: ME/CFS confers no elevated late-life dementia risk (HR ≈ 1)

**Null hypothesis.** Despite sharing an "energy deficit" label, acquired ME/CFS does not reproduce the developmental ADHD→dementia effect; ME/CFS-to-dementia hazard ratio is ~1.0 in any adequately-powered, long-duration cohort.

**What this null means.** If true, it is *not* a refutation of the ADHD finding — it refutes the *forward extension* only. The document's `@spec:energy-deficit-neurodegeneration-bridge` would need explicit restriction to developmental/lifelong deficits, and its closing "unifying three conditions (ADHD, autism, ME/CFS) under a single chronic-energy-deficit architecture" sentence would need rewriting to exclude acquired deficits. The open question (@oq:mecfs-energy-deficit-dementia-risk) would resolve "no," and the forward consequence claim would be withdrawn without touching the onset-side Architecture C.

**Certainty:** 0.45 (that this null is the more likely outcome)

**Falsifiable prediction.** A ME/CFS late-life cohort yields HR ≈ 1.0 for dementia; the null is rejected only by a robustly elevated, duration-dependent HR.

**Non-specialist consequence:** If this null holds — which is the current best guess — then ME/CFS patients should not be told they face elevated dementia risk, and the paper's language must make clear that only lifelong neurodevelopmental deficits (ADHD) show the effect.

### 11.2 Null: ADHD→dementia is fully confounded (no causal energy-deficit effect)

## 12. Evidence-Quality Concerns Beyond Certainty Scores (critical)

### 12.1 All observational register/EHR data; no trial, no formal MR, no dose–response

**Concern.** Every ADHD→dementia estimate is observational (register/EHR cohort). There is no randomized exposure, no formal Mendelian randomization, and no clean dose–response curve. The 0.60 certainty on `@hyp:adhd-dementia-risk` embeds an association-strength judgment that observational design alone does not license, and the psychiatric-comorbidity attenuation in Sweden (2.92→1.62, @Dobrosavljevic2021ADHDdementia) shows the point estimates are fragile to adjustment. The certainty rating should be read as "robust association," not "robust causal effect."

**Certainty (of the concern):** 0.60

**Non-specialist consequence:** These are correlations from medical records, not proof of cause — a distinction that matters because the headline numbers (4-fold risk) can shrink by half once other conditions are accounted for.
