## Category 1: Novel Hypotheses / Mechanistic Connections (research-facing, NOT treatments)

### Idea 1.1: Care-Delivery Model as a Modifiable Determinant of Severe-ME/CFS Outcomes (single-contact vs MDT)

- **Category:** 1
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The factsheet asserts single-point-of-contact (specialist-nurse-led) care outperforms multidisciplinary-team (MDT) care for severe patients. The proposed mechanism is *energy-coupling*: each interface with a new clinician (referral, handover, repeated history-taking, transport between appointments) imposes a discrete sensory + cognitive + postural cost. An MDT multiplies these cost-events per unit of care, and — because no single professional holds longitudinal continuity — increases the chance of care exceeding the patient's energy budget (triggering PEM) and of delayed recognition of deterioration. Single-contact care minimises interface-count per unit of benefit and couples the "stop-signal" system to one accountable decision-maker. This frames "care architecture" not as logistics but as a dose-exposure variable with a direct mechanistic link to PEM induction.
- **Evidence link:** Indirect — nurse-delivery model documented in FINE (Wearden2006/2010); harm-monitoring gap in MDT centres (McPhee2019). No direct RCT of single-contact vs MDT in severe ME/CFS exists.
- **Preliminary certainty:** 0.22
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In a head-to-head comparison (Idea 2.1), single-point-of-contact care yields a lower per-care-session interface-cost index and fewer care-triggered PEM episodes than MDT care at equal total clinician-hours, among severity-matched severe/very-severe patients.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If true, redesigning how care is organised (fewer, more continuous contacts) could reduce the daily toll on housebound patients without new drugs or equipment.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

### Idea 1.2: Environmental-Stimuli Reduction Works Through a Neurophysiological "Sensory Debt" Model, Not Just Avoidance

- **Category:** 1
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The factsheet treats environmental-stimuli reduction as a *medical necessity* that prevents deterioration. A mechanistic reading: severe ME/CFS patients have impaired sensory gating (central sensitisation, TRP channelopathy, sympathetic overdrive), so each environmental stimulus is metabolically "priced" beyond its objective intensity — a transient autonomic and cortical load. Chronic cumulative load pushes the patient toward a PEM/decompensation threshold; stimuli reduction lowers the baseline load so the patient stays below threshold. This is analogous to an energy-budget or threshold model (cf. the paper's rolling-PEM and multi-lock framing) in which sensory input is a first-class metabolic cost term rather than a nuisance.
- **Evidence link:** Indirect — Montoya2021 severe-care guidance; central-sensitisation and autonomic findings in the corpus. The "sensory input = metabolic cost" formulation is not directly measured in ME/CFS.
- **Preliminary certainty:** 0.18
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Severity-stratified objective proxies of stimulus load (light, sound, social interaction counts) will correlate within-subject with subsequent PEM/deterioration frequency, and experimentally reducing them will lower that frequency — measurable without invoking any subjective expectation effect.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Gives a concrete, testable reason for the guidance to "rest in a dark quiet room," and would tell clinicians which stimuli matter most.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

## Category 2: Research Directions That Resolve Key Uncertainties

### Idea 2.1: Pragmatic Cluster-Randomised Trial — Single-Point-of-Contact vs Multidisciplinary Care in Severe/Very-Severe ME/CFS

- **Category:** 2
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The single-contact-vs-MDT claim is the factsheet's most actionable but least tested position. A cluster-randomised pragmatic trial (randomising care teams/geographic units, not individuals — necessary because severe patients cannot be individually randomised to care architecture) with endpoints of care-triggered PEM episodes, hospitalisation, caregiver strain, and deterioration over 12 months would directly test Idea 1.1. It is the single highest-value experiment because it addresses the delivery model that shapes every other intervention's uptake in the housebound population.
- **Evidence link:** FINE (Wearden2010) provides the nurse-delivery prototype; McPhee2019 shows the harm-monitoring gap in current MDT centres. No direct comparator exists.
- **Preliminary certainty:** 0.30
- **Falsifiable prediction:** If single-contact care is not superior (or is equivalent) to MDT care on care-triggered PEM and deterioration endpoints, the factsheet's core organisational recommendation fails — and the null result itself would be informative (care architecture may not drive outcomes; severity and environmental control may).
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Would tell health systems whether reorganising services (cheaper, single coordinator) actually helps severely ill patients or whether current fragmented care is adequate.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

### Idea 2.2: Prospective Study of Environmental-Stimuli Reduction as a Deterioration-Prevention Intervention

- **Category:** 2
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Environmental-stimuli reduction is prescribed as a medical necessity but has no primary evidence. A prospective cohort or single-arm (with rigorous pre/post) study in housebound severe/very-severe patients — measuring objectively the intensity of stimuli (illuminance, sound level, interaction counts) and tracking deterioration events, PEM frequency, and caregiver-reported functional status — would convert a consensus claim into a testable exposure-response relationship and establish whether a dose-response (more reduction → less deterioration) exists.
- **Evidence link:** Montoya2021 severe-care guidance (consensus); no prospective data.
- **Preliminary certainty:** 0.25
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A dose-response gradient is observed: patients with the greatest objective reduction in daily stimulus load show the lowest deterioration/PEM frequency after controlling for baseline severity; absence of a gradient would refute the "necessity" framing.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Would legitimise or overturn the common clinical advice to "reduce stimulation," giving families and clinicians an evidence base rather than anecdote.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

### Idea 2.3: Controlled Study of Activity/Heart-Rate Tracking Tools in Severe Pacing — Help or Harm?

- **Category:** 2
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The factsheet critiques wearable/activity-diary tools (pacing-tracking-no-evidence limitation). The mechanism question: tools impose their own cognitive/behavioural load (frequent checking, alarm responses, data interpretation), which may add sensory and decision cost — potentially outweighing any pacing benefit in severe patients whose budget is already exceeded. Whether tracking helps (objective envelope) or harms (added load, nocebo alarm) is untestable from advocacy; it needs a controlled comparison. This resolves a genuine clinical fork for housebound patients.
- **Evidence link:** Corpus pacing literature is null/inconclusive on tool value; the factsheet positions tools as not-yet-validated. No severe-ME/CFS-specific trial.
- **Preliminary certainty:** 0.20
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In a crossover trial, severe patients using mandatory continuous activity/HR tracking show either (a) fewer PEM episodes (tool helps) or (b) higher PEM rate and worse perceived control (tool harms via added load) relative to tracking-free pacing; the direction is the empirical answer.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Determines whether the common "wear a tracker" advice is helpful or counterproductive for the most severely affected.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

## Category 10: Reasons This May NOT Be Relevant / What Evidence Would Disprove It

### 10.1 The Single-Point-of-Contact Model May Be a Caregiver-Burden Artifact, Not a Patient-Benefit

- **Rationale:** The advocacy for single-contact care may reflect the reality of *scarce specialist resources* and *caregiver strain* rather than demonstrably better patient outcomes. If the true driver of worse outcomes in MDT care is the burden on the lone caregiver (who coordinates a fragmented system), then reorganising into single-contact care helps the *caregiver system* but may not change the *patient's biology*. The prescription conflates a health-system efficiency argument with a patient-outcome claim.
- **What would disprove it:** If single-contact and MDT care produce indistinguishable patient outcomes (PEM, deterioration, hospitalisation) in Idea 2.1, while caregiver strain differs — the recommendation is a workforce/carer-relief intervention, not a clinical one, and should be reframed as such.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

### 10.2 Environmental-Stimuli Reduction May Be Indistinguishable From General Rest/Deconditioning

- **Rationale:** Reducing environmental stimuli is confounded with total rest and reduced physical activity. If the benefit comes from reduced *metabolic/orthostatic* load (simply doing less) rather than reduced *sensory* load specifically, then the "stimuli reduction" framing is a proxy for "do less," and the specific mechanism (Idea 1.2) is wrong even though the guidance works. It would not generalise to interventions that reduce activity while preserving sensory input, or vice versa.
- **What would disprove it:** An experiment that holds total rest constant while varying only the sensory component (e.g., dark/quiet vs lit/noisy rest of identical duration) and finds no difference in PEM/deterioration would refute the sensory-specific mechanism.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

### 10.3 The Factsheet's Recommendations May Not Transfer From Advocacy Contexts to Other Care Systems

- **Rationale:** This is a UK/S4ME advocacy-consensus document. Its prescriptive claims (single-contact model, hospital-admission adjustments, enteral-feeding thresholds, MUST screening) encode the UK NHS resource context and its specific harm experiences (McPhee2019). Applicability to systems with different funding, staffing, or hospital-admission norms is unestablished; a care model shown valid in one health system may be actively wrong in another. The document's authority (expert consensus) does not establish external validity.
- **What would disprove it:** Demonstration that the recommendations fail or harm in a differently-configured health system, or that the underlying evidence (all consensus/indirect) does not generalise across settings.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

## Category 11: Null Hypothesis Assessment

### 11.1 H0₁: Care-Delivery Model (Single-Contact vs MDT) Has No Effect on Severe-ME/CFS Outcomes

- **Statement:** Outcomes in severe/very-severe ME/CFS (care-triggered PEM, deterioration, hospitalisation, function) are identical regardless of whether care is delivered through a single point of contact or a multidisciplinary team, once baseline severity and care volume are controlled.
- **Evidence already available:** No direct RCT exists; FINE nurse-delivery and McPhee2019 harm-monitoring provide only indirect support. The claim is currently unfalsified largely because it is untested.
- **What would reject H0₁:** The pragmatic cluster trial (Idea 2.1) finding a significant, reproducible outcome difference favouring one model.
- **Consequence of failing to reject H0₁:** The factsheet's headline organisational recommendation would be demoted from "medical necessity" to "preference/implementation choice"; resources should instead go to severity-stratified care and environmental control.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

### 11.2 H0₂: Off-Label Drug "Little/No Benefit" Reflects Absence of Evidence, Not True Ineffectiveness

- **Statement:** Antihistamines, naltrexone, fludrocortisone, and ivabradine are no better than placebo in severe ME/CFS specifically because no adequately powered severe-ME/CFS RCT has been run — the "little/no benefit" appraisal is an absence-of-evidence conclusion, not a demonstrated null.
- **Evidence already available:** Existing corpus harm entries (Rowe2001, Marchetta2025, Bolton2019, NaculLDN2025) and general safety data; no severe-ME/CFS-specific efficacy or safety RCT. The factsheet's caution is reasonable but rests on lack of data, not on a demonstrated null.
- **What would reject H0₂:** A severe-ME/CFS-specific RCT of any one of these agents returning a meaningful efficacy signal; conversely, a well-powered null would confirm the caution.
- **Consequence of failing to reject H0₂:** The caution stands as the correct evidence-based position (do not use off-label without data), but it must be framed as provisional pending trials, not as proof of ineffectiveness.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

### 11.3 H0₃: Severe-ME/CFS Care Is Not Distinct From Generalised Severe-Chronic-Illness Palliative Care

- **Statement:** The factsheet's prescriptions (environmental-stimuli reduction, single-contact care, enteral feeding for nutritional failure, hospital-admission adjustments) are generic severe-chronic-illness supportive-care principles and confer no ME/CFS-specific benefit; the same care would help any housebound patient with a similar energy deficit regardless of diagnosis.
- **Evidence already available:** The components mirror established palliative/supportive-care practice (energy conservation, symptom-triggered care, nutrition support); no ME/CFS-specific comparator exists.
- **What would reject H0₃:** Evidence that severe-ME/CFS patients specifically — with their distinctive PEM and stimulus-intolerance — deteriorate less under these interventions than non-ME/CFS chronically-ill controls receiving identical care, or that ME/CFS-specific modifications (e.g., PEM-anchored pacing, stimulus-intensity thresholds) outperform generic supportive care.
- **Consequence of failing to reject H0₃:** The factsheet's value is real but not ME/CFS-specific; it should be framed as adapting generic severe-care principles, weakening the case that ME/CFS needs its own bespoke severe-care pathway.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

## Category 12: Evidence Quality Concerns

### 12.1 All Prescriptive Claims Rest on Consensus/Advocacy, Not Primary Evidence

- **Concern:** Every non-rehabilitation claim (single-contact model, environmental-stimuli necessity, severity prevalence ~1/1000, enteral-feeding thresholds, hospital-admission adjustments) traces to expert consensus and indirect corpus support. The factsheet's bibliographic evidence base includes a mix of null RCTs (Gaunt2024, Wearden2010 — which *support* rehab rejection), a service evaluation (Devasahayam2012), an incident analysis (Page2019), and a FoI harm-monitoring study (McPhee2019) — none of which directly test the factsheet's own prescriptive positions.
- **Impact:** The document's authority (senior clinician + expert consensus) substantially exceeds its empirical base. There is a risk that strong consensus presentation is read as strong evidence, especially for the off-label-drug and care-delivery-model claims.
- **Mitigation:** Any integration must tag these claims as consensus/clinical-guidance with WEAK-EVIDENCE, and must not present the rehab-rejection nulls as evidence for the unrelated organisational/nutritional claims.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

### 12.2 Confounding of Severity-Grading Claims With Care-Need Circularity

- **Concern:** The severity grading is tied to *care needs* (housebound/bedbound → high care need). This is partly circular: severity defined by care burden predicts "more care needed." The ~1/1000 housebound prevalence estimate derives from advocacy/consensus, not a dedicated severe-prevalence study. Care-need-based grading may misclassify patients whose disability reflects lack of support (not biological severity) as "severe," and may undercount biologically severe patients with adequate support.
- **Impact:** Prevalence figures and severity-stratified care recommendations inherit this circularity; a patient's measured "severity" can change with caregiver availability, not disease.
- **Mitigation:** Severity should be anchored to a care-need-independent biological/functional measure where possible; prevalence claims should be flagged as consensus estimates.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**

### 12.3 Selection Bias and Missing Harm Data in the Rehabilitation Nulls

- **Concern:** The strongest evidence (rehab has no place: Gaunt2024, Wearden2010) is paediatric/primary-care RCT data, not severe/very-severe populations. Generalising a paediatric/ambulatory null to severe and very-severe patients assumes the mechanism of non-benefit transfers across severity strata — unverified. Separately, McPhee2019 shows adverse events are not systematically monitored, meaning harm from *any* severe-care intervention (including enteral feeding and environmental modifications) may be systematically under-reported.
- **Impact:** The rehab-rejection, which is the factsheet's most confident claim, is actually the least directly applicable to the severe/very-severe population the document addresses; and the safety of the recommended interventions themselves (feeding, care-model changes) is unmeasured.
- **Mitigation:** State the severity-transfer assumption explicitly; treat harm-absence as a monitoring gap, not evidence of safety.
- **`origin: brainstorm`**
