# Brainstorm: HSV Dormancy-Undormancy Probe

**Topic:** Using HSV-1/2 outbreak patterns as a visible, patient-reportable, timestamped probe of endogenous viral reactivation during PEM crashes in ME/CFS.
**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Phase:** 4 (creative brainstorming)
**Decision:** PARTIAL — caps enforced; Categories 3–9 skipped

---

## Category 1: Novel Hypotheses

### Idea 1.1: PEM-to-HSV Reactivation Catecholamine Relay
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.20
**Rationale:** PEM → sympathetic surge → norepinephrine → β-adrenergic receptors on trigeminal ganglion neurons → JNK/c-Jun pathway transactivating HSV-1 ICP0 lytic promoter. Simultaneously, catecholamine surge expands Tregs suppressing CD8+ T-cell surveillance at the ganglion — dual-hit: neuronal trigger + immune disinhibition. ~2–5 day latency from PEM to cold sore reflects anterograde axonal transport time. Evidence: Chida 2009 (stress→HSV, 0.53), Yu 2018 (Treg-CD8-HSV, 0.30), Sepúlveda 2019 (Treg-herpesvirus model, 0.50). Falsifiable: prospective diary → HSV outbreak probability within 7 days post-PEM not exceeding probability in PEM-free windows. Consequence: Cold sore = biological "receipt" confirming PEM occurred — objective anchor for a symptom patients are told is psychological. Covered by Phase 3 spec:hsv-pem-probe.

### Idea 1.2: HSV Outbreak Latency as Immune-Dysfunction Stopwatch
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.15
**Rationale:** Interval from PEM onset to first prodromal HSV symptom encodes quantitative immune competence. In healthy immune system, CD8+ T cells intercept reactivating neurons before viral particles reach epithelium. In ME/CFS, outbreak penetrance rate and shorter latency may correlate with CD8+ T-cell exhaustion markers (PD-1, TIM-3, LAG-3) and NK cytotoxicity. Evidence: CD8+ T-cell exhaustion documented in some ME/CFS studies; Yu 2018 CD8-HSV axis. Falsifiable: HSV outbreak latency shows r < 0.2 with CD8+ exhaustion markers. Consequence: Cold-sore journal as cheap at-home immune-function readout.

### Idea 1.3: HSV Lesion Severity as Type I IFN Competence Readout
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.15
**Rationale:** HSV-1 replication in keratinocytes limited by local type I IFN via cGAS-STING. Schreiner 2020 paradox: ME/CFS serum transfers antiviral state blocking HSV-1, yet patients are sick — suggests tissue-level IFN deficiency despite normal/ elevated serum markers. Lesion severity (duration × pain × diameter) reflects local IFN response. Evidence: Schreiner 2020 (0.44); known ME/CFS type I IFN heterogeneity. Falsifiable: Lesion severity composite score shows no significant difference vs seropositive controls, no correlation with lesion biopsy IFN gene expression. Consequence: Simple oral swab could reveal local antiviral defense failure even when blood tests look normal.

### Idea 1.4: PEM-Induced Microvascular Collapse at Ganglion
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.10
**Rationale:** CPET studies document cerebral hypoperfusion during PEM. Trigeminal ganglion receives blood from trigeminal and middle meningeal artery branches. Transient ganglion-level hypoxia/ischemia during PEM → ATP depletion, ROS, cellular acidosis → destabilizes HSV latency via HIF-1α binding to HSV-1 LAT promoter. Vascular mechanism independent of catecholamine-immune pathway. Evidence: CPET hypoperfusion studies; HIF-1α-HSV reactivation in vitro. Falsifiable: Outbreaks correlate with catecholamines but not cerebral-artery blood-flow velocity during PEM provocation. Consequence: Volume expansion / vasoconstrictor avoidance may prevent HSV outbreaks AND reduce PEM severity (same vascular mechanism).

## Category 2: Research Directions

### Idea 2.1: Smartphone Diary Study (n ≥ 200 HSV-1+ ME/CFS)
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a
**Rationale:** 3–6 month prospective study pairing daily PEM logs (DSQ-PEM) with HSV prodromal symptom tracking and lesion photo documentation. Primary endpoints: (a) outbreak probability within 48h of PEM vs PEM-free windows; (b) outbreak frequency during PEM-heavy weeks vs PEM-light weeks; (c) outbreak timing prediction by PEM vs stress scores. Requires HSV-1 serostatus confirmation. Falsifiable: outbreak timing independent of PEM. The core validation study for the entire probe concept.

### Idea 2.2: Salivary HSV DNA Kinetics During CPET Provocation
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a
**Rationale:** Daily salivary HSV-1 DNA qPCR before/ during/ after CPET PEM provocation. HSV DNA appears in saliva during reactivation — quantitative viral shedding kinetics could provide continuous readout more sensitive than visible lesions (most reactivation events are subclinical). Combine with lesion photography for penetrance rate. Falsifiable: HSV DNA concentrations show no temporal relationship with CPET or PEM onset.

### Idea 2.3: LCM Outbreak Frequency vs CPET Performance
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a
**Rationale:** At the 2-day CPET LCM type, patients with more frequent HSV outbreaks should show WORSE 2nd-day CPET performance (greater VO2peak drop, greater work-efficiency decline) — because PEM severity and HSV reactivation susceptibility may share a common upstream mechanism (catecholamine surge magnitude, immune-exhaustion depth). Falsifiable: HSV outbreak frequency shows no correlation with 2nd-day CPET decline.

### Idea 2.4: Valacyclovir Suppression RCT with Co-Primary Endpoints
**Category:** 2 (research design)
**Certainty:** n/a
**Rationale:** Double-blind crossover RCT: valacyclovir 1g daily vs placebo in HSV-1+ ME/CFS patients with ≥3 outbreaks/year. Co-primary endpoints: (a) HSV outbreak frequency reduction, (b) PEM severity (DSQ-PEM), (c) weekly fatigue score. Secondary: salivary HSV DNA. If valacyclovir suppresses HSV but PEM severity unchanged → HSV reactivation is downstream, not causal. This is a category-2 research design (not category-3 drug recommendation), but under PARTIAL caps, integration is skipped — the valacyclovir trial design element edges toward category-3 territory. Noted in plan but not integrated into chapter text.

## Category 10: Reasons This Mechanism May NOT Be Relevant

### Idea 10.1: Base Rate Too High for ME/CFS-Specific Signal
**Category:** 10
**Rationale:** HSV-1 prevalence ~67% in adults; 20–40% of seropositive individuals experience recurrent outbreaks. ~13–27% of the adult population already experiences cold sores without ME/CFS. Buchwald 1996 (n=548) found no serological differences. If the absolute outbreak frequency in ME/CFS is similar to population baseline, the probe has zero discriminant value — it tests temporal pattern (PEM coupling) rather than absolute frequency, but background rates make temporal patterns harder to detect. The burden of proof is on the probe to show PEM-specific temporal clustering ABOVE background recurrence rates.

### Idea 10.2: Epiphenomenal — Shared Trigger, Independent Pathways
**Category:** 10
**Rationale:** PEM and HSV reactivation may share a trigger (SNS activation, catecholamine surge) but be mechanistically independent. HSV reactivation does not cause PEM; PEM does not cause HSV reactivation; both are downstream of the same catecholamine pulse. Under this model, HSV outbreaks are a marker of catecholamine activity — which is already measurable (HRV, norepinephrine) — and add no additional information beyond what existing autonomic monitoring provides. The probe would be redundant, not additive.

### Idea 10.3: ~33% HSV-1 Seronegative Exclusion
**Category:** 10
**Rationale:** ~43% of adults are HSV-1 seronegative (per US data). These patients cannot contribute data to the probe. If HSV-2 is included, additional prevalence constraints apply (~12% US adults). The probe is inherently restricted to a subset — and Buchwald 1996 suggests the seropositive subset is NOT different from seronegative in ME/CFS-relevant outcomes. The probe population is seropositive, outbreak-prone (~13–27% of seropositive), and willing to self-document — a small sub-subset with selection bias toward health-literacy and disease-management engagement.

## Category 11: Null Hypothesis Assessment

### Idea 11.1: No HSV Outbreak Frequency Difference vs Controls
**Category:** 11
**Rationale:** The most parsimonious null: ME/CFS patients have the same HSV outbreak frequency as matched seropositive controls. If null confirmed: HSV is not a ME/CFS-specific probe — it's a normal population phenomenon. The probe collapses. This would be consistent with Buchwald 1996 null (n=548, no serological differences).

### Idea 11.2: HSV Outbreaks Do NOT Temporally Track PEM
**Category:** 11
**Rationale:** Even if outbreak frequency is elevated, the probe requires temporal coupling. If outbreaks are more frequent but randomly distributed relative to PEM, they reflect general immune dysregulation or stress rather than PEM-specific reactivation. The null hypothesis: outbreak timing is independent of PEM after controlling for stress scores.

### Idea 11.3: Valacyclovir Suppresses HSV but PEM Severity Unchanged
**Category:** 11
**Rationale:** HSV reactivation is downstream, not causal. Valacyclovir eliminates outbreaks but PEM continues — HSV is a PEM consequence marker, not a PEM driver. The probe retains utility (still a PEM-correlated readout), but no therapeutic implication. If valacyclovir eliminates both outbreaks AND PEM → HSV reactivation is rate-limiting for PEM. This would be a major finding (BUT PARTIAL caps block therapeutic brainstorming).

## Category 12: Evidence Quality Concerns

### Idea 12.1: Retrospective Recall Unreliability
**Category:** 12
**Rationale:** Patient recall of "how many cold sores in the past 6 months" and "were they PEM-related" is unreliable — recall bias in chronic illness populations is well-documented. Prospective diary with photographic documentation is required to validate retrospective estimates. Without prospective validation, any retrospective survey data (including existing HSV serology studies that ask about outbreak history) are unreliable.

### Idea 12.2: Aphthous Ulcer vs HSV Lesion Misclassification
**Category:** 12
**Rationale:** Patients cannot reliably distinguish herpes labialis from aphthous stomatitis (canker sores) without laboratory confirmation. Aphthous ulcers are immune-mediated (T-cell mediated keratinocyte destruction), not viral. If patients report canker sores as cold sores, the "HSV outbreak" signal is noise. PCR confirmation of HSV DNA from lesion swabs is required for validation studies. Self-report without PCR has unknown sensitivity and specificity.

### Idea 12.3: HSV-1 vs HSV-2 Conflation
**Category:** 12
**Rationale:** HSV-1 and HSV-2 have different latency sites (trigeminal vs sacral ganglia), different outbreak patterns (oral vs genital), and different reactivation triggers. Most ME/CFS-relevant research (PEM, catecholamines, systemic SNS activation) targets the systemic autonomic axis, not the genital-specific sacral axis. If HSV-2 outbreaks are conflated with HSV-1 in patient reports, the signal is diluted. Studies should distinguish HSV-1 from HSV-2 serologically and anatomically.
