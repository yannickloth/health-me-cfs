# Brainstorm: Hyperparathyroidism as a Differential Diagnosis for ME/CFS

**Date:** 2026-08-21
**Origin:** integrate-topic Phase 4 (hyperparathyroidism-differential)
**Context:** Core content established in `req-endocrine-exclusions.typ` — hyperparathyroidism added to the pre-ME/CFS-diagnosis endocrine-exclusion workup (serum calcium via CMP; PTH if elevated). Brainstorm limited to testable hypotheses, research directions, and diagnostic-recognition nuances directly relevant to the DIFFERENTIAL-DIAGNOSIS topic. No treatment recommendations beyond the established descriptive management (parathyroidectomy for primary; vitamin D correction for secondary; cinacalcet/bisphosphonates where surgery unsuitable).

**Evidence base (discounted certainty per task; bib `certainty` field in parentheses):**
- Adkisson 2014 (0.56): FM symptoms respond to parathyroidectomy; 89% improved; cognition/memory improved most (80%) — *bib 0.70*
- Koman 2022 (0.53): psychiatric comorbidity (depression/anxiety) improves after parathyroidectomy — *bib 0.70*
- Liu 2021 (0.53): psychiatric comorbidity improves after parathyroidectomy — *bib 0.70*
- Costa 2016 (0.44): 6% hypercalcemic HPT + 17% normocalcemic HPT in a small FM sample — *bib 0.55*
- Ferrari 2015 (0.48, NULL): PHPT prevalence NOT elevated in FM vs general population — *bib 0.60*
- Borgia 2012 (0.24): case series, HPT as forgotten cause of musculoskeletal pain mistaken for FM — *bib 0.30*
- Reviews (0.38): Mairinger 2026, Oberger Marques 2020, Wootton 2026 — symptom overlap + workup — *bib 0.50 each*

## Category 1 — Testable hypotheses

| # | Idea | Certainty | NEW vs covered |
|---|------|-----------|----------------|
| H1 | **Occult normocalcemic hyperparathyroidism is a correctable minority subgroup in ME/CFS cohorts.** Costa found 17% normocalcemic HPT in FM; if ME/CFS (an overlapping syndrome) shows similar or lower prevalence, the standard Step-2 CMP (total calcium only) would MISS these cases because total calcium can be normal while PTH is elevated. Testable: measure PTH alongside calcium in a ME/CFS cohort and quantify the normocalcemic-HPT subgroup that current screening would miss. | 0.35 | **NEW** — the core requirement screens only via serum calcium; the normocalcemic-PTH gap is an extension not yet in the paper |
| H2 | **Hypercalcemia-driven muscle weakness, thirst/polyuria, and depression explain a portion of "ME/CFS" symptom burden in the mislabelled minority.** Adkisson (cognition improved 80%) and Liu (depression 27.5%→8.2%) show specific symptom reversibility after PTX. If a ME/CFS-labelled patient's fatigue is truly HPT-driven, symptoms should partially remit after PTX — a falsifiable prediction tied to the parathyroidectomy outcome evidence. | 0.45 | Partial — symptom *overlap* is covered (req line 25); the *reversibility-by-PTX prediction in ME/CFS-labelled patients* is a NEW framing |
| H3 | **The neuropsychiatric route (monoamine/neuroinflammation, per Mairinger) is the dominant mechanism by which HPT mimics ME/CFS cognitive symptoms** — implying cognitive-predominant presentations are the highest-yield subgroup for calcium/PTH screening. | 0.38 | **NEW** — mechanism attribution for *which* ME/CFS-like symptom should trigger screening |

## Category 2 — Research directions

| # | Idea | Certainty | NEW vs covered |
|---|------|-----------|----------------|
| R1 | **Retrospective review of ME/CFS-diagnosed patients' archived calcium/PTH to estimate the misdiagnosis rate.** The cheapest decisive study: pull CMPs from a ME/CFS cohort, count elevated-calcium + elevated-PTH cases, and report how many would have been reclassified. Directly quantifies the exclusion's real-world yield. | n/a | **NEW** — quantifies misdiagnosis risk; no such review exists for ME/CFS (Costa/Ferrari are FM-only) |
| R2 | **PTH measurement added to the standard exclusion panel in a prospective ME/CFS diagnostic cohort.** Tests H1 directly — whether normocalcemic HPT is present at meaningful prevalence in ME/CFS and whether Step-2 calcium-only screening misses it. | n/a | **NEW** — extends Costa's FM finding to ME/CFS; the current algorithm screens calcium-only |
| R3 | **Case-series audit of parathyroidectomy outcomes in patients carrying a ME/CFS or FM label.** Tests H2 — whether a documented "ME/CFS" symptom set remits after PTX, mirroring Adkisson in FM. | n/a | **NEW** — the reversibility evidence is FM/PHPT-only, never a ME/CFS-labelled cohort |

## Category 3 — Diagnostic nuances / clinical-recognition gaps

| # | Idea | Certainty | NEW vs covered |
|---|------|-----------|----------------|
| N1 | **Rarity + specialist-diagnosis gap.** PHPT is ~1% prevalence (Wootton); a GP seeing a chronic-fatigue patient rarely considers it, and the diagnosis is usually made by an endocrinologist/surgeon — so a ME/CFS label can be applied without the workup being owned by anyone. The exclusion requirement mitigates this only if the calcium value is actually reviewed. | n/a | **NEW** — the core requirement lists testing but not the GP-recognition/ownership barrier |
| N2 | **Distinguishing hypercalcemia-driven symptoms from ME/CFS.** Thirst/polyuria and bone/joint pain are more HPT-typical than PEM; the presence of PEM (post-exertional malaise) is the discriminating feature that HPT does not classically produce. A clinical-recognition flag: absence of true PEM should lower suspicion of ME/CFS and raise it for HPT. | n/a | **NEW** — the requirement lists overlap but not the *discriminator* (PEM) that separates the two |
| N3 | **"Inappropriately-normal PTH" trap.** The requirement states elevated *or inappropriately-normal* PTH. In practice, a normal PTH alongside hypercalcemia is often read as "not HPT" by clinicians unfamiliar with the term — a recognition gap worth a one-line flag. | n/a | **NEW** — nuance in interpreting the PTH result not currently spelled out |

## Category 4 — Interactions with existing ME/CFS findings

| # | Idea | Certainty | NEW vs covered |
|---|------|-----------|----------------|
| I1 | **Do NOT conflate HPT hypercalcemia with ME/CFS TRPM3/TRPM7 calcium dysregulation.** The ME/CFS channelopathy is *intracellular* Ca²⁺ influx impairment (TRPM3/TRPM7 in NK cells, @DuPreez2023trpm7) in the context of *normal circulating* calcium. HPT is *extracellular/hormonal* hypercalcemia with normal channels. These are mechanistically opposite calcium problems; a brainstorm MUST flag that elevated serum calcium is a separate axis from the TRP-channelopathy content, not a "same calcium mechanism" story. | n/a | **NEW (warning)** — prevents a false mechanistic bridge between two unrelated calcium findings |
| I2 | **Independent axis confirmation.** Because the two calcium findings are distinct, the exclusion workup should not be framed as "supporting" the TRP channelopathy — it is a differential-exclusion task on a separate hormonal axis. No cross-link to ch14d/ch17 channelopathy content is warranted. | n/a | **NEW (warning)** — scope boundary |
| I3 | **Vitamin D as confounder on both sides.** Secondary HPT from low vitamin D is the common non-primary cause (req line 29); the ME/CFS workup already includes vitamin D. A low 25(OH)D with raised PTH (secondary HPT) must be distinguished from primary HPT — a recognition nuance that affects both the endocrine workup and the vitamin-D content elsewhere. | n/a | **NEW** — bridges to existing vitamin-D workup; not currently stated in the requirement |
