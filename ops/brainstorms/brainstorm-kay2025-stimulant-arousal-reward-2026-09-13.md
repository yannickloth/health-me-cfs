**Key angle:** the reward-effort / effort-invigoration axis is an under-explored ME/CFS probe. Central questions: is PEM "effort intolerance" partly an effort-cost valuation signal?; are dopamine reward-salience deficits vs noradrenergic arousal deficits separable ME/CFS endophenotypes with different drug responses?; does the arousal/reward finding change the masking-hazard model?; can objective readouts (effort-discounting, pupillometry, EEG) separate arousal vs reward vs attention?

**Idea format:** each idea carries `origin: brainstorm`, mechanistic rationale, verified evidence link, certainty (0.0–1.0), falsifiable prediction, and a non-specialist consequence.

| 1.3 | **The masking hazard is a reward-effort phenomenon, not an attention phenomenon.** Stimulants raise willingness to invest effort without restoring capacity, so the drug decouples subjective readiness from physiological reserve. | 0.50 | `@Bowman2023NotSoSmart` (effort level up, quality down); `@Eckey2025PatientReported` (+77.1% brain fog, −1.5% PEM worsening); `@Kay2025stimulantarousalreward`. origin: brainstorm | Objective activity monitoring during stimulant use should show increased activity/effort expenditure that **precedes** and predicts PEM worsening, while self-rated fatigue stays improved; if PEM does not track increased effort output, the reward-effort masking account is wrong. | A patient who feels ready to do more is being pushed by the drug's reward signal, not given more energy — this is why activity tracking matters more than how the patient feels. |
