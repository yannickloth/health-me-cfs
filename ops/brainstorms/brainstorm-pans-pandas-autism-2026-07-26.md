## Category 1: Novel Hypotheses

### H1.1 — The "Undiagnosed PANS" Hypothesis (Nosological)

**Mechanism:** The user's clinical observation — children diagnosed autistic later evaluated for PANS after sudden regression — implies an unrecognised phenotype: immune-mediated acute/subacute neuropsychiatric regression that meets PANS/PANDAS clinical criteria but receives only an autism diagnosis. The syndromic label (autism) obscures the mechanism-based subgroup (immune-mediated encephalopathy). This is the nosological error the paper's Architecture B argument predicts: a biologically immune-mediated condition classified by surface behavioural phenotype rather than mechanism.

**Evidence link:** PANS criteria (Chang 2015) = abrupt-onset OCD/restricted eating + ≥2 neuropsychiatric symptoms temporally linked to infection — a phenotype easily mistaken for "regressive autism" if the regression is global and communication-focused rather than OCD-specific. Masterson 2025 (10-year Stanford PANS data) documents the clinical spectrum. Aslan 2021 found ~20% of ASD carry anti-neuronal antibodies regardless of regression status — immune-mediated subset exists but is not cleanly correlated with regression phenotype.

**Provisional certainty:** 0.20

**Falsifiable prediction:** If systematic PANS diagnostic criteria screening is applied to a regressive-autism cohort (n ≥ 200) at the time of regression, 10–25% will meet PANS criteria, and anti-neuronal antibody positivity will be enriched in the PANS-criteria-positive subset (≥ 3× controls). If <3% meet PANS criteria or antibody profiles are identical to the non-PANS-autism group, this hypothesis fails.

**Paper connection:** Architecture B existence proof. The PANDAS controversy → stratification resolution prefigures the same arc the paper forecasts for ME/CFS.

### H1.2 — The Neuroimmune Encephalopathy Spectrum (NES) Hypothesis (Disease-Model)

**Mechanism:** PANS, post-infectious ME/CFS, Long COVID neurocognitive syndrome, and the immune-mediated subset of regressive autism form a single disease class sharing Architecture B logic: immune trigger → autoantibody generation → circuit-specific CNS dysfunction → phenotype determined by circuit identity, not pathogen. The "spectrum" is defined by which CNS circuits are targeted:

| Circuit | Autoantibody targets | Phenotype | Clinical label |
|---------|---------------------|-----------|----------------|
| Basal ganglia | Anti-D1/D2, anti-lysoganglioside, anti-tubulin | OCD, tics, behavioural regression | PANDAS/PANS |
| Brainstem autonomic | Anti-adrenergic, anti-muscarinic | Orthostatic intolerance, fatigue, autonomic instability | ME/CFS-POTS dominant |
| Thalamocortical + diffuse | Anti-GPCR broad panel, anti-NMDAR, anti-neuronal | Cognitive dysfunction, brain fog, sensory amplification | ME/CFS-cognitive dominant / Long COVID |
| Cortical + limbic | Anti-NMDAR, anti-GABA, anti-AMPA | Psychosis, catatonia, seizure | Autoimmune encephalitis |
| Global + prenatal | Maternal anti-fetal brain Ab (7 antigens) | ASD-like developmental phenotype | Regressive autism (immune-mediated subset) |

The circuit targeted is determined by the autoantibody specificity, not the pathogen. A patient infected with SARS-CoV-2 could develop basal-ganglia PANS-like OCD (if the autoantibody targets D1R) or brainstem ME/CFS (if it targets β2-AdR) — the pathogen is not informative beyond being the trigger.

**Evidence link:** Kirvan 2003 + Kirvan 2006 (CaMKII pathway, PANDAS). Braunschweig 2013 (maternal Ab → ASD). Endres 2022 (autoimmune OCD spectrum). Vreeland 2023 (post-infectious OCD). Whiteley 2021 (autoimmune encephalitis → autism phenotype). The paper's existing cross-disease section already sketches this spectrum (ch14d, ch16).

**Provisional certainty:** 0.25

**Falsifiable prediction:** In a multi-disease anti-neuronal antibody panel (n ≥ 50 per group: PANDAS, ME/CFS, ASD-regression, Long COVID-neurocognitive, healthy), antibody profiles will cluster by circuit-target pattern (basal ganglia vs autonomic vs thalamocortical) rather than by diagnostic label. If all groups show identical antibody profiles or no clustering by circuit, the spectrum model is falsified in favour of a single, non-specific autoantibody phenomenon.

**Paper connection:** Gives the "neuroimmune encephalopathy spectrum" concept formal structure. Elevates it from speculation-env paragraph to a named, testable disease class.

### H1.3 — The "PANS-Adult" Hypothesis (Mechanistic)

**Mechanism:** PANS/PANDAS is operationally defined as a pediatric condition (Chang 2015 criteria explicitly paediatric). But the mechanism — infection → anti-neuronal antibody → CaMKII activation → basal ganglia dysfunction — has no age-dependency. If an adult develops the same sequence after streptococcal infection, the clinical phenotype may differ (adult basal ganglia dysfunction produces more subtle OCD/cognitive rigidity than dramatic childhood regression) and may be misclassified as "anxiety disorder," "OCD spectrum," or "ME/CFS with neuropsychiatric features." The hypothesis: a subset of adult-onset OCD, anxiety, or ME/CFS with OCD features represents undiagnosed "adult PANS" — same mechanism, different developmental window, different clinical label.

**Evidence link:** No adult PANS study exists — this is a pure evidence gap. PANDAS mechanism (CaMKII) is age-independent. Adult post-streptococcal OCD exists in Sydenham chorea literature (rheumatic fever can present in adulthood). OCD treatment literature shows adult onset is common (~50%). Masterson 2025 shows relapsing subtype may persist into young adulthood.

**Provisional certainty:** 0.10

**Falsifiable prediction:** Screening adults (n ≥ 200) with acute-onset OCD or ME/CFS with prominent OCD/cognitive-rigidity features for (a) recent streptococcal infection (ASO/anti-DNase B), (b) Cunningham Panel, (c) CaMKII activation by sera → will identify a subset (5–15%) with PANDAS-compatible antibody profile. If zero adults meet PANDAS antibody criteria, PANS is genuinely age-limited (or paediatric-specific antibody kinetics differ from adult).

**Paper connection:** Tests whether the PANDAS Architecture B parallel extends to adult ME/CFS or is exclusively a paediatric model.

### H1.4 — The Two-Strike Model: Prenatal Priming → Postnatal Trigger (Mechanistic)

**Mechanism:** Braunschweig 2013 shows ~23% of ASD attributable to maternal anti-fetal brain antibodies — a prenatal Architecture B. Meltzer 2017 documents both prenatal and postnatal immune axes. The two-strike model: (1) maternal anti-fetal brain antibodies cross the placenta during a critical developmental window, creating a CNS vulnerability (altered synaptic pruning, microglial priming, blood-brain barrier immaturity) but insufficient alone to produce clinical symptoms; (2) a postnatal infection triggers anti-neuronal antibodies that, in a brain already primed by prenatal antibody exposure, produce acute regression. This explains why only a subset of maternally-exposed children develop ASD — the second strike (postnatal) is required. It also explains why regressive autism overlaps with PANS temporally and phenomenologically: the postnatal strike IS the PANS mechanism, but the phenotype is modified by the prenatal priming.

**Evidence link:** Braunschweig 2013 (maternal Ab prevalence). Meltzer 2017 (dual axes). Aslan 2021 (~20% Ab prevalence across ASD). Connery 2018 (IVIG response in ASD — removing both prenatal and postnatal antibodies may be required for response).

**Provisional certainty:** 0.20

**Falsifiable prediction:** In a birth cohort with archived prenatal sera (e.g., Finnish Maternity Cohort, ALSPAC, or ABC cohort): (1) maternal anti-fetal brain antibody positivity will predict ASD diagnosis in offspring (OR ≥ 3); (2) among antibody-exposed children, those who develop ASD will have significantly more postnatal infectious episodes (≥ 2×) than antibody-exposed children who remain neurotypical; (3) those with both prenatal antibody exposure + postnatal infection will show highest ASD severity. If postnatal infection does not interact with prenatal antibody status, the two-strike model fails.

**Paper connection:** Parallels the paper's Architecture C (metabolic reserve deficit as latent vulnerability exposed by trigger). The two-strike model is Architecture C applied to immune rather than metabolic reserve.

### H1.5 — The "Post-PANDAS ME/CFS Transition" Hypothesis (Nosological)

**Mechanism:** Childhood PANDAS may leave behind a lasting CNS lesion — altered basal ganglia-thalamocortical connectivity, persistent low-grade CaMKII pathway dysregulation, or chronic dopaminergic hypofunction — that converts to ME/CFS in adolescence/adulthood. Alternatively, the same immune dysregulation that produced PANDAS in childhood may persist subclinically and reactivate under adult triggers (infection, stress, hormonal change) to produce ME/CFS. The critical unknown: the natural history of PANDAS into adulthood. No study has followed PANDAS children beyond 5–10 years. The Stanford PANS cohort (Masterson 2025) is the closest, but follow-up data on adult outcomes do not exist.

**Evidence link:** Masterson 2025: progressive subtype = deteriorating course, cumulative disability, increasing trigger sensitivity — trajectory mirrors ME/CFS. Frankovich 2015/2017: immunotherapy reduces but does not always eliminate symptoms — persistent subclinical immune activity. Kirvan 2003/2006: CaMKII activation may have long-term consequences on dopaminergic neuron function.

**Provisional certainty:** 0.10

**Falsifiable prediction:** Longitudinal follow-up of the Stanford PANS cohort into young adulthood (age 18–30) will show: (a) 10–25% meet ME/CFS diagnostic criteria (Fukuda or IOM) in early adulthood, (b) the progressive PANS subtype will have the highest conversion rate to ME/CFS, (c) persistent CaMKII activation in childhood is associated with adult fatigue phenotype. If zero PANS patients develop ME/CFS by age 30, childhood PANS is a self-limiting condition with no adult sequelae.

**Paper connection:** Fills the missing temporal bridge between paediatric autoimmune encephalopathy and adult ME/CFS. If the transition occurs, paediatric PANS is a ME/CFS prodrome in a subset.

### H1.6 — The Autoantibody Pathway Convergence Hypothesis (Mechanistic)

**Mechanism:** PANDAS anti-neuronal antibodies (targeting lysoganglioside Gm1 → CaMKII activation) and ME/CFS GPCR autoantibodies (targeting β2-adrenergic, muscarinic receptors → altered cAMP) converge on the same intracellular signaling collapse. CaMKII and cAMP/PKA are interconnected: CaMKII phosphorylates adenylyl cyclase and phosphodiesterase, modulating cAMP levels; PKA phosphorylates CaMKII regulatory subunits, modulating CaMKII activity. This is a coupled oscillator system. Disruption from either angle — CaMKII hyperactivation (PANDAS) or receptor-level cAMP blunting (ME/CFS) — produces similar downstream effects: impaired dopamine synthesis (tyrosine hydroxylase phosphorylation requires both CaMKII and PKA), altered synaptic plasticity (both CaMKII and PKA phosphorylate AMPA receptors), and disrupted energy allocation signalling.

**Evidence link:** Kirvan 2003 (CaMKII in PANDAS). Hartwig 2020 (ME/CFS IgG fails to activate β2-AdR — receptor-level cAMP blunting). Walitt 2024 (CSF catecholamine reductions). CaMKII-PKA crosstalk is biochemically established (Wong 2019; Hell 2007 reviews). Kirvan 2006 (CaMKII autoantibodies in ASD respond to treatment — same pathway).

**Provisional certainty:** 0.20

**Falsifiable prediction:** Head-to-head in vitro assay: PANDAS sera + ME/CFS sera + control sera on SH-SY5Y or SK-N-SH neuronal cell lines. (1) PANDAS sera will activate CaMKII > ME/CFS sera > controls. (2) ME/CFS sera will blunt forskolin-induced cAMP accumulation > PANDAS sera > controls. (3) A subset of ME/CFS sera (10–20%) will show both CaMKII activation AND cAMP blunting — the "dual-pathway" subgroup with most severe cognitive/OCD features. If PANDAS and ME/CFS sera produce non-overlapping, entirely distinct intracellular effects (PANDAS = CaMKII-only, ME/CFS = cAMP-only), the convergence hypothesis is restricted to a small subpopulation rather than a core mechanism.

**Paper connection:** Unifies the paper's PANDAS and GPCR-autoantibody threads at the intracellular signaling level.

### H1.7 — The "ME/CFS CaMKII Subset" Hypothesis (Nosological)

**Mechanism:** A subset of ME/CFS patients with prominent cognitive rigidity, OCD traits, sensory hypervigilance, and behavioural stereotypy may carry PANDAS-type anti-CaMKII pathway antibodies (anti-D1, anti-D2, anti-lysoganglioside, anti-tubulin, CaMKII activity) rather than — or in addition to — the classical GPCR autoantibodies (anti-adrenergic, anti-muscarinic). These patients occupy the nosological overlap zone between PANDAS (basal ganglia OCD/tics) and ME/CFS (fatigue/PEM). Their clinical phenotype should include: OCD-like cognitive perseveration, intolerance of routine disruption, sensory hypersensitivity with behavioural rigidity, and onset temporally linked to infection. This is a testable splitting hypothesis: within the ME/CFS umbrella, the CaMKII autoantibody profile defines a mechanistically distinct subgroup that mirrors PANDAS biology.

**Evidence link:** No ME/CFS study has ever tested the Cunningham Panel (anti-D1/D2, anti-lysoganglioside, anti-tubulin, CaMKII). The existing paper documents basal ganglia involvement in ME/CFS (diminished globus pallidus activation, dopamine receptor autoantibodies, reward processing deficits). Endres 2022 (autoimmune OCD spectrum bridges to ME/CFS). Connery 2018 (IVIG response in CaMKII-autoantibody-positive ASD).

**Provisional certainty:** 0.15

**Falsifiable prediction:** Cunningham Panel screening of ME/CFS patients (n ≥ 200) will find: (a) 10–20% have ≥ 1 elevated anti-neuronal antibody, (b) these patients will score significantly higher on OCD and sensory-sensitivity measures (Y-BOCS, SOR scales), (c) anti-neuronal antibody-positive ME/CFS patients will show better immunotherapy response than antibody-negative patients (mirroring Connery 2018 and Frankovich 2017 stratification logic). If anti-neuronal antibody positivity is <3% in ME/CFS or does not correlate with OCD/rigidity phenotype, the CaMKII subset hypothesis fails.

**Paper connection:** Directly tests the paper's claim that "basalgangliotropic autoantibodies should be enriched in ME/CFS patients with prominent OCD/cognitive-rigidity features" (ch16).

### H1.8 — The Progressive PANS as Pediatric ME/CFS Hypothesis (Mechanistic)

**Mechanism:** Masterson 2025 identified two PANS subtypes: relapsing (acute episodes → full remission → recurrence) and progressive (cumulative decline, no remission, increasing sensitivity to triggers). The progressive subtype's clinical trajectory — compounding disability, PEM-like crash episodes after minor triggers, autonomic instability, and cognitive decline between episodes — is phenotypically identical to progressive ME/CFS in adults. If the progressive PANS subtype and adult progressive ME/CFS share the same underlying process (autoantibody-mediated, multi-circuit CNS dysfunction with metabolic decompensation), then the same biological markers (GPCR autoantibodies, mitochondrial spare respiratory capacity, CSF cytokine profile) should differentiate progressive from relapsing subtypes in both conditions. Progressive PANS may literally BE paediatric ME/CFS — classified differently because it began in childhood and the dominant symptom was OCD rather than fatigue.

**Evidence link:** Masterson 2025 (relapsing vs progressive, 10-year Stanford data). Frankovich 2017 (immunotherapy for PANS — progressive subtype more treatment-resistant, mirroring chronic ME/CFS immunotherapy response). Progressive ME/CFS natural history documented by Nacul, Pheby, and Chu.

**Provisional certainty:** 0.15

**Falsifiable prediction:** Biomarker profiling (GPCR autoantibodies, inflammatory cytokines, mitochondrial respirometry) of Masterson 2025 relapsing vs progressive PANS subtypes will show: (1) progressive subtype has significantly more autoantibody diversity (≥ 3 GPCR specificities vs ≤ 1), (2) progressive subtype has lower PBMC spare respiratory capacity (Seahorse) than relapsing, (3) progressive PANS biomarker profile is indistinguishable from severe adult ME/CFS — while relapsing PANS biomarker profile is closer to controls. If relapsing and progressive PANS have identical biomarker profiles, the clinical-divergence is not driven by the same mechanisms as ME/CFS progression.

**Paper connection:** Extends the paper's severity-stratification framework to paediatric autoimmune encephalopathy. Bridges PANS progression to ME/CFS natural history.

## Category 2: Research Directions

### R2.1 — PANS/PANDAS Criteria Screening in ME/CFS Cohorts

**Rationale:** No study has asked ME/CFS adults about childhood PANS/PANDAS history. The hypothesis that a subset of ME/CFS is "adult PANS" (H1.3) or "post-PANDAS ME/CFS" (H1.5) requires prevalence data.

**Design:** Prospective screening of adult ME/CFS cohorts (n ≥ 500, recruitment via specialist clinics + patient registries). Three domains: (a) retroactive PANS clinical criteria questionnaire (abrupt-onset OCD/restricted eating + ≥2 neuropsychiatric symptoms in childhood), (b) streptococcal infection history (recurrent pharyngitis, rheumatic fever, scarlet fever), (c) current Cunningham Panel + GPCR autoantibody panel. Control: age/sex-matched healthy + fibromyalgia + primary OCD.

**Expected outcome:** 5–15% of ME/CFS patients will have PANS/PANDAS-compatible history OR elevated anti-neuronal antibodies. This subgroup will differ clinically (more OCD/rigidity, more infection-linked onset) and biochemically (CaMKII pathway activation). If prevalence <2%, PANDAS-ME/CFS overlap is clinically negligible.

**Certainty of feasibility:** 0.80

**Timeline:** 18–24 months (questionnaire development, ethics, recruitment, lab analysis).

### R2.2 — Head-to-Head Anti-Neuronal Antibody Profiling: PANDAS vs ME/CFS vs ASD

**Rationale:** The NES hypothesis (H1.2) predicts antibody profiles cluster by circuit-target, not diagnostic label. The circuit-specific autoantibody hypothesis (H1.5 from ch16) predicts basalgangliotropic autoantibodies in OCD/rigidity-dominant patients, autonomic-targeting autoantibodies in POTS/fatigue-dominant patients — regardless of which DSM/ICD label they carry.

**Design:** Cross-sectional profiling. Same comprehensive antibody panel run through: PANDAS (n ≥ 30), ME/CFS (n ≥ 100), ASD with regression (n ≥ 50), ASD without regression (n ≥ 50), adult OCD (n ≥ 50), Long COVID neurocognitive (n ≥ 50), healthy controls (n ≥ 100). Panel: Cunningham Panel (anti-D1, anti-D2, anti-lysoganglioside, anti-tubulin, CaMKII activity) + GPCR autoantibody ELISA (β2-AdR, M1/M2/M3/M4, α1-AdR, AT1, ETA) + anti-NMDAR/GABA/AMPA. Analysis: unsupervised clustering (UMAP, hierarchical) of antibody profiles. Primary test: do clusters segregate by diagnostic label or by antibody-target circuit?

**Expected outcome:** Three clusters emerge: (a) basal-ganglia-dominant (anti-D1/D2, anti-lysoganglioside, CaMKII), (b) autonomic-dominant (anti-β2-AdR, anti-M3/M4), (c) low/absent antibodies. Clusters (a) and (b) will each contain patients from multiple diagnostic labels — some "autism" patients in cluster (a), some "ME/CFS" patients in cluster (b), some "OCD" patients in cluster (a). Diagnostic labels will have AUROC <0.65 for predicting cluster membership.

**Certainty of feasibility:** 0.70

**Timeline:** 24–36 months (multi-site, multi-diagnosis, panel validation).

### R2.3 — Retrospective PANS Screening of Regressive Autism Datasets

**Rationale:** The user's clinical observation (autism→PANS after regression) is anecdata. Quantifying how many regressive-autism cases meet PANS criteria is an epidemiological question answerable without new sample collection — reanalysis of existing autism cohort data.

**Design:** Reanalyze existing regressive autism datasets (Autism Speaks ATN, CHARGE study, Simons Simplex Collection, AGRE, MIND Institute Autism Phenome Project) for: (a) PANS/PANDAS clinical criteria at time of regression — abrupt onset? infection temporally linked? OCD/tics present? restricted eating? (b) streptococcal culture/serology data collected proximally to regression, (c) immuno-inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, ESR, cytokine panels) at regression vs post-regression steady state.

**Expected outcome:** 10–30% of regressive autism cases meet PANS clinical criteria but received only an autism diagnosis. Infection-triggered regression will be more common than currently recognised. PANS-criteria-positive children will have distinct inflammatory profiles at regression vs PANS-criteria-negative children.

**Certainty of feasibility:** 0.80

**Timeline:** 12–18 months (data access agreements, chart review, statistical analysis).

### R2.4 — Longitudinal PANDAS-to-Adult Outcomes Study

**Rationale:** The natural history of PANDAS beyond adolescence is unknown. This is the single largest evidence gap for the PANDAS-ME/CFS nosological bridge (H1.5). The Masterson 2025 Stanford PANS cohort (n ≥ 200, oldest ~25–30) exists and can be followed.

**Design:** Prospective longitudinal follow-up of the Stanford PANS clinic cohort (and/or other large PANS cohorts: Rothman Center, MIND Institute, NIMH PANDAS cohort). Primary outcomes at ages 18, 25, 30: (a) ME/CFS diagnostic criteria (Fukuda, IOM, Canadian Consensus), (b) autoimmune comorbidity (thyroiditis, SLE, Sjögren's, APS), (c) persistent neuropsychiatric symptom burden, (d) occupational/educational attainment, (e) ongoing immunotherapy use. Biomarkers: Cunningham Panel, GPCR autoantibody panel, inflammatory cytokines, mitochondrial function (PBMC respirometry), tilt-table/autonomic testing.

**Expected outcome:** 10–25% will meet ME/CFS criteria by age 25. Progressive subtype (Masterson 2025) will have highest ME/CFS conversion rate. Autoimmune comorbidities will be enriched vs general population. A notable minority will have persistent but subclinical neuropsychiatric symptoms — "PANDAS in remission but never quite normal."

**Certainty of feasibility:** 0.55 (cohort exists but needs dedicated funding for adult-outcome follow-up; ethics for paediatric-to-adult transition tracking is complex but precedented — e.g., ALSPAC, Dunedin).

**Timeline:** 5–10 years (prospective follow-up window).

### R2.5 — CaMKII Activation Assay Using ME/CFS Sera

**Rationale:** The Kirvan 2003/2006 CaMKII activation assay is the definitive test for PANDAS-like pathophysiology. Running ME/CFS sera through the same assay directly tests the "ME/CFS CaMKII subset" (H1.7) and autoantibody convergence (H1.6) hypotheses. Simple, specific, high-impact.

**Design:** Culture neuronal cell line (SK-N-SH or SH-SY5Y — same as Kirvan 2006). Expose to IgG purified from: (a) ME/CFS sera (n ≥ 50, stratified by OCD/cognitive-rigidity phenotype), (b) PANDAS sera (n ≥ 15, positive control), (c) healthy sera (n ≥ 30, negative control), (d) ASD sera with known CaMKII antibody status (n ≥ 30, from Connery cohort or similar). Measure CaMKII activity via phospho-CaMKII Western blot + CaMKII activity assay (γ-32P-ATP incorporation). Primary outcome: proportion of ME/CFS sera producing CaMKII activation >2 SD above healthy control mean.

**Expected outcome:** 10–20% of ME/CFS sera activate CaMKII — enriched in the subset with prominent OCD/cognitive rigidity. These sera will be indistinguishable from PANDAS sera in the assay. Total ME/CFS CaMKII activation magnitude will be lower than PANDAS (more diffuse autoimmune process, fewer basal-ganglia-specific antibodies). If 0% of ME/CFS sera activate CaMKII, the PANDAS pathway is genuinely disease-specific and not shared with ME/CFS.

**Certainty of feasibility:** 0.65

**Timeline:** 12–18 months (assay established, sera collection, replication).

### R2.6 — Maternal Anti-Fetal Brain Antibody Screening in ME/CFS Families

**Rationale:** Braunschweig 2013 found ~23% of ASD cases had maternal anti-fetal brain antibodies. If the NES hypothesis (H1.2) and two-strike model (H1.4) are correct, the same maternal antibodies should be enriched — at a lower but measurable prevalence — in ME/CFS families.

**Design:** Screen stored maternal sera (from biorepositories with mother-child paired samples: ALSPAC, MoBa, Finnish Maternity Cohort, or prospective collection from ME/CFS family studies) for the Braunschweig 2013 panel of 7 fetal brain antigen reactivities. Three groups: mothers of ME/CFS probands (n ≥ 100), mothers of ASD probands (n ≥ 100, positive control), mothers of healthy probands (n ≥ 100). Secondary: test whether maternal antibody profile predicts proband clinical phenotype (ASD-like features in ME/CFS; OCD/rigidity vs fatigue-dominant).

**Expected outcome:** ME/CFS mothers will show intermediate antibody prevalence (10–18%) between ASD mothers (~23%) and controls (~1–3%). ME/CFS mothers with specific reactivities matching Braunschweig 2013 targets will have probands with more ASD-like cognitive features. If ME/CFS mothers show control-level prevalence, maternal antibody exposure is ASD-specific, not part of a broader neuroimmune spectrum.

**Certainty of feasibility:** 0.55 (depends on biorepository access and existing paired maternal sera; ALSPAC/MoBa access requires collaborative agreements with established investigators).

**Timeline:** 24–36 months.

### R2.7 — Natural History of Untreated PANDAS: Registry-Based Retrospective Study

**Rationale:** The debate over whether PANDAS is a "real disease" (Chiarello 2017) cannot be resolved without knowing what happens when it ISN'T treated. Yet most published PANDAS cohorts are treated — either with antibiotics (early) or immunotherapy (later). A registry-based retrospective study of untreated PANDAS would inform the nosological question: does untreated PANDAS spontaneously remit, persist as chronic OCD, or convert to a broader syndrome (ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, autoimmune comorbidity)?

**Design:** Identify cases meeting PANDAS clinical criteria from pre-1998 (before Swedo defined PANDAS) in longitudinal registries (ALSPAC, Dunedin, Swedish Twin Registry, Danish National Patient Registry). Classify by: (a) documented streptococcal infection + acute neuropsychiatric onset in childhood, (b) ICD codes for OCD, tic disorders, or acute behavioural regression. Follow registry records into adulthood for: ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, autoimmune disease, psychiatric comorbidity, disability status. Comparison: general population matched by age, sex, and streptococcal infection history (without neuropsychiatric sequelae).

**Expected outcome:** Untreated PANDAS will show: (a) 20–30% spontaneous remission by adulthood (self-limiting in minority), (b) 30–50% persistent neuropsychiatric symptoms (OCD, tics) into adulthood, (c) 10–20% develop broader multi-system illness (ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, autoimmune disease) — the progressive/converter subgroup.

**Certainty of feasibility:** 0.50 (retrospective registry coding is imprecise for PANDAS criteria pre-1998; sensitivity analysis with varying case definitions needed; relies on national registry access).

**Timeline:** 24–36 months.

## Category 10: Clinical Implications

### C10.1 — PANS/PANDAS Screening Questions for ME/CFS Intake

**Rationale:** Current ME/CFS clinical intake focuses on fatigue, PEM, sleep, pain, and orthostatic intolerance — neuropsychiatric history is limited to depression/anxiety screens. If a subset of ME/CFS is PANDAS-adjacent (H1.7) or post-PANDAS (H1.5), systematic screening is the first step to identifying this subgroup.

**Proposed 5 screening questions for ME/CFS clinical intake:**

1. "Did you experience a sudden, dramatic change in behaviour or personality in childhood or adolescence (onset within days–weeks)?" — captures abrupt-onset criterion.
2. "Was this change temporally linked to an infection (strep throat, flu, mono, other)?" — captures infection trigger criterion.
3. "During these episodes, did you develop any of: repetitive behaviours/OCD, motor or vocal tics, severe anxiety/separation anxiety, restricted eating, or behavioural regression?" — captures PANS neuropsychiatric symptom domains (Chang 2015).
4. "Do you have a history of recurrent streptococcal infections (frequent strep throat, scarlet fever, rheumatic fever)?" — captures GAS exposure history.
5. "Do you currently experience: intrusive/repetitive thoughts that are hard to stop, sensory hypervigilance or discomfort with routine change, or a feeling that your brain 'locks onto' things?" — captures active OCD/cognitive-rigidity features.

**Action pathway:** ≥3 positive → refer to neuroimmunology for Cunningham Panel + ASO/anti-DNase B + GPCR autoantibody panel. 1–2 positive → monitor; retest at next flare.

**Falsification condition:** If systematic screening finds <2% positive rate in ME/CFS clinic intake, routine inclusion is not cost-effective. Screening positive rate below 5% → no screening, context-dependent only.

**Paper connection:** Operationalises the paper's "post-infectious syndrome cluster" concept at the clinical workflow level.

### C10.2 — Lab Orders for the Post-Infectious Neuropsychiatric Differential in ME/CFS

**Rationale:** When an ME/CFS patient reports acute/subacute onset with prominent neuropsychiatric features (OCD, tics, behavioural regression, severe cognitive rigidity, sensory intolerance), the standard ME/CFS workup (CBC, CRP, TSH, iron studies, ANA, EBV/IgG, cortisol) is insufficient to evaluate for immune-mediated neuropsychiatric disease. A structured post-infectious neuropsychiatric lab panel would identify patients in the PANDAS-ME/CFS overlap zone and guide subspecialty referral.

**Proposed lab panel — post-infectious neuropsychiatric differential:**

### C10.4 — Differential Diagnosis: PANDAS vs ME/CFS-Associated OCD

**Rationale:** When an adult ME/CFS patient develops new or worsening OCD/tics/cognitive-rigidity features, the question is: "Is this ME/CFS-associated neuropsychiatric symptom (expected under Architecture B of the paper — neuroinflammation producing OCD-like features in a brain not previously OCD-prone, perhaps via non-specific neuroinflammation in basal ganglia circuits) or is this new-onset PANDAS (post-streptococcal, antibody-mediated, potentially immunotherapy-responsive) in an adult who previously had childhood PANDAS poorly recognised?" These are mechanically different: ME/CFS-associated OCD = diffuse neuroinflammation disinhibiting OCD circuits; PANDAS = specific anti-neuronal antibodies actively targeting OCD circuits. Treatment differs: the former responds to treating the ME/CFS process; the latter may require targeted immunomodulation.

**Clinical discriminating features (proposed, not yet validated):**

| Feature | ME/CFS-associated OCD | PANDAS/PANS-associated OCD |
|---------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Onset | Gradual, parallels ME/CFS severity | Abrupt (days), may coincide with infection |
| Infection link | Weak or absent | Strong temporal link to strep/infection |
| OCD content | Contamination, checking — typical OCD themes | May include unusual content: sensory hypersensitivity, intolerance of routine change, severe separation anxiety |
| Tics | Rare | Common (motor + vocal) |
| Neurological soft signs | Autonomic instability, brain fog, PEM | Choreiform movements, handwriting deterioration, urinary frequency |
| ASO/anti-DNase B | Normal (unless coincident infection) | Elevated |
| Cunningham Panel | Negative (expected) | Positive |
| Response to SSRIs | Typical OCD response | Often resistant or paradoxical |
| Response to antibiotics | None | May improve (in PANDAS subset) |

**Workflow recommendation:** Adult ME/CFS patient with new/worsening OCD/tics → screen with C10.1 questions → if ≥3 positive → ASO/anti-DNase B + Cunningham Panel. ## Category 12: Meta-Framework

### M12.1 — PANS/PANDAS as the Paper's "Rosetta Stone" — Architecture B's Missing Piece

**Framing:** The paper's Architecture A/B/C framework has always had a structural asymmetry. Architecture A (shared genetic vulnerability) is empirically populated: GWAS, twin studies, comorbidity data. Architecture C (metabolic reserve deficit) is populated: DecodeME, mitochondrial studies, CPET data. Architecture B (ME/CFS → secondary neuropsychiatric phenotype via immune-metabolic cascade) had plausible mechanisms — neuroinflammation → dopamine dysfunction, autoantibody generation, BBB disruption — but no "worked example" of the identical disease logic in a separate, independently validated condition. The paper was arguing Architecture B by extrapolation from within-ME/CFS data.

PANS/PANDAS fills this gap. It is NOT ME/CFS. It IS Architecture B in both its purest and best-characterised form:

- **Trigger:** Well-characterised (GAS, molecular mimicry — Kirvan 2003)
- **Intermediate:** Autoantibodies cross-react with neuronal targets (lysoganglioside Gm1 → CaMKII)
- **Phenotype:** Acquired neuropsychiatric syndrome (OCD, tics) indistinguishable at symptom level from developmental OCD
- **Reversibility:** Immunotherapy-responsive in a subset (Latimer 2015 = 89% TPA; Frankovich 2017 tiered protocol)
- **Heterogeneity problem:** Identical to ME/CFS — dramatic responders alongside non-responders; positive case series, equivocal RCT (Williams 2016)
- **Controversy-to-resolution arc:** PANDAS debated as "not real" for 20+ years → resolution through stratification: some patients are immune-mediated, some are not (Masterson 2025 classification)

This transforms Architecture B from a ME/CFS-specific extrapolation into a cross-disease pattern with at least one independently validated instance. The paper can now argue: "Architecture B is not a theory ABOUT ME/CFS — it is a disease class FOR WHICH PANDAS is the proof of principle and ME/CFS the hypothesised member."

**Certainty:** 0.55 within PANDAS domain (Architecture B proven for basal ganglia circuit). 0.25 → ME/CFS domain (extrapolation — same disease logic, different circuit, never directly tested).

**Consequence for the paper:** The paper's core argument — syndromic labels obscure mechanism-based subgroups — is now anchored externally. PANDAS provides a worked example that does not depend on any ME/CFS data or interpretation. The paper can point to PANDAS and say: "This is what Architecture B looks like when the science is mature. ME/CFS is the same story, told from an earlier chapter."

### M12.2 — The Stratification Imperative Gets Its Archetype (External to ME/CFS)

**Framing:** The paper's single most consistent argument is that syndromic diagnostic labels — ME/CFS, ADHD, ASD, fibromyalgia — collapse biologically heterogeneous populations into bins defined by surface symptoms, and that this is the root cause of failed treatment trials, contradictory biomarker studies, and slow therapeutic progress. The paper makes this argument using ME/CFS data and ADHD/ASD convergence.

PANS/PANDAS provides the clearest external example of this argument:

- **The Connery 2018 vs Plioplys 1998 contrast:** IVIG in ASD. Plioplys 1998 (n=10): unselected ASD → IVIG null/none response. Connery 2018 (n=31): ASD preselected for CaMKII autoantibodies → 62% IVIG response. Same treatment. Same outcome label (ASD). Opposite results. The difference: stratification by mechanism.

- **The Latimer 2015 vs Williams 2016 contrast:** immunomodulation in PANDAS. Latimer 2015: TPA in selected PANDAS → 89% response. Williams 2016 RCT: IVIG in PANDAS by syndromic criteria → not superior to placebo (overall). Post-hoc GAS+ subgroup showed better response — the stratification signal buried in an underpowered unstratified trial.

These are the stratification imperative in microcosm — in a literature adjacent to but independent of ME/CFS. The paper can argue that the stratification problem is not parochial to ME/CFS. It is a universal property of immune-mediated neuropsychiatric conditions: when the mechanism is heterogeneous but the outcome label is syndromic, treatment trials will fail until the population is stratified by mechanism.

**Consequence for the paper:** The paper's methodological argument (heterogeneity → treatment failure → need mechanism-based stratification) is no longer just an idea proposed for ME/CFS — it is a principle that has already been demonstrated in a closely related disease domain. The question is not whether stratification works (PANDAS shows it does) but whether the ME/CFS community implements it.
