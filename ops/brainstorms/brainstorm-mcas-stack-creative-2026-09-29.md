## A. Untargeted receptors and mediators (gaps)

| ID | Idea | Mechanistic rationale | Falsifiable prediction | Cert |
|----|------|----------------------|------------------------|------|
| A1 | **H4 receptor as the untargeted 4th histamine receptor** | H4R on immune cells (mast cells, eosinophils, T cells, DCs) drives chemotaxis and itch; the stack blocks H1/H2/CysLT1 only. | An H4 antagonist reduces MCAS symptoms in patients who fail H1+H2, i.e. response is dissociated from H1/H2 occupancy. | 0.25 |
| A2 | **Mediator escape: tryptase and PGD2 unblocked** | Histamine + leukotrienes are blocked; tryptase (PAR2→microglia) and PGD2 escape → residual MCAS. | Patients with high tryptase/PGD2 metabolites relative to histamine show residual symptoms on maximal H1+H2+CysLT1. | 0.35 |
| A3 | **Clearance vs receptor arm: DAO/HNMT defect masks as MCAS** | Receptor blockade does not reduce histamine load; a degradation defect (DAO gut, HNMT CNS) persists. | In antihistamine-partial responders, adding DAO ± addressing clearance improves symptoms where dose escalation does not. | 0.35 |

## B. Counterintuitive cross-arm interactions

| ID | Idea | Mechanistic rationale | Falsifiable prediction | Cert |
|----|------|----------------------|------------------------|------|
| B1 | **Copper → DAO → histamine bridge** | DAO (diamine oxidase) is a **copper-dependent** enzyme; functional copper deficiency lowers histamine breakdown. Links queued gap G4 (copper) to the MCAS stack. | MCAS patients with low serum copper/ceruloplasmin have low DAO activity; copper repletion improves histamine tolerance where antihistamines plateau. | 0.30 |
| B2 | **Histamine redistribution: H1 block shifts histamine to H2** | Histamine displaced from H1 binds H2 (immunosuppressive: ↓Th1/IFN-γ). H1 monotherapy may therefore worsen immune suppression; H1+H2 avoids it. | H1 monotherapy raises Th2/Th1 ratio vs H1+H2. | 0.30 |
| B3 | **H2 autocrine brake: famotidine may increase degranulation** | Mast cells carry H2 (and H4); histamine feedback via H2 inhibits further degranulation. Blocking H2 removes the brake. | Famotidine raises urinary N-methylhistamine in a subset while relieving gut symptoms. | 0.20 |
| B4 | **Cholinergic–mast-cell paradox: pyridostigmine stabilises OR triggers mast cells** | ACh via α7/M3 generally inhibits mast-cell degranulation (cholinergic anti-inflammatory), but cholinergic stimulation can trigger degranulation in some contexts. | Pyridostigmine response splits by mast-cell cholinergic phenotype: helps MCAS in one subset, flares it in another. | 0.25 |

## C. The four drugs as diagnostic instruments

| ID | Idea | Mechanistic rationale | Falsifiable prediction | Cert |
|----|------|----------------------|------------------------|------|
| C1 | **Pyridostigmine as a functional M3-receptor bioassay** | Mestinon raises ACh at muscarinic synapses; a symptom that improves was cholinergic-reversible (M3 AAb block or cholinergic deficit). | Pyridostigmine-responsive sicca/sweating/GI motility co-occurs with M3 autoantibodies. | 0.35 |
| C2 | **Four-arm mediator-map trial** | Each drug probes a distinct arm (H1/PAF, H2/immune, CysLT1, cholinergic); the response pattern defines the dominant arm. | Response profiles cluster into reproducible subtypes by mediator biomarker panel. | 0.30 |
| C3 | **Non-response fingerprinting (inverse negative control)** | The pattern of nulls constrains mechanisms: fail all MCAS drugs → mast-cell hypothesis constrained; fail mestinon → cholinergic/perfusion axis constrained. | Null-pattern classification predicts biomarker profile better than any single drug response. | 0.30 |
| C4 | **Histamine-as-compensation: H1 block may unmask fatigue** | Histamine H1 drives wakefulness; some patients may rely on histamine surges for compensatory arousal. | Rupatadine worsens fatigue in the hyperarousal subtype and improves it in the mast-cell/flushing subtype. | 0.25 |

## D. Bridges to pain and arousal systems

| ID | Idea | Mechanistic rationale | Falsifiable prediction | Cert |
|----|------|----------------------|------------------------|------|
| D1 | **H1 → TRPV1: rupatadine as indirect TRPV1 modulator** | Histamine H1 sensitises TRPV1 nociceptors; H1 blockade reduces TRPV1 sensitisation (links to exertional-soreness cycle). | H1 blockade reduces capsaicin-evoked/heat-evoked pain and exertional soreness in mast-cell-driven patients. | 0.35 |
| D2 | **Histamine–orexin–arousal ("wired but tired")** | Histamine is wake-promoting and interacts with orexin; MCAS histamine surges may drive the arousal mismatch. | H1-blocker sedation threshold (dose to sedation) indexes central histamine arousal load and tracks the hyperarousal phenotype. | 0.30 |
| D3 | **PAF → platelet/microclot: rupatadine as microclot modulator** | PAF activates platelets and is implicated in microclot/fibrin-amyloid pathology; rupatadine is an H1+PAF antagonist. | Rupatadine reduces platelet aggregation or microclot burden in MCAS-positive ME/CFS. | 0.25 |
| D4 | **Montelukast as the only CNS-penetrant stack member → "brain MCAS" probe** | Montelukast crosses the BBB; CysLT1 in brain links to neuroinflammation. It is the CNS arm of the stack. | Montelukast (not peripherally acting stack drugs) reduces brain fog; neuropsychiatric reaction marks CNS-leukotriene-driven fog. | 0.30 |
| D5 | **Mast-cell activation as an energy-envelope drain** | Degranulation costs ATP and Ca²⁺ handling; chronic MCAS imposes a continuous metabolic tax. | Effective MCAS blockade raises measured energy-envelope capacity without changing the primary mitochondrial defect. | 0.25 |

## E. Timing and dose

| ID | Idea | Mechanistic rationale | Falsifiable prediction | Cert |
|----|------|----------------------|------------------------|------|
| E1 | **Circadian-timed stack** | Histamine/mast-cell activity peaks at night; montelukast is already dosed PM. Links to queued gap G5 (chronopharmacology). | Phase-shifting H1/H2 dosing to the patient's mediator peak improves control at equal dose. | 0.30 |
| E2 | **Cholinergic reserve index** | The pyridostigmine dose for benefit vs the dose at which cholinergic side effects appear defines a therapeutic window. | This index predicts pyridostigmine tolerability and dose ceiling. | 0.35 |
| E3 | **Antihistamine tachyphylaxis and pulsed dosing** | Chronic H1 blockade upregulates H1 receptors → rebound on withdrawal; links to the repo's tachyphylaxis/hormesis framework. | Pulsed H1 dosing with a drug-holiday restore-test distinguishes desensitisation from true tolerance. | 0.30 |
| E4 | **Cycle-timed MCAS dosing** | Estrogen increases mast-cell degranulation (repo's estrogen–mast-cell axis). | Symptoms track menstrual phase; cycle-synchronised dosing beats fixed dosing. | 0.30 |
