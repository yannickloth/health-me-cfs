## Constructive Categories

### Category 1 — Novel hypotheses

**1.1 Low-antibody-gated chronicity as a general post-infectious principle** *(origin: brainstorm)*
Low/absent acute antibody response predicts Long COVID @GarciaAbellan2021antibodyLC @Augustin2021postcovid; if the same holds for other ME/CFS triggers (EBV, Q fever, Ross River, West Nile), then an inadequate early adaptive response may be a common gate for post-infectious chronicity, not a SARS-CoV-2-specific quirk.
- *Mechanistic rationale:* failure to clear/contain pathogen → persistent reservoir → chronic immune drive.
- *Evidence link:* Long COVID low-antibody predictive (García-Abellán, Augustin); EBV/other triggers cause ME/CFS (Choutka 2022 in review).
- *Preliminary certainty:* 0.35.
- *Falsifiable prediction:* prospective cohorts of acute EBV/Q-fever/West-Nile infection will show lower acute antibody/immune-response titers in those who later develop ME/CFS than in recovered controls.
- *Consequence:* If a weak early immune response commonly gates post-infectious chronicity, it could justify acute-phase immune-supportive trials across multiple triggers, not just SARS-CoV-2.

**1.2 Fungal translocation as an amplifier, not initiator, of post-infectious immune activation** *(origin: brainstorm)*
Elevated fungal translocation induces NF-κB/cytokine production in Long COVID @Giron2022fungaltranslocation; in ME/CFS the fungal-gut-lung-immune axis is likely an amplifier of an already-dysregulated immune state rather than the initiating insult — testable via fungal translocation markers + gut permeability in ME/CFS cohorts.
- *Preliminary certainty:* 0.30.
- *Falsifiable prediction:* ME/CFS patients with high fungal-translocation markers will show higher cytokine levels and worse PEM than marker-negative ME/CFS patients, controlling for gut-microbiome composition.
- *Consequence:* If fungal translocation amplifies ME/CFS immune activation, gut-barrier-supportive or antifungal strategies (carefully) could become adjunctive levers for a subset.

**1.3 Seroreversion as an under-recognized confound in ME/CFS viral-trigger studies** *(origin: brainstorm)*
The seroreversion testing-bias documented in Long COVID @VanElslande2021seroreversion likely extends to ME/CFS: studies classifying prior EBV/HHV-6 exposure by antibody titer may misassign seroreverted patients, underestimating the viral-trigger association.
- *Preliminary certainty:* 0.35.
- *Falsifiable prediction:* Re-analysis of ME/CFS viral-trigger studies using symptom/clinical diagnosis (not antibody titer alone) for exposure will show a stronger trigger association than titer-based classification.
- *Consequence:* Correcting this bias could strengthen (or correct) the viral-trigger evidence base in ME/CFS and improve study design.

### Category 2 — Research directions

**2.1 Acute-to-chronic immune-response kinetic study across post-infectious triggers** *(origin: brainstorm)*
A multi-trigger prospective cohort (SARS-CoV-2, EBV, Q fever, Ross River) measuring acute antibody/immune kinetics and tracking conversion to chronic fatigue would test whether a shared "insufficient-early-response" window predicts post-infectious chronicity.
- *Preliminary certainty:* 0.40.
- *Falsifiable prediction:* the earliest-to-lowest antibody responders across triggers have the highest 12-month chronic-fatigue conversion rate, independent of acute severity.
- *Consequence:* If confirmed, this gives a shared early predictor and intervention window across all post-infectious triggers relevant to ME/CFS.

**2.2 Fungal-translocation marker panel in ME/CFS** *(origin: brainstorm)*
Measure fungal-translocation markers (e.g., 1,3-β-D-glucan, fungal DNA, related markers) + intestinal permeability in ME/CFS vs controls to test whether the Long COVID fungal-translocation finding generalizes.
- *Preliminary certainty:* 0.30.
- *Falsifiable prediction:* ME/CFS patients show elevated fungal-translocation markers vs healthy controls, correlated with gut-permeability and cytokine measures.
- *Consequence:* A positive result would extend a Long COVID mechanism to ME/CFS and identify a measurable, potentially targetable axis.

### Category 4 — Supplement/nutraceutical ideas

**4.1 Pycnogenol as an endothelial/microcirculatory candidate for ME/CFS (research-stage only)** *(origin: brainstorm)*
Pycnogenol improved microcirculation/endothelial function and oxidative stress in COVID-19 recovery @Belcaro2022pycnogenol; given the shared microclot/endothelial pathology in ME/CFS, it is a candidate for a small mechanistic pilot — explicitly NOT validated, no dosing from ME/CFS data.
- *Preliminary certainty:* 0.25.
- *Falsifiable prediction:* an ME/CFS pilot measuring endothelial/microcirculatory and oxidative-stress endpoints would show improvement only if the endothelial-dysfunction subset is present.
- *Consequence:* If a subset of ME/CFS with demonstrable endothelial/microcirculatory dysfunction responds, it would identify a targetable subtype — but this is speculative and unvalidated.

### Category 7 — Mathematical model extensions

**7.1 Two-hit model with an "insufficient-adaptive-response" first hit** *(origin: brainstorm)*
The review's low-antibody-predicts-LC finding suggests the DAG/ODE post-infectious model should include an "inadequate early adaptive clearance" node upstream of chronic immune drive — a distinct first-hit variable from overactive inflammation.
- *Preliminary certainty:* 0.35.
- *Falsifiable prediction:* model simulations with low-early-clearance parameter predict higher probability of persistent post-infectious state; longitudinal data would validate the parameter.
- *Consequence:* Adding an insufficient-response node would refine the trigger-vs-amplifier model and clarify which patients transition to chronicity.

### Category 8 — Cross-disease bridges

**8.1 Seroreversion/bias as a cross-disease methodological bridge (Long COVID → ME/CFS → other post-infectious)** *(origin: brainstorm)*
The Long COVID seroreversion critique provides a transferable methodological template for re-examining exposure classification in ME/CFS and other post-infectious illness research.
- *Preliminary certainty:* 0.30 (methodological, not mechanistic).
- *Falsifiable prediction:* applying symptom-based exposure classification to existing ME/CFS datasets changes estimated viral-trigger prevalence.
- *Consequence:* A cheap, high-leverage methodological improvement that could correct a broad class of studies.

## Critical Categories (MANDATORY)

### Category 10 — Reasons this mechanism may NOT be relevant to ME/CFS

**10.1 Low-antibody-predictive may be SARS-CoV-2-specific** *(origin: brainstorm)*
Low antibody gating post-infectious chronicity may reflect unique SARS-CoV-2 features (ACE2 tropism, viral persistence) not shared with EBV/Q-fever/West-Nile ME/CFS triggers.
- *Preliminary certainty:* n/a.
- *Consequence:* If the low-antibody gate is SARS-CoV-2-specific, it may not generalize to ME/CFS from other triggers — a real limitation on the cross-trigger inference.

**10.2 Long COVID-ME/CFS overlap may reflect diagnostic-criteria inflation, not shared biology** *(origin: brainstorm)*
The ≈50% of Long COVID meeting ME/CFS criteria could partly reflect overlapping, self-referential symptom criteria rather than a shared underlying mechanism — the CPET-2 divergence (no Day-2 deterioration in LC) is evidence against full equivalence.
- *Preliminary certainty:* n/a.
- *Consequence:* Overlapping criteria can create an illusory shared-mechanism impression; direct head-to-head mechanistic studies are needed before assuming Long COVID data transfer to ME/CFS.

### Category 11 — Null hypothesis assessment

**11.1 What if Long COVID and ME/CFS are distinct despite overlap?** *(origin: brainstorm)*
If Long COVID resolves in a majority (GI/respiratory symptoms resolve; some immune changes reversible at 24 months) while ME/CFS persists lifelong, the two may be overlapping-acute-versus-chronic states rather than the same disease — Long COVID data would then NOT be validly extrapolated to established ME/CFS.
- *Preliminary certainty:* n/a.
- *Consequence:* If Long COVID largely resolves and ME/CFS does not, integrating Long COVID mechanisms into ME/CFS must be caveated as acute-to-chronic extrapolation, not identity.

**11.2 What if the "shared post-infectious mechanism" framing is wrong and each trigger leaves a distinct signature?** *(origin: brainstorm)*
If post-infectious syndromes are each mechanistically distinct (SARS-CoV-2-specific, EBV-specific, etc.) with only superficial symptom overlap, the whole "post-infectious spectrum" model (already in ch14d) would overstate convergence.
- *Preliminary certainty:* n/a.
- *Consequence:* The paper's central post-infectious-spectrum claim would need weakening if head-to-head multi-trigger studies show distinct molecular signatures.

### Category 12 — Evidence quality concerns

**12.1 The review is narrative, not systematic** *(origin: brainstorm)*
Davis 2023 is a narrative review; many cited findings rest on preprints (Peluso EBV, Fernández-Castañeda myelin, Talla immune) and small cohorts. Certainty transfer to ME/CFS must discount preprint and single-cohort evidence.
- *Preliminary certainty:* n/a.
- *Consequence:* The review is a high-value synthesis but not itself a primary data source; its claims require primary anchors (added in Phase 1) before being treated as established ME/CFS-relevant facts.

**12.2 Cohort overlap / replication risk** *(origin: brainstorm)*
Several Long COVID findings (immune profiling, GI) derive from overlapping cohorts or the same research groups; treating them as independent replications inflates apparent certainty when extrapolating to ME/CFS.
- *Preliminary certainty:* n/a.
- *Consequence:* ME/CFS integration should treat Long COVID findings with the same cohort-overlap skepticism applied to ME/CFS primary research.
