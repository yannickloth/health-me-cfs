## Evidence base (verified bib keys, all in `src/main/typst/mecfs/bib/genetics-epigenetics.bib`)

**AS side (cross-disease analogy, no direct ME/CFS link):**
- Buiting2016AngelmanReview (cert 0.85, review): UBE3A loss (15q11-q13 imprinting) → proteostasis (E6AP E3 ubiquitin ligase), reduced tonic GABA-A inhibition, mitochondrial/energy deficits; severe developmental neurogenetic disorder.
- Su2011UBE3Amitochondria (cert 0.65, animal): Ube3a-deficient mouse CA1 hippocampus → reduced ATP, mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired oxidative phosphorylation.
- Panov2020Ube3aMito (cert 0.60, in silico): Ube3a-dependent transcriptomic alterations in mitochondrial / oxidative-phosphorylation pathways.
- Simchi2023ROS (cert 0.60, animal): early-development Ube3a-deficient neural precursors → elevated ROS, altered apoptosis.
- Qu2024SleepAS (cert 0.60, clinical): sleep disturbance in AS — reduced total sleep, fragmentation, abnormal architecture.
- Watanabe2026TonicInhibition (cert 0.60, animal): region-dependent reduction in tonic GABA-A inhibition underlies AS epileptic features.
- Kurmashev2026UBE3Adosage (cert 0.65, review): UBE3A dosage imbalance (loss/gain) → E6AP proteostasis framework; both directions pathogenic.

**ME/CFS side (the bridge):**
- WirthScheibenbogen2026glutamate (cert 0.55, review): excitatory/inhibitory imbalance in ME/CFS — glutamatergic excess / GABAergic deficit ("wired but tired").
- Maccallini2026metaGWAS (cert 0.45, preprint): ME/CFS GWAS → glutamatergic synapse enrichment, specific neuronal cell types.

**Zero direct AS–ME/CFS clinical or genetic link exists.** This is hypothesis-generating cross-disease analogy only.

## Category 10 — Reasons this mechanism may NOT be relevant to ME/CFS (critical)

| # | Idea | Rationale / evidence link | What would change the verdict |
|---|------|---------------------------|------------------------------|
| C10.1 | **AS is a congenital, developmental disorder; ME/CFS is typically adult-onset and acquired.** | The mitochondrial/GABA/ROS evidence (Su2011, Panov2020, Simchi2023, Watanabe2026) is all from developing/immature Ube3a-deficient nervous systems. A developmental mechanism cannot be assumed to transfer to an acquired adult disorder. This is the single largest reason the analogy may fail. | Demonstration of the same UBE3A/GABA/mitochondrial changes in an adult, inducible, acquired context (e.g., inducible adult Ube3a knockdown) — not just the developmental models. |
| C10.2 | **No direct UBE3A link to ME/CFS exists; the shared surface is broad "E/I balance."** | Maccallini2026metaGWAS shows glutamatergic-synapse enrichment but does NOT implicate UBE3A/E6AP or GABA-A specifically. Wirth2026glutamate's E/I imbalance could arise from immune, metabolic, or ion-channel mechanisms entirely independent of UBE3A/E6AP. The AS analogy is only ONE route to E/I imbalance. | Evidence that ME/CFS E/I imbalance specifically involves tonic GABA-A inhibition or UBE3A-pathway proteostasis — not just any glutamatergic/GABA change. |
| C10.3 | **Symptom-surface overlap (sleep, fatigue) is non-specific.** | Qu2024 sleep disturbance in AS may reflect severe epilepsy / neurodevelopmental disability and anticonvulsant medication, not a shared ME/CFS-relevant mechanism. ME/CFS patients are not epileptic nor developmentally disabled. | A shared mechanism (biomarker), not merely a shared symptom, between AS and ME/CFS sleep/energy findings. |
| C10.4 | **Monogenic high-penetrance phenotype generalizes poorly to a polygenic/acquired common disorder.** | Kurmashev2026 shows UBE3A dosage is tightly regulated — BOTH loss and gain cause severe disease — implying a narrow, dosage-sensitive axis unlike the common, graded ME/CFS genetics. A rare monogenic extreme does not establish a ME/CFS-relevant continuum. | Evidence of graded UBE3A/E6AP variation correlating with ME/CFS-relevant outcomes across the population, not just the monogenic extreme. |

## Category 11 — Null hypothesis assessment

| # | Idea | What would it mean if the mechanism has no role? | Existing ME/CFS claims requiring revision |
|---|------|------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| N11.1 | **Null: UBE3A / tonic-GABA / proteostasis plays NO role in ME/CFS.** | The E/I-imbalance ME/CFS claims (Wirth2026glutamate) and glutamatergic-synapse GWAS signal (Maccallini2026metaGWAS) stand independently — they are grounded in ME/CFS-specific evidence, NOT in the AS analogy. The AS analogy was never load-bearing; it was hypothesis-generating and is disposable. Cost to the ME/CFS story is minimal. | Only a FUTURE claim ("GABAergic disinhibition analogous to AS underlies ME/CFS E/I imbalance") would be removed. No current ME/CFS paper claim needs revision. |
| N11.2 | **Null: ME/CFS E/I imbalance is NOT specifically a reduced-tonic-GABA-A deficit.** | The E/I imbalance could still be real but phasic, activity-dependent, or non-GABAergic. The AS analogy's specific contribution (narrowing to *tonic* GABA-A, Watanabe2026) is lost. | Would invalidate any inference from AS that the ME/CFS imbalance is tonic-inhibition-specific. Wirth2026glutamate's broader E/I claim survives. |
| N11.3 | **Null: AS sleep fragmentation shares no mechanism with ME/CFS sleep pathology.** | Sleep fragmentation in both becomes a non-specific final common pathway (many etiologies, including meds/epilepsy in AS). No mechanistic leverage gained from AS for ME/CFS sleep. | No ME/CFS sleep claim requires revision; the AS sleep bridge (H3) is dropped. |

## Category 12 — Evidence quality concerns (not captured by certainty scores)

| # | Concern | Detail / evidence link |
|---|---------|------------------------|
| E12.1 | **Developmental/animal + in-silico extrapolation risk.** | Su2011, Simchi2023, Watanabe2026 are animal models; Panov2020 is in silico. Certainty scores (0.60–0.65) discount population weight but do NOT capture species, developmental-stage, or tissue-context (CA1 hippocampus, neural precursors) transfer risk to adult human ME/CFS. |
| E12.2 | **Confounder: medication/seizure burden in AS sleep data.** | Qu2024 AS patients have severe epilepsy and take anticonvulsants; sleep disturbance may be treatment- or seizure-related. This confounder is not in the certainty score and inflates the apparent AS–ME/CFS sleep overlap. |
| E12.3 | **Measurement + preprint bias.** | Elevated-ROS findings (Simchi2023) are artifact-prone; Maccallini2026metaGWAS is a non-peer-reviewed preprint (cert 0.45). Neither limitation is captured by a single certainty number. |
| E12.4 | **Fundamental external-validity gap.** | Rare monogenic disease evidence (Buiting2016 review weight 0.80, but disease is rare and not ME/CFS) to a common, polygenic, acquired, largely adult-onset ME/CFS population. The core analogy is an extrapolation across a very wide gap. |
