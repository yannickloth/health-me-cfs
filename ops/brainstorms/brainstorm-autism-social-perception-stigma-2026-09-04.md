## Category 1 — Novel hypotheses (relational / mechanism-extension)

### 1.1 Style-substance "decoupling" is NOT specific to neurotype — it scales with perceiver-expertise
**Idea:** The style-over-substance first-impression bias is not intrinsic to autism targets; it is a
general property of human first impressions of any *low-affect / slow-moving* target, and its
magnitude is set by perceiver expectation. Clinicians with high ME/CFS-specific training may show
attenuated bias because their priors shift weight to *reported substance*; untrained (GP, lay, some
specialists) retain the lay default toward style. Autism is only the most-studied case.
**Rationale:** Wanigasekera meta shows verbal transcript alone carries no bias → the bias lives in the
audio-visual style channel, which is presentational-general, not autism-specific. Slepian clinical
thin-slice review establishes the same style-channel logic in clinician judgment generally.
**Evidence link:** @Sasson2017ThinSlice, @Wanigasekera2025FirstImpressionsMeta, @Slepian2014ThinSliceClinical.
**Preliminary certainty:** 0.35 (transdiagnostic strand; ME/CFS application by analogy).
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a clinician thin-slice study, ME/CFS-training level will moderate the
style effect: trained clinicians' impressions will track transcript substance more than untrained
clinicians', who will track audio-visual style. `origin: brainstorm`

### 1.2 The "energy-cost" cue misread as "motivational" deficit hypothesis
**Idea:** The specific first-impression failure for ME/CFS is not generic coldness but a specific
attribution error: perceivers read ME/CFS patients' low-motor/affect presentation (which reflects
neurometabolic exhaustion) as low *motivation or malingering* — the style channel triggers an
effort/agency inference, not just a liking inference. This differs from autism (where style is read
as *social awkwardness*, per Grossman). If true, ME/CFS first-impression dismissal is a misattributed-
cause error (medically-explainable fatigue read as volitional), which is the specific vector into the
already-integrated psychologization/invalidation content.
**Rationale:** Sasson shows the anchor bias is independent of perceived intelligence/trustworthiness but
largest on "awkwardness" for autism. A parallel domain-specific attribution for fatigue-targets
(energy-impairment → laziness) is plausible from the Froehlich causal-stigma finding that psychosomatic
attributions are the strongest dismissal predictor. Distinct mechanism label, same style-channel.
**Evidence link:** @Sasson2017ThinSlice (awkwardness dimension), @Foster2025FacialAffectFirstImpressions,
@Hemmesch2014ParkinsonFirstImpressions (slowed movement read negatively); ME/CFS causal-stigma content
(@Froehlich2022CausalStigma, already integrated).
**Preliminary certainty:** 0.30 (ME/CFS-transfer → #speculation ceiling).
**Falsifiable prediction:** Thin-slice raters asked to free-label why they dislike a ME/CFS patient will
use motivation/effort words for audio-visual clips more than transcript, and this attribution, not
global likeability, will mediate reduced care-intent. `origin: brainstorm`

### 1.3 Rapport-co-production gives ME/CFS a target-side protective lever that needs no patient change
**Idea (relational-extension, autism-domain strong):** Because first impressions are co-produced by
perceiver expectation and interaction (Morrison dyad, Edey double mind-blindness), a perceiver who is
*told the interaction partner is physically unwell and will speak slowly* may rate the interaction more
favorably without the target changing anything. This is the relational model applied to invisible
illness — the modifiable locus is the perceiver's frame, exactly as the autism double-empathy program
argues. Distinct from H4: H4 asserts the relational frame generalizes; 1.3 specifies a *discrete
expectation-set prime* as the causal lever and predicts its direction.
**Rationale:** Efthimiou 2025 shows diagnostic-awareness moderates cross-neurotype rapport without the
target altering behavior. Morrison 2020 shows same-frame dyads improve rapport. This is autism-domain
evidence at its own certainty.
**Evidence link:** @Morrison2020AutisticPartnersSocialInteraction, @Edey2016InteractionTakesTwo,
@Efthimiou2025DiagnosticStatusRapport, @Morrison2019FirstImpressionsRaterVariability.
**Preliminary certainty:** 0.40 (autism-domain strand).
**Falsifiable prediction:** Perceivers given a one-line "partner is managing a chronic energy-limiting
illness; speech/pace may be slow" prime will rate a scripted ME/CFS-patient interview higher on
care-intent and lower on hostility than unprompted perceivers, holding the target video identical.
`origin: brainstorm`

### 1.4 First-impression bias is a gateway to *care-withdrawal*, distinct from diagnostic-delay (severity-specific)
**Idea:** The thin-slice mechanism may matter most at the *severe/bedbound* end, where presentational
style is most deviant (near-zero affect/movement, gaze, sparse prosody) and where reduced
willingness-to-interact maps onto reduced willingness to provide *care* (home visits, escalation,
palliative-like support) — a distinct downstream from the H2 diagnostic-delay claim. Bedbound patients
cannot "compensate" stylistically, so any style-driven bias is maximal exactly where objective
need is maximal (an inverse-burden trap).
**Rationale:** Sasson's reduced-willingness-to-interact component is the stigmatizing behavioral
correlate; the anchor's effect is stable and does not attenuate over exposure. Severity was never
stratified in any source study (all ambulatory) — this is a genuine unknown, flagged as such.
**Evidence link:** @Sasson2017ThinSlice (willingness component), @Wanigasekera2025FirstImpressionsMeta,
@Boucher2023FirstImpressionsChildren (age span); severe-ME/CFS care content (already integrated).
**Preliminary certainty:** 0.25 (ME/CFS-transfer → #speculation).
**Falsifiable prediction:** A care-intent vignette study will show care-withdrawal (not just negative
liking) correlates more strongly with audio-visual style deviance in a severe-presentation condition
than a moderate one. `origin: brainstorm`

## Category 2 — Research directions (resolve the analogy gap)

### 2.1 ME/CFS-cohort thin-slice first-impression experiment (the decisive gap-filler)
**Idea:** Run the Sasson paradigm on ME/CFS patients: brief video/audio/transcript clips of ME/CFS
patients vs healthy controls (and vs a fatigue-symptom but non-stigmatized comparator) rated by naive
perceivers and by clinicians; outcome = first-impression favorability + willingness to interact/care.
This directly tests whether the transdiagnostic style mechanism transfers to ME/CFS and whether
clinician priors attenuate it. This is the single highest-value unresolved direction (fills the zero-
cohort gap that caps every ME/CFS claim at #speculation).
**Rationale:** The whole ME/CFS-transfer cap rests on "no ME/CFS-cohort study exists." A protocol that
mirrors Sasson (three-modality thin-slice) closes that gap decisively and cheaply (paradigm validated).
**Evidence link:** @Sasson2017ThinSlice (design), @Slepian2014ThinSliceClinical (clinical adaptation),
@Grossman2015SocialAwkwardness (precursor paradigm).
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a (research direction, not a claim). Priority: HIGH.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A single well-run study would show whether doctors' snap judgments of
ME/CFS patients track how the patient looks rather than what the patient says. `origin: brainstorm`

### 2.2 Clinician-bias training trial (perceiver-side de-stigmatization)
**Idea:** RCT of a brief perceiver-side intervention for clinicians: (a) education on the style-not-
substance bias, (b) structured-symptom-checklist consultation format that forces weight onto reported
substance, vs (c) usual care. Outcome = change in first-impression favorability and care-intent toward
scripted ME/CFS-patient consultations. Tests whether bias reducible-by-information (Norris) transfers
to the ME/CFS label in a clinical context.
**Rationale:** Norris 2024 shows disclosure/information reduces employment-interview bias; Parisi 2026
warns the ME/CFS label may trigger a *negative* pre-formed stereotype (psychosomatic), so the training
must pre-empt the label, not invoke it. Designed to test H4's intervention arm at perceiver level.
**Evidence link:** @Norris2024DisclosureInterview, @Parisi2026SchizophreniaFirstImpressions,
@Slepian2014ThinSliceClinical.
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a (intervention trial). Priority: HIGH.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A short clinician-training module could reduce the common experience of
ME/CFS patients being disbelieved at first consult. `origin: brainstorm`

### 2.3 Attribute-mediation experiment (tests 1.2: motivation misread)
**Idea:** Thin-slice rating task where perceivers free-attribute reasons and rate agency/effort vs
separately rating likeability; test whether a *motivation* attribution (not global coldness) is the
specific mediator of reduced care-intent for fatigue-presentation targets. Disambiguates 1.1/1.2.
**Rationale:** The anchor isolates mechanism to style channel and shows independence from intelligence;
whether the consequential attribution for an energy-illness is motivational is untested and would
link thin-slice work to causal-stigma theory.
**Evidence link:** @Foster2025FacialAffectFirstImpressions, @Hemmesch2014ParkinsonFirstImpressions,
@Sasson2017ThinSlice; ME/CFS causal-stigma content (@Froehlich2022CausalStigma, integrated).
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a. Priority: MEDIUM. `origin: brainstorm`

### 2.4 Same-paradigm cross-condition comparator (ME/CFS vs depression vs fatigue-in-healthy)
**Idea:** Run the thin-slice task with targets matched for low affect across ME/CFS, depression, and
high-fatigue-but-well people, holding perceived-energy constant via labels. Isolates whether the bias
follows the *presentation* (all low-affect) or the *label* (ME/CFS vs depression stigma). Parisi's
condition-specific disclosure result implies the label matters independently of presentation.
**Rationale:** Directly tests the autism-vs-psychosomatic label asymmetry that the disclosure open-
question (@oq:disclosure-attenuation, already integrated) raises for ME/CFS.
**Evidence link:** @Parisi2026SchizophreniaFirstImpressions (label-specificity), @Norris2024DisclosureInterview,
@DeBrabander2019FirstImpressionsAutisticObservers (label/in-group).
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a. Priority: MEDIUM. `origin: brainstorm`

### 2.5 Longitudinal stability of clinician impressions (does exposure correct the bias?)
**Idea:** Because the anchor shows the bias is stable over repeated exposure in lay peers, test whether
the same holds for *clinicians* over a multi-visit care trajectory — i.e., does a dismissive first
impression persist once ME/CFS is established, or does clinical knowledge/serial exposure correct it?
If stable in clinicians too, that is a care-quality mechanism; if it corrects, the first-visit window
is the intervention target.
**Rationale:** Sasson anchor reports stability over exposure; whether a *professional* perceiver's
impression updates with diagnosis/serial contact is untested and is the practical question for the
H2 dismissal claim.
**Evidence link:** @Sasson2017ThinSlice (stability), @Efthimiou2025DiagnosticStatusRapport,
@DeBrabander2019FirstImpressionsAutisticObservers.
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a. Priority: MEDIUM. `origin: brainstorm`

## Category 3 — Perceiver-side interventions (NOT patient behavior change)

### 3.1 Structured-substance consultation format (clinician tool, not patient change)
**Idea:** A consultation format that forces the clinician to register *reported substance* (PEM
thresholds, orthostatic triggers, crash data) in structured fields BEFORE any gestalt judgment, shifting
weight off the style channel. Implemented as an intake template, not a demand on the patient.
**Rationale:** Slepian thin-slice clinical work + Wanigasekera transcript-null both imply that when the
perceiver is forced onto verbal substance, the bias collapses. An intake that surfaces substance first
is a perceiver-side structural fix.
**Evidence link:** @Slepian2014ThinSliceClinical, @Wanigasekera2025FirstImpressionsMeta,
@Parisi2026SchizophreniaFirstImpressions.
**Preliminary certainty:** 0.30 (intervention, indirect evidence → Tier 2/3). Priority: HIGH.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Standardized intake questions would give ME/CFS symptoms equal weight to
the doctor's first impression. `origin: brainstorm`

### 3.2 Peer/advocate-framed disclosure wording guidance (perceiver-frame prime)
**Idea:** Because the ME/CFS label may trigger a negative stereotype (psychosomatic) rather than
attenuate (the @oq:disclosure-attenuation risk), develop and test *frame-first wording* that establishes
an energy-limiting-illness context before the contested label appears — operationalizing 1.3 as a
practical communication support offered to patients/families, without requiring style change.
**Rationale:** Norris shows information reduces bias; Parisi shows label content determines direction.
Wording is the modifiable disclosure variable and is perceiver-side in effect.
**Evidence link:** @Norris2024DisclosureInterview, @Parisi2026SchizophreniaFirstImpressions,
@Efthimiou2025DiagnosticStatusRapport.
**Preliminary certainty:** 0.30 (intervention, indirect → Tier 2/3). Priority: MEDIUM.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Better opening wording could stop an ME/CFS diagnosis from being
pre-judged as "it's all in your head." `origin: brainstorm`

## Category 5 — Diagnostic / measurement tools

### 5.1 A "thin-slice impression bias" audit instrument for clinician-patient interaction
**Idea:** A standardized, low-cost instrument that flags a consultation as at-risk for style-driven
dismissal: capture a short clip + transcript, compare a blinded transcript-only rating to the live
rating; a gap flags bias. Useable as a training/audit metric, not a diagnostic test. Mirrors the
two-modality contrast that is the field's established probe.
**Rationale:** The whole field's operative signal is the audio-visual-vs-transcript gap (Sasson,
Wanigasekera). Codifying it as a reproducible audit metric gives healthcare services a measurable
bias indicator.
**Evidence link:** @Sasson2017ThinSlice, @Wanigasekera2025FirstImpressionsMeta,
@Slepian2014ThinSliceClinical.
**Preliminary certainty:** 0.35 (tool concept; autism-domain mechanism). Priority: MEDIUM.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Hospitals could measure whether their clinicians judge patients by
presentation rather than symptoms. `origin: brainstorm`

### 5.2 Presentational-style deviance as a measured covariate (not a target of "fixing")
**Idea:** In future ME/CFS outcome/consultation studies, quantify presentational-style deviance (affect,
movement, prosody via available coding) as a *covariate* predicting clinician dismissiveness — giving
the field a variable to control for and to study, never a target the patient is asked to change.
**Rationale:** Foster 2025 isolates facial affect; Hemmesch 2014 quantifies reduced movement's effect.
Adding the measure to ME/CFS studies would convert an uncontrolled confound into an analyzed variable.
**Evidence link:** @Foster2025FacialAffectFirstImpressions, @Hemmesch2014ParkinsonFirstImpressions,
@Morrison2019FirstImpressionsRaterVariability.
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a (measurement recommendation). Priority: MEDIUM. `origin: brainstorm`

## Category 6 — Mathematical / modeling of stigma

### 6.1 A two-channel impression model (substance vs style weights)
**Idea:** Formalize the style-not-substance result as a simple additive/logistic model:
`impression = α·substance + β·style_deviance + ε`, where the transcript-null finding implies β≈0 in the
absence of audio-visual, and β dominates when present. Model predictions: (a) bias magnitude grows with
style deviance independent of substance; (b) perceiver priors (clinician training) modulate β by
reweighting α. This gives the qualitative finding a tractable quantitative scaffold consistent with the
paper's existing relational framing.
**Rationale:** The anchor's three-modality design and the meta's modality-specificity map cleanly onto a
two-channel weight model; it is the natural formalization of "style not substance."
**Evidence link:** @Sasson2017ThinSlice, @Wanigasekera2025FirstImpressionsMeta,
@Morrison2019FirstImpressionsRaterVariability.
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a (formalization). Priority: LOW-MEDIUM. `origin: brainstorm`

### 6.2 Interaction-willingness as a decision threshold over impression
**Idea:** Model reduced willingness-to-interact as a thresholded function of negative impression
(mirroring the in-group dissociation: autistic perceivers show the trait-judgment but not the
willingness drop — i.e., the two components decouple). Predicts that the behavioral (willingness/care)
component, not the affective impression, is the one that differs across perceiver groups and is the
true stigmatizing output to target.
**Rationale:** DeBrabander's in-group dissociation cleanly separates the judgment from the behavioral
component; a threshold model makes that separation explicit and identifies the care/behavior output as
the intervention target.
**Evidence link:** @DeBrabander2019FirstImpressionsAutisticObservers, @Morrison2020AutisticPartnersSocialInteraction.
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a (formalization). Priority: LOW. `origin: brainstorm`

### 7.1 Long-COVID presentational-style parallel
**Idea:** Long-COVID (PEM-congruent) patients share ME/CFS's low-affect/slowed-presentation phenotype;
the style-not-substance mechanism likely transfers there too, but Long-COVID has a *less contested*

**Rationale:** Parisi shows label, not presentation alone, shapes the direction. Long-COVID vs ME/CFS is

## Category 9 — (Adapted) Relational-frame / social-model implications
### 9.1 Re-anchoring clinician failure as perceiver failure, not patient non-compliance
**Idea (social-model normative):** The relational framing (impression co-produced) implies that when a
clinician dismisses an ME/CFS patient, the correct locus of remediation is the clinician's judgment
process (structure substance intake, train on the style bias), not a demand that the severe/bedbound
patient "look more credible" by exerting affect they cannot afford. This normative corollary of H4 is
distinct in that it names the *care-withdrawal* output (1.4) as the failure to prevent, aligning
first-impression science with patient-experience advocacy.
**Rationale:** Morrison dyad/Edey co-production + the anchor's willingness-drop; directly relevant to
ch09 patient-side and ch40 clinician-side already-integrated content.
**Evidence link:** @Jones2024DoubleEmpathyEvaluation, @Edey2016InteractionTakesTwo,
@Morrison2020AutisticPartnersSocialInteraction.
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a (normative framing; autism-domain strong, ME/CFS corollary by analogy).
Priority: MEDIUM. `origin: brainstorm`

## Category 10 — Reasons this mechanism may NOT apply to ME/CFS (MANDATORY)

### 10.1 Clinicians are not lay perceivers — profession may immunize against the bias
**Reason:** All 15 source studies used lay/general or student perceivers. Clinicians may (a) use
structured symptom checklists that force substance registration, (b) have prior ME/CFS knowledge that
shifts priors to the report, or (c) in long-running consult relationships build impressions over serial
contact rather than seconds of thin slice. If so, the lay thin-slice effect may not generalize to the
clinical encounter that H2 claims it drives.
**Evidence link:** @Sasson2017ThinSlice (lay perceivers), @Slepian2014ThinSliceClinical (thin-slice in
clinical contexts is a distinct, weaker literature), @Wanigasekera2025FirstImpressionsMeta.
**Preliminary certainty (this IS a counterclaim):** 0.40.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A doctor who already knows ME/CFS may not form the same snap judgment as
a stranger meeting the patient cold. `origin: brainstorm`

### 10.2 ME/CFS "style" may be less deviant than autism/Parkinson presentational deviance
**Reason:** The anchor's effect is largest when style deviance is extreme (autism d≈2.96 on
awkwardness). If ambulatory/mild-moderate ME/CFS patients present with only subtle affect reduction,
the thin-slice bias may be too small to matter clinically; only the severe/bedbound subset would show it,
so the population-level dismissal mechanism could be weak. No ME/CFS study stratifies this.
**Evidence link:** @Sasson2017ThinSlice (effect scales with deviance), @Hemmesch2014ParkinsonFirstImpressions
(measurable motor deviance), @Boucher2023FirstImpressionsChildren.
**Preliminary certainty:** 0.35.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Mildly affected ME/CFS patients may not look visibly different enough
for first impressions to matter much. `origin: brainstorm`

### 10.3 The contested-label effect may dominate style (label, not presentation)
**Reason:** Parisi shows disclosure direction is condition-specific — for a heavily psychosomatized
label, the pre-formed *stereotype* (contested, "psychogenic") may swamp any presentational-style
mechanism, i.e., dismissal could arise from explicit causal beliefs, not from an implicit thin-slice
style heuristic. That would mean the fix is belief-change/stigma-education, not the perceptual
interventions (structured intake, prime wording) that target the style channel.
**Evidence link:** @Parisi2026SchizophreniaFirstImpressions, @Norris2024DisclosureInterview,
@DeBrabander2019FirstImpressionsAutisticObservers; ME/CFS psychosomatic-framing stigma (integrated).
**Preliminary certainty:** 0.40.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Some doctors may dismiss ME/CFS because of what they believe the
diagnosis means, not because of how the patient looks. `origin: brainstorm`

### 10.4 Evidence would be disproven by a null ME/CFS thin-slice result
**Reason:** The single most decisive disproof: a faithful ME/CFS-cohort Sasson-paradigm replication that
returns no audio-visual-vs-transcript gap (or no impression deficit vs healthy controls). Given every
ME/CFS claim here is analogy, one well-powered null study would cap the entire mechanism at #limitation
and remove the justification for clinician-bias training aimed at style. This is a positive scientific
feature — the claim is genuinely falsifiable.
**Evidence link:** the design gap itself (no ME/CFS study exists); paradigm from @Sasson2017ThinSlice.
**Preliminary certainty:** n/a (explicit falsification route). Priority: HIGHEST.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If a real study found no bias, the "patients judged by looks" story for
ME/CFS would be wrong and would be dropped. `origin: brainstorm`

## Category 11 — Null hypothesis assessment (what if ME/CFS patients do NOT face thin-slice bias)

### 11.1 Structured consultation + clinician knowledge may already neutralize the effect
**Null:** ME/CFS patients do not face meaningful thin-slice first-impression bias because the real-world
diagnostic context is not a cold thin-slice: clinicians use symptom criteria, take histories over time,
and (in ME/CFS-aware services) hold a positive prior. The lay/naive paradigm that shows the autism effect
may not map onto the graded, substance-driven clinical assessment.
**Rationale:** The transcript-null (Wanigasekera) itself shows that when substance is the only input, no
bias appears — real consultations are substance-rich. Whether the *unseen* style channel still leaks in is
exactly what is untested.
**Evidence link:** @Wanigasekera2025FirstImpressionsMeta, @Slepian2014ThinSliceClinical,
@Morrison2019FirstImpressionsRaterVariability.
**Preliminary certainty (null framing):** 0.30.
**Non-specialist consequence:** In practices that already take ME/CFS seriously and use structured
assessment, first impressions may not change outcomes. `origin: brainstorm`

### 11.2 Peer/lay bias may exist but not translate to reduced care (behavioral dissociation)
**Null:** Even if lay first impressions of ME/CFS patients are less favorable (mirroring autism), the
*willingness-to-interact/care* component — the stigmatizing output — may not follow, because (unlike
autism, where DeBrabander shows the dissociation is by perceiver group) ME/CFS peers/family already
carry a supportive prior and clinicians a duty-of-care override. Impression deficit ≠ behavioral
deficit; the anchor's own two-component structure leaves this open.
**Evidence link:** @DeBrabander2019FirstImpressionsAutisticObservers (judgment/willingness dissociation),
@Morrison2020AutisticPartnersSocialInteraction.
**Preliminary certainty (null framing):** 0.30.
**Non-specialist consequence:** People may think less warmly of an ME/CFS patient at first glance yet
still offer help — the two may not be linked. `origin: brainstorm`

Norris, Edey, Hemmesch, Slepian/Ambady, Parisi). The core effect does have ≥2 independent confirmations
(so PROCEED stands), but the *mechanism detail* (rater-locus, relational co-production) rests heavily on
one lab's framing.
**Evidence link:** all 15 keys; overlap analysis in the integration guide.
**Preliminary certainty (of the concern being real):** 0.80.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Much of the underlying research comes from one research group, so its
conclusions need independent replication before being treated as settled. `origin: brainstorm`

### 12.2 Translation gap: zero ME/CFS-cohort study; all perceivers are of *another* condition
**Concern:** Every paper studies general-population perceivers rating autism/Parkinson/schizophrenia.
The ME/CFS application is mechanism-transfer by analogy with a transdiagnostic precedent — legitimate
as hypothesis, but none of the 15 papers measured an ME/CFS patient, an ME/CFS-context perceiver, or a
care/clinical setting. Population weight 0.75 throughout; no ME/CFS-specific environment may exceed
#speculation.
**Evidence link:** population column in literature summary; cap in Phase 2 decision.
**Preliminary certainty (of the gap):** 0.90.
**Non-specialist consequence:** No study has actually tested whether ME/CFS patients specifically are
judged this way — it is currently a reasonable guess, not a proven fact. `origin: brainstorm`

### 12.3 Disclosure/nuance findings are condition-specific and may not transfer
**Concern:** The reducibility-by-information finding (Norris) and the direction-of-label finding (Parisi)
are demonstrated for autism/schizophrenia labels. Neither tests an ME/CFS (or any psychosomatized chronic
illness) label, which plausibly behaves like the schizophrenia case (label worsens) not the autism case
(label improves). Any disclosure-based intervention inherits this condition-specificity risk and cannot be
generalized from the 15 papers.
**Evidence link:** @Norris2024DisclosureInterview, @Parisi2026SchizophreniaFirstImpressions,
@DeBrabander2019FirstImpressionsAutisticObservers.
**Preliminary certainty (of the concern):** 0.60.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Advising ME/CFS patients to "just disclose the diagnosis" may backfire,
since disclosure helps for some conditions and hurts for others. `origin: brainstorm`

### 12.4 Age/severity/ambulatory sampling limits generality
**Concern:** Source studies used ambulatory, largely adult, non-severity-stratified samples (children in
Boucher). None captured severe/bedbound targets, where ME/CFS style deviance (and 1.4) would be largest,
nor older perceivers (Hemmesch is the exception, older raters of Parkinson). Severity and setting
generality to ME/CFS's most affected subgroup is unsupported.
**Evidence link:** @Boucher2023FirstImpressionsChildren, @Hemmesch2014ParkinsonFirstImpressions,
@Sasson2017ThinSlice.
**Preliminary certainty (of the concern):** 0.55.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Even the evidence that exists does not cover the most severely affected
ME/CFS patients, so it cannot tell us how they are judged. `origin: brainstorm`
