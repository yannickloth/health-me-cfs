# Brainstorm: Prolactin Response to Buspirone Challenge in ME/CFS

**Topic:** ME/CFS patients mount a much higher prolactin response to buspirone (5-HT1A agonist + D2 antagonist) than healthy or depression controls, despite normal baseline prolactin. Replicated 1992–2001, abandoned, rediscovered by S4ME; modern replication proposed. Two candidate mechanisms: (a) altered dopamine system (dopamine is the main brake on prolactin), (b) estradiol regulation of prolactin (candidate for ~80% female predominance).

**Date:** 2026-08-29

**Phase 2 decision:** PROCEED — all categories 1–12 allowed (no PARTIAL cap).

**Evidence anchors (discounted certainty):**
- Sharpe1996Buspirone 0.62 (11 male CFS, Fukuda; GH null → not pure postsynaptic 5-HT1A hypersensitivity)
- Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (CFS prolactin HIGHEST, depression LOWEST → discriminator)
- Bakheit1992Buspirone 0.55 (original PVFS report; D2/5-HT ambiguous)
- Yatham1995Fenfluramine 0.58 (NULL — pure 5-HT releaser; localizes abnormality away from global 5-HT)
- Bearn1995Fenfluramine 0.60 (no fenfluramine PRL enhancement)
- Dinan2001Buspirone 0.49 (general pop; prolactin AUC correlates with baseline cortisol)
- BenJonathan2001ProlactinDopamine 0.64 (canonical dopamine-prolactin brake review)
- Fitzgerald2008ProlactinDopamine 0.60 (dopamine D2 tonic brake)
- QiLytle2023TIDA 0.60 (TIDA/estradiol-modulated dopamine review)
- Haghighi2021OSU6162 0.45 (dopamine-stabilizer trial in ME/CFS)
- Newman1998Fenfluramine 0.51 (challenge-test methodology review)

## Category 1: Novel Hypotheses

### Idea 1.1 — Reduced Hypothalamic Dopaminergic Tone (D2 Brake Hypoactivity) Is the Primary Defect; 5-HT1A Is Secondary
- **Certainty:** 0.55
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Prolactin is tonically suppressed by hypothalamic (TIDA) dopamine acting on lactotroph D2 receptors — the "brake." Buspirone raises prolactin by BOTH 5-HT1A agonism (small) and D2 antagonism (large, by lifting the brake). If the D2 brake is chronically weak (reduced dopaminergic tone), the same D2-blocking dose disinhibits a larger prolactin pool, producing the exaggerated rise. The null fenfluramine result (Yatham 1995, Bearn 1995) — a pure 5-HT releaser with no D2 effect — does NOT reproduce the signal, which is the key probe-specificity argument: the abnormality sits in the dopamine/D2 pathway, not in global serotonin. Sharpe 1996's null GH (a purer 5-HT1A postsynaptic readout) further rules out primary 5-HT1A hypersensitivity. This unifies the small literature into one coherent mechanism: hypoactive dopamine brake, not hypersensitive serotonin receptor.
- **Evidence link:** BenJonathan2001ProlactinDopamine 0.64, Fitzgerald2008ProlactinDopamine 0.60 (canonical D2 brake); Sharpe1996Buspirone 0.62 (GH null); Yatham1995Fenfluramine 0.58 (null 5-HT releaser); Bearn1995Fenfluramine 0.60 (no PRL enhancement); Haghighi2021OSU6162 0.45 (dopamine dysregulation in ME/CFS).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In a modern replication with a D2-pure antagonist comparator (e.g., domperidone, which does not cross the blood-brain barrier), ME/CFS patients will show a larger prolactin AUC than controls to the D2 antagonist alone, and the buspirone response will correlate (r > 0.6) with the D2-antagonist response but NOT with a pure 5-HT1A probe (ipsapirone) response.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The exaggerated hormone response is most plausibly a sign that the brain's dopamine "brake" on a stress/energy hormone is running weak, rather than the serotonin system being overactive — redirecting which treatments would make sense.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 1.2 — Prolactin as a Peripheral Window on Hypothalamic Dopamine: Challenge-Test Imaging of a Deep-Brain Signal
- **Certainty:** 0.50
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Hypothalamic dopamine tone is invisible to peripheral measurement; prolactin is the only easily-assayed readout of TIDA/D2 tone. A standardized buspirone challenge converts a deep-brain dopaminergic state into a peripheral blood value — effectively a peripheral "biomarker of central dopamine brake strength." If Idea 1.1 holds, the challenge test is not just a prolactin measurement; it is a non-invasive surrogate for the dopaminergic deficit that Haghighi's OSU6162 trial targeted. This reframes prolactin from an incidental endocrine finding to a pharmacodynamic probe of a central mechanism, and gives the replication study a mechanistic (not just correlational) rationale.
- **Evidence link:** QiLytle2023TIDA 0.60 (TIDA regulates prolactin, modulated by estradiol); Fitzgerald2008ProlactinDopamine 0.60; Haghighi2021OSU6162 0.45 (dopaminergic hypothesis in ME/CFS).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** The buspirone-prolactin AUC in ME/CFS will correlate with an independent dopaminergic measure — striatal dopamine terminal integrity by DAT-SPECT or dopamine-reuptake modulation response — such that patients with the highest prolactin AUC show the lowest DAT signal (r < −0.5), supporting shared dopaminergic pathology across brain regions.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A simple blood hormone test could stand in for expensive brain scans, giving a cheap, repeatable readout of whether a patient's brain-dopamine "brake" is weak.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 1.3 — Medium-Spiny-Neuron Dopaminergic Link: Prolactin Axis as a Cortico-Striatal Dopamine Surrogate
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Mechanistic rationale:** ME/CFS shows dopaminergic reward-processing deficits in the basal ganglia (context: nigrostriatal/VMAT2 line). Medium spiny neurons (MSNs) — the principal striatal neurons — are dopamine-modulated, and striatal dopamine tone is coupled to the same dopaminergic systems that regulate tuberoinfundibular dopamine. If ME/CFS has a generalized dopaminergic deficit (not just in the hypothalamus), the exaggerated buspirone-prolactin response may be a peripheral indicator of a brain-wide dopaminergic dysfunction that includes MSN dysregulation. The buspirone challenge could then index a striatal/cortico-striatal dopaminergic state that no peripheral test currently captures. This bridges the endocrine finding to the motor/cognitive effort deficits of ME/CFS.
- **Evidence link:** Haghighi2021OSU6162 0.45 (dopamine stabilizer targets MSN-related dopamine circuitry); dopamine-prolactin brake reviews (BenJonathan2001, Fitzgerald2008); cross-link to VMAT2-striatum-LongCOVID topic.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In ME/CFS, buspirone-prolactin AUC will correlate with basal-ganglia dopamine-dependent task performance (reward/motivation task) and with subjective effort intolerance, even after controlling for baseline prolactin — consistent with a shared dopaminergic deficit across hypothalamus and striatum.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The hormone response may reflect a broader brain-dopamine shortfall that also drives the deep fatigue and effort-intolerance of ME/CFS, not just the pituitary.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 1.4 — Buspirone-Prolactin as a Subtype Marker: Dopaminergic ME/CFS Subtype Versus Serotonergic-Dominant
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The ME/CFS population is heterogeneous. If the exaggerated buspirone-prolactin response localizes to dopamine (Idea 1.1), then patients with a large response define a "dopaminergic-hypoactive" subtype, distinct from patients whose neuroendocrine profile is serotonergic or HPA-driven. This subtype would be predicted to respond preferentially to dopamine-modulating interventions (e.g., OSU6162-type stabilizers, low-dose D2-partial agonism) and to have distinct symptom weighting (reward/motivation/executive deficits vs. HPA/autonomic). The challenge test becomes a stratification tool for future trials, not just a diagnostic discriminator.
- **Evidence link:** Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (prolactin separates CFS from depression); heterogeneity of the disorder; Haghighi2021OSU6162 0.45 (dopamine-targeted intervention); null-subtyping line.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In a dopamine-targeted trial, baseline buspirone-prolactin AUC will predict treatment response (effect size d ≥ 0.5 separating responder tertiles), whereas a serotonergic probe will not — defining an intervention-stratifying subtype.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A hormone challenge could help sort ME/CFS patients into biologically meaningful groups, so a dopamine-based treatment is tested only on those whose test suggests it will help.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 1.5 — State-Dependence and Recovery: Prolactin Response as an Objective, Modifiable Neuroendocrine Signal
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Sharma2001 reported that buspirone/prolactin neuroendocrine change tracks clinical recovery — the signal is state-dependent, not a fixed trait. This suggests the exaggerated response is a dynamic readout of current dopaminergic/TIDA tone that could normalize as patients improve. If so, the challenge test has value as a longitudinal, objective outcome measure — a much-needed supplement to subjective fatigue scales — and its trajectory could track response to interventions, exercise-avoidance recovery, or relapses. It also means the signal is unlikely to be an immutable genetic trait, favoring an acquired (e.g., post-infectious, metabolic) driver.
- **Evidence link:** Sharma2001Buspirone 0.40 (recovery-associated change); state-dependence of neuroendocrine markers; Haghighi2021OSU6162 0.45 (modifiable dopaminergic target).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Buspirone-prolactin AUC will be higher during ME/CFS relapse and will decrease toward control values in patients who recover (by ≥2-point clinical scale change), tracking improvement better than baseline prolactin or cortisol alone.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The hormone test could act like an objective "lab thermometer" for recovery, rising with relapse and falling with improvement — useful in treatment trials that today rely on self-report.
- **origin:** brainstorm

## Category 2: Research Directions

### Idea 2.1 — Sex-Stratified Replication With Estradiol Measurement (the Definitive Disambiguator of Mechanism a vs b)
- **Certainty:** 0.55
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The single most decisive open question is whether the exaggerated prolactin response reflects dopamine (mechanism a) or estradiol (mechanism b). These are confounded because estradiol modulates BOTH the dopamine brake and lactotroph prolactin synthesis, and ME/CFS is ~80% female. A replication must stratify by sex, measure serum estradiol and phase-of-cycle (or menopausal status), and include pre-/peri-/post-menopausal women plus matched men. If the exaggerated response persists in men (low estradiol), mechanism b is weakened and mechanism a dominates. If it tracks estradiol within and across menstrual phases, mechanism b is supported. This directly resolves the competing-mechanism open-question (#4) and is a prerequisite for all downstream interpretations. The proposed S4ME replication study should make this its primary design axis.
- **Evidence link:** BenJonathan2001ProlactinDopamine 0.64, QiLytle2023TIDA 0.60 (estradiol-dopamine-prolactin coupling); Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (matched menstrual cycle in the discriminating study); spec:fra-estradiol-prolactin-link (cert 0.30, ch21).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** If mechanism a dominates: exaggerated prolactin AUC appears in both sexes and does not covary with estradiol. If mechanism b dominates: the exaggerated AUC is female-specific and correlates with estradiol (r > 0.5) and menstrual phase.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Testing the hormone response in men and across the menstrual cycle would definitively show whether the effect is about brain-dopamine or about female sex hormones — and therefore which explanation for the ~80% female predominance is right.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 2.2 — Three-Arm Challenge Panel: Buspirone + Pure D2 Antagonist + Pure 5-HT1A Agonist in ME/CFS vs Depression vs Healthy
- **Certainty:** 0.50
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The existing literature is confounded because buspirone is a multi-receptor drug (5-HT1A + D2) and no study compared it against a pure D2 antagonist (isolating the dopamine component) or a pure 5-HT1A agonist (isolating the serotonin component) within the SAME patients. A within-subject panel — buspirone, domperidone (peripheral D2 antagonist; does not cross BBB, so isolates lactotroph D2), and ipsapirone (pure 5-HT1A agonist) — in ME/CFS vs depression vs healthy would decompose the response into dopamine-specific vs serotonin-specific components. This is the experiment that finally adjudicates mechanism a vs a serotonergic contribution, and it would validate buspirone as a clean-enough probe or reveal it as confounded. It also re-establishes the depression-vs-ME/CFS discriminator with modern criteria.
- **Evidence link:** Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (depression contrast, but d-fenfluramine not buspirone); Yatham1995Fenfluramine 0.58 + Bearn1995Fenfluramine 0.60 (null 5-HT releaser); Newman1998Fenfluramine 0.51 (probe methodology).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** If mechanism a: ME/CFS patients show exaggerated prolactin to the D2 antagonist (domperidone) specifically, with a normal response to ipsapirone. If serotonin-driven: the ipsapirone response is also exaggerated.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Giving patients three carefully chosen challenge drugs and comparing their hormone responses would cleanly separate "too much serotonin" from "too little dopamine" as the cause.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 2.3 — Cortisol-Interaction Control: Measure the Dinan Cortisol-Prolactin Coupling Within the ME/CFS Cohort
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Dinan2001 showed that buspirone-prolactin AUC correlates highly with baseline cortisol in healthy volunteers. ME/CFS is characterized by HPA-axis abnormalities (low baseline cortisol in Cleare1995's CFS group). If the exaggerated prolactin response is partly cortisol-mediated, a replication that does not measure cortisol could misattribute an HPA-driven effect to the dopamine system. The replication must measure baseline cortisol and model prolactin AUC as a function of cortisol, dopamine tone, and estradiol simultaneously. This controls a major potential confound and could reveal that the "dopamine" signal is partly an HPA-cortisol signal.
- **Evidence link:** Dinan2001Buspirone 0.49 (prolactin AUC ~ baseline cortisol); Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (lowest baseline cortisol in CFS).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** After controlling for baseline cortisol, the ME/CFS-vs-control difference in buspirone-prolactin AUC will either persist (dopamine-driven) or shrink to non-significance (cortisol-driven) — adjudicating the HPA confound.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The hormone test needs to control for the stress-hormone system too, because stress-hormone levels can independently inflate the same prolactin reading — without that, the dopamine interpretation could be wrong.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 2.4 — Severity Stratification: Does the Exaggerated Response Scale With Severity and Orthostatic/Effort Intolerance?
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Mechanistic rationale:** None of the 1990s studies stratified by severity. Prolactin is a stress-responsive hormone, and ME/CFS severity tracks autonomic/neuroendocrine dysregulation. If the exaggerated response scales with severity (mild → severe/very-severe), it supports a state/severity biomarker and clarifies applicability to the most disabled patients. A severity-stratified replication (mild/moderate/severe/very-severe per ch14a definitions) would determine whether the probe is informative in bedbound patients — for whom a blood test is feasible even when exercise testing is not.
- **Evidence link:** Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (matched groups but not severity-stratified); Sharma2001Buspirone 0.40 (state-dependence); severity-applicability gaps noted in Phase 3.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Buspirone-prolactin AUC will increase monotonically with severity grade and will correlate with objective orthostatic/effort-intolerance measures (r > 0.4), providing a severity and disability biomarker.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If the hormone rise scales with how severe the illness is, a single blood test could grade severity and disability — useful for the most bedbound patients who cannot do exercise testing.
- **origin:** brainstorm

## Category 3: Drug/Medication Ideas

### Idea 3.1 — Domperidone/Metoclopramide as Pure D2-Antagonist Comparator Probes (Diagnostic, Not Therapeutic)
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** To isolate the dopamine component of the buspirone signal, a pure peripheral D2 antagonist is the ideal comparator. Domperidone does not cross the blood-brain barrier and acts on lactotroph D2 to raise prolactin; metoclopramide is a central+peripheral D2 antagonist. Comparing the prolactin AUC to domperidone (isolates lactotroph/dopamine-brake) vs buspirone (adds 5-HT1A) in the same ME/CFS patient decomposes the response. This is a diagnostic/probe use, not a treatment — it tests whether the exaggerated response survives removal of the 5-HT1A component. It is cheap, generic, and has an established safety profile for single-dose challenge use (nausea, transient).
- **Evidence link:** Idea 1.1 (dopamine-brake hypothesis); BenJonathan2001ProlactinDopamine 0.64 (D2 = prolactin brake); Sharpe1996Buspirone 0.62 (GH null → serotonin not primary).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** ME/CFS patients will show a larger prolactin AUC to domperidone alone than controls (d ≥ 0.5), confirming the dopamine-brake mechanism independent of 5-HT1A; if not, the exaggerated buspirone response requires the serotonin component.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A second, purer challenge drug that only touches the dopamine brake would confirm whether the abnormal test is really about dopamine — without any serotonin involvement.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 3.2 — Haloperidol as a Central D2-Antagonist Probe to Localize Central vs Peripheral Dopamine Defect
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Haloperidol is a potent central D2 antagonist that potently raises prolactin by blocking pituitary AND tuberoinfundibular D2. Contrasting domperidone (peripheral, does not cross BBB) with haloperidol (central) distinguishes a lactotroph-level sensitivity (both raise prolactin similarly) from a central/hypothalamic dopaminergic-tone defect (haloperidol-specific exaggeration). If ME/CFS patients over-respond to haloperidol but not domperidone, the defect is central (hypothalamic/TIDA tone), supporting the brain-dopamine hypothesis; if they over-respond to both, the abnormality is at the lactotroph/D2-receptor level. This disambiguates the anatomical locus. Use is probe/diagnostic only; single low-dose challenge.
- **Evidence link:** Idea 1.2 (prolactin as central-dopamine window); BenJonathan2001ProlactinDopamine 0.64 (D2 pharmacology across compartments).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Differential prolactin response to haloperidol vs domperidone in ME/CFS (haloperidol > domperidone) indicates central dopaminergic-tone defect; equal responses indicate lactotroph-level D2 sensitivity.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Comparing two challenge drugs, one that reaches the brain and one that does not, would pinpoint whether the dopamine problem is in the brain itself or just in the pituitary.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 3.3 — OSU6162-Type Dopamine-Stabilizer Probe: Does Modulating Dopamine Normalize the Prolactin Response?
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Haghighi2021 tested the monoamine/dopamine stabilizer (−)-OSU6162 in ME/CFS, motivated by a dopaminergic-dysfunction hypothesis. If the exaggerated buspirone-prolactin response is a marker of that same dopaminergic dysfunction, then a dopamine-modulating agent should partially normalize it. A small pharmacodynamic substudy — measure buspirone-prolactin AUC before and during OSU6162 treatment — would directly test whether the hormone signal is dopamine-tone-sensitive and whether it tracks the therapeutic mechanism. This connects an existing ME/CFS trial line to the challenge test, giving both a mechanistic anchor.
- **Evidence link:** Haghighi2021OSU6162 0.45 (dopamine stabilizer in ME/CFS); Idea 1.1 (dopamine-brake mechanism).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Buspirone-prolactin AUC will decrease significantly during dopamine-stabilizer treatment in ME/CFS (effect d ≥ 0.4), and the decrease will correlate with clinical improvement — confirming dopamine-tone sensitivity of the probe.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If an existing dopamine-targeting drug normalizes the hormone test, it would both confirm the dopamine explanation and offer a way to track whether the drug is working.
- **origin:** brainstorm

## Category 4: Supplement/Nutraceutical

### Idea 4.1 — None Supported Unless a Dopamine-Precursor Rationale Emerges
- **Certainty:** 0.10
- **Mechanistic rationale:** If mechanism a (reduced dopaminergic tone) is confirmed, a plausible nutraceutical axis would be dopamine-precursor or dopamine-support supplementation (e.g., L-tyrosine as the rate-limiting dopamine precursor). However, there is NO direct evidence linking any supplement to the prolactin-buspirone signal in ME/CFS, and prolactin suppression by dopamine means a supplement would need to raise central dopamine tone without off-target effects. This is explicitly premature: the mechanism (a vs b) is unresolved, and recommending a dopamine-precursor supplement on a candidate mechanism with certainty <0.40 would risk misleading readers. Flagged as research-stage only, contingent on the sex-stratified replication (Idea 2.1) supporting mechanism a.
- **Evidence link:** Idea 1.1 (dopamine-brake, provisional); no direct supplement evidence in literature summary.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** None ethical to run without mechanism resolution; the gate is that NO supplement idea proceeds until the replication adjudicates dopamine vs estradiol.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** No supplement is currently justified — proposing one before the underlying mechanism is proven would be guessing with patients' money and health.
- **origin:** brainstorm

## Category 5: Non-Pharmacological Interventions

### Idea 5.1 — Dopamine-Tone-Sensitive Challenge as a Pacing/Recovery Monitoring Tool (Diagnostic Use, Not an Intervention)
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** If the prolactin response is state-dependent (Sharma2001 recovery signal) and dopaminergic, then it could serve as an objective readout for non-pharmacological management — pacing, graded recovery programs, sleep, and activity regulation — all of which plausibly modulate central dopaminergic tone. The challenge test would be administered longitudinally (e.g., at baseline, during structured pacing, at recovery) to see whether the signal normalizes with non-drug management. This is a monitoring application, not a treatment; it gives ME/CFS non-pharmacological care an objective neuroendocrine anchor where only subjective scales exist today.
- **Evidence link:** Sharma2001Buspirone 0.40 (state-dependence/recovery); Idea 1.5 (state signal).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Structured pacing/recovery management that produces ≥2-point clinical improvement will also produce a significant decrease in buspirone-prolactin AUC, while stable/severe patients show no change.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The hormone test could tell clinicians whether a non-drug recovery program is actually shifting the underlying biology, not just how the patient reports feeling.
- **origin:** brainstorm

## Category 6: Combinations + Access

### Idea 6.1 — Buspirone-Prolactin Challenge as Part of a Standardized Neuroendocrine Diagnostic Panel
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The discriminative value (CFS prolactin HIGHEST vs depression LOWEST; Cleare1995) means buspirone-prolactin could join a diagnostic panel alongside cortisol, DHEA-S, TSH, and sex hormones to separate ME/CFS from depression and other neuroendocrine conditions. Because challenge tests are more invasive and time-consuming than resting blood draws, the value proposition is as a second-line/confirmatory test in patients where depression-vs-ME/CFS is clinically ambiguous, not a first-line screen. Combining it with the cortisol-interaction control (Idea 2.3) and estradiol measurement (Idea 2.1) in one panel gives a multi-axis neuroendocrine profile. Access concern: requires a supervised oral challenge with serial blood draws (~2–3 h), so it is feasible in outpatient research and referral centers but not home settings.
- **Evidence link:** Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (CFS vs depression discriminator); Newman1998Fenfluramine 0.51 (challenge-test methodology); Idea 2.1/2.3 (panel design).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In a cohort of clinically ambiguous fatigue cases, the buspirone-prolactin panel will classify ME/CFS vs depression vs healthy with sensitivity + specificity ≥ 80% using prospectively defined cutoffs.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** An hour-long hormone challenge test could eventually help doctors tell apart two illnesses that look similar (chronic fatigue syndrome vs depression) when routine blood tests are inconclusive.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 6.2 — Access and Reproducibility: Standardized Protocol + Multi-Center Validation Before Clinical Use
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The 1990s studies used inconsistent protocols (buspirone dose 0.5 mg/kg in Sharpe vs fixed oral in Bakheit; different prolactin assay methods; d-fenfluramine vs buspirone). For the proposed replication to be valid and for any clinical use, a single standardized challenge protocol — fixed buspirone dose, timed serial samples, fasting state, phase-of-cycle/estradiol control, defined AUC computation — must be locked and validated across multiple centers. A common-probe protocol also enables pooling and meta-analysis. This is a method/access/standards idea rather than a biological one, but it is the practical gate to whether the finding can ever reach clinical utility.
- **Evidence link:** Newman1998Fenfluramine 0.51 (probe variability review); Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (matched-cycle design as gold-standard protocol); Phase 3 note on diagnostic-era/cohort issues.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Two independent centers running an identical locked protocol will reproduce a similar ME/CFS-vs-control prolactin AUC difference (ICC ≥ 0.6 between centers on healthy controls), establishing protocol reproducibility.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Before this test can help anyone in the clinic, researchers must prove that two different labs doing the exact same test get the same result — which is not yet established.
- **origin:** brainstorm

## Category 7: Mathematical Model Extensions

### Idea 7.1 — ODE Model of the Prolactin-Dopamine Brake: D2 Occupancy vs Prolactin AUC With Estradiol-Modulated Gain
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The prolactin-dopamine system is a canonical negative-feedback loop amenable to ODE modeling: lactotrophs synthesize/release prolactin; dopamine (TIDA) inhibits release via D2; estradiol increases prolactin synthesis and modulates dopamine delivery; prolactin feeds back (short loop) to regulate TIDA. A model of the form dPRL/dt = synthesis(estradiol) − degradation·PRL − D2inhibition(DA), with D2inhibition as a saturating function of dopamine/occupancy, can predict how a weak dopamine brake (reduced DA) vs estrogen upregulation (increased synthesis) each produce an exaggerated prolactin AUC to a D2-blocking challenge. This distinguishes the two mechanisms quantitatively: a low-dopamine model inflates the response to D2 antagonism; a high-estrogen model inflates both baseline dynamics and the challenge. Fitting to the existing AUC data (Sharpe 1996, Bakheit 1992, Dinan 2001 cortisol-coupled) yields parameter estimates and generates specific predictions for the replication.
- **Evidence link:** BenJonathan2001ProlactinDopamine 0.64, QiLytle2023TIDA 0.60, Fitzgerald2008ProlactinDopamine 0.60 (system structure); Dinan2001Buspirone 0.49 (cortisol as gain modulator).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** The model fit to Sharpe1996 (male CFS) data will require low-dopamine (weak brake) parameters, not high-estrogen ones; a model with estradiol gain fixed at male levels will fail to reproduce the exaggerated male AUC unless dopamine is reduced — falsifying mechanism b for the male-only study.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A mathematical model of the hormone-control loop could use the existing small datasets to show quantitatively whether the "weak brake" or "extra estrogen" explanation better fits the data — before spending on a new trial.
- **origin:** brainstorm

## Category 8: Cross-Disease Bridges

### Idea 8.1 — Long COVID Dopamine Bridge: VMAT2-PET and DAT Findings as Independent Dopaminergic Evidence
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Long COVID shows dopaminergic findings (VMAT2-PET striatal dopaminergic deficits; the nigrostriatal/VMAT2 line in this paper). If ME/CFS and Long COVID share dopaminergic pathology, the buspirone-prolactin challenge could be a peripheral marker of the same dopaminergic deficit seen by PET in Long COVID — and a far cheaper, more scalable probe. Testing the challenge in both ME/CFS and Long COVID cohorts, correlated with VMAT2-PET or DAT-SPECT, would bridge the two conditions and validate prolactin-AUC as a surrogate for neuroimaging dopaminergic measures. This extends the dopamine hypothesis across post-infectious syndromes.
- **Evidence link:** Haghighi2021OSU6162 0.45 (dopamine in ME/CFS); VMAT2-striatum-LongCOVID topic; Idea 1.2 (prolactin as central-dopamine window).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Buspirone-prolactin AUC will be similarly elevated in ME/CFS and Long COVID and will correlate with striatal VMAT2/DAT dopaminergic deficit (r > 0.5) in both groups.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A blood hormone test could link two closely related post-infectious illnesses and stand in for expensive brain scans that detect the same dopamine signal.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 8.2 — Depression Contrast Validation: The Discriminator Is Only Useful If It Survives Modern Criteria
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The discriminative value (CFS prolactin HIGHEST vs depression LOWEST) rests on a 1995 d-fenfluramine study with small groups and pre-modern ME/CFS criteria. Depression has been repeatedly shown to blunt prolactin responses (Newman1998). A modern three-arm study (ME/CFS by CCC vs major depressive disorder vs healthy) with buspirone would validate whether the discriminator holds under current diagnostic criteria — critical because ME/CFS is frequently misdiagnosed as depression, and a biological discriminator would have major clinical value. If the discrimination disappears under modern criteria, the diagnostic claim must be retracted.
- **Evidence link:** Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (original discriminator); Newman1998Fenfluramine 0.51 (depression blunts prolactin); diagnostic-era concern (Category 12).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Under modern criteria, buspirone-prolactin AUC will still separate ME/CFS (highest) from depression (lowest) from healthy (intermediate) with p < 0.05 between all pairs.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** This test could give doctors a biological way to tell chronic fatigue syndrome apart from depression — but only if it still works using today's stricter diagnostic definitions.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 8.3 — Fibromyalgia and Chronic Pain Bridge: Shared Neuroendocrine/Dopaminergic Dysregulation
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Fibromyalgia shares neuroendocrine and dopaminergic dysregulation with ME/CFS (HPA abnormalities, pain-processing dopamine deficits, chronic-overlapping-pain multimorbidity). If buspirone-prolactin is a dopaminergic-tone probe, fibromyalgia patients may show a similar exaggerated response, supporting a shared central-dopamine deficit across chronic overlapping pain conditions. This would extend the finding beyond ME/CFS and strengthen the case that dopamine hypofunction is a transdiagnostic mechanism in chronic post-infectious/pain-fatigue conditions.
- **Evidence link:** Chronic-overlapping-pain-multimorbidity line; fibromyalgia dopaminergic findings; Idea 1.1 (dopamine-brake).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Fibromyalgia patients will show an exaggerated buspirone-prolactin AUC intermediate between healthy controls and ME/CFS, and the AUC will correlate with pain-processing dopaminergic deficits.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The same hormone test might also help explain the overlap between chronic fatigue and chronic-pain conditions, pointing to a shared brain-dopamine problem.

## Category 9: Diagnostic/Biomarker Ideas

### Idea 9.1 — Challenge-Test Discriminator vs Depression: The Primary Diagnostic Proposition
- **Certainty:** 0.50
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The strongest diagnostic claim is the depression discriminator: CFS prolactin response HIGHEST, depression LOWEST, healthy intermediate (Cleare1995). A standardized buspirone-prolactin challenge could be a biological aid where ME/CFS vs depression is clinically ambiguous — a frequent and consequential diagnostic error with wrong-treatment implications (antidepressants vs pacing). Unlike resting prolactin (normal in ME/CFS), the challenge is dynamic and reveals the group separation. The clinical value depends on surviving modern-criteria validation (Idea 8.2).
- **Evidence link:** Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (highest CFS / lowest depression); normal baseline prolactin (literature summary); Newman1998Fenfluramine 0.51.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Prospectively defined buspirone-prolactin AUC cutoffs classify ME/CFS vs depression with sensitivity + specificity ≥ 80%, outperforming resting prolactin (which shows no separation).
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A hormone challenge could one day be a tie-breaker test for doctors unsure whether a patient has chronic fatigue syndrome or depression, pointing to the right treatment.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 9.2 — Baseline Prolactin Insufficiency / Ratio Measures as Low-Cost Alternatives
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** A full challenge test (serial draws, 2–3 h) is burdensome. If the finding reflects dopaminergic tone, simpler measures — resting prolactin relative to estradiol (a prolactin:estradiol ratio), prolactin relative to cortisol, or a single post-challenge time-point (e.g., 90-min prolactin) — might retain discriminative value at lower cost. Sharpe1996 found elevated prolactin at specific time-points; a single-timed sample or ratio could be a triage test that flags candidates for the full challenge. This is a biomarker-optimization idea contingent on the replication defining the best readout.
- **Evidence link:** Sharpe1996Buspirone 0.62 (timed prolactin elevations); BenJonathan2001ProlactinDopamine 0.64 (prolactin:estradiol coupling); Dinan2001Buspirone 0.49 (prolactin:cortisol coupling).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A single 90-min post-buspirone prolactin sample, or a prolactin:estradiol ratio, will correlate with full-AUC classification (r > 0.7) and retain ≥ 70% of the discrimination at far lower cost.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A single blood sample instead of a two-hour test could make the diagnostic signal much cheaper and more practical to use.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 9.3 — Prolactin Response as an Objective Outcome Measure for Trials (Endpoint)
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Subjective fatigue endpoints are vulnerable to placebo, expectancy, and demand characteristics. If the buspirone-prolactin response is state-dependent (Idea 1.5) and reflects an intervention-modifiable dopaminergic signal, it could serve as an objective secondary/exploratory endpoint in ME/CFS treatment trials — particularly those targeting dopaminergic or neuroendocrine mechanisms. It provides a biological anchor that complements patient-reported outcomes, improving trial sensitivity and credibility. (Not a replacement for primary patient-reported endpoints, but a mechanistically grounded secondary marker.)
- **Evidence link:** Sharma2001Buspirone 0.40 (recovery-associated change); Idea 1.5 (state signal); objective-measurement gaps in ME/CFS trials.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In a dopaminergic-targeting trial, change in buspirone-prolactin AUC will correlate with change in a validated fatigue scale (r > 0.4) and separate responders from non-responders.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A blood-based measure that tracks with improvement could make drug trials in chronic fatigue more trustworthy by adding an objective readout to self-reported symptoms.
- **origin:** brainstorm

## Category 10: Reasons This Mechanism May NOT Be Relevant to ME/CFS (MANDATORY)

### Idea 10.1 — Artifact of Pre-Fukuda Diagnostic Criteria and Diagnostic Contamination
- **Certainty:** 0.50 (for the possibility that it is an artifact)
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Bakheit1992 used pre-Fukuda PVFS clinical criteria; only Sharpe1996 used Fukuda. The 1992 cohort may have included patients who would not meet modern CCC/ICC criteria, and "postviral fatigue" may overlap with depression or other conditions. If the exaggerated response was partly a property of a broader, loosely-defined fatigue group — not a cohesive ME/CFS entity — then a modern CCC-based replication could fail. Diagnostic contamination (some "CFS" patients actually having depression or other neuroendocrine disease) could have produced a spurious signal. The strongest counter-test is a modern replication under CCC/ICC criteria; until it passes, the finding's relevance to current ME/CFS is unproven.
- **Evidence link:** Diagnostic-era notes (Bakheit pre-Fukuda; Sharpe Fukuda); literature-summary cohort notes; diagnostic-criteria concerns from Phase 3.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** If the finding is an era artifact, a modern CCC-based cohort will show NO significant ME/CFS-vs-control difference in buspirone-prolactin AUC, and the depression-vs-CFS discriminator will vanish.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The striking hormone finding may have come from a looser, older definition of the illness and might not appear in patients diagnosed under today's stricter criteria.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 10.2 — Small-Sample Bias and Non-Replication: The Effect May Be a Type-I Error or Fragile Finding
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The positive studies are n=11–20, single-center, 1990s-era, and the line was abandoned — a pattern consistent with a small-sample false positive that failed to survive or was never adequately pursued. The depression-vs-CFS discriminator rests on one d-fenfluramine study (Cleare1995, n=10 CFS). No power analysis, no pre-registration, and no independent modern replication exist. Small neuroendocrine challenge studies are prone to type-I error, and the "clean" mechanistic story (Idea 1.1) could be an over-interpretation of fragile data. The burden is on the proposed replication; a null result would be entirely consistent with a false-positive history.
- **Evidence link:** n=11–20 sample sizes; single-center; abandoned line; Newman1998Fenfluramine 0.51 (challenge-test variability); no post-2010 replication.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A sufficiently powered, pre-registered, multi-center replication will show a null or much smaller ME/CFS-vs-control difference than the original reports (effect not reproducible).
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The result comes from very small studies that were never repeated in 25 years, so the "finding" could simply be a statistical fluke of tiny samples.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 10.3 — The Buspirone Probe Is Confounded: Multi-Receptor Drug Cannot Localize the Mechanism
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Buspirone is not a clean probe — it agonizes 5-HT1A AND blocks D2 (and has partial 5-HT2 activity). The literature interpretation (dopamine-brake) rests on the *contrast* with fenfluramine (null), but no study directly isolated the D2 component with a pure D2 antagonist in the same patients. The exaggerated response could be a serotonin-driven effect (5-HT1A hypersensitivity in the original Bakheit interpretation), a D2 effect, or an interaction — and the proposed dopamine mechanism (Idea 1.1) may be an over-read of a confounded probe. A different mechanism entirely (e.g., altered prolactin clearance, lactotroph sensitivity, or pituitary D2-receptor upregulation) could explain the same observation. Until a within-subject panel (Idea 2.2) adjudicates, the dopamine attribution is not secure.
- **Evidence link:** Bakheit1992Buspirone 0.55 (interpreted as 5-HT, not dopamine); buspirone multi-receptor pharmacology; no pure-D2 comparator in the literature.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** If buspirone is confounded, the exaggerated response will NOT be reproduced by a pure D2 antagonist alone (domperidone), and the dopamine-brake hypothesis (Idea 1.1) fails.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The test drug affects two brain systems at once, so no one actually knows whether the abnormal response is about dopamine, serotonin, or an entirely different effect — the clean story may be wrong.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 10.4 — Medication and Confounder Effects: Unmeasured Drugs and Lifestyle Confounds
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Prolactin is exquisitely sensitive to many drugs (antipsychotics, antidepressants, metoclopramide, opiates, estrogens, some antihistamines) and to stress, sleep, fasting, and exercise. The 1990s studies did not uniformly document medication washout or control for these confounds. If ME/CFS patients were more likely to be on medications that raise prolactin responsivity (or off drugs that blunt it), the group difference could be a medication artifact rather than disease biology. No study reported a formal assessment of all confounding agents. A modern replication must enforce medication washout and document stress/sleep state.
- **Evidence link:** Newman1998Fenfluramine 0.51 (probe affected by age/sex/drugs); prolactin pharmacology; literature-summary limitations on confounder control.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** After strict medication washout and confounder control, the ME/CFS-vs-control buspirone-prolactin difference will shrink or vanish if it was medication-driven.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The patients in those old studies may have been on other medications that artificially boosted the hormone reading, so the effect could be from the drugs, not the illness.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 10.5 — The Finding Does Not Require a Novel Mechanism: It Could Be an Epiphenomenon of Known ME/CFS Physiology (HPA / Stress / Autonomic)
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The exaggerated prolactin response could be a downstream epiphenomenon of well-known ME/CFS features — HPA-axis dysregulation (low cortisol), autonomic dysfunction, or chronic stress — rather than a distinct dopaminergic lesion. Dinan2001 showed prolactin AUC tracks baseline cortisol; Cleare1995 showed low baseline cortisol in CFS. The "dopamine" story may be an unnecessary new mechanism when a cortisol/HPA explanation (or a stress/arousal explanation) already fits the data. If the effect disappears once cortisol is statistically controlled (Idea 2.3), it adds nothing mechanistically distinct to the paper's existing HPA/neuroendocrine coverage and may not warrant novel-mechanism status.
- **Evidence link:** Dinan2001Buspirone 0.49 (prolactin AUC ~ cortisol); Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (low CFS cortisol); HPA-axis coverage in ch08.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** After covarying baseline cortisol, the ME/CFS-vs-control prolactin AUC difference becomes non-significant — indicating the "dopamine" signal is really an HPA/cortisol epiphenomenon.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The hormone finding might just be another reflection of the well-documented stress-hormone problems in chronic fatigue, not a genuinely new brain-dopamine defect.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 10.6 — Sex-Bias Artifact: The ~80% Female Predominance Could Drive a Spurious Sex-Confounded Signal
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Because ME/CFS is ~80% female and estradiol raises prolactin and modulates prolactin responsivity, an unstratified female-majority cohort could produce an exaggerated mean prolactin response purely because of sex composition — NOT a disease mechanism. If the effect is simply "more women → higher estradiol → higher prolactin response," then it is a sampling artifact, and the "estradiol mechanism" (candidate b) is not ME/CFS-specific biology at all. This is a critical alternative explanation: the finding could be sex-confounded rather than disease-driven. The sex-stratified replication (Idea 2.1) is the decisive test — it must include enough men and properly matched female controls to rule this out.
- **Evidence link:** ~80% female predominance; estradiol-prolactin coupling (BenJonathan2001, QiLytle2023); male-only Sharpe1996 partially controls this but is the exception.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** If the finding is a sex artifact, it will disappear entirely when sex and estradiol are matched (equal prolactin AUC in ME/CFS women vs healthy women, and in men vs men), leaving no disease-specific signal.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Because most chronic-fatigue patients are women and estrogen raises this hormone, the "finding" could just reflect who happened to be in the study, not a real difference caused by the illness.
- **origin:** brainstorm

## Category 11: Null Hypothesis Assessment (MANDATORY)

### Idea 11.1 — What If the Exaggerated Response Has No Functional Role: Minimal Consequence for Most Claims
- **Certainty:** 0.45 (for the assessment that impact would be small)
- **Mechanistic rationale:** If the exaggerated buspirone-prolactin response is a real but functionally silent epiphenomenon (a biomarker of dopamine tone with no causal role in fatigue, PEM, or severity), then it has value ONLY as a diagnostic/probe marker — not as a mechanism of disease. Crucially, the paper's current integration (Phase 3) already frames it primarily as a research-question/biomarker-challenge and an open-question about mechanism — NOT as a causal driver. Therefore a null causal role would NOT require retracting the integrated environments; it would simply reclassify the finding as a correlational biomarker rather than a mechanistic one, and would cap its certainty below causal-claim territory. The claims that WOULD need revision are any that imply the dopamine/prolactin axis causes ME/CFS symptoms — the hypothesis registry and ch10 open-question already avoid this.
- **Evidence link:** Phase 3 integration framing (clinical-finding + open-question + prediction, not causal mechanism); hypothesis-registry rows; ch10 sec-09.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** If the response has no functional role, it will not correlate with symptoms, severity, or treatment response despite being reproducible as a group discriminator — i.e., it is a marker without a mechanism-of-action.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Even if the test merely flags a difference without causing symptoms, it could still be a useful diagnostic hint — the paper's cautious framing already treats it that way, so little would need to change.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 11.2 — Revision Map: Which Existing Claims Would Need Correction Under a Null Result
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** A null replication (no exaggerated response in modern ME/CFS) would force revision of several current entries: (1) the ch10 `#clinical-finding` `cf:prolactin-buspirone-exaggerated` (0.62) would need downgrading to a historical/superseded finding; (2) the `#open-question` mechanism framing `oq:prolactin-buspirone-mechanism` would need re-framing toward "refuted by replication"; (3) the ch47 proposed-study prediction `pred:buspirone-challenge-replication` is self-correcting by design (it is a falsifiable prediction — a null confirms the prediction structure but invalidates the hoped-for confirmation); (4) the hypothesis-registry rows would need status flags; (5) the ch21 estradiol speculation `spec:fra-estradiol-prolactin-link` (0.30) would lose its motivating evidence and should be weakened or removed. This revision map makes the pipeline's claims auditable and prevents over-commitment to a fragile finding.
- **Evidence link:** Phase 3 integration list (ch10, ch21, ch47, registry); specificity of each environment.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A null replication will trigger the specific downgrade/removal of the five entries above; the registry and ch10/ch21 text will be audited for consistency within one cycle.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The paper has built-in safeguards: if a new, larger study fails to reproduce the old finding, the specific paragraphs, hypotheses, and study proposal would be flagged and corrected rather than silently left standing.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 11.3 — Depression Discriminator: The Highest-Stakes Claim Under a Null Result
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The single most clinically valuable claim — that buspirone-prolactin separates ME/CFS from depression (Cleare1995) — is also the most fragile (one d-fenfluramine study, n=10 CFS, pre-modern criteria). A null result for THIS claim would remove the primary rationale for the whole topic's clinical utility (a biological discriminator vs depression), even if the ME/CFS-vs-healthy difference survived. This must be assessed separately from the general null: the discriminator could fail while the dopamine-mechanism question remains open, or vice versa. The proposed replication must power specifically for the three-way (ME/CFS vs depression vs healthy) contrast, not just the two-way difference.
- **Evidence link:** Cleare1995DepressionCFS 0.62 (the discriminator); no buspirone-with-depression-arm study (Sharpe had healthy controls only — literature-summary gap).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A three-arm replication will show whether the depression-vs-ME/CFS prolactin separation survives modern criteria; if it fails, the diagnostic-discriminator claim (Idea 9.1) is retracted even if the dopamine mechanism is retained.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The most valuable promise of this research — a blood test to tell depression from chronic fatigue apart — is the one most at risk of being a small-study artifact, so it needs its own careful test.

## Category 12: Evidence Quality Concerns (MANDATORY)

### Idea 12.1 — Cohort Overlap and Non-Independence of the "Replication"
- **Certainty:** 0.50 (for the assessment that independence is overstated)
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The literature is presented as "replicated," but the independent sources are fewer than they appear. Dinan is a shared author on Bakheit1992 (the original positive) and Dinan2001 (the cortisol-mechanism study) — same group. The Wessely/Cleare King's College cluster produced Cleare1995, Bearn1995, and Cleare1996 — overlapping cohorts, not independent replications. Only Sharpe1996 (Cowen, Oxford) and Yatham1995 (Dalhousie, null) are genuinely independent. The "positive replication" is therefore substantially one-lineage (Bakheit/Dinan + Sharpe) with the depression-contrast and fenfluramine-null both coming from the KCL cluster. Any certainty given to "replication" must be discounted for this non-independence.
- **Evidence link:** Literature-summary cohort-overlap notes (Dinan shared; KCL cluster; Sharpe/Yatham independent).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A bibliometric/net analysis of the 14 papers will confirm ≤3 genuinely independent investigator groups, with the majority of positive and contrasting findings from the KCL cluster.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The claim that the finding was "independently repeated" is weaker than it sounds — several of the studies come from the same research group and possibly the same patients.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 12.2 — Small, Single-Center, Underpowered Studies Without Pre-Registration or Power Analysis
- **Certainty:** 0.50
- **Mechanistic rationale:** All positive studies are n=11–20 (Sharpe: 11; Cleare CFS group: 10), single-center, 1990s, no pre-registration, no prospective power analysis, and several are brief reports/letters (Sharma2001 brief report; Cleare1996 letter). The effect size needed to reach significance at n=11 is large, and large effects in small samples are disproportionately likely to be inflated (the "winner's curse"). The certainty scores (0.55–0.62) already encode small n, but the *fragility* — how many patient outcomes flipping would nullify the result — is not captured and is likely high. This is a structural quality concern beyond any single certainty number.
- **Evidence link:** n values (Sharpe 11, Cleare 10); brief-report formats (Sharma2001, Cleare1996); abandoned line.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A fragility index (number of patient outcomes that, if flipped, nullify significance) computed on Sharpe1996 and Cleare1995 will be ≤3, indicating high fragility.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The studies were so small that a handful of patients' results flipping could erase the finding — a sign of how fragile the evidence really is.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 12.3 — No Depression Arm in the Buspirone Studies and No Sex Stratification
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Sharpe1996 (the key buspirone replication) had healthy controls only — NO depression arm. The depression-vs-CFS discriminator comes from a different probe (d-fenfluramine, Cleare1995), not buspirone. Therefore the claim "buspirone-prolactin separates ME/CFS from depression" is an extrapolation across probes, not a directly tested buspirone finding. Separately, none of the buspirone studies stratified by sex or measured estradiol, so the female-predominance/estradiol mechanism (candidate b) has NO direct buspirone evidence. These two gaps are structural and cannot be repaired from the existing literature — they are the central rationale for the proposed replication's design (Idea 2.1, 2.2).
- **Evidence link:** Sharpe1996 (healthy only); Cleare1995 (d-fenfluramine, not buspirone); no sex-stratified buspirone study (literature-summary gaps).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Re-analyses (if raw data exist) will confirm the absence of a buspirone-with-depression-arm and of sex stratification in all positive buspirone studies, leaving the discriminator and estradiol claims as cross-probe/cross-gap extrapolations.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The two most important promises — telling depression apart and explaining why more women are affected — were never directly tested with the actual test drug, so they rest on indirect evidence.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 12.4 — Diagnostic-Era Heterogeneity and Confounder-Control Limits
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Studies span pre-Fukuda PVFS (Bakheit1992) to Fukuda (Sharpe1996, etc.), with no CCC/ICC cohort. Diagnostic criteria changed across the era, and the "CFS" studied in 1992 may not match current case definitions. No study comprehensively controlled for medication washout, stress, sleep, fasting, or menstrual phase (except Cleare1995's matched menstrual cycle). Prolactin's extreme sensitivity to these confounders (Idea 10.4) means the group differences could reflect unmeasured covariates. These limits are not fully captured by the per-paper certainty scores, which measure sample size and replication but not confounder completeness.
- **Evidence link:** Pre-Fukuda (Bakheit) vs Fukuda (Sharpe/Bearn); Cleare1995 matched cycle (the exception); literature-summary confounder notes.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A systematic confounder-audit of the 1990s protocols will show most failed to document medication washout and sleep/stress state, with only Cleare1995 controlling menstrual cycle.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The old studies were done under inconsistent patient definitions and didn't consistently account for medications, sleep, or stress — all of which can change the hormone reading on their own.
- **origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 12.5 — Assay, Dosing, and Protocol Non-Standardization Across Studies
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The 1990s used different buspirone doses (Sharpe: weight-based 0.5 mg/kg; Bakheit: oral), different prolactin assay methods, and different probes across studies (buspirone vs d-fenfluramine vs dl-fenfluramine). Newman1998 documented how challenge-test results vary with dose, assay, age, and sex. This protocol heterogeneity means the "replicated" finding may partly be an artifact of comparing across non-equivalent methods, and it undermines pooling. A standardized modern protocol (Idea 6.2) is required before the effect can be considered robust.
- **Evidence link:** Dose differences (Sharpe weight-based vs Bakheit fixed); probe differences (buspirone vs fenfluramine); Newman1998Fenfluramine 0.51 (methodology variability).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A methods-reproducibility analysis will show the reported effect sizes vary with dose and assay method across the 14 studies, consistent with protocol-driven heterogeneity rather than a single true effect.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The studies didn't use the same drug dose or the same measurement method, so the "consistent finding" may partly be an artifact of comparing studies that weren't really equivalent.
- **origin:** brainstorm
