## Evidence-Synthesis Opener

A lifetime-low-energy constitution is a real, measurable population construct (Collin 2015 in adolescence; Harvey 2009 in adults), but it is NOT cleanly a low-baseline phenotype — Harvey's prospective cohort finds the premorbid population risk factor is *excessive* childhood energy (OR 2.63), directly challenging the topic's low-baseline premise. Within ME/CFS, mild and low-reserve severity subsets exist (Jason 2010, Brown 2013), and the "lifelong fatigue" exclusion criterion that would gate these cases out of diagnosis is non-operationalizable and non-discriminating, supporting its removal (Sunnquist 2015). The defensible synthesis is a **continuous energy-constraint spectrum** in which both a lifetime-low-baseline reserve AND an overactivity-then-crash history can converge on a low usable energy ceiling, with **PEM as the discriminator** between a tired constitution and ME/CFS disease — Sunnquist argues the no-PEM state should not exclude recognition but does not equate it with ME/CFS. No longitudinal cohort demonstrates that mild/lifetime cases progress to moderate/severe; that transition is the central open question. Underdiagnosis of mild cases is implied by the exclusion-criterion critique but not yet quantified.

## Category 1 — Novel Hypotheses

### P1.1: PEM, Not Baseline Energy Level, Is the Constitution-versus-Disease Discriminator
**Mechanism:** The central synthesis finding: a lifetime-low-baseline reserve and ME/CFS sit on one continuous energy-constraint spectrum, and the binary separator is the *acquisition of PEM*, not the baseline energy level. A person can be constitutionally tired (low reserve, no PEM) and be healthy-but-low-energy; the same reserve with an added acquired post-exertional crash defines ME/CFS. Sunnquist 2015 supports removing the lifelong-fatigue exclusion (so low-baseline does not bar recognition) while explicitly not equating the no-PEM state with disease. This makes PEM the single operational boundary — it predicts that two people with identical baseline energy differ categorically on disease status purely by the presence/absence of PEM, and that the "mild case" is recognized not by energy level but by the PEM signature.
**Evidence:** Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45, defined without PEM), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45).
**Certainty:** 0.45
**Falsifiable:** In a cohort of low-baseline-energy individuals, the presence of reproducible post-exertional malaise (objective 2-day CPET or validated PEM screen) separates those meeting ME/CFS case criteria from those who do not, independent of baseline energy level; if baseline energy alone predicts ME/CFS status better than PEM presence, the discriminator hypothesis is wrong.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A naturally tired person and a person with ME/CFS can feel equally exhausted, but the one who crashes specifically after exertion is the one who has the illness — the crash, not the tiredness, is what a doctor should listen for.
`origin: brainstorm`

### P1.2: The Low-Reserve-Ceiling Model Reconciles the Low-Baseline Premise with Harvey's High-Energy Finding
**Mechanism:** Harvey 2009's population finding (excessive childhood energy predicts later fatigue) and the topic's low-baseline premise appear contradictory but reconcile under a *ceiling* model: the relevant vulnerability is a low *usable energy envelope ceiling*, which two opposite trajectories both reach. A lifetime-low-baseline person lives near a chronically low ceiling; an overactive-then-crash person (Harvey's high-energy premorbid) repeatedly overshoots and depletes the same ceiling. Both end at the same low reserve — so population-level premorbid predictors look high-energy (the overshooters are the ones visible in registry statistics) while the clinical presentation is low-energy (the ceiling they all converge on). PEM marks the point where the ceiling can no longer re-expand after overshoot — the boundary between a fixed low constitution and acquired disease.
**Evidence:** Harvey2009neurasthenia (0.51), Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48, low-reserve cluster), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45).
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Falsifiable:** Under the ceiling model, both high-energy premorbid and low-baseline premorbid individuals converge to similar measured daily energy-envelope (activity/expenditure) profiles after decompensation; if the two premorbid groups show distinct post-onset energy profiles, they are separate trajectories, not a common ceiling.
**Non-specialist consequence:** It can look like the opposite people are at risk — the extremely energetic child and the always-tired person — but both may be heading to the same place: a small and fragile daily energy budget.
`origin: brainstorm`

### P1.3: Mild/Lifetime Cases Represent Pre-PEM Reserve Erosion, Not a Distinct Disease
**Mechanism:** The mild, low-severity subsets within ME/CFS (Jason 2010) and the low-daily-reserve cluster (Brown 2013) may be the *early or pre-PEM stage* of the same disease rather than a categorically milder illness. In this reading, lifelong low reserve is a vulnerability that, once a trigger (infection, stressor) erodes the reserve below a functional threshold, acquires PEM and "crosses" into diagnosable ME/CFS. The mild case is then the same pathophysiology before the PEM threshold is crossed or at low amplitude — explaining why mild cases are under-recognized (they have not yet developed the hallmark PEM that triggers diagnosis) and why "lifelong fatigue" was wrongly used to exclude them (Sunnquist 2015). This is a *transition/continuum* claim, deliberately not a distinct-trajectory `#hypothesis-box` (Phase 2 cap).
**Evidence:** Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45), Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45), Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55).
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Falsifiable:** A longitudinal cohort of mild/lifetime-low-energy individuals that develops ME/CFS should show PEM emerging after a documented trigger and then persist (irreversible threshold), rather than staying permanently PEM-free; if a distinct subset of lifelong-low-energy individuals never develops PEM despite the same exposure, they are a separate constitution, not an early stage.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A person who has "always been tired" may be in the early part of the same illness as someone who developed it suddenly — so dismissing lifelong tiredness could delay catching the illness at its mildest, most treatable point.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 2 — Research Directions

### R2.1: Prospective Cohort of Lifetime-Low-Energy Individuals Tracking the Onset of PEM (the Transition Gap)
**Design:** The single largest gap is that no cohort demonstrates mild/lifetime-energy → moderate/severe ME/CFS. Design a prospective longitudinal cohort (n≥300) of adults reporting lifetime constitutional low energy ("always more tired than expected," no PEM at baseline), plus an overactivity-then-crash comparison arm (Harvey-type). Baseline: energy-envelope/activity monitoring, PEM screen, severity, psychiatric comorbidity. Follow-up 24–36 months with trigger capture (infection, stressor) and quarterly PEM reassessment. Primary: does a defined subset acquire reproducible PEM after a trigger? Secondary: does acquired PEM track functional decline (the mild→moderate/severe transition)?
**Rationale:** Directly tests the central open question the Phase 2 synthesis flags as unsupported. Collin (childhood, cross-sectional), Harvey (adult, but only premorbid predictors, no ME/CFS outcome), and Jason/Brown (within-ME/CFS severity) each supply a slice; none connects the slices longitudinally.
**Evidence:** Harvey2009neurasthenia (0.51), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45), Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48).
**Certainty:** n/a (proposal — transition untested)
**Falsifiable:** If lifetime-low-energy is a genuine mild/disease-continuous trajectory, a measurable fraction of the cohort acquires PEM and declines after a trigger, distinct from an age-matched high-energy control arm; if no subset acquires PEM, the mild/low-energy state is a benign constitution, not an ME/CFS prodrome.
**Non-specialist consequence:** This study would answer whether "always tired" people really go on to develop ME/CFS after an illness, or whether that is just a coincidence that feels meaningful.
`origin: brainstorm`

### R2.2: Empirically Test What Removing the "Lifelong Fatigue" Exclusion Changes Diagnostically
**Design:** Sunnquist 2015 argues the lifelong-fatigue exclusion is non-operationalizable and non-discriminating. Operationalize its removal: apply a case-definition with and without the lifelong-fatigue exclusion to the SAME community cohort of patients presenting with persistent fatigue, and measure: (a) how many additional patients are captured, (b) whether the newly-captured (lifetime) patients differ from acute-onset patients on symptom domains, PEM prevalence, function, and biomarker levels, (c) whether they respond to energy-management differently.
**Rationale:** The exclusion critique is the strongest novel support for the mild/lifetime angle. But no study quantifies the diagnostic and clinical consequence of removing it. Without that, the critique is a recommendation, not a demonstrated improvement.
**Evidence:** Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45).
**Certainty:** n/a (proposal)
**Falsifiable:** If removing the exclusion improves recognition without diluting the syndrome, newly-captured lifetime cases have PEM and functional impairment comparable to acute-onset cases (just longer duration); if they lack PEM and functional impairment, removing the exclusion over-broadens ME/CFS to include tired-but-well people (see C10.1).
**Non-specialist consequence:** This would show, with numbers, whether dropping the "you were too tired your whole life to count" rule finds real patients or just labels more healthy-tired people with the disease.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 3 — Drug/Medication Ideas

### DR3.1: Reserve-Building Agents as a Discriminating Probe Between Low-Reserve Constitution and PEM Disease
**Mechanism:** Because the topic's central claim is that PEM (not baseline energy) separates constitution from disease, an agent that raises the energy reserve ceiling *without* resolving PEM would test the separation. A low-reserve person (constitution) should improve with a reserve-supporting agent (e.g., an NAD+/CoQ pathway or B-complex support), whereas an ME/CFS patient whose limiting factor is acquired PEM should NOT improve proportionally — the PEM signature should persist even if baseline energy rises. This is a *mechanism-discriminating probe*, not a treatment recommendation. It differs from the parked ADHD stimulant/CoQ10-reserve work by using the agent's differential effect on *constitution vs PEM* as the diagnostic readout, applied to the mild/lifetime population specifically. Framed strictly as a probe to avoid over-claiming treatment benefit on a non-treatment topic.
**Evidence:** Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48, low-reserve cluster), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45), Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55); corpus CoQ/NAD reserve literature.
**Certainty:** 0.20 (probe rationale; no ME/CFS mild-cohort dosing data)
**Falsifiable:** In a crossover of low-baseline-energy patients, a reserve-supporting agent raises measured daily-energy-envelope in the no-PEM constitution group but not the PEM group, and PEM (2-day CPET) persists in the PEM group despite any envelope gain; if both groups improve equally or neither does, reserve level does not discriminate.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If a tired-but-not-crashing person improves with energy support while a crashing ME/CFS patient does not, that difference itself becomes a clue separating the two — but this is a research probe, not a reason to start treatment.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 4 — Supplement/Nutraceutical Ideas

### SU4.1: A Reserve-Marker Panel Applied to the *Discrimination* Question, Not the Reserve Mechanism
**Mechanism:** Rather than re-proposing mitochondrial-reserve measurement (already covered), use a low-cost reserve-linked biomarker panel (e.g., 2-day CPET-adjacent VO2/exertion response, resting mitochondrial/NAD proxies, or a simple functional-capacity measure) specifically to answer the discrimination question: does the panel separate mild/lifetime-low-energy-with-PEM (ME/CFS) from mild/lifetime-low-energy-without-PEM (constitution)? The panel's value is as a *case-finding and boundary tool* for the underdiagnosed mild end, not as a novel reserve mechanism. A positive finding would operationalize the "mild case is the invisible illness" claim by giving a measurable separator.
**Evidence:** Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45).
**Certainty:** 0.25
**Falsifiable:** A reserve/functional panel separates PEM-positive from PEM-negative mild/low-energy patients with AUC ≥ 0.75, and tracks with PEM status over time; if the panel is indistinguishable between the two mild groups, it cannot operationalize the mild-case boundary.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A simple test that separates "tired constitution" from "mild ME/CFS" could help doctors recognize the illness in people who are just barely above the radar.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 5 — Non-Pharmacological Interventions

### NP5.1: Energy-Envelope/Pacing Delivered to Mild/Lifetime Cases as Both Care and a PEM-Emergence Probe
**Mechanism:** The Brown 2013 low-daily-reserve cluster responds to energy-envelope (pacing) theory — staying within a limited envelope prevents the crash. Applied to mild/lifetime cases *before* a PEM diagnosis, pacing serves a dual role: (a) it is a low-harm, threshold-independent care tool for a currently-underserved population (access), and (b) it is a *diagnostic probe* — a low-baseline person whose function and symptom burden respond to envelope-constrained pacing, and who crashes when the envelope is breached, reveals the PEM signature that identifies the mild ME/CFS case. This gives clinicians a non-pharmacological way to surface the hidden discriminator (P1.1) rather than relying on history-taking.
**Evidence:** Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45), Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55).
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Falsifiable:** In mild/lifetime-low-energy patients, a structured pacing/envelope intervention produces a crash-on-breach response in the PEM subset but not in the no-PEM constitution subset; if neither subset crashes on breach, pacing does not reveal the PEM boundary.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Rather than asking someone to recall whether they crash after exertion, a structured pacing trial can *show* it — safely surfacing the difference between everyday tiredness and true ME/CFS.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 6 — Combinations + Access

### C6.1: A Recognition Pathway for the Under-Diagnosed Mild/Lifetime Case (Operationalizing Sunnquist)
**Framework:** A clinical pathway that operationalizes Sunnquist 2015's recognition critique for the mild end: (1) screen for lifetime energy level AND current PEM separately (they are independent axes — P1.1), (2) do NOT use "lifelong fatigue" as an exclusion, (3) use PEM presence as the gate to full ME/CFS assessment, (4) offer low-harm energy-envelope/pacing care to all screened low-energy patients regardless of diagnosis (access), (5) escalate to biomarker/functional workup where PEM is present. This is a recognition/access pathway for the "invisible illness" mild population, not a treatment protocol — deliberately framed to avoid over-broadening diagnosis (see C10.1 guardrail).
**Evidence:** Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55), Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45).
**Certainty:** n/a (framework proposal)
**Falsifiable:** In a clinic pilot, the pathway identifies and appropriately manages a mild/low-energy population that a criteria-gated (lifelong-exclusion) pathway misses, without inflating the false-positive ME/CFS rate against a PEM-confirmed gold standard (see C10.1); if it raises false positives, the recognition is over-broad.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A checklist that treats "tired all your life" and "crash after exertion" as two separate questions could help doctors find mild ME/CFS patients currently slipping through because they were always tired.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 7 — Mathematical Model Extensions

### M7.1: A Reserve-Ceiling ODE in the Existing Energy-Triage Model — PEM as a Reserve-Depletion Threshold
**Mechanism:** The existing energy-triage model (ch30 sec-08) encodes energy expenditure and recovery. Extend it with an explicit *usable reserve ceiling* variable, so that two trajectories converge on the same low ceiling: (a) a chronically-low initial reserve (lifetime constitution) and (b) repeated overactivity that depletes reserve faster than it re-expands (Harvey overshoot). In the model, PEM is a *threshold crossing* — a point where post-exertion recovery becomes incomplete and the ceiling ratchets downward irreversibly. This formalizes P1.2: baseline energy (initial reserve) and premorbid activity (depletion rate) become interchangeable routes to the same fixed low ceiling, and PEM is the state variable that distinguishes a "tired constitution" (ceiling stable) from "disease" (ceiling ratcheting down). The model makes concrete predictions about who crosses the PEM threshold.
**Evidence:** Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48), Harvey2009neurasthenia (0.51), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45); model context ch30 sec-08.
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Falsifiable:** The reserve-ceiling ODE, fitted to energy-envelope data from both low-baseline and overactivity-then-crash patients, reproduces the observed convergence to similar post-onset reserve levels and predicts that PEM onset (the ratchet) is best predicted by a reserve-depletion-threshold variable rather than by baseline energy alone; if baseline energy alone fits the transition better, the ceiling model is unnecessary.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A simple model of a "daily energy budget" that shrinks with overspending could predict when someone tips from just-tired into actually-crashing, from either a small starting budget or repeated overspending.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 8 — Cross-Disease Bridges

### CD8.1: ME/CFS ⇔ Childhood/Adolescent Chronic Fatigue — The Developmental Trajectory Bridge
**Mechanism:** Collin 2015 (ALSPAC, childhood-onset chronic disabling fatigue without PEM) and Smith 1991 (adolescent) show a pediatric low-functioning fatigue phenotype that precedes adulthood. The bridge: if a childhood/developmental low-energy phenotype (in neurodivergent or constitutionally-tired youth) is continuous with adult mild ME/CFS, then pediatric longitudinal cohorts are a natural laboratory for the lifetime→acquired transition question (R2.1). This is a classification-translation bridge: case-finding tools and PEM-emergence monitoring validated in pediatric chronic fatigue could be transported to adult mild cases, addressing the same underdiagnosis/recognition problem earlier in the lifespan.
**Evidence:** Collin2015childFatigue (0.45), Smith1991AdolescentCF (0.30), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45).
**Certainty:** 0.25
**Falsifiable:** A pediatric low-energy phenotype (PEM-absent at baseline) that is followed into adulthood shows a detectable PEM-emergence/ME/CFS-conversion rate that is also detectable with an adult mild-case screen; if the pediatric phenotype does not map to adult mild ME/CFS, the developmental bridge fails.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Because mild fatigue often starts young, tracking tired teens could reveal whether it becomes full ME/CFS in adulthood — and let recognition start decades earlier.
`origin: brainstorm`

### CD8.2: ME/CFS ⇔ Long-COVID "Mild" Underdiagnosis — A Shared Invisible-Illness Recognition Problem
**Mechanism:** Long-COVID shares ME/CFS's problem of the mild/under-recognized end: a large post-infectious fatigue burden where the boundary between "still recovering" and "developed a chronic condition" is fuzzy, and where mild cases may not present with the hallmark symptom until later. Both illnesses may be losing the same population — mild cases whose constitutional "tiredness" is normalized or dismissed. The bridge: a recognition instrument that separately scores baseline-energy constitution and acquired-PEM (the P1.1 two-axis approach) could be validated in one post-infectious population (Long-COVID) and transported to the other (ME/CFS). This is a recognition-translation bridge, distinct from the covered mechanistic LC-ME/CFS bridges.
**Evidence:** Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45); corpus LC-ME/CFS overlap literature.
**Certainty:** 0.20
**Falsifiable:** A two-axis (baseline-energy vs PEM) recognition instrument validated in a Long-COVID cohort transfers to an ME/CFS cohort with comparable sensitivity/specificity for identifying PEM-positive mild cases; if the post-infectious presentations diverge, the instrument needs population-specific adaptation.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The same two-part screen — "are you always tired" plus "do you crash after effort" — could find hidden mild cases in both ME/CFS and Long-COVID, two illnesses that often hide tired people in plain sight.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 9 — Diagnostic/Biomarker Ideas

### D9.1: A Two-Axis Clinical Screen — Baseline Energy Level × PEM Presence (Independent, Not a Single "Fatigue" Score)
**Description:** Operationalize P1.1 as a clinical instrument that scores TWO independent axes: (a) lifetime/current baseline energy level (constitution) and (b) acquired post-exertional malaise (disease signature). The current practice of conflating "fatigue" into one score is precisely what lets mild ME/CFS hide inside "constitutional tiredness" and lets Sunnquist's exclusion misfire. A two-axis screen separates the "tired constitution" (high baseline-impairment, low PEM) from "mild ME/CFS" (high baseline-impairment, high PEM) and from "recovered" (low baseline-impairment). This directly supports the mild-case underdiagnosis angle and is immediately actionable in primary care.
**Evidence:** Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45), Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48).
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Falsifiable:** The two-axis screen, scored independently, identifies PEM-positive mild patients that a single "fatigue severity" score misclassifies, with the PEM axis predicting ME/CFS case-status better than the baseline-energy axis (AUC comparison); if a single fatigue score is as predictive, the two-axis separation adds no value.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A doctor questionnaire that asks "how tired are you all the time" separately from "do you crash after effort" could catch mild ME/CFS that a single tiredness number would miss.
`origin: brainstorm`

### D9.2: Quantify Mild-Case Underdiagnosis — A Severity-Stratified Prevalence/Screening Measure
**Description:** The literature implies mild cases are under-recognized (Sunnquist's exclusion critique) but no study quantifies it. Design a severity-stratified screening prevalence study: apply the two-axis screen (D9.1) + ME/CFS case criteria to a primary-care or community sample, and estimate the fraction of cases in the mild severity band who are undiagnosed, comparing against the diagnosed prevalence. This turns the "invisible illness" claim into a number and gives researchers a baseline for the recognition burden. It is a measurement/methodological contribution, distinct from any mechanistic claim.
**Evidence:** Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45, severity clusters), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45).
**Certainty:** n/a (proposal — no existing prevalence figure)
**Falsifiable:** If mild underdiagnosis is real and driven by normalized constitutional tiredness, the undiagnosed mild fraction exceeds the diagnosed mild fraction by a defined margin, and undiagnosed mild cases cluster among those with lifelong low baseline (the group Sunnquist says was excluded); if mild cases are not disproportionately undiagnosed, the "invisible illness" framing overstates the problem.
**Non-specialist consequence:** This study would put a number on how many mildly-affected people are never diagnosed, showing whether the "invisible illness" story is real or exaggerated.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 10 — Reasons This Mechanism May NOT Be Relevant to ME/CFS

### C10.1: The Mild/Lifetime Phenotype May Be the Normal Tail of Fatigue Variance — Removing the Exclusion Could Over-Broaden ME/CFS
**Mechanism/reason:** Sunnquist 2015 shows "lifelong fatigue" rarely meets a strict operational definition and doesn't discriminate among those it does identify — but that is a *negative* result about the exclusion criterion, not evidence that low-baseline people are ME/CFS. The risk: treating a lifetime low-energy constitution as a "mild ME/CFS" over-broadens the diagnosis to include people who are simply at the low-energy tail of a normal population distribution, or whose tiredness reflects deconditioning, chronic sleep debt, mood disturbance, or lifestyle — none of which is ME/CFS. Harvey 2009's key point is that persistent fatigue in the general population is a *construct distinct from psychiatric disorder* but still not validated as an ME/CFS prodrome; most population fatigue never progresses. For ME/CFS specifically, adding a large, loosely-defined "lifetime mild" category could inflate prevalence, dilute the syndrome's specificity, and drive unnecessary assessment in a population already prone to over-investigation. This is the strongest critical counterweight to the constructive ideas.
**Evidence:** Harvey2009neurasthenia (0.51), Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45).
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Falsifiable:** If broadening recognition is safe, a two-axis screen (D9.1) will NOT increase the false-positive ME/CFS rate against a PEM-confirmed (objective 2-day CPET) gold standard, nor materially inflate diagnosed mild prevalence; if false-positive rates rise, the mild-recognition angle over-reaches.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Loosening "who counts as ME/CFS" to include the always-tired could label many healthy-tired people with a serious illness — trading one problem (missed mild patients) for another (over-diagnosed ones).
`origin: brainstorm`

### C10.2: PEM as Discriminator May Not Hold at the Mild End — Mild Cases May Lack a Detectable PEM Signature
**Mechanism/reason:** The P1.1 discriminator (PEM separates constitution from disease) assumes mild cases HAVE a detectable PEM signature. But Collin 2015 defines childhood chronic disabling fatigue *without* PEM, and Jason 2010's mild cluster may not manifest the reproducible 2-day-CPET crash that moderate/severe cases show. If mild ME/CFS frequently lacks an observable PEM (either because it's below measurement threshold or genuinely absent), then "PEM as the boundary" fails to recognize the mild end it is meant to catch, and the discriminator offers no help for the very population it targets. This undercuts the central constructive thesis: the mild case may be precisely the one where the proposed boundary is invisible.
**Evidence:** Collin2015childFatigue (0.45, no PEM in definition), Jason2010FatigueTypes (0.45, mild cluster), Brown2013EnergyEnvelope (0.48, low-reserve cluster).
**Certainty:** 0.25
**Falsifiable:** If mild ME/CFS cases reliably show a reproducible PEM signal on a sensitive 2-day CPET or PEM screen, the discriminator holds; if a large fraction of clinically-mild ME/CFS shows no measurable PEM even with sensitive testing, then PEM cannot be the operating boundary for the mild end.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The idea that "the crash is what separates real ME/CFS" may be useless for the mildest patients — because their crash may be too small to notice or measure.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 11 — Null-Hypothesis Assessment

### C11.1: Null — Lifetime Low Energy Is Unrelated to ME/CFS; the Real Premorbid Signal Is High Energy
**Assessment:** The null hypothesis: lifelong low-baseline energy is NOT a predisposing trajectory or mild form of ME/CFS — it is an independent, benign population characteristic, and the appearance of a relationship arises from (a) the base-rate of "tiredness" in the general population (Harvey: persistent fatigue is common and mostly non-progressive), and (b) Harvey's finding that the *true* premorbid risk factor is excessive (high) childhood energy, OR 2.63 — the OPPOSITE of low baseline. Under this null, the low-baseline premise is a red herring: what predisposes is overactivity/emotional-instability (already partly in `prodromal-syndrome`), not constitutional tiredness. The papers that would need revision if the null holds: the low-baseline framing would be weakened (only the case-definition critique survives), Harvey 2009 would be elevated to the primary premorbid model, and the "mild constitutional trajectory" becomes a recognition gap rather than a predisposing biology. The `spec-mild-constitutional-energy-spectrum` (Phase 3) would need re-reading as a *recognition* statement, not a *predisposition* statement.
**Evidence:** Harvey2009neurasthenia (0.51), Sunnquist2015lifelongFatigue (0.55, supports recognition not predisposition), Collin2015childFatigue (0.45).
**Certainty:** 0.30

## Category 12 — Evidence Quality Concerns

### C12.1: Cohort Overlap, No Longitudinal Transition Data, and Self-Reported Severity Limit Every Mild-End Claim
**Assessment:** Several structural limits cap the strength of all mild/lifetime conclusions. (1) **Cohort overlap:** 3/6 Phase-1 papers (Sunnquist 2015, Jason 2010, Brown 2013) are the DePaul University/Jason group — treated as 1 independent source, so the "mild cluster / low-reserve" evidence is effectively single-institution. (2) **No longitudinal transition data:** no paper demonstrates mild/lifetime → moderate/severe progression; the central question is entirely open, so every constructive idea (P1.1–P1.3, R2.1) rests on inference, not measurement. (3) **General-population certainty discounts:** Harvey (0.75) and Collin (0.75) are general-population weights — their fatigue findings may not hold in a chronic-illness context; and Collin's phenotype is defined without PEM, so its continuity with ME/CFS is assumed, not shown. (4) **Self-reported severity:** Jason/Brown severity clusters rest on self-report (DePaul Symptom Questionnaire / energy-envelope self-monitoring), not objective functional measures, so the "mild" band is not anchored to objective capacity. (5) **Selection:** Mild cases, by definition the target, are least likely to be captured in ME/CFS clinic cohorts (they're less likely to seek care), so cohort-based mild prevalence is downward-biased by design. These limits mean the mild/lifetime-recognition angle should integrate as `#speculation`/`#open-question`/`#clinical-finding` (recognition), NOT as a high-certainty predisposing `#hypothesis-box` — consistent with the Phase 2 conservative caps.
**Evidence:** All six topic papers; population weights per Phase 1.
**Certainty:** n/a (methodological assessment)
**Falsifiable:** (applicability check rather than prediction) The mild-end claims gain high-certainty support only if a prospective, longitudinal, non-DePaul cohort (R2.1/R2.2) with objective functional/CPET measures replicates the transition and discriminator findings; until then they remain inference on overlapping, self-reported, non-longitudinal data.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Much of the "mild tired people become ME/CFS" case comes from a single research group, self-reported tiredness, and no study that actually followed people over time — so it is a promising idea, not an established fact.
