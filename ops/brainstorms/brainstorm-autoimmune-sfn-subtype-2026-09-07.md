## Category 1 — Novel hypotheses (mechanistic, not yet in literature)

### Idea 1.1: Anti-FGFR3 IgG targets a *cytosolic* epitope — the "cellular-access" paradox reframes what the antibody actually marks (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.20
**Rationale:** Anti-FGFR3 is reported as IgG against the *intracellular* domain of
FGFR3 (Antoine2015FGFR3). A circulating IgG cannot ordinarily reach a cytosolic/
membrane-intracellular epitope of a live neuron. If the antibody is genuinely
pathogenic (Salih2026FGFR3Pathogenic), it either (a) requires a breach — prior
ischemic/inflammatory/ISR-DRG membrane damage exposing an intracellular epitope —
or (b) is an *epiphenomenal marker of prior cell injury*, not a driver. This
untested distinction matters: it would determine whether seropositivity reflects an
active autoimmune process or a scar of earlier DRG damage (cf. the ISR-DRG mechanism
already in ch09, @hyp:drg-sfn-igg-mecfs adjacent). The intracellular-epitope paradox
is largely unaddressed in the supportive lineage and is a genuine mechanistic gap.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If FGFR3-IgG is pathogenic-requiring-access, titres
should correlate with markers of DRG membrane damage/injury and with the ISR-DRG
member; if it is a pure injury-scar marker, then experimental sera (Salih) should
lose pathogenicity when epitope access is blocked, and healthy controls with prior
neuronal injury should show the antibody. Falsified if FGFR3-IgG shows no
association with any neuronal-injury correlate.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Today none — it tests whether a positive antibody
blood test means "your nerves are under autoimmune attack" or "your nerves were
damaged earlier," a distinction that changes what the test can mean.

### Idea 1.2: Anti-FGFR3 *neuronal* positivity (IgG at DRG) may belong to the established IgG-DRG passive-transfer axis, not a separate FGFR3-specific disease (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.18
**Rationale:** ch09 already has direct passive-transfer support that IgG from
post-infectious (long-COVID/fibromyalgia) cohorts accumulates at DRG and sensitises
nociceptors (@hyp:drg-sfn-igg-mecfs; Mignolet2026passiveTransferLC,
Goebel2021passiveTransferFM). Salih2026FGFR3Pathogenic shows FGFR3-IgG binds DRG
neurons in mice. A novel, non-conflating hypothesis: part of what the FGFR3 assay
captures is *generic DRG-reactive IgG* (the passive-transfer cargo), and FGFR3 is one
epitope among several rather than a subtype-defining antigen. If so, "anti-FGFR3
subtype" is a narrower readout of a broader IgG-DRG phenomenon already in the paper,
not a distinct entity — and the negative IVIG RCTs (Gibbons2023) are explained by
measuring the wrong (epitope-specific) marker while the effector cargo is broader.
This is testable and would reconcile the supportive lineage with the null RCTs
without asserting a validated biomarker.
**Falsifiable prediction:** IgG-depleted and FGFR3-depleted fractions of the same
patient sera retain differential DRG-binding in passive transfer; if FGFR3-specific
depletion does NOT remove DRG sensitisation while total-IgG depletion does, then
FGFR3 is a passenger epitope, not the effector. Falsified if FGFR3-depleted sera lose
DRG pathogenicity.
**Non-specialist consequence:** None today — it tests whether the antibody test names
a unique disease or just one flavour of a broader immune effect on nerve-cell bodies.

### Idea 1.3: TS-HDS (IgM, anti-heparan-sulfate-like) may mark a *glycocalyx/endoneurial-microenvironment* perturbation, not a neuron-specific autoimmune target (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.15
**Rationale:** TS-HDS (trisulfated heparin disaccharide) is a heparan-sulfate-like
epitope; anti-TS-HDS is IgM. Rather than a neuronal-cell-body antigen, TS-HDS
reactivity may reflect immune recognition of altered heparan-sulfate in the
perineurial/vascular glycocalyx or of immune-complexed glycosaminoglycans — a
*connective-tissue/microvascular* perturbation rather than a specific neuropathy.
This is mechanistically plausible given heparan-sulfate's role in barrier and
endoneurial homeostasis, and it would explain why anti-TS-HDS seropositivity does NOT
track objective small-fiber damage (Trevino2021TSHDSFGFR3, Murin2024SFNsero: normal
biopsy in many seropositive) — the antibody may track a non-neuronal compartment.
Distinct from the DRG-soma IgG axis; unsupported by any direct experiment, so low
certainty.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If TS-HDS reactivity reflects glycocalyx/microvascular
status, anti-TS-HDS titre should correlate with endothelial/glycocalyx or
microvascular dysfunction markers and NOT with IENFD or nociceptor function, and
should not bind DRG neurons in passive transfer. Falsified if anti-TS-HDS sera
produce DRG-neuron binding/hypersensitivity in transfer.
**Non-specialist consequence:** None today — it asks whether this antibody reflects
nerve damage or a broader vessel/tissue-surface change, which changes how a positive
result is interpreted.

## Category 2 — Research directions (resolve key uncertainties)

### Idea 2.1: A proper ME/CFS-cohort serostudy of anti-TS-HDS/FGFR3, pre-registered, with healthy + fibromyalgia + IENFD-stratified controls and blinded multi-lab assay (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a (the definitive missing experiment)
**Rationale:** The single most decisive gap is structural: **no ME/CFS-cohort study of
these antibodies exists anywhere**. Every claim of ME/CFS relevance is extrapolation
from cryptogenic-SFN (Levine2020TSHDSFGFR3, Tholance2020FGFR3) or fibromyalgia
proxies. The discriminating design must fix the three failure modes the challenge
literature exposes: (1) an unvalidated assay → run samples in ≥2 labs using the
Appeltshauser2025FGFR3assay concordance protocol; (2) no specificity arm → include
IENFD-confirmed and IENFD-normal ME/CFS, plus fibromyalgia and healthy controls (the
fibromyalgia nulls Malik2019TSHDSfibromyalgia, Nissan2023TSHDSfibromyalgia require
replication before any ME/CFS signal is credible); (3) referral/selection bias →
recruit from a general ME/CFS cohort, NOT from an antibody-testing centre. Pre-register
cut-offs and analysis; power for a low-prevalence subtype. This is the study Phase-3
text already names as the missing step; it must be methodologically clean to be worth
running.
**Discriminating test:** Does anti-TS-HDS/FGFR3 prevalence differ between ME/CFS and
(blinded) fibromyalgia + healthy controls, above assay noise, when samples are split
across two independent labs? A null result here would be decisive against the
ME/CFS-subtype claim; a positive would resurrect it under rigorous conditions.
**Non-specialist consequence:** None for patients today — it is the research study
that would tell us whether this blood test has any real meaning in ME/CFS at all.

### Idea 2.2: Antibody–antigen characterisation: bind the DRG target, confirm epitope identity and cellular access, and test FGFR3-dependency in a neutral (non-lineage) lab (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a
**Rationale:** The mechanistic claims rest on Salih2026FGFR3Pathogenic (FGFR3-Ab
binds DRG, CRISPR-knockout blocks it), which shares lineage/interest with the FGFR3
discoverers (Antoine/Tholance/Camdessanché + Moutal/Kafaie). No independent,
non-lineage lab has replicated the DRG-binding mechanism or confirmed that the
serum antibody's target is truly FGFR3 (versus a cross-reacting surface antigen; the
cytosolic-epitope paradox in Idea 1.1 makes identity unverified). Research direction:
(a) mass-spec / peptide-array to confirm the precise epitope; (b) passive transfer of
affinity-purified patient anti-FGFR3 in a *neutral* lab to test whether the phenotype
is FGFR3-specific or DRG-generic (feeds Idea 1.2); (c) test whether intracellular
antigen is even reachable (Idea 1.1). Resolution of the single-lineage asymmetry is
the epistemic crux of the whole topic.
**Discriminating test:** Do FGFR3-affinity-purified antibodies from a separate,
independently-recruited cohort reproduce the DRG-hypersensitivity phenotype, and is
that phenotype abolished by FGFR3 knockout/blockade — in a lab with no financial or
authorship tie to the discoverers?
**Non-specialist consequence:** None today — it verifies the science under
independent eyes before anyone should trust the antibody test's mechanism.

### Idea 2.3: Reproducibility study of the commercial assay in the very population it would be used on, tied to a diagnostic-utility (not prevalence) endpoint (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a
**Rationale:** Appeltshauser2025FGFR3assay already shows only 81% inter-lab
concordance and lab-specific cut-offs; Chompoopong2023TSHDS shows 34% of real-world
antibody-positive patients lack neuropathy and no immunotherapy-response prediction.
A research direction distinct from 2.1's prevalence question is a *clinical-utility*
reproducibility study: in consecutive ME/CFS + fibromyalgia patients presenting for
SFN evaluation, does the commercial TS-HDS/FGFR3 result (a) replicate across labs,
(b) add discrimination over clinical exam + IENFD alone, and (c) predict any outcome?
This directly measures the patient-protection concern (that a poorly-specific test
misleads clinicians into a false "treatable autoimmune subtype" narrative) — the
Phase-2 checklist #6 clinical-hazard. It answers "does ordering this test change
anything correct" rather than "is the antibody present."
**Discriminating test:** Net reclassification: does adding serology change diagnosis
or management correctly in ≥X% of patients, or does it merely re-label the same
patients (Chompoopong-style: no response prediction)?
**Non-specialist consequence:** This tells a doctor whether ordering this blood test
in an ME/CFS/fibromyalgia patient helps or simply adds a misleading label — directly
protecting patients from over-testing and false "treatable subtype" messages.

## Category 10 — Reasons this mechanism may NOT be relevant to ME/CFS (MANDATORY)

### Idea 10.1: The whole "subtype" may be a testing-lineage artefact — one Franco-US laboratory method, not an independent biological finding (origin: brainstorm)
**Certainty:** 0.45 (that the subtype signal is substantially lineage-confounded)
**Rationale:** All supportive association/phenotype/mechanism papers share ONE
testing lineage: the WashU/Phoenix/Saint-Louis sensory-neuropathy panel (Levine,
Zeidman, Malik/Pestronk, and co-author Kafaie) plus the Saint-Etienne/Lyon FGFR3
group (Antoine, Tholance, Moritz, Camdessanché, and Moutal/Salih). Independent centres
(Mayo Chompoopong, Brigham Trevino, BIDMC Gibbons, Maastricht Geerts, Singapore Ng,
Bologna Morelli, Sheba Nissan, Toronto Katzberg) produce only nulls and validity
challenges. When a "subtype" is defined by the antibodies only one lab measures well,
the most parsimonious explanation is a shared assay/reagent/referral-pool artefact
rather than a real ME/CFS disease subtype. This is the single strongest reason to
doubt ME/CFS relevance and caps every supportive reading of the topic.
**Evidence to disprove:** An independent, non-lineage lab, using a different assay,
replicates TS-HDS/FGFR3 elevation in an unselected ME/CFS cohort over healthy +
fibromyalgia controls.
**Non-specialist consequence:** It may be that the "autoimmune subtype" exists mainly
inside one laboratory's test, not in patients — so patients should not be told they
have a defined treatable subtype on this evidence.

### Idea 10.2: Fibromyalgia overlap explains any apparent ME/CFS signal — and the fibromyalgia proxies are null or reversed (origin: brainstorm)
**Certainty:** 0.40 (that fibromyalgia overlap fully accounts for the absence of a
distinct ME/CFS signal)
**Rationale:** ME/CFS and fibromyalgia overlap heavily; fibromyalgia is the closest
proxy population. The two fibromyalgia studies are the most ME/CFS-relevant negatives
available and are both null: Malik2019TSHDSfibromyalgia (p=0.41 vs controls) and
Nissan2023TSHDSfibromyalgia (anti-TS-HDS IgM LOWER than healthy controls, p=0.013).
If the antibody does not discriminate in the population most similar to ME/CFS —
indeed runs in the reverse direction in one study — then any "ME/CFS subtype" claim
that borrows from general-SFN prevalence is on weak footing. Either the antibody is
not ME/CFS-relevant, or ME/CFS must be specifically different from fibromyalgia in
this marker, which no study shows.
**Evidence to disprove:** A ME/CFS cohort shows TS-HDS/FGFR3 elevation that (a)
survives controlling for fibromyalgia comorbidity and (b) separates ME/CFS-with-SFN
from fibromyalgia-with-pain.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Because ME/CFS and fibromyalgia overlap so much and
the antibody does not separate fibromyalgia from healthy people, it may not separate
ME/CFS either — the test may carry little meaning here.

### Idea 10.3: Healthy-control reactivity may be high — the antibodies may be common non-pathogenic bystanders, not a disease marker (origin: brainstorm)
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Rationale:** Nissan2023TSHDSfibromyalgia found anti-TS-HDS LOWER in patients than in
healthy controls — implying healthy people carry the antibody at comparable-or-higher
levels. If healthy controls are frequently seropositive, then the antibody is not
specific to any disease and its "association" in cryptogenic SFN (Levine2020) may
reflect low background discrimination rather than a real subtype. Combined with
Trevino2021TSHDSFGFR3 (antibodies present regardless of normal/abnormal biopsy) and
Murin2024SFNsero (normal biopsy in many seropositive), the markers appear not to track
objective small-fiber pathology — weakening any claim they define a neuropathic
disease relevant to ME/CFS.
**Evidence to disprove:** Titres in ME/CFS exceed a healthy-control seroprevalence
baseline by a clinically meaningful margin with assay-validated cut-offs.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Many healthy people may carry this antibody, so a
positive test may not mean the person has a nerve disease at all.

### Idea 10.4: The antigenic target is not accessible or not specific — FGFR3's cytosolic epitope and TS-HDS's glycosaminoglycan-like target argue against a clean neuronal-autoimmune disease (origin: brainstorm)
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Rationale:** Anti-FGFR3 targets an *intracellular* FGFR3 domain (Antoine2015) —
physiologically hard for a pathogenic circulating IgG to reach (Idea 1.1). Anti-TS-HDS
targets a heparan-sulfate-like disaccharide, not a neuron-specific antigen (Idea 1.3).
Neither target fits the clean picture of a surface receptor autoantibody causing
neuronal dysfunction the way the passive-transfer DRG-IgG axis does. If the targets
are non-specific (glycocalyx, injury debris, or low-accessibility epitopes), the
"autoimmune SFN subtype" is a mischaracterisation, and the mechanism is NOT a
distinct neuronal autoimmune disease in ME/CFS.
**Evidence to disprove:** Demonstration that patient antibodies bind live, intact DRG
neurons at a surface (accessible) FGFR3 epitope and cause dysfunction in a way that
is FGFR3-blockade-sensitive, in an independent lab.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The antibody may be hitting a generic or internal
molecule rather than a specific nerve-surface target, which would make "autoimmune
nerve disease" the wrong label.

### Idea 10.5: If a large, IVIG-responsive autoimmune-SFN ME/CFS subtype existed, the controlled trials and real-world data would likely have surfaced it (origin: brainstorm)
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Rationale:** IVIG has been trialled in antibody-positive SFN (Gibbons2023IVIGpilot,
double-blind, negative) and idiopathic painful SFN (Geerts2021IVIG, Class I, negative);
Ng2026IVIGsystrev rates the whole IVIG-for-SFN evidence GRADE low-to-very-low;
Chompoopong2023 found immunotherapy no better in seropositive than seronegative
(p=0.30). If a clinically meaningful, antibody-defined, treatment-responsive ME/CFS
subtype were common, these controlled data — which included antibody-positive
patients — would plausibly have shown it. The absence across a double-blind RCT, a
Class-I RCT, real-world Mayo data, and a systematic review is substantial negative
evidence that antibody-guided IVIG does not define a real treatment-relevant ME/CFS
subtype.
**Evidence to disprove:** An adequately powered, blinded RCT in ME/CFS stratified by
validated serostatus shows IVIG benefit confined to seropositive patients — the
design every existing trial was too small or too unselected to provide.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If a common treatable antibody-defined ME/CFS subtype
existed, well-run trials would likely have already shown IVIG helping it; they did
not, so patients should not expect antibody-guided IVIG to help.

## Category 11 — Null hypothesis assessment (MANDATORY)

### Idea 11.1: Null hypothesis — the TS-HDS/FGFR3 serological subtype has no role in ME/CFS; seropositivity is lineage artefact, fibromyalgia-overlap noise, or non-pathogenic bystander reactivity (origin: brainstorm)
**Certainty:** 0.45 (that the null is true)
**Rationale:** The most parsimonious null combines 10.1–10.5: (a) supportive evidence
comes from one Franco-US testing lineage and is not independently replicated;
(b) the closest ME/CFS-proxy population (fibromyalgia) shows null-or-reversed
serology; (c) healthy-control reactivity and lack of tracking with objective small-
fiber damage (Trevino, Murin) suggest non-specific bystander antibody; (d) controlled
IVIG trials and real-world utility data are negative. Under this null: no ME/CFS-
specific TS-HDS/FGFR3 subtype exists; the antibodies neither define a neuropathic
subset nor select IVIG responders; and the correct chapter treatment is the 
patient-protective limitation already integrated (`@lim:autoimmune-sfn-serology-not-validated`).
**What existing claims would need revision if the null holds:** The legacy
`new-contents-sfn` stub's inherited 0.65 "distinct treatable subtype" is already
corrected to ~0.25–0.35 open-question/limitation framing (Phase 3) — the null is
consistent with, and reinforces, that downward correction. If the null were instead
*falsified* (2.1/2.2 positive), the open-question would need elevation and a
hypothesis-box/subtype claim would become warranted.
**Evidence to reject the null:** Exactly the clean ME/CFS serostudy (2.1) + neutral
lab antibody–antigen reproduction (2.2) this backlog proposes. Absent both, the null
stands and the topic stays a documented-but-unvalidated hypothesis.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The default, best-supported view is that this
antibody test does not define a real ME/CFS subtype — patients should not be
diagnosed or treated on it until far stronger evidence appears.

### Idea 11.2: Minimum design required to reject the null (origin: brainstorm)
**Certainty:** n/a (methodological)
**Rationale:** Because cross-sectional serology in proxy populations is already
null-or-reversed, only a design that simultaneously fixes assay standardisation,
population relevance, and single-lineage independence can reject the null. Required
elements: (a) pre-registered ME/CFS cohort; (b) samples split across ≥2 independent
labs per the Appeltshauser2025 concordance protocol; (c) matched fibromyalgia + healthy
+IENFD-stratified controls; (d) analysis controlling for fibromyalgia comorbidity;
(e) a neutral-lab mechanistic arm (2.2) confirming the DRG target is genuinely FGFR3
and accessible. Rejecting the null requires TS-HDS/FGFR3 to separate ME/CFS-with-SFN
from fibromyalgia and healthy controls above assay noise — a bar no current study
meets. Absent that, the serological subtype stays at open-question/speculation and the
only defensible clinical content is the existing patient-protective limitation.
**Non-specialist consequence:** None — it defines the high bar of evidence that would
have to be met before this blood test could be taken seriously in ME/CFS.

## Category 12 — Evidence quality concerns (MANDATORY)

### Idea 12.1: Referral and selection bias — supportive cohorts were recruited through antibody-testing centres, inflating apparent prevalence and association (origin: brainstorm)
**Rationale:** Levine2020TSHDSFGFR3, Zeidman2022IVIG, and the WashU/Phoenix panel
recruit through centres that *run the antibody test*; seropositivity thus reflects
referral of patients already suspected of autoimmune SFN (pre-test enrichment), not a
population prevalence. This positive-selection bias can inflate the "48% seropositive"
(Levine) figure and the apparent association — the very selection effect that the
independent real-world Mayo cohort (Chompoopong2023, unselected, 34% no neuropathy)
and fibromyalgia nulls (Malik, Nissan) partially expose. Any prevalence/association
claim drawn from antibody-centre cohorts overstates the subtype's relevance to ME/CFS.
This is a selection-bias concern the certainty scores do not fully capture.

### Idea 12.2: Assay validity — no standardised anti-FGFR3 or anti-TS-HDS protocol; inter-lab concordance only 81%; results are lab-specific (origin: brainstorm)
**Rationale:** Appeltshauser2025FGFR3assay found lab-specific cut-offs (OD median 0.076
vs 0.293) and 81% concordance (kappa 0.61) — not good enough for a reproducible
biomarker. Nissan2023 used the CellTrend assay, a different platform from the
WashU/Saint-Louis panel; cross-platform comparability is unknown. Every
cross-sectional prevalence number in the supportive lineage rests on an unvalidated,
platform-dependent measurement. Certainty scores assigned to individual papers cannot
reflect that the *underlying measure* is not standardised — a validity threat that
undermines even well-designed supportive studies and should cap any claim built on
seropositivity prevalence.

### Idea 12.3: Cohort overlap / single-lineage problem — supportive papers are not independent replications (origin: brainstorm)
**Rationale:** The supportive papers are NOT six independent replications. Levine,
Zeidman, and Malik share the WashU/Saint-Louis sensory panel; Salih/Moutal and Kafaie
overlap both the WashU/Phoenix lineage and the Saint-Etienne/Lyon FGFR3 group
(Antoine/Camdessanché/Tholance/Moritz) who co-author the discovery AND the
reproducibility study. Counting Levine/Zeidman/Malik/Salih/Tholance/Antoine as
separate confirmations would multiply-count a single methodology and referral pool.
The genuinely independent evidence (Mayo, Brigham, BIDMC, Maastricht, Singapore,
Bologna, Sheba, Toronto) is uniformly null/challenge. This asymmetry — not any single
study's flaw — is the decisive evidence-quality issue and must prevent the supportive
cluster from being weighed as multiple independent studies.

### Idea 12.4: Diagnostic-era and IENFD-standard drift — serology studies span 2015–2026 across changing SFN diagnostic criteria (origin: brainstorm)
**Rationale:** The supportive cohort definitions span Antoine2015 (discovery, sensory
neuropathy) to Murin2024 (TS-HDS/FGFR3 phenotypes) across a period when SFN diagnostic
standards and IENFD normative data were evolving. Several studies used normal IENFD in
many seropositive patients (Trevino2021, Murin2024) — meaning "seropositive SFN" may
include patients who would not meet current small-fiber criteria, contaminating the
phenotype. Cross-era and cross-centre IENFD normative differences make the "subtype's"
denominator unstable. Combined with the absence of any ME/CFS-standardised cohort, this
limits generalisability of prevalence and phenotype claims to a modern ME/CFS
population.

### Idea 12.5: The primary mechanism paper (Salih2026) is single-lineage, shared-interest, and mouse-only — the DRG-pathogenicity claim is not independently replicated (origin: brainstorm)
**Rationale:** Salih2026FGFR3Pathogenic — the load-bearing evidence that FGFR3-IgG is
pathogenic at DRG — is (a) authored by the Moutal/Kafaie group overlapping the FGFR3
discoverer lineage, (b) explicitly shares patents/interest with the discoverers
(noted in the literature summary), and (c) is mouse/preclinical only. Its conclusion
that FGFR3-Ab binds DRG and drives hypersensitivity (blocked by CRISPR knockout)
therefore carries both a single-lineage replication risk and a translation gap to
human ME/CFS. It cannot stand alone as the mechanistic anchor for a human ME/CFS
subtype; it requires neutral-lab replication (2.2) before it supports any claim about
FGFR3 as the effector target in patients.
