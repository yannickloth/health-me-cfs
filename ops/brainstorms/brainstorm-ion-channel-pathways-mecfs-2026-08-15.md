## Ranked ideas

### Rank 1 — Cat 1 — Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase unification (muscular + neurogenic)
- **Rationale:** The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase maintains resting membrane potential in ALL excitable cells — muscle fibre AND motor-nerve terminal. Wirth's muscular model (pump failure → sarcolemmal depolarization) and the neurogenic model (nerve-terminal depolarization) may be ONE pump failure in two adjacent cell types. Reframes the dichotomy.
- **Evidence:** @WirthSteinacker2025fasciculations (muscular), @Czesnik2015HCN (neurogenic), @Petter2022sodium (muscle Na+), AIMM cascade.
- **Certainty:** 0.15 (mechanistically coherent, unmeasured).
- **Falsifiable:** Paired sarcolemmal + motor-axonal excitability in same limb shows correlated abnormalities.
- **Consequence:** If one shared pump failure explains both nerve and muscle cramping, clinicians need not choose between two rival explanations.

### Rank 2 — Cat 1 — TRPM3 sensory-neuron channelopathy as ME/CFS-specific neurogenic bridge
- **Rationale:** ME/CFS has its own ion-channel finding (TRPM3, replicated); TRPM3 in DRG nociceptors could dysregulate sensory gating of the spinal cramp reflex — an ME/CFS-specific neurogenic route, not borrowed from cramp-fasciculation syndrome.
- **Evidence:** @hyp:trpm3-root, @Craighead2017TRPcramp (sensory TRP gates cramp threshold).
- **Certainty:** 0.20.
- **Falsifiable:** (a) DRG TRPM3 current reduced in ME/CFS; (b) TRPM3 agonists shift cramp threshold.
- **Consequence:** The ion-channel defect in ME/CFS immune cells may also explain cramps, unifying two currently-unrelated findings.

### Rank 3 — Cat 12 — "Excludes neuronal cause" is an assertion, not a measurement
- **Rationale:** Wirth's muscular model (cert 0.35, review) asserts rather than measures the exclusion of a neuronal cause. Neither side has ME/CFS-specific electrophysiology.
- **Evidence:** @WirthSteinacker2025fasciculations (sole ME/CFS-specific source, cert 0.35).
- **Certainty:** 0.85 (confidence that the question is untested).
- **Falsifiable:** Refuted by finding a published ME/CFS axonal-excitability or membrane-potential study. None exists.
- **Consequence:** Both competing explanations are opinions, not measurements — neither should be stated as settled.

### Rank 4 — Cat 10 — Neurogenic default does not transfer (population mismatch)
- **Rationale:** The neurogenic pillars (Czesnik benign CFS, Maughan exercise, Craighead exercise) are not spontaneous chronic-disease cramps. ME/CFS has local muscle pathology available — more parsimonious.
- **Evidence:** @Czesnik2015HCN, @Maughan2019MuscleCramping, @Craighead2017TRPcramp; muscle pathology corpus (ch20, ch07).
- **Certainty:** 0.60 (argument for non-relevance).
- **Falsifiable:** Fails if ME/CFS axonal-excitability study shows the Czesnik HCN signature.
- **Consequence:** "Cramps are usually nerve-driven" comes from athletes and a rare benign condition — may not apply to ME/CFS.

### Rank 5 — Cat 2 — Paired threshold-tracking + ²³Na-MRI decisive experiment
- **Rationale:** Run Czesnik's threshold-tracking (HCN readout) + Petter's ²³Na-MRI (sarcolemmal readout) in the SAME cramp-stratified cohort. Directly answers the open-question.
- **Evidence:** @Czesnik2015HCN, @Petter2022sodium (both methods validated).
- **Certainty:** n/a (proposal); resolution certainty ~0.80.
- **Falsifiable:** Null on both axes falsifies both models, forcing a third mechanism.
- **Consequence:** One study reusing two existing tests could settle nerve-vs-muscle cramp origin.

### Rank 6 — Cat 1 — Reduced cortical excitability compatible via disinhibition
- **Rationale:** Reduced TMS motor-cortical excitability (@Starr2000TMS) may cause spinal DISINHIBITION (less drive to inhibitory interneurons), not less excitation — compatible with neurogenic cramps. Softens the "argues against" framing.
- **Evidence:** @Starr2000TMS, @Maughan2019MuscleCramping ("sustained abnormal spinal reflex activity"), @Davey2003TMS.
- **Certainty:** 0.20.
- **Falsifiable:** Paired TMS + H-reflex shows reduced cortical excitability co-occurring with reduced spinal inhibition.
- **Consequence:** Reduced motor-cortex activity does not rule out nerve-based cramps — they can coexist.

### Rank 7 — Cat 10 — SFN-subtype mismatch weakens the neurogenic bridge
- **Rationale:** Lopate's SFN→cramp link is length-dependent somatic SFN; ME/CFS SFN is mostly non-length-dependent/autonomic. The cramp-generating subtype may not be the one present.
- **Evidence:** @Lopate2013CrampsSFN, @Azcue2023sfn (34% non-length-dependent).
- **Certainty:** 0.45.
- **Falsifiable:** If non-length-dependent SFN patients also report cramps, mismatch weakens.
- **Consequence:** ME/CFS small-fiber damage may not be the kind that causes cramps.

### Rank 8 — Cat 2 — Cramp-phenotype mapping as free discriminator
- **Rationale:** Neurogenic cramps (distal, sporadic, neuropathic accompaniments) vs muscular (proximal, exertional, "difficulty releasing grip") predict different mechanisms; test against IENFD + ²³Na-MRI.
- **Evidence:** @Lopate2013CrampsSFN, @Petter2022sodium, ch04 cramp symptom list.
- **Certainty:** 0.35.
- **Falsifiable:** Phenotype fails to segregate with the two biomarkers.
- **Consequence:** Clinical history of where/when cramps happen may predict mechanism, sparing testing.

### Rank 9 — Cat 11 — Null: symptom subsumed by AIMM; downgrade speculation
- **Rationale:** Under the null, cramps are fully explained by the muscular/metabolic cascade. Consequences: reframe the 0.30 neurogenic speculation as minority alternative; no nerve-targeted approach warranted.
- **Evidence:** AIMM/sodium corpus, @Petter2022sodium, @WirthSteinacker2025fasciculations, @Garrison2020MagnesiumCramps.
- **Certainty:** prior probability of null ~0.55.
- **Falsifiable:** Null falsified by finding peripheral axonal hyperexcitability in ME/CFS cramp patients.
- **Consequence:** If nerve-based cramps ruled out, treatment focus returns to muscle-energy/ion pathway.
