# Brainstorm: Social Energy Cost — Aftermath Ideas

**Topic:** Social energy cost in ME/CFS — psychosocial consequences of energy depletion
**Date:** 2026-08-08
**Status:** Phase 4 — Creative brainstorming (agent unavailable, main session)

## Category 1 — Novel Hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Certainty | Rationale |
|---|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | Social PEM dose-response curve: the energy cost of social interaction follows a predictable function of (number of participants × interaction duration × emotional intensity). Mapable in theory. | 0.25 | Implied by the existing evidence but never formalized |
| 2 | Pre-diagnosis social attrition is a prodromal biomarker: the rate of friendship/relationship loss in the 2-3 years before formal ME/CFS diagnosis is elevated relative to other chronic illnesses. | 0.20 | Clinical observation; testable via retrospective survey |
| 3 | Emotional synchrony cost is dopamine-dependent: the DLPFC mismatch cost (Kuhn 2011) is modulated by tonic dopamine levels; patients with higher residual dopamine (less depleted) can sustain synchrony longer. | 0.25 | Theory extension of Salamone/Treadway effort-reward framework |

## Category 2 — Research Directions

| # | Direction | Certainty | Rationale |
|---|----------|-----------|-----------|
| 4 | Emotional contagion task in ME/CFS: administer the Tamm 2020 emotional contagion paradigm (happy/angry face viewing + EMG + fMRI) to ME/CFS patients vs controls. Would be the first direct test of emotional synchrony in ME/CFS. | 0.40 | Directly addresses the identified research gap |
| 5 | Social-cognition network resting-state fMRI in ME/CFS: seed-based connectivity of mPFC, TPJ, IFG in ME/CFS vs controls. Predict reduced connectivity correlated with social withdrawal severity. | 0.35 | BenSimon 2022 shows these regions deactivate under sleep loss; no ME/CFS study exists |
| 6 | Conservation-withdrawal validated questionnaire for ME/CFS: adapt Ironside 1980 criteria into a patient-reported outcome measure. Test correlation with PEM severity, energy envelope, and social functioning scores. | 0.30 | Would operationalize a framework currently purely conceptual |

## Category 5 — Diagnostic/Biomarker

| # | Biomarker | Certainty | Rationale |
|---|----------|-----------|-----------|
| 9 | Social exhaustion scale: a validated patient-reported outcome measuring "interpersonal metabolic cost" — how many minutes of conversation before crash, how many people in room before overload, etc. Would distinguish ME/CFS from depression (where social withdrawal is amotivational, not metabolic). | 0.30 | Direct clinical need; no instrument exists |

## Category 10 — Risk/Protective Factors

| # | Factor | Certainty | Rationale |
|---|--------|-----------|-----------|
| 10 | Pre-existing social network size as protective factor: patients with larger, more distributed social networks before onset may retain more low-cost relationships post-onset because they can selectively maintain the lowest-demand subset while losing the rest. | 0.20 | Extrapolation from energy gravitation logic |

## Category 11 — Patient Self-Management

| # | Strategy | Certainty | Rationale |
|---|---------|-----------|-----------|
| 11 | Asynchronous-only social policy during PEM windows: restrict communication to asynchronous channels (text, email, recorded voice messages) that allow response at the patient's pace rather than demanding real-time synchrony. | 0.30 | Practical, no research data |
| 12 | Social energy budgeting: treat social interaction as a budget line item in pacing — allocate a fixed number of "social minutes" per day with explicit recovery time after, same as physical exertion budgeting. | 0.25 | Logical extension of pacing theory |

## Category 12 — Controversies/Debates

| # | Controversy | Certainty | Rationale |
|---|------------|-----------|-----------|
| 13 | Is social withdrawal in ME/CFS fully explained by energy depletion, or is learned avoidance/stigma a significant independent contributor? The energy model predicts that restoring ATP would restore social engagement; the stigma model predicts residual withdrawal even after energy normalization. Currently untestable (no energy-restoring treatment exists). | 0.15 | Both mechanisms likely contribute; disentangling them is a genuine scientific challenge |
