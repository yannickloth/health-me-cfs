## Category 1 — Novel Hypotheses

### 1a. KCC2/NKCC1 Expression Ratio as a Molecular Set-Point for Individual Brain Fog Susceptibility

**Mechanism.** Alfonsa et al.\ (2023, Nat Neurosci) demonstrated that intracellular chloride accumulation — governed by the KCC2 (chloride extrusion) / NKCC1 (chloride import) transporter ratio — is the molecular driver of local sleep pressure: the longer a cortical region is active, the more chloride accumulates, and the lower its KCC2/NKCC1 ratio, the faster it tips into slow-wave oscillations. ME/CFS involves chronic cortical microglial activation (Section @sec:ch15-microglia), and neuroinflammation is a well-established KCC2 downregulator (Coull et al.\ 2003, Nature — microglial BDNF via TrkB downregulates KCC2 in dorsal horn neurons; Ferrini & De Koninck 2013 — cytokine-mediated KCC2 suppression). If chronic neuroinflammation in ME/CFS downregulates cortical KCC2, patients would have a constitutively lowered chloride-extrusion capacity — making their cortical columns hypersensitive to local sleep induction at lower cognitive loads than healthy individuals. The KCC2/NKCC1 ratio in CSF neuronal exosomes (L1CAM+) would function as a molecular set-point for brain fog severity: patients with the lowest KCC2/NKCC1 ratios would show the highest waking SW density and the most severe subjective cognitive dysfunction, independent of catecholamine tone or absolute cytokine levels.

**Evidence.** Alfonsa 2023: Cl⁻ accumulation → local sleep pressure in mouse cortex; KCC2 overexpression reduced SW density. Coull 2003: microglial BDNF → KCC2 downregulation → chloride dysregulation → neuropathic allodynia — same molecular pathway in cortex. Cortical KCC2 downregulation documented in neuroinflammatory models (Ferrini 2013 review). KCC2 expression is neuronal, exportable via exosomes. No ME/CFS KCC2/NKCC1 data exist.

**Certainty: 0.10.** KCC2 downregulation by neuroinflammation is established in spinal cord and brainstem; its relevance to cortical local-sleep susceptibility is mechanistically coherent but completely untested in humans or any waking-EEG context.

**Falsifiable prediction:** L1CAM+ CSF exosome KCC2/NKCC1 mRNA ratio correlates negatively with waking SW density over parieto-temporal electrodes (r < −0.5) in a combined CSF + hd-EEG study. If KCC2/NKCC1 ratio does not predict SW density despite successful exosome isolation and CSF chloride measurement → chloride transport is not the rate-limiting gate on local sleep susceptibility in ME/CFS.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "Your brain cells have pumps that clear out a sleep-promoting chemical called chloride. Chronic inflammation wears those pumps down — so your brain cells fall asleep during the day faster than a healthy person's."

### 1b. Waking Slow Waves as a Neuroprotective Safety Valve — Not Bug, but Feature

**Mechanism.** ME/CFS involves chronic low-grade excitotoxic stress: kynurenine pathway metabolites (quinolinic acid — NMDA agonist, Section @sec:ch15-kynurenine), impaired astrocytic glutamate clearance (Chapter @ch:neurological), and ATP-dependent ion-pump dysfunction that leaves neurons closer to depolarization threshold. In this environment, cortical columns are at elevated risk of excitotoxic damage — sustained depolarization during cognitive demand → excessive Ca²⁺ influx → mitochondrial permeability transition → apoptotic or necrotic cell death. Waking slow-wave transitions — the neuron alternating between depolarized up-states and hyperpolarized down-states — may serve a neuroprotective function: the down-state provides a recovery window during which Ca²⁺ is extruded, Na⁺/K⁺ gradients are partially restored, and metabolic waste is cleared at the local circuit level. In this framing, local sleep in ME/CFS is not a failure of arousal maintenance but a *necessary neuroprotective program* — the brain is sacrificing momentary cognitive function to prevent permanent structural damage. The same mechanism that produces brain fog is also preventing excitotoxic neuronal death.

**Evidence.** Cortical spreading depression (CSD) — the extreme form of slow-wave propagation — is known to precondition against ischemic damage in animal models (ischemic tolerance). Slow oscillations during sleep are associated with synaptic downscaling (Tononi & Cirelli 2014, SHY hypothesis) — they restore synaptic homeostasis and prevent saturation. The down-state of slow oscillations is hypometabolic (reduced O₂ consumption). In ME/CFS, where energy metabolism is impaired and excitotoxic risk is elevated, the neuroprotective value of local sleep may be proportionally higher. No direct evidence for this adaptive interpretation exists — it is a conceptual reframing, not an observation.

**Certainty: 0.05.** Entirely speculative. No experimental paradigm has tested whether waking SW protects against excitotoxic damage in any condition. The corollary prediction — that *suppressing* waking SW in ME/CFS would increase neuronal injury markers — is ethically complex and has never been tested.

**Falsifiable prediction:** In ME/CFS patients, waking SW density should correlate negatively with CSF neurofilament light chain (NfL) — a marker of axonal injury. Patients with the highest SW density should show the *lowest* NfL if SW are neuroprotective. Falsified if SW density correlates positively with NfL (SW = damage marker) or shows no correlation (SW = epiphenomenon).

**Non-specialist consequence:** "What feels like a broken brain might actually be your brain protecting itself — shutting down individual circuits before they overheat, like a computer throttling its processor to prevent permanent damage."

### 1c. BDNF/TrkB Isoform Imbalance — TrkB-T1 Dominant-Negative Sequestration as the Hidden Driver of Mixed BDNF Findings

**Mechanism.** ElGrawani et al.\ (2024, Cell Reports) demonstrated that BDNF-TrkB signaling drives local sleep buildup — BDNF infusion in mouse cortex increased SWA in a TrkB-dependent manner. The ME/CFS literature reports mixed BDNF findings: both elevated and reduced serum/CSF BDNF have been reported. This paradox is unresolved in the existing paper's local-sleep speculation (spec:ch15-local-sleep-brain-fog). A resolution: BDNF signals through full-length TrkB (TrkB-FL), but a truncated isoform (TrkB-T1) acts as a dominant-negative — it binds BDNF with equal affinity but lacks the intracellular kinase domain, sequestering BDNF in an inactive complex. TrkB-T1 is upregulated by neuroinflammation (TNF-α, IL-1β) and oxidative stress — both present in ME/CFS. If cortical TrkB-T1 is elevated, BDNF-TrkB signaling for local sleep buildup would be *impaired* rather than *enhanced*, even if total BDNF is elevated. The mixed BDNF findings in ME/CFS could reflect different TrkB-T1/TrkB-FL ratios across patient subsets: elevated total BDNF + high TrkB-T1 = BDNF sequestration, low functional TrkB signaling → altered local sleep dynamics; normal/low BDNF + normal TrkB-T1/T1 ratio = BDNF signaling intact. This reframes BDNF from a simple "high = more local sleep" to a ratio-dependent regulator.

**Evidence.** TrkB-T1 is well-characterized: dominant-negative BDNF sequestration (Eide 1996, PNAS), upregulated by TNF-α and IL-1β in neurons and astrocytes, elevated in neuroinflammatory and neurodegenerative conditions (AD, MS, TBI). ElGrawani 2024: BDNF-TrkB-FL signaling required for local sleep buildup. ME/CFS: mixed BDNF findings documented. TrkB isoform ratios measurable in PBMCs and CSF exosomes. No ME/CFS TrkB isoform data exist.

**Certainty: 0.08.** The mechanism is pharmacologically coherent but entirely untested in the context of local sleep. TrkB-T1 upregulation in ME/CFS neuroinflammation is plausible but unmeasured.

**Falsifiable prediction:** In ME/CFS patients, the TrkB-T1/TrkB-FL mRNA ratio in L1CAM+ CSF exosomes correlates positively with waking SW density — counterintuitively, *higher* TrkB-T1 (less functional BDNF signaling) predicts *more* local sleep because the dominant-negative blockade impairs the normal synaptic homeostasis that prevents pathological slow-wave buildup. Falsified if TrkB-T1/FL ratio does not predict SW density, or if the direction is opposite (higher TrkB-FL → more SW, consistent with ElGrawani's finding that BDNF-TrkB-FL drives local sleep).

**Non-specialist consequence:** "It's not how much BDNF your brain makes — it's whether the receptors can use it. Think of BDNF as a key and TrkB as the lock. Inflammation makes decoy locks that grab the key but can't turn."

### 1d. Post-Exertional Cytokine Surge → Waking SW Intrusion → Cognitive PEM Amplifier Loop

**Mechanism.** Leemburg et al.\ (2025, Brain Behavior and Immunity) showed that a single LPS injection in rats produces region-specific waking SW intrusions — peripheral inflammation can trigger local sleep *without* the cognitive-load precondition normally required. ME/CFS PEM involves a post-exertional cytokine surge (IL-6, TNF-α peaking at 24h; documented in multiple CPET studies and the NIH deep phenotyping). This creates a forward-feed amplifier: physical or cognitive exertion → cytokine release (IL-6 from contracting muscle, TNF-α from activated microglia) → waking SW intrusion in frontoparietal attention networks → cognitive impairment → reduced ability to compensate for deficits with effort → more strain on remaining circuits → further metabolic stress → more cytokine release. The cytokine surge *directly* increases local sleep density, independently of the use-dependent mechanism. This predicts that: (a) the cognitive PEM timecourse (peak brain fog at 24–48h) should align with the cytokine peak, not the metabolic recovery timecourse, and (b) anti-inflammatory pre-treatment (e.g., low-dose corticosteroid, IL-6 blockade) should blunt the cognitive PEM response without affecting the metabolic PEM (muscle fatigue, ATP recovery), because it targets the cytokine → SW amplifier loop specifically.

**Evidence.** Leemburg 2025: LPS → waking SW in somatosensory and motor cortex in rats. PEM cytokine surge: elevated IL-6 and TNF-α post-CPET in ME/CFS (multiple replications). Pinggal 2026: waking SW → attention lapses. The temporal alignment (cytokine peak 24h, cognitive PEM peak 24–48h) is circumstantial but mechanistically coherent. No study has measured cytokines AND waking SW simultaneously during PEM timecourse.

**Certainty: 0.15.** Each component is individually evidenced; the integrated amplifier-loop model is a novel synthesis. The prediction that anti-inflammatory intervention selectively blunts cognitive PEM while sparing metabolic PEM has never been tested.

**Falsifiable prediction:** In ME/CFS patients undergoing CPET with serial EEG and cytokine sampling (0, 1, 6, 24, 48, 72h), waking SW density increases at 24h — temporally aligned with the IL-6 peak — and the magnitude of the SW increase predicts cognitive PEM severity (omission errors, subjective brain fog) independently of metabolic PEM metrics (VO2max decline, ATP recovery kinetics). Falsified if SW density does NOT increase post-CPET, OR if SW increase peaks at a different time than the cytokine peak, OR if SW density does not predict cognitive outcomes after controlling for metabolic PEM.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "Your brain fog after exertion isn't just tiredness — it's your immune system actively putting parts of your brain into mini-sleep. The same chemicals that fight infection are switching off your attention circuits."

## Category 2 — Research Directions

### 2a. Sleep Inertia EEG — A Low-Burden Window into Local Sleep Burden and Dissipation Rate

**Rationale.** Ruby et al.\ (2024, Current Sleep Medicine Reports) identify sleep inertia as persistent local sleep in specific brain regions after behavioral awakening — the sleep-to-wake transition is neither instant nor uniform. ME/CFS patients consistently report profound, prolonged morning impairment and unrefreshing sleep. The sleep-inertia window (first 30–60 min after spontaneous awakening) provides a uniquely informative experimental paradigm: (a) it captures SW dissipation dynamics — how fast the brain clears local sleep after a full night of sleep opportunity — without requiring a sustained attention task that taxes already-fatigued patients; (b) it directly tests whether "unrefreshing sleep" is electrophysiologically quantifiable as a failure to dissipate SW after awakening; (c) the procedure is minimally burdensome — patients sleep at home with ambulatory EEG, and the first 30 min of waking recording captures the sleep inertia period without requiring them to travel to a lab while impaired. If ME/CFS patients show prolonged SW persistence after awakening (SW density remains elevated at 30 min post-awakening vs controls who clear SW within 10–15 min), this provides the first objective correlate of unrefreshing sleep — conceptually independent of PSG sleep architecture metrics that are largely normal in ME/CFS.

**Design.** Home-based ambulatory 32-channel EEG. Patients maintain normal sleep schedule. Upon spontaneous awakening (actigraphy-confirmed), they press an event marker and rest quietly with eyes open for 30 min. SW density measured in 5-min bins; group comparison on: (a) SW density at 0–5 min (peak sleep inertia), (b) SW dissipation half-life (time to 50% reduction from 0–5 min peak), (c) SW density at 25–30 min (residual sleep inertia). Correlation with subjective unrefreshing sleep (PROMIS Sleep Disturbance, morning VAS sleep quality).

**Certainty: 0.55 (feasibility) / 0.35 (impact).** Ambulatory EEG is well-established; sleep inertia SW measurement is conceptually validated (Ruby 2024 review); patient burden is low. The main uncertainty: does SW persist after awakening in ME/CFS, or does the unrefreshing sleep experience arise through a different mechanism (glymphatic failure, orexin dysregulation)?

**Falsifiable prediction:** ME/CFS patients show prolonged SW persistence: SW dissipation half-life >25 min vs <15 min in controls, and SW density at 25–30 min remains above pre-sleep baseline. Falsified if SW dissipation curves are identical between groups → unrefreshing sleep is not a persistent-local-sleep phenomenon; the problem occurs *during* sleep, not in the wake transition.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "We could measure how long it takes your brain to fully wake up in the morning — using a simple headband you wear at home. For some patients, it might take hours when it should take minutes."

### 2b. CSF Chloride + Simultaneous Waking hd-EEG — Mapping the Molecular-to-Electrophysiological Axis

**Rationale.** Alfonsa et al.\ identified intracellular chloride as the molecular driver of local sleep pressure. CSF chloride concentration is a routine clinical chemistry parameter — it is measured in every lumbar puncture. If waking SW density correlates with CSF chloride in ME/CFS patients, this establishes a direct molecular-substrate → electrophysiological-phenotype link, grounding the local-sleep hypothesis in a measurable, modifiable biochemical parameter. Furthermore, CSF chloride is partially determined by the KCC2/NKCC1 activity of CSF-contacting neurons; elevated CSF chloride would be consistent with impaired KCC2-mediated extrusion. Combined with CSF exosome KCC2/NKCC1 mRNA measurement (Idea 1a), this maps all three levels: transporter expression → ionic milieu → electrophysiology.

**Design.** N=30 ME/CFS + 15 HV + 15 fibromyalgia controls. Study visit: morning LP for CSF chloride + catecholamines + KCC2/NKCC1 exosome mRNA, followed by 1h rest, then 128-channel hd-EEG sustained attention task. Primary analysis: correlation between CSF chloride and waking SW density (parieto-temporal ROI). Secondary: correlation between KCC2/NKCC1 exosome ratio and SW density. Mediation analysis: does KCC2/NKCC1 → CSF chloride → SW density form a causal chain?

**Certainty: 0.25.** All measurements are individually validated; their simultaneous combination in a waking-EEG protocol is novel. LP + EEG in the same visit is logistically complex but feasible (LP → 1h rest → EEG is standard in CSF biomarker studies).

**Falsifiable prediction:** CSF chloride correlates positively with waking SW density (r > 0.4), and KCC2/NKCC1 ratio mediates this relationship. Falsified if no correlation between CSF chloride and SW density → chloride, while mechanistically implicated in animal models, is not the rate-limiting factor in human waking-SW generation.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "We could measure a single chemical in your spinal fluid — chloride — and predict how much your brain fog interferes with attention, without you having to do any cognitive tests."

### 2c. Pharmacological Disambiguation Trial — Crossover Comparison of Atomoxetine vs Daridorexant vs Placebo on Waking SW Density

**Rationale.** The existing prediction protocol (pred-ch15-waking-sw-me-cfs-eeg-study) proposes a single-dose atomoxetine probe. However, atomoxetine tests only the catecholamine-suppression pathway. A three-arm crossover would disambiguate which upstream mechanism drives waking SW in ME/CFS: (a) atomoxetine 40 mg → tests noradrenergic suppression (per Pinggal 2022 — NE suppresses SW); (b) daridorexant 25 mg → tests orexin-mediated arousal gating (orexin sustains waking desynchronisation; DORAs suppress orexin and should *increase* SW — a paradoxical prediction: if orexin is the primary gate, DORAs should worsen waking SW in ME/CFS, not help); (c) placebo. If atomoxetine reduces SW density but daridorexant increases it → catecholamine deficiency, not orexin dysregulation, drives ME/CFS local sleep. If daridorexant *reduces* SW density → the mechanism is orexin-mediated (and DORAs have daytime pro-cognitive benefit in ME/CFS — a novel clinical claim). If neither reduces SW → the mechanism is non-pharmacologically accessible (chloride accumulation, metabolic exhaustion) and the catecholamine/orexin link is epiphenomenal.

**Design.** N=24 ME/CFS, within-subjects crossover, 3 sessions separated by ≥1 week washout. Each session: drug administration → 2h wait → 30-min sustained attention hd-EEG. Primary outcome: waking SW density. Secondary: omission errors, RT CV, subjective alertness VAS.

**Certainty: 0.45.** The paradigm is directly adapted from Pinggal 2022 (n=32 crossover with methylphenidate, modafinil, citalopram), which established feasibility and effect sizes. The novel element is the DORA arm (never used in waking-EEG pharmacology) and the ME/CFS population.

**Falsifiable prediction:** Atomoxetine reduces waking SW density vs placebo. Daridorexant *increases* SW density vs placebo (consistent with its sleep-promoting mechanism). Only atomoxetine reduces omission errors. Falsified if daridorexant also reduces SW density → unifying mechanism is through general arousal enhancement rather than specific catecholamine gating, OR if neither drug affects SW → pharmacological catecholamine/orexin modulation is insufficient to overcome the chloride/metabolic drive to local sleep in ME/CFS.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "We could figure out, in a single study, whether brain fog is driven by missing adrenaline-like signals or by an overactive sleep-promoting system — and match the drug to the mechanism."

## Category 10 — Reasons This Mechanism May NOT Be Relevant to ME/CFS

### 10a. Brain Fog in ME/CFS May Be Primarily Subcortical (Thalamic/Striatal), Not Cortical

**Critical assessment.** The local-sleep mechanism is intrinsically cortical: cortical columns enter slow-wave oscillations. But the subjective character of ME/CFS brain fog — "my brain won't turn on," effortful thinking, mental exhaustion disproportionate to task difficulty — has features more consistent with subcortical arousal-gating failure than with localized cortical inactivation. The thalamus (particularly the intralaminar and midline nuclei) gates cortical arousal: thalamic dysfunction produces global cortical slowing that would appear as elevated delta power on scalp EEG — indistinguishable from "local sleep" in a surface recording — but the mechanism is thalamic, not cortical. Thalamic hypometabolism is documented in ME/CFS (FDG-PET studies), and thalamocortical dysrhythmia is proposed in multiple chronic pain/fatigue conditions. If ME/CFS brain fog is thalamic, waking SW density would be elevated on EEG (falsely confirming the local-sleep hypothesis), but catecholaminergic therapy would not help — thalamic arousal is primarily gated by cholinergic (pedunculopontine/laterodorsal tegmental → thalamus) and histaminergic (tuberomammillary → thalamus) systems, not noradrenergic. The distinction matters: the treatment would be cholinesterase inhibitors or histamine H3 antagonists, not NRIs. The local-sleep hypothesis may be identifying the correct electrophysiological signature (elevated delta) but attributing it to the wrong anatomical locus.

**Certainty: 0.20.** Thalamic hypometabolism in ME/CFS is documented but inconsistent across studies. Thalamocortical dysrhythmia is a well-developed framework but not specifically validated in ME/CFS. The cortical-vs-thalamic distinction is testable with source-localized hd-EEG or combined EEG-fMRI.

**Falsifiable prediction (of the local-sleep hypothesis, NOT this critique):** Source-localized hd-EEG (128+ channels, sLORETA or beamforming) localizes waking SW generators to thalamus rather than cortical columns → the EEG signature is thalamocortical dysrhythmia, not cortical local sleep. Cortical SW generators (parieto-temporal, per Pinggal 2026 ADHD) are NOT the primary source. Falsified if SW generators are cortical → the thalamic critique is wrong.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "The electrical pattern looks the same on a scalp EEG, but the problem might be in a deep brain relay station — the thalamus — not in the brain's surface. Different location means different treatment."

### 10b. Catecholamine Deficiency Topographic Mismatch — NE May Be Deficient in Regions That Do NOT Generate Waking SW in ME/CFS

**Critical assessment.** Walitt et al.\ (2024) documented central noradrenergic deficiency via CSF DHPG — a global measure sampling the ventricular space. But noradrenergic innervation from the locus coeruleus is not uniform: prefrontal cortex receives densest NE innervation (LC → PFC projection), while parieto-temporal regions — the regions where Pinggal 2026 found elevated waking SW in ADHD — receive sparser NE input. Locus coeruleus projections to parieto-temporal cortex are less dense and may rely more on volume transmission (extrasynaptic NE spillover from distant varicosities) than on synaptic release. If ME/CFS preferentially impairs prefrontal NE signaling (consistent with the "brain fog = executive dysfunction" clinical picture), the parieto-temporal regions where waking SW are hypothesized to occur may have relatively preserved NE tone — and the SW mechanism would operate independently of the NE deficiency. The correlation between CSF NE and SW density may be near-zero because the NE-deficient regions (PFC) and the SW-generating regions (parieto-temporal) are anatomically dissociated. If so, atomoxetine would not reduce SW density (it would increase NE in PFC, not in parieto-temporal SW generators), and the pharmacological probe would falsely suggest the local-sleep mechanism is absent.

**Certainty: 0.12.** LC projection topography is well-mapped in non-human primates and rodents; human data are indirect. Regional NE tone in ME/CFS has never been measured — CSF NE is the best available but is spatially uninformative. The topographic mismatch is a logical possibility but not an established fact.

**Falsifiable prediction:** hd-EEG source localization shows that ME/CFS waking SW are generated primarily in prefrontal regions (where NE is deficient), NOT in parieto-temporal regions (as in ADHD). If so, the topographic match is preserved and the critique is wrong. If SW are parieto-temporal but prefrontal NE tone (measured via PET with NE transporter ligand or fMRI LC functional connectivity) is normal → mismatch confirmed, and the catecholamine → SW gating model fails in ME/CFS.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "The brain chemical that's missing — noradrenaline — might be missing from the *wrong part* of the brain. The attention-control hub is starved, but the slow-wave generator is well-fed."

### 10c. ADHD → ME/CFS Translation Failure — Developmental vs Acquired, Trait vs State

**Critical assessment.** The Pinggal 2026 ADHD finding is the single strongest human evidence for elevated waking SW mediating cognitive dysfunction — and the entire ME/CFS extrapolation rests on it. But ADHD and ME/CFS produce waking SW through fundamentally different etiological pathways that may not produce the same SW-EEG phenotype:

1. **Developmental vs acquired.** ADHD catecholamine dysfunction is neurodevelopmental — altered receptor distributions, transporter densities, and network connectivity set during development and stable in adulthood. ME/CFS catecholamine deficiency is acquired post-infectiously and is potentially dynamic (fluctuating with inflammation, exertion, sleep). A neurodevelopmental NE deficiency may produce SW elevations with specific topography (parieto-temporal) and stable density across time; an acquired NE deficiency may produce different topography, variable density, or no SW elevation at all if the system has compensated differently.

2. **Trait vs state.** ADHD SW elevation is a trait marker — present at baseline, persisting across conditions, stable over time. ME/CFS SW elevation may be a state marker — present only during PEM, cognitive fatigue, or inflammatory flares, and normal or near-normal during well-rested baseline. If SW are state-dependent in ME/CFS, the "baseline" elevation documented in ADHD may not apply, and studies measuring SW only at rested baseline would produce false negatives.

3. **Comorbidity confound.** ADHD and ME/CFS overlap: a subset of ME/CFS patients meet ADHD criteria, and ADHD traits (inattention, executive dysfunction) are elevated in ME/CFS cohorts. If the ADHD-relevant subset of ME/CFS patients shows elevated SW but the non-ADHD subset does not, the finding would be an ADHD biomarker in ME/CFS, not an ME/CFS biomarker per se.

**Certainty: 0.18.** The developmental-vs-acquired and trait-vs-state distinctions are established in clinical neuroscience. Their specific application to waking SW — a nascent field with a single positive clinical study — is necessarily speculative.

**Falsifiable prediction:** In ME/CFS patients stratified by ADHD status (ASRS ≥24 = ADHD-positive, ASRS <24 = ADHD-negative), waking SW density is elevated ONLY in the ADHD-positive subset — and the SW elevation is identical to that reported in Pinggal 2026 ADHD. In ADHD-negative ME/CFS, SW density is normal despite equivalent brain fog severity → the local-sleep mechanism applies to ADHD comorbidity, not ME/CFS pathophysiology itself.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "Not every patient with brain fog has the same kind of brain fog. The slow-wave pattern may only show up in patients who also have ADHD — an important distinction that would change who gets this test and what it means."

## Category 11 — Null Hypothesis Assessment

### 11a. If Waking Local Sleep Has No Role in ME/CFS — What Must Be Revised

**Null scenario.** A well-powered EEG study finds zero difference in waking SW density between ME/CFS patients and controls, SW density does not correlate with brain fog severity or cognitive performance, and pharmacological noradrenergic enhancement does not reduce SW density. The local-sleep mechanism is falsified.

**Paper revisions required:**

**Downstream consequences for the paper's explanatory architecture:**

- **Brain fog model loses one mechanistic candidate.** The remaining candidates — glymphatic failure, orthostatic cerebral hypoperfusion, direct cytokine effects on prefrontal function, kynurenine neurotoxicity, BDNF dysregulation, and sleep fragmentation — would need to carry more explanatory weight. None of these is individually sufficient, but collectively they may cover the phenotype.
- **The catecholamine deficiency finding loses one pathophysiological consequence.** Walitt 2024's CSF NE deficiency would still be relevant for fatigue (via orexin/LC drive), PEM (via cardiac chronotropic incompetence), and orthostatic intolerance (via baroreflex gain), but its specific relevance to cognitive dysfunction would need to be justified through different mechanisms (e.g., prefrontal NE → working memory via α2A receptors; glymphatic NE → vasomotion → clearance).
- **The prediction protocol is not wasted.** A negative result is scientifically informative — it eliminates one candidate mechanism and directs resources toward alternatives. The prediction protocol is published and serves as a record that the mechanism was considered, tested, and rejected.

**Certainty: 0.40.** The paper's brain fog model does not depend on local sleep — it is one of ~7 candidate mechanisms. The intellectual cost of falsification is modest and localized to a single speculation and one integrative paragraph. No chapter structure, central argument, or clinical recommendation changes.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "If this idea turns out to be wrong, the paper stays mostly intact — it was one of many possible explanations for brain fog. The study that proves it wrong is still worthwhile because it tells us where NOT to look next."

### 11b. Positive Waking-SW Finding Would Challenge Existing Negative-Association Hypotheses

**Null-flip assessment.** A *positive* finding — elevated waking SW density in ME/CFS — would itself falsify or challenge several existing hypotheses or assumptions in the paper:

| Challenged claim | Why a positive SW finding challenges it |
|------------------|----------------------------------------|
| "Brain fog in ME/CFS is best explained by glymphatic failure" | If SW are elevated during wake → neurotoxic metabolite clearance is occurring (SW drives glymphatic flow during sleep; waking SW may also drive it) → glymphatic impairment cannot simultaneously be the primary cause if SW-driven clearance is active |
| "Cognitive dysfunction in ME/CFS reflects structural or permanent network changes" | If SW are pharmacologically reducible (atomoxetine reduces SW density) → the dysfunction is dynamic and reversible, not structural |
| "EEG is normal in ME/CFS" (common clinical teaching) | Quantitative waking-EEG SW analysis would reveal covert abnormality not visible on routine clinical EEG reading → clinical EEG reporting conventions for ME/CFS would need updating |
| "Catecholamine deficiency only affects autonomic function" | Direct EEG evidence of catecholamine-gated cortical state → catecholamines directly modulate cognitive function at the millisecond timescale, not just autonomic tone |

**Certainty: 0.25.** The challenged claims vary in strength (glymphatic failure hypothesis is itself speculative; EEG normality is clinical conventional wisdom with little evidence; catecholamine-cognition link is less controversial).

**Non-specialist consequence:** "If the waking slow-wave signature IS found in ME/CFS, it doesn't just confirm this one idea — it challenges several other things we thought we knew about the disease."

## Category 12 — Evidence Quality Concerns

### 12a. Cross-Disease Extrapolation with Near-Zero Validation Benchmark

**Quality assessment.** The entire inferential chain from waking local sleep → ME/CFS brain fog rests on one positive clinical study (Pinggal 2026, ADHD, n=63) and zero replication of that finding in any other cognitive disorder. The local-sleep field has the following validation status:

| Step | Evidence | Validation status |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| Waking SW exist in humans | Multiple labs, multiple paradigms, well-replicated | **Validated** (level: meta-analytic) |
| Waking SW cause attention lapses | Pinggal 2026 ADHD n=63; Gong 2026 ADHD n=120 (pediatric) | **Single-disorder** — no replication in non-ADHD populations |
| Waking SW are catecholamine-gated | Pinggal 2022 RCT n=32 crossover in healthy adults | **Single-study** — no independent replication of the pharmacological modulation |
| Waking SW in clinical populations predict real-world outcomes | None | **None** — no study has followed ADHD patients longitudinally or tested whether SW reduction improves function |
| Waking SW in post-infectious cognitive disorders | Zero studies | **None** — the post-COVID attention literature (Ortelli 2022) measured behavioral RT variability, not EEG SW |
| Waking SW in ME/CFS | Zero studies | **None** |

The history of clinical neuroscience includes many cross-disease extrapolations that failed when tested directly: ADHD drug mechanisms did not generalize to TBI; TBI EEG biomarkers did not generalize to post-stroke cognitive impairment; P300 latency as a general cognitive-decline marker failed to replicate across disorders. The local-sleep field has no cross-disease validation benchmark — it cannot yet claim that "waking SW mediate cognitive dysfunction across conditions" because the finding exists in exactly one condition. Until waking SW are measured and found elevated in at least 2–3 additional cognitive-disorder populations, the ADHD → ME/CFS extrapolation has unknown validity.

**Certainty: 0.35.** The gap is an empirical fact, not an opinion. The field's youth (the first human waking-SW attention study was published in 2026) means the validation gap is expectable but real.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "The science is very young — the key study connecting slow waves to attention problems has only been done in one condition (ADHD). We don't yet know if it applies to anybody else."

### 12b. EEG Slow-Wave Detection in Drowsy/Fatigued Populations — Systematic Overestimation Risk

**Quality assessment.** Detecting slow waves (delta 1–4 Hz) in waking EEG requires distinguishing them from: (a) muscle artifact (temporalis and neck EMG overlaps with delta; ME/CFS patients have higher baseline muscle tension and pain, potentially increasing EMG contamination); (b) microsleeps and drowsiness (genuine sleep onset episodes of 3–15 seconds that produce delta but are global sleep, not "local sleep during wake"); (c) eye-blink and eye-movement artifacts (broadband noise overlapping with delta); and (d) alpha intrusion (8–12 Hz alpha during eyes-open waking, which when fragmented or intermittent can produce spectral leakage into delta after windowing). The Pinggal 2026 algorithm uses strict artifact rejection (ICA + amplitude thresholding) and simultaneous EOG/EMG to reject microsleeps and eye movements. However, its performance characteristics — sensitivity and specificity for local sleep vs global microsleep vs EMG — have been validated only in healthy young adults and medication-withdrawn ADHD adults, populations with low baseline drowsiness and normal EMG. ME/CFS patients are: (1) more drowsy at baseline, increasing the base rate of microsleeps that may pass artifact rejection; (2) more prone to movement during cognitive tasks (discomfort, orthostatic shifting), increasing EMG contamination; (3) more likely to produce alpha intrusion during eyes-open conditions, potentially inflating delta estimates via spectral leakage. The risk is asymmetric: all three confounders would *inflate* SW density estimates, producing a false-positive group difference. Without population-specific validation of the SW detection algorithm against simultaneous video scoring of drowsiness and independent EMG channels, the ME/CFS literature would inherit an unknown false-positive rate.

**Certainty: 0.30.** The artifact concerns are standard EEG methodology issues, well-documented in clinical EEG literature. Their specific severity in ME/CFS is plausible but unquantified — no study has compared SW detection algorithm performance between healthy and chronically-fatigued populations.

**Mitigation.** Any ME/CFS waking-SW study must: (a) include simultaneous video recording with frame-by-frame drowsiness scoring (Karolinska Drowsiness Scale or equivalent); (b) include high-density EMG (temporalis, frontalis, sternocleidomastoid) in addition to standard EOG; (c) report algorithm performance metrics (rejection rates, % epochs retained) separately by group; (d) include a "worst-case" sensitivity analysis excluding the most artifact-prone epochs. Without these, a positive group difference in SW density cannot be attributed to cortical local sleep vs global drowsiness or EMG.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "The brain-wave pattern that looks like 'local sleep' could just be regular drowsiness or muscle tension — especially in patients who are exhausted and uncomfortable during testing. We need extra-strict methods to tell them apart."

### 12c. Selection Bias in ADHD Cohorts — Medication History as Unmeasured Confound in the Pinggal 2026 Reference

**Quality assessment.** Pinggal et al.\ (2026) explicitly studied medication-withdrawn ADHD adults — participants were required to discontinue psychostimulants for ≥48 hours (methylphenidate) or ≥72 hours (amphetamine preparations) before EEG recording. This creates two selection pressures invisible in the published data:

1. **Ascertainment bias toward milder ADHD.** Patients with severe ADHD may be unable to tolerate 48–72 hours without medication and would self-exclude from the study. The Pinggal cohort may therefore represent milder ADHD than the general ADHD population — and the SW density effect size may be *underestimated* (severe ADHD → more SW) or *overestimated* (mild ADHD → more compensatory mechanisms → less SW; or the medication withdrawal itself causes rebound SW elevation).

2. **Medication withdrawal as a confound, not a control.** Psychostimulant withdrawal produces a well-characterized syndrome: fatigue, hypersomnia, increased slow-wave EEG activity, and worsened attention — precisely the dependent variables measured. The 48–72h washout cannot distinguish between: (a) ADHD trait-related SW elevation, (b) medication-withdrawal-related SW elevation, and (c) interaction (medication withdrawal unmasks SW in patients who would show normal SW when treated). A medication-naïve ADHD cohort (never treated) would be needed to isolate the disorder effect from the withdrawal effect, but Pinggal 2026 did not include one.

For the ME/CFS extrapolation, this matters: if Pinggal's SW elevation is partly withdrawal-driven, the ADHD reference effect size is an overestimate — and the expected ME/CFS effect size (no medication withdrawal) would be smaller, potentially below the detectable threshold for a similarly-sized study.

**Certainty: 0.22.** Stimulant withdrawal effects on EEG are documented. The magnitude of the withdrawal confound in Pinggal 2026 is unknown — there is no medication-naïve comparator to quantify it.

**Non-specialist consequence:** "The ADHD study that this whole idea is built on may have measured drug withdrawal symptoms, not ADHD itself — the patients had just stopped their stimulant medications, which causes sleepiness and slow brain waves in anyone."

**Origin:** brainstorm-local-sleep-waking-slow-waves-2026-08-01
