## Constructive categories

### Novel hypotheses

**1.1 Mediators directly generate sickness symptoms (proposal-to-hypothesis):** Each shared abnormality (endothelial dysfunction → reduced CBF; serotonin deficiency → fatigue/cognition; autoantibodies vs neural targets) directly produces specific symptoms, but the Dantzer/Komaroff insight is that these CONVERGE on neuroinflammation → dedicated sickness/torpor circuits. (Origin: brainstorm — derived from review Fig 1.)
- Rationale: Komaroff-Dantzer 2025 delineate direct vs indirect (circuit-mediated) symptom production.
- Evidence link: @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.45
- Falsifiable prediction: Interventions reducing endothelial/serotonergic/autoantibody loads should produce symptom reductions proportional to their direct (circuit-independent) component — measurable as partial, abnormality-specific relief.
- Non-specialist consequence: Understanding that symptoms come both directly from specific bodily problems AND from a shared brain warning-circuit helps patients and clinicians see why treating one marker alone often only partly helps.

**1.2 Torpor-like hypometabolic switch (AMPK/mTOR) as causal energy-conservation state (not damage):** Reduced ATP generation and systemic hypometabolism reflect an adaptive, neural-circuit-mediated torpor-like switch (AMPK↑/mTOR↓), paralleling metabolic-safety-mode but with a named circuit anchor. (Origin: brainstorm)
- Rationale: Review's torpor-metabolic argument (sec "The reduced generation of ATP", AMPK/mTOR).
- Evidence link: @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence @Hrvatin2020TorporNeurons
- Preliminary certainty: 0.35
- Falsify: If the hypometabolism is pure damage, it should not be reversible by disrupting the median-preoptic/torpor circuit or by AMPK/mTOR manipulation; and should track structural mitochondrial loss, not circuit activity.
- Consequence: If fatigue is partly a reversible energy-conservation program rather than damage, then "switching off" the circuit (not adding energy) becomes a concrete therapeutic direction.

## Re-run additions (2026-08-10, deepseek-v4-pro — Phase 4 creative brainstorm re-run)

**Rationale:** The first-pass brainstorm ran inline during integration and produced 15 ideas. This re-run adds genuinely new ideas emphasising: (a) the EP3R two-way switch as a persistence mechanism (the "stuck-switch" hypothesis — absent from first pass), (b) area postrema as dual-role gate (GPCR AAb entry AND GLP-1 anti-inflammatory access), (c) cross-disease hibernation biology as a drug-discovery platform, (d) circuit-engagement biomarkers beyond TSPO-PET, (e) deeper epistemic/critical items.

### Category 1 — Novel hypotheses (additive)

**1.3 EP3R "stuck switch" hypothesis: defective fever↔torpor toggle as persistence mechanism independent of ongoing neuroinflammation.** The Machado 2025 discovery of preoptic EP3R neurons as a two-way switch (fever = hypermetabolic, torpor = hypometabolic) implies that the switch itself can fail. If EP3R receptor desensitization (from sustained PGE2 during the acute infection), biased signaling toward torpor-effector pathways, or epigenetic silencing of the fever arm occurs, the circuit could remain in the torpor position even after neuroinflammation resolves. This is a genuinely novel persistence mechanism: NOT requiring ongoing neuroinflammation (resolving the Omdal 2026 null), NOT requiring permanent damage (resolving why some patients recover), and making a specific molecular prediction (EP3R internalization or β-arrestin bias). (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: Machado2025 shows EP3R neurons toggle both fever and torpor. Komaroff-Dantzer propose a "defective neural-circuit switch" as a persistence mechanism but do not specify the molecular lesion.
- Evidence link: @Machado2025PreopticSwitch @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.40
- Falsifiable prediction: ME/CFS patients with dominant fatigue/cold-intolerance/hypometabolism will show reduced EP3R surface expression or β-arrestin-biased signaling in accessible cell types (PBMCs), or show blunted febrile response to LPS challenge. EP3R antagonist infusion in rodent sickness models should reverse established torpor-like behavior without reducing neuroinflammation (dissociating switch from inflammation).
- Non-specialist consequence: If the brain has a molecular switch that got stuck in "energy-saving mode" during the initial infection, then finding a way to flip it back — without needing to treat the original infection — could be a direct path to recovery, especially for patients whose initial trigger resolved long ago.
- Usefulness: mech=4 / tx=3 / expl=4 / math=3 / dx=2
- Tier: T1 (mech≥4, expl≥4)

**1.4 Area postrema as dual-role gate: GPCR autoantibody entry portal AND GLP-1 anti-inflammatory access route.** The area postrema is (a) the route for circulating GPCR autoantibodies (Azcue2026) to access brainstem autonomic nuclei (NTS, DMV), AND (b) one of the densest GLP-1 receptor-expressing regions in the brain (implicated in nausea/emesis control). This dual role creates a therapeutic paradox: the same structure that admits pathological autoantibodies is also the primary target for GLP-1 receptor agonist anti-neuroinflammatory action (Spezani 2026). If GPCR AAbs occupy area postrema receptors, they may competitively block endogenous GLP-1's anti-inflammatory signaling — creating a specific, testable autoimmune mechanism for neuroinflammation persistence. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: GPCR AAb binding at area postrema (Azcue2026) combined with GLP-1R expression in the same structure.
- Evidence link: @Azcue2026GPCRautoAbs @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence @Spezani2026GLP1neuroinflamm
- Preliminary certainty: 0.35
- Falsifiable prediction: ME/CFS patients with GPCR AAbs will show blunted GLP-1-induced nausea (a specific area-postrema-mediated response) vs. AAb-negative patients, and GLP-1 RA anti-inflammatory effects (CRP reduction) will be reduced in AAb-positive patients. In vitro: GPCR AAbs from ME/CFS sera will competitively displace GLP-1 from area postrema tissue sections.
- Non-specialist consequence: If patient autoantibodies are physically blocking the brain receptor that GLP-1 drugs target, it would explain why inflammation persists and predict that GLP-1 drugs would work better in autoantibody-negative patients but fail (or require higher doses) in autoantibody-positive ones.
- Usefulness: mech=4 / tx=3 / expl=3 / math=2 / dx=1
- Tier: T1 (mech≥4)

**1.5 Peripheral-to-central interoceptive amplification loop: sickness-circuit output feeds back to amplify peripheral symptom signals.** The sickness-behavior circuits (area postrema→brainstem) not only suppress eating/movement but also project to interoceptive integration regions (insula, anterior cingulate). If these circuits are chronically engaged, they would lower the threshold for perceiving normal peripheral signals as aversive (allodynia, gastrointestinal discomfort, fatigue). This creates a testable positive-feedback loop: neuroinflammation → circuit engagement → lowered interoceptive threshold → amplified perception of benign signals → stress/immune activation → more neuroinflammation. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: Known insular/ACC projections from brainstem sickness circuits; interoceptive hypersensitivity documented in ME/CFS.
- Evidence link: @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence @Dantzer2001SicknessBehavior
- Preliminary certainty: 0.30
- Falsifiable prediction: ME/CFS patients will show heightened insular BOLD response to standardized interoceptive stimuli (graded gastric distension, mild thermal pain) that correlates with resting-state connectivity between area postrema and insula. Pharmacological blockade of area-postrema output (e.g., NK1 antagonist) should normalize interoceptive sensitivity.
- Non-specialist consequence: If the brain's warning circuit is stuck in "everything hurts" mode, it would explain why ME/CFS patients experience normal bodily sensations (hunger, fullness, mild temperature changes) as painful or exhausting — the amplifier is turned up, not the signal.
- Usefulness: mech=3 / tx=2 / expl=4 / math=2 / dx=2
- Tier: T2

### Category 2 — Research directions

Prediction: ME/CFS transcriptomes will cluster with "deep torpor" rather than "aroused" or "euthermic" states, and severe patients will show the most torpor-like signatures. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: Naviaux 2016 already identified a "dauer-like" metabolomic state; the Komaroff-Dantzer torpor-circuit model provides the neural mechanism. Cross-species benchmarking is now computationally feasible.
- Evidence link: @Hrvatin2020TorporNeurons @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: n/a (proposal)
- Falsifiable: If ME/CFS transcriptomes fail to cluster with any torpor state, the torpor analogy is purely phenomenological.
- Non-specialist consequence: If ME/CFS blood cells show the same gene-activity pattern as a hibernating squirrel's brain, it would be the strongest evidence yet that the disease is a biologically conserved energy-conservation program — not a collection of unrelated problems.

- Tier: T2

**2.4 Exercise-provoked preoptic BOLD fMRI as circuit-engagement challenge.** Perform resting-state fMRI of the median preoptic nucleus and connected torpor-effector regions (dorsomedial hypothalamus, raphe pallidus) before and after a standardized submaximal exercise challenge. Prediction: PEM-sensitive ME/CFS patients will show increased preoptic→effector connectivity after exercise, correlating with PEM severity 24–48h later, while healthy controls and PEM-resistant patients will not. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: The torpor circuit, if engaged, should show activity changes proportional to PEM provocation. This is NOT the same as TSPO-PET (which measures neuroinflammation, not circuit activity).
- Evidence link: @Machado2025PreopticSwitch @Hrvatin2020TorporNeurons @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: n/a (proposal)
- Falsifiable: No difference in preoptic BOLD signal or connectivity pre/post exercise.
- Non-specialist consequence: A brain scan that changes after a short exercise test — but only in ME/CFS patients — would provide an objective, visible marker of PEM that patients, doctors, and disability evaluators could see.

### Category 3 — Drug/intervention ideas

**3.3 EP3 receptor antagonist (L-798,106 or next-generation) as circuit-switch reset probe.** The Machado2025 finding identifies EP3R as the molecular basis of the fever↔torpor toggle. An EP3 antagonist could, in principle, disengage the torpor arm of the switch regardless of ongoing PGE2 levels — a direct circuit-level intervention. Research-stage probe (rodent models first; no human EP3 antagonist is clinically available). (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: EP3R two-way switch; specificity of EP3 antagonism for the torpor arm.
- Evidence link: @Machado2025PreopticSwitch @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.10
- Falsifiable: If EP3 antagonist infusion fails to reverse torpor-like behavior in a validated mouse sickness model, the EP3R-switch hypothesis for the torpor arm is falsified (fever arm may remain intact).
- Non-specialist consequence: This is pure basic science with no translational horizon currently. It would confirm or refute whether a single receptor controls the brain's energy-saving program.
- Usefulness: mech=3 / tx=1 / expl=2 / math=1 / dx=0
- Tier: T3 (research-stage probe)

**3.4 GLP-1 receptor agonists as circuit-hub-targeting, not just anti-inflammatory.** The existing GLP-1 RA content in the paper focuses on general anti-inflammatory and metabolic mechanisms. The Komaroff-Dantzer framing adds a specific circuit-localisation argument: GLP-1 receptors are densely expressed in the area postrema and NTS — the same regions identified as the sickness-behavior circuit hub. GLP-1 RAs could thus directly suppress the neuroinflammation→circuit activation pathway at its most upstream CNS node, not just reduce systemic inflammation. This predicts faster onset of anti-fatigue effects (central) than anti-inflammatory effects (peripheral). (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: GLP-1R expression in area postrema/NTS; Spezani2026 neuroinflammation disruption.
- Evidence link: @Spezani2026GLP1neuroinflamm @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.25
- Falsifiable: GLP-1 RA administration should reduce resting-state BOLD signal in area postrema and NTS before measurable systemic CRP reduction. If CRP falls without central signal change, the anti-fatigue effect is peripheral.
- Non-specialist consequence: If GLP-1 drugs reduce fatigue by calming a specific brain circuit rather than by reducing body-wide inflammation, they could work faster than expected — and weight loss (a major concern in ME/CFS patients who cannot afford to lose lean mass) might not be required for the brain benefit.
- Usefulness: mech=2 / tx=3 / expl=2 / math=1 / dx=1
- Tier: T2 (tx≥3)

**3.5 COX-2 selective inhibitors (celecoxib) as PGE2-lowering circuit probes.** If the EP3R torpor switch is driven by PGE2, then reducing PGE2 synthesis at its source (COX-2) should reduce torpor-circuit drive. Celecoxib is FDA-approved, oral, and crosses the BBB. A pilot crossover (celecoxib 200mg BID × 2 weeks vs placebo) with pre/post resting-state fMRI of median preoptic connectivity would directly test whether PGE2→EP3R→torpor signaling drives circuit engagement. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: COX-2→PGE2→EP3R pathway; celecoxib BBB penetration; testable with fMRI readout.
- Evidence link: @Machado2025PreopticSwitch @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.20
- Falsifiable: If celecoxib fails to reduce median preoptic BOLD connectivity and fails to improve fatigue scores, PGE2 is not the dominant driver of torpor-circuit engagement.
- Non-specialist consequence: If a common anti-inflammatory drug (celecoxib) changes ME/CFS brain circuit activity on a scan and patients feel better, it would provide the first direct demonstration that a specific brain circuit causes the fatigue — and that an existing drug can target it.
- Usefulness: mech=2 / tx=3 / expl=2 / math=1 / dx=2
- Tier: T2

### Category 4 — Supplement ideas

**4.1 PGE2-modulating supplements as circuit dampeners (Omega-3, curcumin, boswellia).** If PGE2 drives the EP3R torpor switch, supplements that reduce PGE2 synthesis — EPA/DHA (compete with arachidonic acid for COX), curcumin (COX-2 inhibition), boswellia (5-LOX/COX-2 dual inhibition) — could reduce chronic torpor drive without the cardiovascular/GI risks of chronic NSAIDs. This is the "gentle circuit dampener" approach: lowering the PGE2 tone that keeps the switch in the torpor position. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: PGE2→EP3R→torpor pathway; these supplements are OTC, safe, and have documented PGE2-lowering effects.
- Evidence link: @Machado2025PreopticSwitch @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.15
- Falsifiable: High-dose EPA (≥2g/day) × 8 weeks reduces urinary PGE2 metabolites and improves fatigue scores with a correlation between PGE2 reduction and fatigue improvement (r > 0.4). No correlation → PGE2 reduction is not the active mechanism.
- Non-specialist consequence: If fish oil helps ME/CFS fatigue by reducing a specific prostaglandin signal that keeps the brain's energy-saving circuit switched on, it is working through a mechanism that is entirely different from its usual heart-health benefits — and the dose required might be higher.
- Usefulness: mech=1 / tx=2 / expl=2 / math=0 / dx=1
- Tier: T3

**4.2 Adenosine-modulating supplements as torpor-arousal probes (caffeine, cordyceps).** Adenosine A1 receptor activation promotes torpor entry in hibernators; A2A receptor activation promotes arousal. Caffeine is a non-selective adenosine antagonist that could block torpor-promoting A1 signaling. Cordyceps (cordycepin) is an adenosine analogue with A2A-biased activity. The combination could be a safe, OTC probe for the "adenosine tone controls torpor depth" sub-hypothesis. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: Adenosine A1/A2A roles in hibernation/torpor; existing informal ME/CFS patient use.
- Evidence link: @Hrvatin2020TorporNeurons @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.10
- Falsifiable: Caffeine (200mg AM) vs placebo × 2 weeks in ME/CFS does not alter median preoptic BOLD connectivity or fatigue scores. If A1 blockade does nothing, adenosine is not the dominant torpor-maintaining signal.
- Non-specialist consequence: This is a safe, cheap, at-home way to test whether a specific chemical signal (adenosine) keeps the brain in energy-saving mode. If caffeine helps, it would work through a completely different mechanism than its usual wakefulness effect.
- Usefulness: mech=1 / tx=2 / expl=1 / math=0 / dx=0
- Tier: T3

### Category 5 — Non-pharmacological

**5.2 Graduated cold exposure as torpor-circuit antagonist (thermoregulatory arousal challenge).** The Machado2025 two-way switch implies that the fever (hypermetabolic) and torpor (hypometabolic) arms are mutually inhibitory. Cold exposure, which activates the fever arm (heat production via brown adipose tissue, shivering), should antagonize the torpor arm by competing for EP3R neuron population activity. Graduated cold exposure (cool water face immersion → contrast showers → short cold-room exposure) could serve as a non-pharmacological circuit-reset protocol. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: Fever↔torpor mutual inhibition at EP3R neuron level; cold→thermogenesis→fever arm engagement→torpor arm suppression.
- Evidence link: @Machado2025PreopticSwitch @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.25
- Falsifiable: Cold exposure (15°C water face immersion, 3 min, daily × 4 weeks) increases brown-adipose-tissue FDG uptake (PET) and reduces fatigue scores more than thermoneutral sham. If cold exposure worsens symptoms (cold intolerance), the torpor arm may be dominant and cold should be avoided.
- Non-specialist consequence: If brief cold exposure helps ME/CFS by forcing the brain to switch from energy-saving to energy-burning mode, it would work for the opposite reason most people expect — not by cooling inflammation but by activating heat-production circuits that override the energy-conservation program.
- Usefulness: mech=2 / tx=2 / expl=3 / math=1 / dx=0
- Tier: T2

**5.3 Bright-light circadian entrainment as preoptic-circuit reset.** The median preoptic nucleus receives direct and indirect input from the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN), which is entrained by retinal light exposure. If circadian disruption (documented in ME/CFS) desynchronizes SCN→median-preoptic coupling, the torpor circuit may receive inappropriate "nighttime/conserve" signals during the day. Timed bright-light therapy (10,000 lux, 30 min upon waking) could resynchronize the circuit. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: SCN→median preoptic projections; circadian disruption in ME/CFS; light therapy safety.
- Evidence link: @Hrvatin2020TorporNeurons @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.20
- Falsifiable: 4 weeks of morning bright-light therapy (vs. dim-light control) shifts the circadian phase of core body temperature (an indirect preoptic output marker) earlier and reduces morning fatigue scores. No phase shift → light is not accessing the preoptic circuit.
- Non-specialist consequence: If a light box by the bed helps ME/CFS fatigue by telling the brain's energy-control center "it's daytime, time to be active," the benefit would be fundamentally different from light therapy's usual use for seasonal depression — and the timing (strictly morning) would be critical.
- Usefulness: mech=2 / tx=2 / expl=3 / math=1 / dx=0
- Tier: T2

### Category 8 — Cross-disease bridges

**8.1 Hibernation biology as drug-discovery platform for torpor-circuit arousal.** Hibernating mammals (arctic ground squirrels, Syrian hamsters, bears) naturally and reversibly cycle between deep torpor (Tb ~2–8°C, metabolic rate ~1–5% of normal) and euthermia. The molecular signals for spontaneous interbout arousal — specifically the arachidonic acid/prostaglandin cascade shift, adenosine A1 receptor downregulation/A2A upregulation, mTORC1 reactivation, and brown-adipose-tissue thermogenic reactivation — are evolutionary homologs of the human EP3R/mTOR/AMPK pathway. The hibernation literature is a largely untapped drug-discovery resource for ME/CFS torpor reversal, with the unique advantage that hibernation arousal is a *physiological* (not pathological) process — meaning the molecular machinery for circuit reset already exists and needs only to be activated. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: Cross-species torpor biology; interbout arousal as a natural model of circuit reset; existing hibernation transcriptomic/proteomic datasets.
- Evidence link: @Hrvatin2020TorporNeurons @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.35
- Falsifiable: At least one molecular signal that drives spontaneous interbout arousal in hibernators (e.g., a specific eicosanoid shift, A2A receptor upregulation, or mTORC1 reactivation factor) should, when pharmacologically mimicked in a mouse torpor model, accelerate arousal. If no hibernation-arousal signal accelerates torpor arousal, the translational bridge is invalid.
- Non-specialist consequence: Hibernating animals naturally "wake up" from a state that looks metabolically identical to severe ME/CFS — they already know how to switch the circuit off. Finding which molecular signal they use, and testing whether that same signal works in humans, could bypass decades of drug development by borrowing a solution that evolution already built.
- Usefulness: mech=3 / tx=3 / expl=4 / math=3 / dx=1
- Tier: T1 (expl≥4, tx≥3) — **Standalone topic candidate (Gate A: ≥5 separable papers in hibernation molecular biology + drug repurposing)**

**8.2 Cancer cachexia as a non-malignant sickness-circuit analog for drug repurposing.** Cancer cachexia produces an indistinguishable sickness-behavior phenotype (anorexia, fatigue, wasting, neuroinflammation) through the same hypothalamic circuits targeted by Komaroff-Dantzer. Anti-cachexia drugs — ghrelin receptor agonists (anamorelin, approved in Japan), anti-IL-6 antibodies (tocilizumab), and anti-myostatin agents (bimagrumab) — have existing human safety data and could be tested as torpor/sickness-circuit antagonists. The advantage over anti-neuroinflammatory agents: these drugs have been tested in medically fragile populations and target the circuit OUTPUT (not the inflammatory input). (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: Cachexia shares sickness-behavior circuit engagement; existing drugs with human safety data.
- Evidence link: @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence @Dantzer2001SicknessBehavior
- Preliminary certainty: 0.25
- Falsifiable: Ghrelin receptor agonist (anamorelin) in ME/CFS × 12 weeks increases appetite, lean mass, and reduces fatigue scores more than placebo. Null effect → cachexia circuit is not the relevant output pathway.
- Non-specialist consequence: Drugs developed to prevent wasting in cancer patients might help ME/CFS fatigue — not by treating cancer-like biology, but because cancer and ME/CFS both trigger the same ancient brain circuit that tells the body to conserve energy and stop eating. The biology is the same; the trigger is different.
- Usefulness: mech=2 / tx=3 / expl=3 / math=1 / dx=1
- Tier: T2

**8.3 Chemotherapy-induced fatigue ("chemo-brain") as an iatrogenic sickness-circuit model.** Chemotherapy agents (especially doxorubicin, cisplatin) produce neuroinflammation and a fatigue/cognitive-impairment syndrome that recapitulates the Komaroff-Dantzer circuit model. Unlike ME/CFS, the trigger is known, the dose is controlled, and the time-course is predictable. This makes chemo-brain an ideal experimental model for testing anti-neuroinflammatory/circuit-dampening interventions — with results directly translatable to ME/CFS. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: Chemotherapy-induced neuroinflammation→sickness-behavior circuit is well-documented; controlled human model.
- Evidence link: @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence @Dantzer2001SicknessBehavior
- Preliminary certainty: 0.30
- Falsifiable: An anti-neuroinflammatory agent that reduces chemo-fatigue in a controlled trial should also reduce ME/CFS fatigue, with effect sizes proportional across conditions. No correlation → chemo-fatigue and ME/CFS fatigue are mechanistically distinct.
- Non-specialist consequence: Chemotherapy patients and ME/CFS patients may share the same brain mechanism for fatigue, even though their diseases are completely different. This means a drug that helps one group would likely help the other — and chemotherapy patients are a faster, cleaner group to test drugs in because the trigger and timing are known.
- Usefulness: mech=2 / tx=2 / expl=3 / math=1 / dx=1
- Tier: T2

### Category 9 — Diagnostic/biomarker

**9.2 Resting-state preoptic→effector fMRI connectivity as torpor-circuit engagement biomarker.** Resting-state functional connectivity between the median preoptic nucleus and its downstream torpor-effector targets (dorsomedial hypothalamus, raphe pallidus, lateral parabrachial nucleus) could serve as a direct functional biomarker of torpor-circuit engagement that is independent of neuroinflammation (unlike TSPO-PET). This would distinguish patients whose fatigue is circuit-mediated from those whose fatigue is primarily peripheral (mitochondrial, endothelial). (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: rs-fMRI of small deep-brain structures is feasible at 7T; connectivity should reflect circuit engagement.
- Evidence link: @Hrvatin2020TorporNeurons @Machado2025PreopticSwitch @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.30
- Falsifiable: If median preoptic→raphe pallidus connectivity shows significant regression coefficient with fatigue scores (β > 0.4) and increases with PEM provocation, it is a valid circuit-engagement biomarker. Null correlation → the circuit is not measurably engaged in ME/CFS at rest.
- Non-specialist consequence: A 10-minute brain scan that shows whether your fatigue is coming from a stuck brain circuit or from damaged mitochondria would let doctors pick the right treatment — circuit-dampening drugs for the first group, metabolic support for the second — rather than guessing.
- Usefulness: mech=2 / tx=1 / expl=3 / math=2 / dx=4
- Tier: T1 (dx≥4)

**9.3 CSF-to-plasma PGE2 gradient as EP3R-switch driver biomarker.** If the EP3R torpor switch is driven by CNS PGE2 (not peripheral), then the CSF-to-plasma PGE2 ratio should be elevated in ME/CFS and correlate with fatigue severity. This would resolve the Omdal 2026 null (peripheral PGE2/cytokines normal) while confirming CNS-confined prostaglandin drive. Requires lumbar puncture — invasive but definitive. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: EP3R→torpor dependence on PGE2; Omdal null limits peripheral signal; CSF PGE2 can be elevated independently.
- Evidence link: @Machado2025PreopticSwitch @KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence
- Preliminary certainty: 0.35
- Falsifiable: ME/CFS CSF PGE2 is higher than matched controls (by ≥50%) and the CSF:plasma ratio is >2 (normal ~1). If CSF PGE2 is normal, the PGE2→EP3R→torpor pathway is not the dominant driver.
- Non-specialist consequence: A spinal tap showing a specific chemical signal elevated in the brain fluid but normal in the blood would prove that ME/CFS fatigue has a source inside the brain, invisible to blood tests — and would identify exactly which chemical is keeping the energy-saving switch stuck on.
- Usefulness: mech=3 / tx=2 / expl=3 / math=1 / dx=4
- Tier: T1 (dx≥4)

**9.4 Blunted LPS-induced fever as EP3R desensitization readout.** If the EP3R fever/torpor switch is stuck in the torpor position due to receptor desensitization, ME/CFS patients should show a blunted febrile response to a standardized low-dose LPS challenge (0.4 ng/kg IV, as used in human endotoxemia studies). The magnitude of fever blunting should correlate with fatigue severity and hypometabolism. This is an existing human experimental paradigm with established safety data. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Rationale: EP3R neurons mediate both fever and torpor; receptor desensitization predicts blunted febrile response.
- Evidence link: @Machado2025PreopticSwitch @Dantzer2001SicknessBehavior
- Preliminary certainty: 0.35
- Falsifiable: ME/CFS patients show a peak temperature rise of <0.5°C (vs. 1.0–1.5°C in healthy controls) after 0.4 ng/kg LPS, correlating with baseline fatigue scores (r < −0.5). Normal febrile response → EP3R is not desensitized.
- Non-specialist consequence: A controlled, low-dose immune challenge that produces a normal fever in healthy people but almost no fever in ME/CFS patients would be the first direct demonstration that the brain's temperature-control switch is broken — and that the same broken switch explains why patients feel cold all the time AND why they have no energy.
- Usefulness: mech=3 / tx=1 / expl=3 / math=1 / dx=3
- Tier: T2

### Category 10 — Reasons mechanism may NOT be relevant (epistemic)

**10.2 Human torpor circuits may be vestigial or functionally silenced.** The Hrvatin 2020 median preoptic→torpor circuit was discovered in Mus musculus; humans last shared a common ancestor with obligate torpor-using mammals ~90 million years ago. While the anatomical nuclei exist in the human brain, the specific neuronal population (identified by pyroglutamylated RFamide peptide, QRFP, expression) may be sparse, differently connected, or functionally silenced in primates. No QRFP-expressing torpor neuron has been identified in human hypothalamus. The entire circuit may be a rodent-specific adaptation. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Certainty of null: n/a — epistemic
- Consequence: If the human median preoptic nucleus lacks the QRFP-expressing torpor neuron population, the torpor-circuit model for ME/CFS is anatomically falsified. This would force the field back to non-circuit explanations (metabolic damage, autoimmune, epigenetic) for the hypometabolic state.
- Non-specialist consequence: The mouse brain circuit that controls hibernation-like energy saving may simply not exist in humans — we lost it during evolution. If so, the "stuck hibernation switch" explanation for ME/CFS is wrong, and researchers need to look elsewhere.

**10.3 If neuroinflammation is absent (Omdal 2026 null + Raijmakers 2021 TSPO-PET null), the circuit-activation model has no input.** The Komaroff-Dantzer model requires neuroinflammation as the circuit-activating input. Two independent null findings — Omdal 2026 (no peripheral cytokine association with fatigue) and Raijmakers 2021 + the BBB transport-caveated TSPO-PET data (no confirmed central neuroinflammation) — together suggest neuroinflammation may not be present in established ME/CFS. Without neuroinflammation, the circuits would not be activated, and the model collapses regardless of whether the circuits exist in humans. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Certainty of null: n/a — epistemic
- Consequence: If the neuroinflammation premise fails, the sickness/torpor circuit model must be retired or restricted to the acute/post-acute phase (where it is well-supported by LPS-challenge data). The chronic phase would require different explanations.
- Non-specialist consequence: Two separate studies — one showing no inflammation in blood, one showing no inflammation in brain scans — together suggest that the "brain inflammation drives symptoms" model may be wrong for long-established ME/CFS. The problem may be downstream of any inflammation, in how cells permanently changed their behavior after the initial infection.

### Category 11 — Null-hypothesis assessment

**11.2 Alternative: The "torpor" is secondary to prolonged bedrest/deconditioning, not circuit engagement.** Reduced physical activity alone suppresses basal metabolic rate, reduces mitochondrial content, impairs thermogenesis, and produces fatigue — all features of the "torpor" phenotype. The Komaroff-Dantzer circuit model predicts these are primary (circuit-driven) when they may be entirely secondary to inactivity. A critical test: severely deconditioned but non-ME/CFS individuals (e.g., post-ICU syndrome, prolonged bedrest after fractures) should show the same metabolic signature as ME/CFS if deconditioning is the cause. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Certainty of null: n/a — epistemic
- Consequence: If post-ICU syndrome patients show identical hypometabolic signatures (metabolomics, REE reduction, cold intolerance) to ME/CFS patients, the "torpor" is simply deconditioning and the dedicated-circuit model adds unnecessary complexity.
- Non-specialist consequence: If the low-energy state of ME/CFS is indistinguishable from what happens to anyone who stays in bed for months, then the disease may be primarily a problem of getting stuck in a normal deconditioning state — not a unique brain-circuit malfunction.

**11.3 The sickness-behavior model may explain acute symptoms well but CHRONIC symptom persistence not at all.** The Dantzer 2001 framework was developed for acute infection (~days to weeks). Its extension to chronic illness (years to decades) is an extrapolation without direct evidence. The chronic-persistence mechanisms the review proposes (vicious cycles, defective switch, persistent antigen) are conceptually independent of the sickness-behavior framework and do not require the neural-circuit model. A simpler model: chronic symptoms are maintained by a subset of the listed persistence mechanisms (autoimmunity, dysbiosis, mitochondrial dysfunction) without engaging sickness circuits at all. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Consequence: If chronic ME/CFS symptoms are maintained by autoimmune/peripheral mechanisms that do not require central circuit engagement, the sickness-behavior framework should be restricted to the acute phase and the chronic-phase content should focus on autoimmunity, metabolism, and tissue pathology.
- Non-specialist consequence: The brain's sickness-response circuit may explain why you feel terrible during the first weeks of an infection — but not why you still feel terrible three years later. Different mechanisms may be operating at different disease stages.

### Category 12 — Evidence-quality concerns

**12.2 Self-citation and framework-promotion risk.** Komaroff and Dantzer have been the principal architects of the sickness-behavior model applied to ME/CFS for 20+ years. The 2025 review synthesizes evidence that overwhelmingly supports their pre-existing framework while citing their own prior work heavily (Dantzer 2001, 2007, 2008; Komaroff 2023). This is not misconduct — it is expected from leading experts — but it creates a structural confirmation bias: the review's selection of evidence, mechanistic pathways, and persistence mechanisms is filtered through a framework the authors themselves created. Independent corroboration from groups not invested in the sickness-behavior model is needed. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Consequence: The review should be treated as an authoritative-but-not-impartial synthesis. Independent replication of its central claims (circuit engagement in ME/CFS, neuroinflammation as the hub) by groups outside the Komaroff/Dantzer/VanElzakker network would substantially increase confidence.
- Non-specialist consequence: The two most influential scientists in this area are reviewing evidence that largely supports their own life's work. That does not make them wrong — but it does mean the scientific community should test their model independently, with researchers who did not help build it.

**12.3 No primate torpor-circuit data; the mouse→human translation gap is maximal.** The Hrvatin 2020 and Machado 2025 circuit findings are entirely rodent-based. Torpor is a small-mammal adaptation (high surface-area-to-volume ratio, rapid heat loss); no primate naturally enters torpor (the fat-tailed dwarf lemur is the only primate hibernator, and its mechanism may differ). The translational gap is not merely "animal→human" but "mouse torpor→human non-torpor." The circuit may exist anatomically but with fundamentally different function in primates. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Consequence: All claims about median preoptic→torpor circuit engagement in human ME/CFS must carry the strongest available caveat: there is zero direct evidence. The framework is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- Non-specialist consequence: The mice whose brains revealed the "hibernation switch" are 3-inch animals that need to shut down their metabolism to survive cold nights. Humans are 100+ pound primates that never evolved this ability. Assuming the same switch exists in us and causes disease is a very big leap.

**12.4 Severity confounding: mild, moderate, and severe ME/CFS may engage different symptom-production pathways.** The Komaroff-Dantzer review presents the circuit model as general across ME/CFS severities, but severe ME/CFS (bedbound, tube-fed) may involve fundamentally different mechanisms (structural CNS damage from sustained hypoperfusion/hypoxia, irreversible neurodegeneration) that are outside the sickness-behavior framework. Mild ME/CFS may have active but reversible circuit engagement; severe ME/CFS may have progressed to structural pathology. No severity-stratified data exist. (Origin: brainstorm — re-run)
- Consequence: The circuit model may be valid only for mild-moderate ME/CFS. Applying it to severe patients without severity-specific evidence risks mischaracterizing the mechanism.
- Non-specialist consequence: The brain-circuit explanation may work well for people who can still walk and think reasonably clearly — but for the bedbound patients who cannot tolerate light or sound, the mechanism may have shifted to permanent tissue damage, and circuit-targeting treatments that might help early-stage patients could be useless or harmful in late-stage disease.
