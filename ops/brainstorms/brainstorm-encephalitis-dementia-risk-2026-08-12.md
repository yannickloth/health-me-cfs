## 1. Novel Hypotheses

### 1.1 Inflammation-quality discriminator: "autoimmune-style" vs "bacterial-style" CNS inflammation as a two-route template

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The most discriminating finding in the cohort is not the overall encephalitis→dementia association but its *etiology split*: autoimmune/post-infectious-inflammatory etiologies confer the highest risk (RR 3.93), viral a moderate risk (1.99), and bacterial no significant risk (1.35, 0.97–1.87) @Aditi2026EncephalitisDementia. Bacterial encephalitis is a frank tissue-destructive, pyogenic inflammatory insult, yet it does *not* translate into elevated long-term dementia — whereas lower-grade, smoldering, antibody/adaptive-driven inflammation does. This inverts the naive "more inflammation = more degeneration" intuition. The mechanistic implication is that the *adaptive/autoimmune* route drives neurodegeneration through the chronic microglial–NLRP3–tau/amyloid axis @Heneka2025NeuroinflammationAD @Ising2019NLRP3Tau @Heneka2013NLRP3Alzheimer, whereas acute pyogenic inflammation is cleared and reparative rather than self-perpetuating.

**Novel hypothesis.** ME/CFS, which is dominated by an autoimmune/neuroimmune profile (GPCR autoantibodies, anti-pituitary/hypothalamus antibodies, Th17/myeloid signals @Strawbridge2019CFSInflammatory) rather than an active bacterial parenchymal infection, should fall on the *high-risk* side of this quality discriminator if the encephalitis template transfers. Conversely, if ME/CFS neuroinflammation is a low-grade but *peripheral-clearance* or *reparative* state (more like the resolving bacterial-encephalitis aftermath), it would predict minimal degeneration.

**Certainty:** 0.30

**Falsifiable prediction.** ME/CFS patients with detectable CNS-directed autoantibodies (e.g., GPCR or hypothalamic/pituitary AAbs) will show higher baseline glial-activation markers (TSPO-PET, GFAP) and faster cognitive decline over 5+ years than autoantibody-negative ME/CFS patients — *if* the autoimmune-route template transfers. Falsified if autoantibody status does not stratify any neuroinflammation or cognitive-trajectory readout.

**Non-specialist consequence:** If only the "autoimmune-flavored" kind of brain inflammation — and not all brain inflammation — leads to later dementia, then the ME/CFS patients most worth monitoring for long-term cognitive decline are the ones with measurable autoimmune markers, and the field should stop treating "neuroinflammation" as one thing.

### 1.2 Midlife-peak relative risk as a "vulnerability window" shared with ME/CFS onset

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The relative risk of post-encephalitis dementia is *highest* at age 40–60 (RR 5.16, HR 6.74), not in old age @Aditi2026EncephalitisDementia. This is striking because midlife is also the dominant ME/CFS onset window. One reading: in the 40–60 range the baseline dementia rate is low, so any inflammation-driven contribution dominates the relative risk; but a deeper reading is that midlife is a period of *decreased resilience* to sustained glial activation (perimenopausal sex-hormone shifts, age-related decline in repair capacity, rising vascular burden) without yet the selection effects of late-life survival.

**Novel hypothesis.** ME/CFS onset age and encephalitis "amplification" age share a common midlife vulnerability substrate. If ME/CFS neuroinflammation is neurodegenerative, the rate of cognitive decline should be *faster* in patients whose ME/CFS onset falls in midlife (40–60) than in those who develop it earlier or later, after adjusting for age and baseline cognition.

**Certainty:** 0.25

**Falsifiable prediction.** In a longitudinal ME/CFS cohort, an onset-age × time interaction on cognitive slope: midlife-onset patients decline faster than early-onset or late-onset patients matched for current age, disease duration, and severity. Falsified if cognitive slope is flat across all onset-age strata.

**Non-specialist consequence:** If the years 40–60 are when a brain is least able to absorb long-term inflammation, then a person who develops ME/CFS at midlife may be in the highest-risk window for later cognitive problems — and that window, not just disease duration, should guide monitoring.

### 1.3 "Exposure-defined" precedent upgrades the neuroinflammation causal claim from correlation to prediction

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** Prior ME/CFS neuroinflammation→neurodegeneration reasoning rested on *cross-sectional* marker–dementia meta-analyses @Kuring2026InflammatoryDementia. The encephalitis cohort is *longitudinal and exposure-defined*: a dated acute inflammatory episode precedes and predicts the dementia outcome over 10 years @Aditi2026EncephalitisDementia, independently replicated @Granerod2017PostEncephalitis. This is a fundamentally stronger causal-architecture claim — it shows an acute CNS-inflammation "dose" can seed a long-latency degenerative process.

**Novel hypothesis.** The encephalitis data support a *two-stage* model: (i) an acute inflammatory episode sets a microglial/astroglial state that (ii) self-perpetuates after the inciting trigger clears, then (iii) only later drives proteinopathy and atrophy. For ME/CFS, this implies the relevant question is not "is there neuroinflammation now" but "was there a *defined inflammatory episode* at onset (infection, autoimmune flare) that could have imprinted a self-sustaining glial state."

**Certainty:** 0.40

**Falsifiable prediction.** ME/CFS patients whose illness had a well-defined infectious or autoimmune onset (documented seroconversion, elevated acute-phase autoantibodies) will show a stronger longitudinal glial-activation signal and faster cognitive decline than those with insidious/undefined onset. Falsified if onset-type does not predict any neuroinflammation or cognitive readout.

**Non-specialist consequence:** The key question for predicting whether ME/CFS might cause long-term brain decline is not "is the brain inflamed right now" but "was there a clear inflammatory 'hit' at the start" — which would make the medical history at onset a cheap predictive tool.

## 2. Research Directions

### 2.1 Longitudinal ME/CFS plasma NfL/GFAP + cognitive-slope cohort (the decisive falsification test)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The encephalitis/AD axis predicts that sustained neuroinflammation produces measurable axonal (NfL) and astroglial (GFAP) injury markers that precede cognitive decline @Heneka2025NeuroinflammationAD. No such longitudinal ME/CFS study exists — this is the single highest-value gap.

**Design.** Prospective ME/CFS cohort (n ≥ 200, IOM 2015 criteria), baseline + annual plasma NfL and GFAP, annual computerized cognitive battery, TSPO-PET or equivalent glial marker in a subset, 5–10 yr follow-up; stratify by onset age, autoantibody status, and disease duration. Control: age/sex-matched healthy + a chronic-illness control (e.g., fibromyalgia) to control for disability/activity effects.

**Certainty:** 0.55 (design certainty; the hypothesis it tests is low-certainty)

**Falsifiable prediction.** Elevated baseline NfL/GFAP predicts *worsening* cognitive slope; a dose–response between glial signal and decline rate. The decisive null — stable cognition over ≥5 yr despite elevated markers — would argue ME/CFS neuroinflammation is non-degenerative.

**Non-specialist consequence:** One well-run decade-long study of ME/CFS patients measuring a simple blood marker (neurofilament) against cognitive tests would settle whether the disease is quietly neurodegenerative or not — currently the single most important open question this analogy raises.

**Scores:** mech 3 · tx 1 · expl 3 · math 2 · dx 4

### 2.2 Post-encephalitis vs post-ME/CFS glial-phenotype comparison (single-cell / spatial)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The encephalitis→dementia mechanism runs through specific microglial states (disease-associated microglia, NLRP3-active) and astroglial reactivity @Heneka2025NeuroinflammationAD @Ising2019NLRP3Tau. ME/CFS exosomes drive microglia to IL-1β release @Tsilioni2022exosome, but the resulting microglial *phenotype* has never been characterized against the AD/encephalitis DAM signature.

**Design.** Induced-microglia or post-mortem/spatial-transcriptomic comparison of microglial transcriptomes from: (a) post-encephalitis patients, (b) AD, (c) ME/CFS (iPSC-derived microglia exposed to ME/CFS serum/exosomes), (d) healthy. Measure overlap of DAM/NLRP3/IL-1β modules.

**Certainty:** 0.35

**Falsifiable prediction.** ME/CFS serum-exosome-stimulated microglia will show a *partial* DAM signature (IL-1β, NLRP3 up) but *not* the full neurodegeneration-associated DAM profile (e.g., lacking ApoE/Lgals3/LPL modules) — a partial overlap that would explain why ME/CFS has neuroimmune symptoms without established dementia. Falsified if the signatures are either fully identical (would imply ME/CFS is a prodromal AD-like state) or fully disjoint (would sever the analogy).

**Non-specialist consequence:** Looking at the exact "flavor" of activated brain immune cells in ME/CFS versus Alzheimer's would show whether ME/CFS is on a dementia path or is a different, milder immune state — moving beyond the vague word "neuroinflammation."

### 2.3 Reanalysis of existing encephalitis cohorts by outcome granularity (dementia type + age-of-onset)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The Aditi cohort's dementia codes are heterogeneous (post-encephalitic G31 sequelae vs AD F03 vs vascular F01), and the age split matters @Aditi2026EncephalitisDementia. The Javonillo preprint further shows viral encephalitis can *attenuate* DAM/myeloid amyloid clearance rather than accelerate it @Javonillo2026CoronavirusAD.

**Design.** Re-analyze TriNetX/CPRD data stratifying the dementia outcome into (i) AD/proteinopathy, (ii) vascular, (iii) post-encephalitic focal cognitive sequelae; test whether the autoimmune-etiology excess is specifically *proteinopathy-driven* or reflects post-inflammatory focal damage.

**Certainty:** 0.40

**Falsifiable prediction.** The autoimmune-etiology dementia excess is driven by a genuine AD-type proteinopathy signal (not merely focal post-encephalitic cognitive deficit), and the bacterial-etiology null is specific to the *AD/proteinopathy* stratum. Falsified if the autoimmune excess disappears when post-encephalitic focal sequelae are excluded.

**Non-specialist consequence:** Distinguishing "dementia from protein clumping" from "cognitive problems from the original brain damage" tells us whether the autoimmune type of encephalitis truly seeds Alzheimer's — which is the precise thing ME/CFS would need to worry about.

## 3. Drug / Medication Ideas

> Conservative flag: these are long-shot research probes from *indirect* cross-disease evidence; none is a clinical recommendation.

### 3.1 NLRP3-pathway repurposing as a "mechanism-probe" trial (not yet a treatment)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The causal chain encephalitis→dementia runs through NLRP3→tau @Ising2019NLRP3Tau and NLRP3→amyloid @Heneka2013NLRP3Alzheimer. If — and only if — ME/CFS neuroinflammation engages the same inflammasome, then existing NLRP3 inhibitors (e.g., the clinical candidate MCC950/CRID3, or the FDA-adjacent colchicine as a weak inflammasome modifier) would be a *mechanism probe*: a short, biomarker-endpoint trial asking whether damping NLRP3 lowers plasma GFAP/IL-1β or glial PET signal in ME/CFS.

**Certainty:** 0.20

**Falsifiable prediction.** A short-course NLRP3 inhibitor reduces ME/CFS plasma IL-1β and GFAP (or glial PET signal) in a dose-dependent way, and this predicts cognitive-endpoint benefit in a longer trial. Falsified if NLRP3 inhibition moves no neuroinflammatory biomarker (implying ME/CFS neuroinflammation is not inflammasome-driven, or is too low-grade to register).

**Non-specialist consequence:** If a drug that blocks the specific "inflammasome" switch at the heart of Alzheimer's brain inflammation does nothing to ME/CFS markers, that would be strong evidence ME/CFS is *not* on that path — a useful negative even though it is not a treatment yet.

### 4.1 Sulforaphane / other Nrf2 activators as NLRP3-priming modulators

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** NLRP3 activation requires a "priming" step (NF-κB-driven) and an activation step; Nrf2 activators blunt inflammasome priming in macrophages/microglia, downstream of the same IL-1β axis implicated in ME/CFS exosome→microglial activation @Tsilioni2022exosome and in the AD NLRP3 pathway @Heneka2013NLRP3Alzheimer. **Non-specialist consequence:** A cheap, safe dietary compound that dampens the "priming" step of brain inflammation could be tested quickly; if it moves the right blood markers, it would justify a larger trial — but today it is only a hypothesis.

## 5. Non-Pharmacological Interventions

### 5.1 Sleep / glymphatic-clearance optimization as a *prevention* framing, not a symptom therapy

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The encephalitis→dementia mechanism is partly a *clearance* failure: sustained glial activation plus impaired amyloid/tau clearance. ME/CFS is characterized by unrefreshing sleep and reduced slow-wave sleep, which is required for glymphatic clearance (documented in the corpus, ch18 sec-14 chronic glymphatic impairment). If ME/CFS neuroinflammation is even weakly neurodegenerative, then slow-wave-sleep-dependent clearance is a modifiable, non-pharmacological lever that would lower long-term proteinopathy burden.

**Certainty:** 0.20

**Falsifiable prediction.** ME/CFS patients with objectively better slow-wave sleep (or improved slow-wave sleep after an intervention) show slower NfL/GFAP rise and slower cognitive decline over 5+ years than matched poor-sleepers. Falsified if slow-wave sleep quality does not track any injury-marker or cognitive trajectory.

**Non-specialist consequence:** If sleep's "brain-washing" function matters here, then protecting deep sleep becomes a long-term brain-protection strategy in ME/CFS — not just a way to feel less tired tomorrow.

**Scores:** mech 2 · tx 2 · expl 2 · math 1 · dx 0

## 7. Mathematical Model Extensions

### 7.1 Add a slow "neurodegeneration burden" state to the multi-system ODE model

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The existing coupled model (42 state variables, appendix E) has neuroinflammation, immune, and energy modules but no explicit *slow* neurodegeneration variable. The encephalitis template motivates adding a slow-accumulating "proteinopathy/degeneration burden" state driven by a nonlinear (threshold) function of sustained microglial/NLRP3 activation, with a very long timescale (years) separating it from the fast cytokine dynamics @Ising2019NLRP3Tau @Heneka2013NLRP3Alzheimer.

**Design.** New state variable $D$ (degeneration burden) with $\frac{dD}{dt} = \alpha \cdot \Theta(M - M_{thr}) \cdot M - \delta D$, where $M$ is a microglial-activation variable, $\Theta$ a threshold (capturing the etiology-quality discriminator: only sustained, autoimmune-style activation crosses $M_{thr}$), $\alpha$ small (years-scale), $\delta$ slow clearance. This lets the model *predict* whether a given ME/CFS neuroinflammation trajectory would ever reach clinically-relevant $D$.

**Certainty:** 0.25

**Falsifiable prediction.** Calibrated to plausible ME/CFS microglial-activation magnitudes, the model predicts that typical ME/CFS neuroinflammation *does not* cross the degeneration threshold within a human lifespan (matching the observed absence of a ME/CFS dementia epidemic), while autoimmune-enriched ME/CFS subgroups sit near the threshold. Falsified if the model cannot be parameterized to reproduce the observed separation (encephalitis → dementia, ME/CFS → no established dementia) without ad-hoc tuning.

**Non-specialist consequence:** Building a math model of how brain inflammation slowly turns into brain damage would let researchers compute — before any decade-long trial — whether ME/CFS-level inflammation is even capable of causing dementia, or is safely below the threshold.

## 9. Diagnostic / Biomarker Ideas

### 9.1 Baseline plasma NfL/GFAP as a "degeneration-risk" triage marker in ME/CFS

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** NfL (axonal injury) and GFAP (astroglial reactivity) are the canonical readouts of the neuroinflammation→neurodegeneration axis @Heneka2025NeuroinflammationAD. Their *baseline* elevation in ME/CFS — even without current dementia — would be the earliest testable signal of whether ME/CFS neuroinflammation is injurious vs benign. **Non-specialist consequence:** Two blood markers that already exist (neurofilament and GFAP) could give the first real answer to whether ME/CFS brain inflammation is damaging or harmless — cheap, repeatable, and long overdue.

## 10. Reasons This Mechanism May NOT Be Relevant to ME/CFS (Disproof Evidence)

### 10.1 ME/CFS neuroinflammation is low-grade and reversible, not the acute parenchymal destruction of encephalitis

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The encephalitis→dementia effect is dose- and pathology-dependent: it follows *acute, tissue-invasive* parenchymal inflammation. ME/CFS "neuroinflammation" is a low-grade glial-reactive state whose very existence is contested (single null TSPO-PET study @Raijmakers2021TSPOCFS, @lim:neuroinflammation-measurement-challenges), and whose exosome→IL-1β signal @Tsilioni2022exosome is exercise-triggered and transient. Low-grade, regionally-restricted, reversible glial reactivity may simply never reach the NLRP3/proteinopathy threshold that encephalitis and AD cross.

**Certainty:** 0.50 (this is the *default* prior given the evidence)

**Falsifiable prediction.** ME/CFS plasma NfL/GFAP will be indistinguishable from controls, and cognition will be stable over ≥5 years despite any glial signal — the "benign neuroinflammation" outcome that would sever the analogy. (This is itself the falsification test of the pro-degeneration hypotheses above.)

**Non-specialist consequence:** The most likely truth may be reassuring: ME/CFS brain inflammation could be a mild, reversible state that never becomes the destructive kind that causes dementia — and this is the result the longitudinal marker study would need to rule out first.

**Scores:** mech 3 · tx 1 · expl 3 · math 1 · dx 2

`origin: brainstorm`

### 10.2 The bacterial-etiology null undercuts "any inflammation → degeneration" (and ME/CFS may sit with the null)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** Bacterial encephalitis — the most overtly inflammatory — shows *no* significant dementia risk (RR 1.35, 0.97–1.87) @Aditi2026EncephalitisDementia. This directly falsifies the generic "inflammation causes neurodegeneration" claim and means the template is etiology-specific. If ME/CFS lacks the specific autoimmune/adaptive signature that drives the excess (its autoantibody findings are heterogeneous and not universally replicated), it may fall with the null group.

**Certainty:** 0.40

**Falsifiable prediction.** If ME/CFS autoantibody findings fail to replicate as a robust, CNS-directed, high-burden signal, the encephalitis analogy predicts *no* ME/CFS degeneration risk — and the correct inference is that ME/CFS neuroinflammation is etiologically "non-degenerative" like the bacterial group. Falsified (in favor of degeneration) only if ME/CFS autoantibody burden is robust and tracks injury markers.

**Non-specialist consequence:** The fact that the most obvious, severe brain infections (bacterial) do *not* lead to dementia is a warning not to assume ME/CFS's milder inflammation will — the dangerous kind of inflammation is a specific autoimmune kind, and ME/CFS may not have it.

**Scores:** mech 3 · tx 0 · expl 3 · math 0 · dx 1

### 10.3 Viral encephalitis may *attenuate*, not accelerate, amyloid clearance (the Javonillo partial-null)

**Mechanistic rationale + evidence link.** The preprint found neurotropic murine coronavirus encephalitis caused minimal Aβ change and *down-regulated* DAM/myeloid amyloid-clearance pathways in 5xFAD mice @Javonillo2026CoronavirusAD. This is a caution that acute viral neuroinflammation does not uniformly accelerate proteinopathy — the direction can be neutral or even protective in some readouts. If ME/CFS is post-viral in origin, the viral-etiology branch (RR 1.99, lower than autoimmune) is the more relevant one, and it may not behave like the autoimmune/AD axis.

**Certainty:** 0.30

**Falsifiable prediction.** Post-viral ME/CFS (the majority) will show *no* accelerated amyloid/proteinopathy signal, consistent with the moderate viral-etiology RR and the Javonillo attenuation finding. Falsified (pro-degeneration) only if post-viral ME/CFS shows AD-like amyloid/tau accumulation.

**Non-specialist consequence:** If ME/CFS mostly follows a virus, then the animal evidence actually suggests viral brain inflammation might *not* speed up the protein-clumping of Alzheimer's — another reason to expect ME/CFS is not on a dementia trajectory.

## 11. Null Hypothesis Assessment

### 11.1 What if neuroinflammation has *no* causal role in ME/CFS at all?

**Assessment.** If neuroinflammation is entirely epiphenomenal or non-causal in ME/CFS, then the encephalitis→dementia template is irrelevant to ME/CFS on the *causal* axis, and its only residual value is diagnostic/predictive (i.e., it never matters for treatment). Under this null:

- The single TSPO-PET null @Raijmakers2021TSPOCFS is the *true* signal, and Nakatomi's positive finding @Nakatomi2014neuroinflammation is a false positive or reflects a different ligand/population.
- ME/CFS exosome→IL-1β @Tsilioni2022exosome is a peripheral-epiphenomenon artifact with no CNS consequence.
- The inflammation markers in ME/CFS @Strawbridge2019CFSInflammatory are low-grade, non-specific, and do not drive the energy-metabolism, autonomic, or cognitive phenotype.
- Therefore: no anti-neuroinflammatory intervention should produce durable benefit, and no degeneration-monitoring is warranted.

**Certainty that this null is true:** moderate-to-high (the honest prior is that the pro-degeneration analogy is a long shot).

**Falsifiable prediction.** If the null holds, then (i) ME/CFS plasma NfL/GFAP equals controls, (ii) cognition is stable over ≥5 years, and (iii) no anti-neuroinflammatory agent (NLRP3 inhibitor, minocycline, LDN) produces biomarker-measurable or clinical benefit beyond placebo. Any durable glial-marker reduction + clinical benefit would reject the null.

**Non-specialist consequence:** The honest baseline is that ME/CFS brain inflammation may be a bystander, not a cause — meaning treatment money should go to energy-metabolism and autonomic pathways, and patients should not fear that their illness is silently becoming dementia.

## 12. Evidence Quality Concerns

### 12.1 EHR/administrative-code ascertainment bias in both encephalitis and dementia

**Concern.** The TriNetX cohort @Aditi2026EncephalitisDementia and CPRD @Granerod2017PostEncephalitis both use coded diagnoses. Dementia is massively under-coded in EHRs, and encephalitis-etiology classification (autoimmune vs viral vs bacterial) is itself uncertain from codes. Propensity matching cannot fix ascertainment bias that is differential across etiologies (e.g., autoimmune encephalitis patients are more intensely worked-up and followed, inflating detected dementia).

**Certainty:** 0.50 (that this is a real, uncaptured limitation)

**Falsifiable prediction / test.** Chart-adjudicated subsample validation would show meaningful dementia under-ascertainment concentrated in specific etiology strata, quantitatively shifting the RR estimates. If adjudication confirms the coded RRs, this concern is resolved.

**Non-specialist consequence:** These results come from billing codes, and billing codes systematically miss dementia and mislabel the *cause* of encephalitis — so the headline risk numbers may be off in ways that matter for which patients are truly at risk.

### 12.2 "Post-encephalitic G31 sequelae" vs true neurodegeneration: outcome conflation

**Concern.** The Aditi note explicitly flags that younger-age-group dementia codes are driven by post-encephalitic G31 sequelae, not AD/F03 proteinopathy @Aditi2026EncephalitisDementia. A large share of the "dementia" signal may be *focal post-inflammatory cognitive deficit* (a direct injury consequence) rather than a *progressive* neurodegenerative process — which is precisely the mechanistic distinction ME/CFS cares about (reversible symptom correlate vs progressive pathology).

**Certainty:** 0.45

**Falsifiable prediction / test.** Stratifying the outcome (G31 sequelae vs AD/F03) will show the autoimmune-etiology *progressive-proteinopathy* excess is much smaller than the composite RR suggests, with much of the signal being focal sequelae. If the AD/F03 stratum still shows a robust autoimmune excess, the neurodegenerative transfer strengthens.

**Non-specialist consequence:** Much of the "dementia" after encephalitis may actually be direct, one-time brain damage from the illness — not an ongoing degenerative disease — which matters enormously because ME/CFS would face the latter concern, not the former.

### 12.3 Survivorship and reverse-causation: encephalitis patients who develop dementia are not representative

**Concern.** Encephalitis survivors are a selected population (those who survive a severe neurological insult). Both encephalitis and dementia share risk factors (vascular, immune senescence, genetic AD predisposition). The cohort cannot exclude that encephalitis is a *marker* of a pre-existing neurodegenerative-prone brain (reverse causation / confounding by shared predisposition) rather than an independent *cause* of dementia.

**Certainty:** 0.40

**Falsifiable prediction / test.** Genetic risk scores (APOE, AD polygenic risk) and pre-encephalitis cognitive/health trajectories would show that a substantial share of the encephalitis→dementia association is explained by shared predisposition; controlling for these would attenuate the RR. If the RR survives genetic-risk adjustment, the causal transfer strengthens.

**Non-specialist consequence:** People who get bad encephalitis and people who later get dementia may share an underlying "brain-vulnerability" tendency — so the encephalitis may be a sign of an already-fragile brain rather than the thing that causes the dementia, which would weaken the lesson for ME/CFS.

**Scores:** mech 0 · tx 0 · expl 0 · math 0 · dx 0
