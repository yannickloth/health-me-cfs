## Category 1 — Novel Hypotheses

### P1.1: Subthreshold Dysautonomia Is the Same Autonomic Deficit as POTS, Not a Separate Entity
**Mechanism:** Parsaik 2013 (n=184) shows OI-without-tachycardia shares clinical presentation, autonomic findings, and deconditioning with POTS, with only "mildly greater dysautonomia" in POTS. This suggests the HR-rise threshold partitions a *continuous* severity distribution, not two disease states. The "non-POTS dysautonomia" construct is then a recognition label for the low-HR end of the same baroreflex/vascular/SFN spectrum — not a distinct pathology. If true, subthreshold patients share the same drivers (hypovolemia, SFN, GPCR-autobody, splanchnic pooling) as threshold POTS, and management (volume expansion, compression, α1 support) applies across the whole spectrum. This directly challenges the implicit assumption that "not meeting the threshold = not affected."
**Evidence:** Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44).
**Certainty:** 0.45
**Falsifiable:** In a single-clinic ME/CFS cohort, if the HR-rise distribution is bimodal (clear trough separating POTS from non-POTS), the "two entities" model is supported; if continuous/unimodal, the spectrum model wins. A spectrum predicts IENFD, supine-to-stand MAP drop, plasma volume, and splanchnic pooling severity all correlate continuously with HR rise (Spearman r, no threshold step) across the subthreshold AND threshold range.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A patient with orthostatic symptoms who just misses the POTS heart-rate cutoff could still have the same treatable autonomic problem as someone who meets it, so the cutoff should not decide who gets help.
`origin: brainstorm`

### P1.2: The HR-Rise Threshold Is a Consequence, Not the Cause, of Under-Detection Bias
**Mechanism:** Chung & Raj 2026 document 24-month diagnostic delay and 30–40% post-infectious onset; Sivakoti 2026 names a subthreshold population that is currently excluded from research. The mechanistic claim: the ≥30/≥40 bpm threshold functions as a *selection filter* that has systematically removed the low-HR, possibly milder/slower-evolving, ME/CFS+OI patients from every POTS research cohort. This biases the evidence base — cohort phenotypes, treatment response, and prevalence all reflect only the high-HR tail. It also plausibly inflates the apparent "POTS prevalence in ME/CFS" figure (only the top of the HR distribution is counted) while the total dysautonomia burden is underestimated. This is a sampling/ascertainment hypothesis, distinct from any biological mechanism.
**Evidence:** ChungRaj2026POTSReview (0.44), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48).
**Certainty:** 0.40
**Falsifiable:** If subthreshold OI patients were included in a prospective ME/CFS cohort and followed with serial HUT, their trajectory (rate of progression to POTS, treatment response, symptom evolution) would differ from threshold POTS in a direction predicted by the "selection filter" model (e.g., longer diagnostic delay, lower baseline severity, but equal functional impairment on self-report).
**Non-specialist consequence:** The heart-rate cutoff may be silently shaping what we think we know about dysautonomia in ME/CFS, because studies only ever counted the fastest-heart subset of patients.
`origin: brainstorm`

### P1.3: Subthreshold Dysautonomia May Contribute PEM-Like Symptoms via Repeated Cerebral Hypoperfusion
**Mechanism:** The existing CBF hub (parked under pots-mecfs, Miranda-Hurtado 2026) shows SV→ETCO2→CBF drops drive orthostatic cognitive symptoms. If subthreshold dysautonomia produces the same CBF deficits (only with a smaller HR compensatory signal — because these patients cannot mount as large a tachycardia), then repeated subthreshold orthostatic CBF dips could contribute to a fatigue/PEM picture even though HR stays below 30 bpm. That is, the low-HR state is not "less dysautonomia" — it may be a *less-compensated* autonomic failure in which the brain takes the perfusion hit instead of the heart rate defending it. This reframes "non-POTS dysautonomia" from "mild" to "possibly worse-compensated" in a subset.
**Evidence:** Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), plus corpus CBF literature (Miranda-Hurtado 2026).
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Falsifiable:** In subthreshold-OI ME/CFS patients, transcranial-Doppler-measured orthostatic CBF drop correlates with PEM symptom intensity (post-exertion flare) as strongly as in threshold POTS; patients with blunted HR rise AND large CBF drop show the worst cognitive orthostatic symptoms.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Missing the heart-rate cutoff does not necessarily mean a milder problem — some patients may actually have a less-compensated system where the brain bears the brunt of blood-flow drops.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 2 — Research Directions

### R2.1: Prospective Outcome Study of Subthreshold Dysautonomia in ME/CFS (Validation of the Construct)
**Design:** Prospective longitudinal cohort of ME/CFS patients (n≥200) stratified by orthostatic status: (a) formal POTS (≥30 bpm), (b) subthreshold dysautonomia (OI + autonomic dysfunction + functional impairment, HR rise 15–29 bpm), (c) no OI. Baseline: HUT + impedance cardiography + COMPASS-31 + symptom domains + severity. Follow-up: 24 months with quarterly reassessment. Primary: does subthreshold dysautonomia (b) have a distinct trajectory vs (a) and (c) on functional status (SF-36), treatment use, and symptom stability? Secondary: does (b) progress to (a) over time (validating the spectrum idea P1.1) or stay stable (validating distinct-entity)?
**Rationale:** The single biggest gap in the consensus's claim is that no study shows reclassifying subthreshold patients changes anything. Parsaik 2013 is cross-sectional (single snapshot); a longitudinal arm is required to test the recognition construct's validity. This is the highest-value research direction.
**Evidence:** Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44).
**Certainty:** n/a (proposal — validity of construct untested)
**Falsifiable:** If subthreshold dysautonomia is a genuine distinct-recognition entity, its 24-month functional trajectory differs from both (a) and (c); if it is just mild POTS, (b) merges with (a) over time and the "non-POTS" label carries no prognostic information.
**Non-specialist consequence:** This study would tell us whether giving a name and diagnosis to patients who miss the POTS cutoff actually improves their care and outcomes, or whether it changes nothing.
`origin: brainstorm`

### R2.2: Multi-Center Validation of a Threshold-Free Orthostatic Classification
**Design:** International multi-center (≥5 sites, adult + pediatric) consensus-grounded study that drops the single HR-rise number in favor of a multi-axial classification: (1) symptom domains (OI, autonomic, functional impairment), (2) objective autonomic findings (HUT hemodynamics, sudomotor/QSART, pupillometry, HRV), (3) severity/functional. Test inter-rater reliability and diagnostic agreement across centers, and whether the multi-axial system captures patients the current HR threshold excludes.
**Rationale:** Sivakoti 2026 calls for standardized nomenclature/classification but adopts none; Boris 2020 (single-center CHOP) and Parsaik 2013 (single-center Mayo) are the only empiric bases. A threshold-free system needs multi-center reliability data before it can replace or supplement the HR cutoff. Directly operationalizes the consensus's stated "unmet need."
**Evidence:** Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44), Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48).
**Certainty:** n/a (proposal)
**Falsifiable:** A multi-axial classification yields acceptable inter-rater agreement (Cohen's κ ≥ 0.6) across centers AND classifies ≥20% more symptomatic patients as having dysautonomia than the HR threshold alone; if agreement is poor or no patients are newly captured, the threshold-free approach lacks added value.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A checklist-based way to recognize dysautonomia — not just a single heart-rate number — could let more patients be diagnosed consistently in different clinics.
`origin: brainstorm`

### R2.3: Prospective Evaluation of Whether HR-Threshold Inclusion Bias Distorts ME/CFS Research
**Design:** Re-analysis + prospective arm. (1) Systematic review of existing POTS-in-ME/CFS cohort studies: report the HR-rise inclusion thresholds actually used, and quantify the fraction of screened symptomatic ME/CFS patients excluded by each. (2) In a fresh ME/CFS cohort, compare outcomes (treatment response, symptom severity, CBF, biomarker levels) between those who meet vs just miss the threshold, holding symptom burden constant.
**Rationale:** The under-recognition argument (Chung & Raj 2026; P1.2) implies a systematic ascertainment bias in the existing ME/CFS+OI literature. Quantifying how many patients were excluded by the threshold — and whether they differ from included patients — tests whether the field's conclusions are threshold-dependent.
**Evidence:** ChungRaj2026POTSReview (0.44), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44).
**Certainty:** n/a (proposal)
**Falsifiable:** If threshold-inclusion bias is real, studies using stricter thresholds report systematically different prevalence and different mean symptom severity than studies using looser thresholds; excluding subthreshold patients materially changes pooled estimates.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Knowing how many patients have been left out of research because their heart rate was a few beats too slow would show whether our picture of dysautonomia in ME/CFS is complete or systematically skewed.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 3 — Drug/Medication Ideas

### DR3.1: Treat Subthreshold Dysautonomia With the Same Mechanism-Labeled Drugs Used in POTS (Response as Diagnostic Probe)
**Mechanism:** Uppal 2026 (n=28) shows symptom response to ivabradine/propranolol is more selective than HR lowering — HR dropped in all, symptoms in a subset. If subthreshold patients share the same drivers (P1.1), the *same* agents (fludrocortisone for hypovolemia, midodrine for α1 vasoconstriction, compression) should help them. The idea: use drug response in subthreshold patients as a *diagnostic probe* — a subthreshold patient who improves on fludrocortisone+volume has hypovolemic-type dysautonomia even without meeting the HR cutoff. This makes treatment-diagnostic rather than threshold-diagnostic.
**Evidence:** Uppal2026IvabradinePropranolol (0.48), Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44).
**Certainty:** 0.30 (all off-label; subthreshold evidence absent)
**Falsifiable:** In a pilot crossover of subthreshold-dysautonomia ME/CFS patients, fludrocortisone+volume (vs placebo) improves COMPASS-31 orthostatic subscale; the response predicts which subthreshold patients have hypovolemia (plasma volume low) vs those who do not respond (non-hypovolemic cause).
**Non-specialist consequence:** If subthreshold patients respond to the same medications as POTS patients, that would confirm they are not being harmed by the cutoff — they can still be treated and could even be diagnosed through treatment response.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 4 — Supplement/Nutraceutical Ideas

### SU4.1: Salt/Volume Strategy as a Threshold-Independent, Low-Harm First-Line Probe
**Mechanism:** Hypovolemia is a documented POTS driver and a candidate shared driver of subthreshold dysautonomia (P1.1). A standardized, low-cost salt-and-fluid load (structured oral rehydration — the corpus C6.2 ORS idea, here applied specifically to the *subthreshold* population rather than POTS) is a low-harm, threshold-independent intervention. In subthreshold patients it is also a *diagnostic probe*: positive symptom response to volume loading suggests a hypovolemic mechanism regardless of HR-rise. Differs from the parked C6.2 (which targeted ME/CFS+POTS) by explicitly testing the below-threshold population the consensus identifies.
**Evidence:** Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), plus corpus volume-expansion literature.
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Falsifiable:** In subthreshold-OI ME/CFS patients, 2 weeks of structured salt+fluid (vs matched-volume water) improves orthostatic symptom VAS by ≥25% and increases measured plasma volume; responders show low baseline plasma volume, non-responders do not.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A safe, inexpensive salt-and-fluid trial could help identify — and help — subthreshold patients without needing a heart-rate diagnosis.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 5 — Non-Pharmacological Interventions

### NP5.1: Compression and Upright-Tolerance Training Delivered to Subthreshold Patients (Pre-emptive Access)
**Mechanism:** Abdominal+leg compression and graded upright exposure improve orthostatic tolerance by mechanical venous-return support — a mechanism independent of the HR-rise threshold. If subthreshold patients share the pooling problem (P1.1), they should benefit from the same non-pharmacological measures currently gated behind a POTS diagnosis. The idea is *access*: non-pharmacological, low-harm first-line care should be offered on symptom presence, not on meeting the HR cutoff. This also serves as a non-invasive probe (responder vs non-responder).
**Evidence:** Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44).
**Certainty:** 0.40
**Falsifiable:** In subthreshold-OI ME/CFS patients, 4 weeks of daily abdominal+leg compression reduces orthostatic symptom scores (COMPASS-31) and increases standing time by a clinically meaningful margin; non-responders segregate by a mechanistic feature (e.g., low IENFD or low plasma volume) that predicts which subthreshold patients need drug support.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Non-drug measures like compression garments could help subthreshold patients without waiting for a formal POTS diagnosis.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 6 — Combinations + Access

### C6.1: Threshold-Free Diagnostic-And-Treatment Pathway (Consensus-Operationalized)
**Framework:** A clinical pathway that operationalizes Sivakoti 2026's recognition construct: (1) symptom screen (OI + autonomic + functional impairment), (2) orthostatic challenge (HUT or active stand) reporting HR rise AND symptom/BP/CBF response — not gating on a single number, (3) objective autonomic workup where indicated (QSART, pupillometry, HRV), (4) tiered first-line care (salt/fluid + compression + pacing) offered to all with symptoms, (5) drug step-up (fludrocortisone/midodrine/β-blocker/ivabradine) gated by mechanism-labeled response rather than by HR-rise cutoff. Deliberately mirrors the parked C6.1 tiered protocol (pots-mecfs) but replaces "standing-tolerance triage" with "threshold-free recognition triage" — the new angle.
**Evidence:** Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), ChungRaj2026POTSReview (0.44), Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44).
**Certainty:** n/a (framework proposal)
**Falsifiable:** In a stepped-wedge cluster trial of clinics adopting the pathway, subthreshold patients identified and treated show improved functional status vs clinics using the HR threshold alone; the pathway does not measurably increase over-diagnosis (see C10.1).
**Non-specialist consequence:** A practical care path that recognizes dysautonomia by symptoms rather than a single heart-rate number could let more patients get consistent, early care.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 7 — Mathematical Model Extensions

### M7.1: Continuous HR-Rise Severity Axis in the Existing Orthostatic Model (Remove the Threshold Discontinuity)
**Mechanism:** The existing ANS/orthostatic ODE (ch29) encodes a POTS regime via a threshold on HR rise. The consensus critique implies the model should treat HR rise as a *continuous* severity axis rather than a binary POTS/non-POTS switch. Extension: replace the discrete regime boundary with a continuous grading of the compensatory-HR response and of functional impairment, parameterized so that "subthreshold" patients sit at a defined point on the same continuum. The model then predicts that the symptom burden/functional-impairment relationship is *flat* across the 30-bpm boundary (matching Boris 2020's finding of no clinical difference), which the current threshold-containing model cannot reproduce.
**Evidence:** Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44), Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44); model context ch29.
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Falsifiable:** The continuous-axis model, fitted to HUT data from patients both above and below the 30-bpm threshold, reproduces the observed flat symptom-burden relationship across the boundary (ΔAIC favoring continuous over thresholded model); if the thresholded model fits better, the critique's "no clinical difference" claim is population-artifact.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Putting heart-rate rise on a sliding scale instead of a pass/fail line would let models — and hopefully clinics — treat dysautonomia as a spectrum rather than a yes/no.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 8 — Cross-Disease Bridges

### CD8.1: ME/CFS ⇔ Long-COVID Post-Infectious Dysautonomia — Shared Subthreshold Recognition Problem
**Mechanism:** Chung & Raj 2026 report 30–40% post-infectious POTS onset; Long-COVID has a large post-infectious dysautonomia burden. Both ME/CFS and Long-COVID share the problem that post-infectious dysautonomia may present below the HR threshold and be under-recognized. The bridge: a validated threshold-free recognition approach tested in one post-infectious population (e.g., Long-COVID) could be transported to the other (ME/CFS). This is a classification-translation bridge, not a mechanism-translation bridge.
**Evidence:** ChungRaj2026POTSReview (0.44), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48).
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Falsifiable:** A threshold-free dysautonomia screen validated in a Long-COVID cohort transfers to an ME/CFS cohort with comparable sensitivity/specificity; if the post-infectious presentations differ (different dominant symptom), the screen needs population-specific adaptation.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A recognition tool proven in one post-viral illness could speed diagnosis in another, since both share the same under-diagnosis problem.
`origin: brainstorm`

### CD8.2: ME/CFS ⇔ Inappropriate Sinus Tachycardia (IST) — The Mirror-Image Threshold Problem
**Mechanism:** IST is a resting tachycardia with no orthostatic trigger — the "always high HR" complement to "OI without tachycardia." The competing-mechanism screen (search log query 7) found IST as the main differential that the ≥30-bpm rise threshold must exclude. The bridge: ME/CFS patients whose orthostatic complaints are actually IST-driven (high resting HR, small orthostatic increment) are at risk of being mislabeled POTS by a raw HR reading, while true subthreshold-dysautonomia patients are mislabeled normal. A recognition system must separate these by *change* + symptom context, not absolute HR — informing a threshold-free classification that distinguishes IST, POTS, and subthreshold dysautonomia.
**Evidence:** Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), ChungRaj2026POTSReview (0.44); IST differential per search log.
**Certainty:** 0.25
**Falsifiable:** In ME/CFS patients with orthostatic complaints, measuring resting HR separately from orthostatic increment cleanly separates an IST subgroup (high resting, low increment) from a subthreshold-dysautonomia subgroup (normal resting, low increment, symptom-positive), improving diagnostic agreement over using a single standing-HR number.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Teasing apart "heart always fast" from "heart speeds up only on standing" could stop two groups of patients from being lumped together or missed.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 9 — Diagnostic/Biomarker Ideas

### D9.1: Orthostatic CBF Drop as a Threshold-Free Autonomic Index
**Description:** Replace/augment the single HR-rise number with a threshold-free index of orthostatic cerebral perfusion (transcranial-Doppler MCA velocity or NIRS prefrontal O2Hb drop during HUT/active stand), reported as a continuous value. Rationale: subthreshold patients (P1.3) may show *equal or worse* CBF drop than POTS patients despite a smaller HR signal — making CBF drop a better reflection of functional impact than HR rise. Differs from the parked D9.1/D9.2 (SV-CO2-CBF triad, CO2 reactivity) by centering the *recognition* problem: the goal is a threshold-free index that captures the below-threshold population the consensus identifies, not subtyping.
**Evidence:** Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44), plus corpus CBF literature.
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Falsifiable:** Orthostatic CBF-drop magnitude correlates with functional impairment and symptom burden (r>0.5) across the whole HR-rise range — including below 30 bpm — better than HR rise does; subthreshold patients with large CBF drops are the ones who benefit from treatment, identifying them without a HR cutoff.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Measuring how much blood flow to the brain drops on standing, rather than how fast the heart beats, could catch patients the heart-rate test misses.
`origin: brainstorm`

### D9.2: Composite Autonomic Dysfunction Score (Multi-Modal, Threshold-Free) for ME/CFS
**Description:** A multi-modal composite that combines hemodynamic (HUT/BP), sudomotor (QSART), pupillometric, HRV, and symptom domains into a continuous dysautonomia score — explicitly designed to classify subthreshold patients rather than apply a single cutoff. This operationalizes the consensus's call for standardized nomenclature/classification. Each sub-modality is individually validated; the composite is tested for its ability to detect the below-threshold population and to predict treatment response.
**Evidence:** Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44).
**Certainty:** 0.30 (composite unvalidated; components partly validated)
**Falsifiable:** A composite dysautonomia score, scored continuously, separates subthreshold dysautonomia from healthy controls with AUC ≥ 0.75 and predicts treatment response better than the HR-rise threshold alone; if it adds nothing beyond the HR number, the recognition construct lacks measurable value.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A multi-signal "autonomic score" could recognize dysautonomia from several measurements combined, not just a single heart-rate cutoff.
`origin: brainstorm`

### D9.3: Subjective–Measurable Discrepancy as a Subthreshold Recognition Signal
**Description:** Subthreshold patients report disabling orthostatic symptoms yet show a sub-criterion HR rise — a reproducible gap between subjective impairment and the single measurable HR index. This *discrepancy itself* is the recognition signal: a patient with high orthostatic symptom burden and functional impairment but sub-threshold HR rise is exactly the population the consensus says is missed. Operationalize as a discrepancy index (symptom-severity vs HR-rise residual) that flags likely missed dysautonomia and triggers a broader autonomic workup (sudomotor, pupillometry, CBF, plasma volume). Connects to the corpus's subjective-measurable discrepancy work without duplicating it.
**Evidence:** Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44), Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44).
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Falsifiable:** The symptom-vs-HR discrepancy residual identifies subthreshold patients who, on fuller autonomic testing, have objective abnormalities (QSART, plasma volume, CBF) at a rate comparable to threshold POTS; if the discrepancy is just symptom-reporting noise, fuller testing finds no objective correlate.
**Non-specialist consequence:** When a patient feels severely affected but their heart-rate test looks normal, that mismatch could itself be the clue that more careful autonomic testing is needed.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 10 — Reasons the Mechanism May Not Be Relevant to ME/CFS

### C10.1: Threshold-Critique Overreach — Risk of Over-Diagnosis and Dilution of a Distinct Syndrome
**Mechanism/reason:** Broadening recognition to all "subthreshold dysautonomia" risks (a) over-diagnosis — labeling normal orthostatic variation or deconditioning as disease, and (b) diluting the POTS syndrome — the ≥30 bpm threshold, whatever its flaws, enforces specificity and separates POTS from IST, deconditioning, and anxiety-related tachycardia. Parsaik 2013 found only "mildly greater dysautonomia" in POTS vs OI-without-tachycardia; if the groups are that similar, the threshold may partly be doing useful work by keeping a narrower, more homogeneous syndrome identifiable. For ME/CFS specifically, adding a large, loosely-defined "non-POTS dysautonomia" category could inflate comorbidity rates, complicate differential diagnosis, and drive unnecessary testing/treatment in a population already prone to over-investigation. This is the strongest critical counterweight to the constructive ideas.
**Evidence:** Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), ChungRaj2026POTSReview (0.44). Note: search log query 6 (overdiagnosis) returned 0 added papers — over-diagnosis risk is asserted, not yet empirically demonstrated.
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Falsifiable:** If broadening recognition is safe, a threshold-free pathway in ME/CFS will NOT increase the rate of false-positive dysautonomia labels (measured against a gold-standard autonomic workup) nor materially increase unnecessary treatment starts; if false-positive rates rise, the critique is overreach.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Loosening the diagnosis could mean more patients labeled with a condition they may not have — trading one problem (missed patients) for another (over-diagnosed patients).
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 11 — Null-Hypothesis Assessment

### C11.1: What If "Non-POTS Dysautonomia" Carries No Prognostic or Therapeutic Meaning?
**Assessment:** The consensus names a subthreshold population and calls for classification, but NO paper shows that recognizing/reclassifying these patients changes outcomes. The null hypothesis: "non-POTS dysautonomia" is a descriptive convenience label, not a clinically useful construct — subthreshold patients, once identified, do not respond differently to treatment, do not have a distinct trajectory, and the label adds nothing beyond symptom management. If true, the existing-paper claims that would need revision are modest: Parsaik 2013 (the "share presentation/deconditioning" finding stands — it just becomes a null for the *recognition construct*, not for the shared biology), and Sivakoti 2026 (the consensus's research call loses force — a call for classification without demonstrated utility is premature). The core OI-in-ME/CFS findings are unaffected; only the *added value of the new label* is null.
**Evidence:** Parsaik2013OInoTachycardia (0.48), Sivakoti2026POTSConsensus (0.44), Boris2020HRnotPredictive (0.44).
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Falsifiable:** The null is supported if R2.1 finds subthreshold patients' trajectory and treatment response are indistinguishable from threshold-POTS patients once symptom burden is controlled (i.e., the label adds no prognostic information). It is refuted if subthreshold patients show a distinct, actionable pattern (e.g., better response to one drug, different progression).
**Non-specialist consequence:** It is possible that calling subthreshold dysautonomia a distinct condition changes nothing for patients — a name alone is not a treatment, and the label needs to prove it helps before it is worth using.
`origin: brainstorm`

## Category 12 — Evidence Quality Concerns

### C12.1: The Threshold-Critique Rests on Consensus and Single-Center Data — No Prospective Multi-Center Validation
**Assessment:** The strongest constructive claim (that the HR threshold is too restrictive and misses a real population) rests on: (a) a modified-Delphi expert consensus (Sivakoti 2026 — expert opinion, not empirical replication; discounted 0.44), (b) two single-center retrospective registries (Parsaik 2013 Mayo n=184; Boris 2020 CHOP), (c) a JAMA review (Chung & Raj 2026, no original data), and (d) one small RCT (Uppal 2026, n=28). No prospective multi-center study validates the subthreshold construct or the proposed threshold-free classification.
