# Brainstorm: Bakken2026 "Making Recovery Conceivable" (bakken2026-recovery-conceivable)

Date: 2026-10-11 | Stream: `bakken2026-recovery-conceivable` | Phase 4 (creative brainstorming)
Decision: PROCEED — all categories 1–12 allowed (no PARTIAL restriction).
Topic class: NON-treatment, NON-mechanistic — sociology / nosology / epistemology of the
BPS-vs-biomedical contest. Categories 3–9 (drug/supplement/intervention) are therefore
inapplicable or sparse; this is noted explicitly below rather than padded.

Primary source: Bakken AK, Mengshoel AM, Synnes O (2026). Qualitative reflexive thematic
analysis; 2 focus groups + 3 interviews; n=16 HCPs from medicine/psychology/physiotherapy/
nutrition/nursing, purposively sampled BECAUSE they use psychobehavioural CFS approaches.
Four themes: (1) no-man's-land (validation; experience ≠ explanation); (2) a new
explanatory "map" (BPS; two orientations — stress/overload/regulation vs maladaptive-
learning/predictive-processing); (3) navigating by the map (graded exposure, "retraining
the brain"); (4) maintaining trust in a contested field (epistemic trust; embodied
improvement; competing narratives; HCPs feel criticised/insecure).

Uncertainties quoted below are the DISCOUNTED Phase-1/2 values (already population-
weighted; e.g. Flückiger 0.70 raw meta-analysis × 0.75 general-population = 0.53).
Keys verified against `bib/stigma.bib` (case-exact).

## Category 1 — Novel hypotheses (epistemic/nosological, not yet in literature)

### Idea 1.1: "Recovery conceivability" is an independent, upstream epistemic variable (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.25
**Rationale:** Bakken's HCPs describe making recovery *conceivable* as the work that
precedes any biological or functional change @Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable. This suggests
conceivability — the capacity to imagine and credit the possibility of recovery — is not a
mere correlate of improvement but a causal precondition: a patient repeatedly invalidated
(epistemic-injustice stream) may have low epistemic trust such that they cannot uptake a new
explanatory map or enact graded change @Fonagy2014EpistemicTrust. Heins already shows
baseline outcome expectations predict CBT outcome (n=217, 0.55) @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship;
conceivability is the deeper, trust-anchored form of "expectations." This EXTENDS
@cf:therapeutic-alliance-embodied-improvement by committing to a directionality the anchor
leaves open: conceivability → engagement → improvement.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A baseline "recovery conceivability" instrument predicts
improvement trajectory after controlling for baseline fatigue, severity, AND generic
outcome expectations. Refuted if conceivability adds nothing beyond the expectations item
Heins already measured.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If true, restoring a patient's ability to imagine recovery
(through validation and felt evidence) is a legitimate clinical target in its own right,
upstream of any specific treatment model.

### Idea 1.2: The two BPS orientations imply two separable change mechanisms with different failure modes (subtype-matching hypothesis) (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.20
**Rationale:** Bakken documents two distinct orientations inside the BPS "map":
stress/overload/regulation vs maladaptive-learning/predictive-processing
@Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable. These are mechanistically different — a regulation/energy
account vs a learning/sensitization account — and should predict different responder
profiles and failure modes. A patient whose fatigue is energy-depletion-driven (biomedical)
may deteriorate under a "maladaptive learning / retraining the brain" framing that
reinterprets pacing as avoidance and PEM as symptom fear @Geraghty2019BPSHarm
@Thoma2023PsychosomaticHarmful. This EXTENDS bps-asymmetry / @sec:psychogenic-debate by
mapping the BPS camp's internal heterogeneity onto a testable response prediction.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If orientation is mere rhetoric, patients respond identically
under both framings; if subtype-matching is real, mismatched patients (e.g. PEM-severe under
a learning framing) show higher dropout/worsening than matched ones, after controlling for
baseline severity.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A low-cost way to reduce BPS harm would be to match the
explanatory framing to the patient's presentation rather than applying one map uniformly —
but this is untested.

### Idea 1.3: Embodied improvement is the trust gateway — improvement precedes and produces trust, not the reverse (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.25
**Rationale:** Bakken's HCPs report explanation alone is consistently insufficient; patients
need *felt* embodied improvement before the map becomes credible @Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable.
This implies a specific causal ordering — embodied improvement → epistemic trust → uptake —
rather than trust → engagement → improvement. The existing @cf:therapeutic-alliance-embodied-improvement
asserts alliance/embodied improvement is the operative ingredient but does not commit to an
ordering. A directionality claim is novel, testable, and clinically consequential.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In repeated-measure data (trust and improvement measured at
multiple time points), if trust reliably *precedes* improvement (Granger/temporal ordering)
the embodied-first hypothesis is weakened; if improvement leads trust, it is supported.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If improvement leads trust, the clinical priority is to
engineer any early *felt* win (however small) to open the trust channel — a strategy neutral
to whether that win is biomedical or behavioural.

### Idea 1.4: Epistemic (mis)trust in CFS is a bidirectional field property, not a patient deficit (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Rationale:** The epistemic-trust literature frames trust as something the patient has or
lacks @Fonagy2014EpistemicTrust @Fisher2025EpistemicTrustPanoramic. Bakken's HCPs show the
reverse pole: clinicians themselves distrust patient organisations, feel criticised, and are
explicitly insecure about future biomedical findings that might invalidate their approach
@Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable. This EXTENDS @cf:hcp-delicate-balancing-act by reframing it
through epistemic-trust theory: in contested illness, trust is a two-sided field condition —
mutual distrust between patient and clinician communities — that cannot be repaired by
treating "patient distrust" as a patient-side deficit. Cross's sociohistorical account
supplies the structural origin of this mutual distrust @Cross2026BiomedicalUncertainty.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If trust were one-sided (patient deficit only), clinician-side
epistemic-trust measures would be uncorrelated with patient outcomes; a bidirectional model
predicts patient and clinician trust are correlated within dyads and that both independently
predict engagement.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Reframes "patient distrust" (often blamed on patients) as a
systems-level trust collapse needing repair on both sides — relevant to how clinicians
approach skeptical patients.

### Idea 1.5: The "recovery" contest is partly definitional drift — one word, three incompatible referents (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 1
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Rationale:** Three different constructs are all called "recovery" in this evidence base:
strict full remission + premorbid function (physician definition, 0.40) @Devendorf2019RecoveryPhysician;
69% "no longer meeting CFS criteria" but only 23% "full recovery" (0.50) @Knoop2007FullRecoveryCBT;
and 44% "self-rated better" in RCTs (0.60) @Ingman2022RecoveryRCTReview. HCPs and patients
may talk past each other because the word has no shared referent, so the apparent contest over
"is recovery possible" is partly a semantic artifact layered on a real factual dispute. This
EXTENDS @sec:psychogenic-debate and the nosology strand.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If the contest were purely factual, parties would agree on a
definition and dispute only the evidence; if definitional, a survey experiment using a shared
recovery taxonomy measurably reduces reported conflict relative to the ambiguous term.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A shared recovery taxonomy (remission / meaningful improvement
/ functional recovery) could de-escalate a conflict currently amplified by one word carrying
three meanings.

## Category 2 — Research directions (experiments resolving key uncertainties)

### Idea 2.1: Prospective measurement of "recovery conceivability" and its predictive value (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a (design)
**Rationale:** Directly operationalizes Idea 1.1 and EXTENDS Heins's baseline-expectations
finding @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship. Design: n≥200 newly diagnosed; at baseline measure
recovery conceivability (bespoke instrument), epistemic trust, and outcome expectations; track
12–24 months with SF-36, actigraphy, and PEM frequency. Tests whether conceivability predicts
trajectory beyond generic expectations.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Conceivability adds incremental prediction over expectations; the
null is no increment.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A validated conceivability measure would let clinicians triage
who needs trust-building before any structured approach.

### Idea 2.2: Dismantling RCT to separate relational (trust/alliance) from content (BPS map) components (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a (design)
**Rationale:** Targets the stated limitation of @cf:therapeutic-alliance-embodied-improvement:
no trial has isolated the relational/trust component from the BPS *content* in CFS. Design:
3-arm — (a) BPS content + high supported alliance, (b) biomedical/pacing content + matched
alliance, (c) waitlist. If (a) and (b) converge, alliance is the operative ingredient and the
BPS map is a vehicle; if they diverge, content matters @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship
@Fluckiger2018AllianceMeta @Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Convergence of (a) and (b) kills the BPS-content-specific claim;
divergence sustains it.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Answers the field's central question — does the BPS map itself
help, or is it only a vehicle for a trusting relationship.

### Idea 2.3: Mirror qualitative study of HCPs who do NOT use (or abandoned) psychobehavioural approaches (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a (design)
**Rationale:** Bakken's sample is selected FOR BPS endorsement — its strongest confound
@Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable. A matched study of biomedical/pacing-oriented or
"disillusioned" HCPs tests whether the four themes (no-man's-land, map, navigation, trust)
generalize or are BPS-specific, and surfaces the counter-narrative from the clinician side,
balanced against the physician definition of recovery @Devendorf2019RecoveryPhysician and the
psychosomatic-critique position @Thoma2023PsychosomaticHarmful.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If the themes replicate across orientations they are features of
contested-illness care generally; if not, they are BPS-specific self-description.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Balances a literature currently skewed toward BPS-endorsing
clinician perspectives, informing whether "conceivability" is a real field property.

### Idea 2.4: Dyadic (patient–clinician) longitudinal study of bidirectional epistemic trust (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a (design)
**Rationale:** Tests Idea 1.4. Measure epistemic trust in BOTH members of patient–clinician
dyads over time, correlated with engagement and outcome @Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable
@Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship @Fonagy2014EpistemicTrust.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A one-sided model predicts clinician trust is inert; a
bidirectional model predicts clinician trust drives patient trust within dyads.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If clinician-side trust matters, training clinicians to
credit patient accounts becomes a lever on outcomes.

### Idea 2.5: "Recovery taxonomy" consensus study (Delphi / mixed-methods) across patients, clinicians, researchers (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 2
**Certainty:** n/a (design)
**Rationale:** Operationalizes Idea 1.5. Build a shared recovery taxonomy (remission /
meaningful improvement / functional recovery) and test whether its use reduces conflict and
improves communication, synthesizing the three incommensurate recovery definitions
@Devendorf2019RecoveryPhysician @Knoop2007FullRecoveryCBT @Ingman2022RecoveryRCTReview and the
patient-side recovery narrative @Ingman2025RecoveryThematic.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A shared taxonomy measurably increases inter-stakeholder agreement
vs the ambiguous term.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A shared vocabulary could remove the definitional layer of a
conflict that otherwise looks purely factual.

## Category 3 — Drug / medication ideas — INAPPLICABLE

No drug angle. The topic concerns relational/epistemic processes (trust, conceivability,
explanatory framing), not pharmacology. Forcing a repurposing idea would be padding and could
mislead. Explicitly not attempted. The single indirect link — that any future disease-modifying
agent producing *felt* improvement would also build trust (per Idea 1.3) — is a category-1
directionality claim, not a drug idea.

## Category 4 — Supplement / nutraceutical ideas — INAPPLICABLE

Same as Category 3. No micronutrient/botanical/dietary mechanism intersects the epistemic-
trust construct. Explicitly not attempted.

## Category 5 — Non-pharmacological interventions — SUBSUMED (sparse)

The only "intervention" in scope is clinician communication practice (validation, embodied-
improvement scaffolding, framing choice). This is fully subsumed under Categories 1–2 (Idea
1.2 framing-matching; Idea 2.2 dismantling; Idea 2.1 conceivability triage). No distinct
breathing/pacing/rehabilitation idea belongs here for a non-treatment topic. Not padded.

## Category 6 — Combinations + access (one honest idea)

### Idea 6.1: "Conceivability of recovery" is a specialist privilege — an access/equity gap (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 6
**Certainty:** 0.20
**Rationale:** Bakken's HCPs are specialist, and their patients are self-selected and
receptive @Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable. Patients with prior invalidation — the documented
epistemic-injustice stream @Byrne2021EpistemicInjustice @Ireson2022EpistemicInjusticeLC — or
who face primary-care dismissal @Bayliss2014BarriersPrimaryCare may never reach a clinician
who scaffolds conceivability. Hypothesis: conceivability-scaffolding care is unevenly
distributed, and its absence is an access inequity, not merely a patient characteristic.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a population sample, receipt of conceivability-scaffolding care
correlates with specialist access and prior dismissal history, independent of disease severity.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If conceivability is a scarce resource, equity requires it not
be confined to BPS-oriented specialist clinics — a health-services argument, not a clinical one.

## Category 7 — Mathematical model extensions (one conceptual idea)

### Idea 7.1: Model "recovery conceivability" as a trust-gated learning rate in belief update (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 7
**Certainty:** 0.20
**Rationale:** The paper's formalization strand (DAG/EPC/ODE) has no epistemic variable.
Conceptual extension: epistemic trust gates the rate at which a patient updates their illness
model in response to embodied evidence — a Bayesian learning-rate parameter that is near zero
under low trust @Fonagy2014EpistemicTrust @Fisher2025EpistemicTrustPanoramic. This explains
why "providing the correct explanation" fails for low-trust patients: it is a learning-rate
problem, not an information problem @Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship.
Flagged as conceptual/borderline — not a mechanistic cascade.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Trust interventions should accelerate recovery-model updating,
measurable as faster shifts in self-rated illness understanding after a controlled embodied-
evidence exposure.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Formalizes a clinical intuition — for a distrustful patient,
no amount of correct information moves them until trust is restored first.

## Category 8 — Cross-disease bridges

### Idea 8.1: "Recovery conceivability" and epistemic trust generalize across contested illnesses (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 8
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Rationale:** The alliance→outcome association is transdiagnostic (r≈0.28, meta-analysis of
295 studies) @Fluckiger2018AllianceMeta, and epistemic trust is framed as a general
psychotherapy mechanism @Fonagy2014EpistemicTrust @Fisher2025EpistemicTrustPanoramic. Bakken's
four themes (no-man's-land, map, navigation, trust) should replicate in fibromyalgia,
functional neurological disorder, IBS, chronic Lyme, and long COVID. Prediction: conceivability
predicts outcome in each contested illness, and the four-theme structure is illness-general
rather than CFS-specific.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A replication of Bakken's design in another contested illness
yields the same four themes; refuted if the themes are CFS-specific.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Would let a validated conceivability measure and trust-
scaffolding practice transfer across contested illnesses rather than being rebuilt for each.

### Idea 8.2: The "no-man's-land" is a structural property of any illness unowned by a specialty — long COVID as the live test (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 8
**Certainty:** 0.25
**Rationale:** Cross shows ME/CFS materialized through the regulation of biomedical
uncertainty — an illness no specialty owns @Cross2026BiomedicalUncertainty. The "no-man's-land"
Bakken's HCPs describe @Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable is that structural gap as experienced
from inside. Long COVID is currently being negotiated into or out of psychobehavioural framing
@Geraghty2019BPSHarm; the same no-man's-land → map → navigation → trust sequence should
reproduce. Prediction: long COVID will repeat the sequence on a compressed timescale.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Long-COVID clinician interviews (matched design) reproduce the
four themes; refuted if long COVID's biomedical anchoring prevents the no-man's-land from
forming.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Predicts, before the literature catches up, that long COVID
will relitigate the same psychosomatic-vs-biomedical contest ME/CFS did.

## Category 9 — Diagnostic / biomarker ideas (one light idea)

### Idea 9.1: Epistemic-trust / readiness screen as a treatment-readiness stratifier (not a disease biomarker) (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 9
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Rationale:** Heins shows baseline expectations and task agreement predict post-treatment
fatigue (0.55) @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship, and alliance predicts outcome transdiagnostically
@Fluckiger2018AllianceMeta. A brief readiness screen (trust + conceivability) could stratify
who should receive trust-building/validation-first care vs who is ready for a structured
approach — a *predict-response* stratification tool, not a disease marker. Explicitly not
positioned as a biological biomarker.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Readiness at baseline predicts which patients benefit from
structured vs validation-first approaches (interaction effect).
**Non-specialist consequence:** A cheap screen could route the right patients to the right
care model, reducing both under-treatment and iatrogenic activation.

## Category 10 — Reasons this mechanism may NOT be relevant to ME/CFS (MANDATORY)

### Idea 10.1: "Conceivability" may be a relabeling of placebo/expectation effects, not a distinct epistemic mechanism (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 10
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Rationale:** If "recovery conceivability" is just outcome expectation (which Heins already
measures) @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship, then the epistemic-trust apparatus adds nothing
beyond known non-specific factors. The alliance→outcome correlation (r≈0.28) is consistent
with common-factors and placebo accounts @Fluckiger2018AllianceMeta @Bordin1979WorkingAlliance,
not evidence of a special epistemic mechanism. The distinctness of the construct is the weak
link.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Conceivability predicts outcome NO better than generic expectation
after controlling for baseline severity.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If it is placebo under another name, clinicians gain nothing
new from the epistemic-trust language beyond what expectation management already offers.

### Idea 10.2: The four themes may be an artifact of purposive sampling of BPS-endorsing HCPs and self-selected receptive patients (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 10
**Certainty:** 0.40
**Rationale:** The study samples clinicians BECAUSE they use psychobehavioural approaches,
describing patients presumably receptive to and improved by them @Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable.
This cannot establish that conceivability/trust is causal; it may describe a mutual-validation
narrative — the clinicians' account of why their approach works, unconstrained by a
no-improvement or deterioration group. The authors themselves flag this gap.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A matched sample of non-BPS HCPs (Idea 2.3) fails to reproduce
the four themes, or reproduces them but with the opposite valence.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The strongest reason to treat the "recovery mechanism" reading
as descriptive, not evidence — the causal claim is not warranted by the design.

### Idea 10.3: Alliance/trust, even if real and general, is treatment-content-neutral — it does not rescue the BPS map specifically (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 10
**Certainty:** 0.45
**Rationale:** The alliance→outcome association is robust but content-agnostic
@Fluckiger2018AllianceMeta @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship. A good alliance would equally
improve biomedical or pacing-based care. Therefore "recovery conceivability" is relevant to
the therapeutic relationship generally and says nothing about whether the BPS *map* is correct.
Any idea that recruits Bakken to defend the BPS causal account of ME/CFS overreaches; the
evidence supports only a relational claim.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In Idea 2.2's dismantling trial, alliance-matched biomedical/pacing
content produces outcomes equivalent to BPS content.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Keeps the alliance finding from being co-opted as vindication
of the psychobehavioural model's content — a boundary the paper's healing-norms stance requires.

### Idea 10.4: Embodied improvement may simply be natural history / spontaneous remission, narrated retrospectively as treatment success (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 10
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Rationale:** CFS has a heterogeneous natural course; some patients improve regardless of
treatment. The "embodied improvement → trust" sequence @Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable may be
natural recovery being retrospectively narrated. Even the strongest CBT recovery estimate —
23% full recovery @Knoop2007FullRecoveryCBT — could partly reflect natural remitters, since
no trial fully controls for it (self-rated better 44% vs 11% worse @Ingman2022RecoveryRCTReview).
**Falsifiable prediction:** A no-treatment comparator with matched baseline shows similar
improvement trajectories to the BPS arm.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Reminds that felt improvement attributed to the map may be the
illness running its own course — central to any honest "conceivability" claim.

### Idea 10.5: The epistemic-trust construct is itself empirically unvalidated as a driver of change (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 10
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Rationale:** Fisher's own review notes empirical support for epistemic trust as a change
mechanism remains limited @Fisher2025EpistemicTrustPanoramic, and the foundational source is
conceptual, not empirical @Fonagy2014EpistemicTrust. Building a ME/CFS-specific hypothesis on
an unvalidated transdiagnostic construct is premature — the construct may not exist as a
measurable, manipulable driver.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Direct manipulation of epistemic trust (a trust intervention)
produces no change in outcome beyond generic relationship quality.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Caution against importing a fashionable but unvalidated
construct into ME/CFS care guidance as if it were established.

## Category 11 — Null hypothesis assessment (MANDATORY)

### Idea 11.1: Null — "recovery conceivability"/epistemic trust has no specific role in CFS recovery beyond generic non-specific factors (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 11
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Rationale:** Under the null, the two new anchors — @cf:therapeutic-alliance-embodied-improvement
(cert 0.53) and @cf:hcp-delicate-balancing-act (cert 0.35) — must be reframed from "operative
ingredient / mechanism" claims to "descriptions of non-specific therapy factors," and the
alliance correlation @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship @Fluckiger2018AllianceMeta is read as
common factors, not a trust mechanism.
**Falsifiable prediction:** The dismantling trial (Idea 2.2) finds no alliance-specific or
trust-specific effect beyond generic relationship quality.
**Non-specialist consequence:** If the null holds, the paper should keep the harm and injustice
evidence but soften any claim that trust/embodied-improvement is a *mechanism* of recovery.

### Idea 11.2: The null is narrower than it looks — it does NOT threaten the harm or epistemic-injustice claims (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 11
**Certainty:** 0.50
**Rationale:** The harm evidence @Geraghty2019BPSHarm @Thoma2023PsychosomaticHarmful and the
epistemic-injustice stream @Byrne2021EpistemicInjustice @Ireson2022EpistemicInjusticeLC
@Buchner2025Psychologization stand regardless of whether trust mediates recovery: invalidation
and iatrogenic harm are documented independently of any recovery mechanism. The null only kills
the "trust → recovery" mechanism claim; it leaves the "psychologization harms" and "patients
are disbelieved" claims intact @ach:psychologization-five-harms.
**Falsifiable prediction:** n/a (boundary statement — clarifies what the null falsifies vs
what it does not).
**Non-specialist consequence:** Prevents an over-broad null from being used to dismiss the
harm evidence along with the recovery-mechanism claim — the two must be adjudicated separately.

### Idea 11.3: Minimum design to reject the null (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 11
**Certainty:** n/a (design)
**Rationale:** The dismantling RCT (Idea 2.2) is the decisive test. Two nested falsifications:
(a) alliance-matched biomedical/pacing content ≈ BPS content → the BPS-content-specific claim
is dead but alliance survives; (b) manipulating alliance produces no effect → the entire
trust-mechanism framing is dead and the anchors revert to non-specific-factor descriptions.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Outcomes in (a) and (b) relative to control decide which layer of
the claim survives.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Gives the field a clean, single experiment that settles how
much of "recovery conceivability" is real vs rhetoric.

## Category 12 — Evidence quality concerns (MANDATORY)

### Idea 12.1: Primary-source selection bias — 16 BPS-endorsing HCPs, no comparison group (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 12
**Certainty:** 0.40
**Rationale:** Bakken purposively sampled HCPs BECAUSE they use psychobehavioural approaches
@Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable. Single Norwegian specialist context, no non-BPS or disaffected
comparison, no deterioration cases. The four themes' prevalence and valence cannot be
established from this design; the authors flag it.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The study tells us how one camp sees its own practice, not how
the field as a whole works.

### Idea 12.2: Certainty inflation risk — the CFS-specific alliance evidence rests on ONE unreplicated cohort (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 12
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Rationale:** The cert 0.53 carried by @cf:therapeutic-alliance-embodied-improvement leans on
Heins (n=217, single cohort, unreplicated) @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship plus a
general-population meta-analysis discounted to 0.53 @Fluckiger2018AllianceMeta. The two
foundational sources are conceptual, non-empirical @Bordin1979WorkingAlliance
@Fonagy2014EpistemicTrust. The "alliance predicts CFS outcome" claim is a single study.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A single unreplicated cohort should temper how strongly the
paper asserts alliance as an established CFS mechanism.

### Idea 12.3: Measurement validity — "recovery" is heterogeneous across sources, so cross-study synthesis compares incomparable constructs (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 12
**Certainty:** 0.45
**Rationale:** Full remission @Devendorf2019RecoveryPhysician vs not-meeting-criteria vs 23%
full recovery @Knoop2007FullRecoveryCBT vs 44% self-rated better @Ingman2022RecoveryRCTReview
are different outcomes. No single metric unifies them, so any claim about "recovery rates" is
a composite of incomparable constructs (the same point as Idea 1.5, stated as a quality
concern rather than a hypothesis).
**Non-specialist consequence:** Any headline recovery number in the paper must name which
recovery definition it uses, or it silently inflates.

### Idea 12.4: Reverse causality and baseline-severity confounding in the alliance–outcome correlation (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 12
**Certainty:** 0.40
**Rationale:** Alliance/trust correlations cannot distinguish cause from consequence: patients
who improve may rate the relationship better retrospectively, and less-severe patients may both
improve more and form better alliances @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship
@Fluckiger2018AllianceMeta. The correlational design is a genuine ceiling on the causal reading.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The alliance finding supports "trust matters," not "trust
causes recovery" — a distinction the paper must preserve.

### Idea 12.5: Generalizability gap — no severity stratification anywhere in this evidence (origin: brainstorm)
**Category:** 12
**Certainty:** 0.40
**Rationale:** None of the alliance sources stratify ME/CFS severity (Heins is Fukuda-criteria
ambulatory) @Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship; Bakken does not report patient severity
@Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable. "Embodied improvement" is plausibly rarer and trust harder to
earn in severe/very-severe bedbound patients, so the whole conceivability/embodied-improvement
mechanism may not transfer to the most disabled subgroup — where harm from activation is
highest @Geraghty2019BPSHarm.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The most disabled patients, where the stakes are highest, are
precisely the group this mechanism is least evidenced for.
