## Category 1 — Novel Hypotheses

### 1.1 Bidirectional mood–inflammation loop in ME/CFS
Depression/anxiety/PTSD-associated inflammation (Kuring 2023) and ME/CFS neuroinflammation may form a
bidirectional loop: pre-existing inflammatory mental-state drives CNS pathology, and CNS
neuroinflammation secondarily degrades mood. Certainty 0.45. Falsifiable: prospective longitudinal
sampling can order mental-symptom onset vs inflammatory-marker rise. *Consequence:* if true, mood and
inflammation should be treated as a coupled system, not independently.
(*Note: this is the Phase 3 ch14d speculation; retained here as the anchor idea.*)

### 1.2 Comorbidity-masking inflates ME/CFS IL-6/TNF-α claims
The dementia experience (excluding comorbid inflammatory conditions removed IL-6/TNF-α elevations)
suggests some ME/CFS IL-6/TNF-α elevations are comorbidity-driven. Certainty 0.45.
*Consequence:* comorbidity-screened ME/CFS cohorts may show a different (possibly weaker) IL-6/TNF-α
signal. (*This is the Phase 3 ch08 limitation; retained as anchor.*)

### 1.3 Th17/myeloid-axis shared-signal hypothesis
G-CSF, GM-CSF, IL-17F (severity-correlated in ME/CFS; Montoya) AND IL-17A, G-CSF, GM-CSF (elevated in AD;
Kuring) may reflect a shared Th17/myeloid hematopoiesis axis operational across chronic
inflammatory-CNS conditions. Certainty 0.30. Falsifiable: comorbidity-screened ME/CFS cohorts will show
G-CSF/GM-CSF elevated vs controls with a Th17-correlated profile. *Consequence:* identifies a specific
measurable axis (not vague "inflammation") worth testing as a ME/CFS biomarker.

## Category 2 — Research Directions

### 2.1 Comorbidity-screened ME/CFS cytokine meta-analysis
Run a Kuring-style meta-analysis on ME/CFS that EXCLUDES comorbid inflammatory conditions, to isolate
disease-inherent (vs comorbidity-driven) markers. Certainty n/a (proposal). *Consequence:* would
sharpen which ME/CFS marker findings are robust vs comorbidity-inflated.

### 2.2 Longitudinal mental-symptom vs cytokine-timecourse study
Prospective study in ME/CFS measuring both mood scores and inflammatory markers over time to resolve
the direction of the mood–inflammation relation. Certainty n/a. *Consequence:* resolves a key
direction-of-causality unknown.

## Category 3 — Drug/Medication Ideas

### 3.1 Anti-Th17 / anti-IL-17 strategies as mood-and-inflammation probes
If the Th17/myeloid axis is shared across AD/ME/CFS, IL-17 blockade (e.g., secukinumab, a biologic) could
serve as a research-stage probe for both mood and fatigue — but this is research-context only, has no
ME/CFS trial data, and requires harm/interaction screens. Certainty 0.15. *Consequence:* speculative,
decades from clinical application; not actionable now.

## Category 4 — Supplement/Nutraceutical Ideas

### 4.1 G-CSF/GM-CSF-axis-modulating nutraceuticals
No validated nutraceutical directly modulates the G-CSF/GM-CSF axis at doses safe for ME/CFS. Some
polyphenol/anti-inflammatory agents broadly dampen myelopoietic cytokine output. Certainty 0.10,
highly speculative. Skip as low-value for this topic.

## Category 5 — Non-pharmacological Interventions

### 5.1 Stress-reduction as a mood–inflammation intervention in the loop
The bidirectional model implies that interventions reducing inflammatory mental-state (stress
management, pacing of emotional exertion) could partially dampen the CNS-inflammation loop.
Certainty 0.20. *Consequence:* reinforces pacing/energy-management as not merely psychological but as
an inflammatory-burden-reducing intervention. (*Note: overlaps with existing pacing coverage; likely
dedup/covered-by-existing.*)

## Category 6 — Combinations + Access

### 6.1 Comorbidity-audit clinical tool for inflammatory-marker interpretation
A clinical checklist/tool to interpret a patient's CRP/cytokine panel in light of comorbid inflammatory
conditions before concluding "ME/CFS inflammation." Certainty n/a. *Consequence:* improves test
interpretation for clinicians; low-risk, practical.

## Category 7 — Mathematical Model Extensions

### 7.1 Bidirectional mood–inflammation coupling node in the causal ODE/DAG
Add a bidirectional coupling edge (mood ↔ inflammation) to the CNS-energy DAG, parameterized so the
wild-type direction is determinable from longitudinal data. Certainty n/a. *Consequence:* makes the
direction-of-causality question formally testable in the model.

## Category 8 — Cross-Disease Bridges

### 8.1 Dementia ↔ ME/CFS inflammatory-template bridge
The inflammatory-mediated-neurodegeneration template (dementia) and the ME/CFS sickness-behavior/
neuroinflammation model share the trigger→neuroinflammation→CNS-pathology form. Certainty 0.45.
*Consequence:* strengthens the generalizability of the neuroinflammation model across chronic
post-viral/inflammatory-CNS conditions. (*This is the Phase 3 ch19/ch14d anchor theme.*)

## Category 9 — Diagnostic/Biomarker Ideas

### 9.1 Comorbidity-adjusted cytokine interpretation for ME/CFS
Interpret IL-6/TNF-α/G-CSF/GM-CSF values relative to the comorbid-inflammatory-disease burden.
Certainty n/a. *Consequence:* more accurate biomarker interpretation; low risk.

## Category 10 — MANDATORY: Why this mechanism may NOT be relevant to ME/CFS

### 10.1 Inflammation may be epiphenomenal in ME/CFS
If the ME/CFS inflammatory signal is weak/inconsistent (Blundell 2015: only TGF-β consistent; Nakatomi
2014 TSPO-PET elevation not replicated), the entire inflammation-mediated model could be a red herring
for a disease more fundamentally driven by bioenergetic/metabolic failure. Certainty 0.60 (caution).
*Consequence:* over-emphasizing inflammation could misdirect treatment toward anti-inflammatories that
don't help an energy- deficit disease.

### 10.2 Causality may run the other direction even in dementia
Kuring's own data cannot exclude prodromal or epiphenomenal inflammation. If so, the "template" would
not transfer to ME/CFS as a causal model at all. Certainty n/a (open). *Consequence:* keeps the
direction-of-causality honest.

## Category 11 — MANDATORY: Null hypothesis assessment

### 11.1 What if inflammation has no role in ME/CFS?
Existing weak/null cytokine findings (Blundell 2015, heterogeneity in Strawbridge 2019) would require
revision of the sickness-behavior/neuroinflammation claims in ch08/ch09/ch19 toward a purely
bioenergetic-primary model. Certainty n/a. *Consequence:* the paper should keep the 
inflammation-as-amplifier (not root-cause) framing, which it largely already does.

## Category 12 — MANDATORY: Evidence quality concerns

### 12.1 Same-group provenance and cross-sectional limits
Kuring 2026 and 2023 are the same research group testing the same hypothesis — the "mental-illness
inflammation in dementia" claim rests on one group's meta-approaches, and included primary studies are
cross-sectional/observational (cannot establish causality). Cohort/methodological overlap reduces
independent-evidence weight. Certainty n/a. *Consequence:* the dementia template should be framed as a
hypothesis-generation parallel, not independent replicated causal proof.

### 12.2 Diagnostic-era and marker heterogeneity
Dementia diagnoses are clinical (not biomarker-confirmed, possible mixed-dementia); most markers were
single-study; ME/CFS primary studies use heterogeneous case definitions. *Consequence:* cross-study
claims must carry the diagnostic- criteria caveat.
