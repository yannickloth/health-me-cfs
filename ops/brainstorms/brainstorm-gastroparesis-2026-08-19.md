## Category 1: Novel Hypotheses

### Idea 1.1 — Autoimmune Ganglionic AChR Blockade as a Reversible Cause of ME/CFS Gastroparesis (Montalvo-Model Generalization)
- **Certainty:** 0.25
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Montalvo 2022 documents post-SARS-CoV-2 autoimmune GI dysmotility with α3 nicotinic ganglionic AChR antibodies, delayed gastric emptying on scintigraphy, and dramatic response to IVIG. If ME/CFS (post-infectious in a majority) shares this autoimmune ganglionic-blockade mechanism, gastroparesis would be a *reversible, immunomodulation-responsive* lesion rather than fixed structural degeneration. This is distinct from the Phase 3 open-question (which asks whether ME/CFS shares ICC/enteric structural loss); the novel claim is a *ganglionic* autoimmune lesion, distinguishable pharmacologically (ganglionic nAChR blockade responds to cholinesterase inhibitors + immunotherapy, whereas structural loss does not).
- **Evidence link:** Montalvo2022LongCovidGIDysmotility (α3 nAChR autoantibody AGID, IVIG response); cross-ref already-integrated vagal-cascade "ganglionic/peripheral (AChR autoantibodies at autonomic ganglia)" lesion level (ch34 subsec-02).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A seroprevalence assay for ganglionic nAChR antibodies in a modern-criteria ME/CFS cohort stratified by GI-dominant phenotype will show a higher titre/prevalence in the GI-dominant subset than in non-GI ME/CFS or healthy controls; if no such enrichment exists, the ganglionic-autoimmune mechanism is not the driver of ME/CFS gastroparesis.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If true, some ME/CFS patients' stomach-slowing could be driven by a reversible autoimmune process treatable with immunotherapy rather than an irreversible nerve-loss state.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 1.2 — Vagal Afferent/Efferent Dissociation: Normal Satiety Signals With Failed Motility Effector Response
- **Certainty:** 0.20
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The Wirth–Scheibenbogen cascade (already integrated in ch34 subsec-02) posits gut-dysbiosis → reduced butyrate → reduced EC-cell serotonin → impaired 5-HT3-mediated *afferent* signaling → reduced *efferent* tone. Gastroparesis symptoms (early satiety, nausea) are classically read as afferent-driven, while delayed emptying is efferent-driven. The novel dissociation hypothesis: ME/CFS gastroparesis patients can have *intact or even heightened* vagal afferent signalling (hence intense nausea/early satiety) while *efferent* gastric motor tone fails. This would explain why symptom severity and emptying delay are only loosely coupled, and why centrally-acting anti-emetics (which target the afferent/chemoreceptor axis) can relieve nausea without fixing emptying.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (emptying delay symptom-correlated but not perfectly — 23/32 liquid vs 12/32 solid); Debourdeau2024GastricVolumetry (gastroparesis overlaps visceral hypersensitivity — afferent axis); cross-ref vagal-cascade afferent→efferent transmission step (ch34 subsubsec-03).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Paired measurement of vagal afferent function (e.g., area-postrema/chemoreceptor response, or symptom-response to a 5-HT3 antagonist) and efferent gastric emptying (GES) will identify patients with a normal/near-normal afferent readout but delayed emptying, and vice versa; if afferent and efferent indices always track together, the dissociation claim fails.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If real, it means treating nausea (afferent) and treating slow emptying (efferent) are separate goals needing separate drugs — one does not guarantee the other.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 1.3 — Loss of Nitrergic (nNOS) Inhibitory Innervation of the Pylorus, Not Just Antral Hypomotility
- **Certainty:** 0.20
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Classic gastroparesis models focus on antral hypomotility, but a mechanistically distinct contributor is loss of nitrergic (nNOS) inhibitory neurons of the pylorus, causing pylorospasm — a functional outlet obstruction that slows emptying independent of antral contractile force. The existing Phase 3 open-question targets ICC/enteric *excitatory* loss; the nNOS/pyloric-inhibitory angle is a distinct and frequently separable lesion with a different, mechanistically targeted treatment (pyloric intervention / nitrergic-support). In ME/CFS, where vagal denervation is documented cross-disease, an nNOS-pyloric component is plausible and currently unexplored.
- **Evidence link:** Grover2011CellularChangesGastroparesis (enteric nerve loss in gastric antral tissue — includes inhibitory neural elements); the nNOS-gastroparesis literature flagged in search-log (query 31) but not integrated; cross-ref vagal-efferent gastric-motility consequence (ch34 subsec-02 item c).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Pyloric manometry or pyloric-pressure assessment in ME/CFS gastroparesis will show elevated basal pyloric tone / failure of pyloric relaxation on gastric distension in a subgroup, and GES delay in that subgroup will be predominantly in the later (distal/pyloric) phase; if pyloric tone is normal and delay is purely antral, the pyloric-obstruction component is falsified.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If a pyloric component exists, a subset of patients could respond to pylorus-targeted treatment rather than general stomach-stimulant drugs.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 1.4 — Enteric (ICC/Enteric-Nerve) Loss Secondary to Chronic Vagal Efferent Failure — a "Disuse Degeneration" Hypothesis
- **Certainty:** 0.15
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Standard models treat vagal denervation and enteric/ICC loss as independent. An alternative bidirectional hypothesis: chronic vagal-efferent failure (the documented ME/CFS/Long-COVID cholinergic denervation) itself drives secondary ICC and enteric-neuron degeneration through loss of trophic/signalling input. Under this model, ME/CFS gastroparesis that *looks* like diabetic/idiopathic ICC loss (the Phase 3 open-question) is actually a late-stage consequence of the vagal lesion, not a separate enteric disease — meaning early vagal-targeted intervention could prevent structural loss, and the ICC-loss finding would not imply a distinct enteric-autoimmune process.
- **Evidence link:** Grover2011CellularChangesGastroparesis + Wang2009ICCLossDiabetes (ICC/enteric loss associated with delayed emptying); Acanfora-2026 cross-ref (vagal cholinergic gastric denervation already integrated in the Long-COVID stream); vagal-cascade lesion-level framing (ch34 subsec-02).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In an animal model of selective vagal gastric denervation (or in serial human biopsy), ICC/enteric-nerve density will decline over time following denervation; if ICC density remains normal after prolonged vagal denervation, the "disuse degeneration" pathway is falsified and ICC loss must be independently driven.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If true, treating the underlying nerve damage early could prevent the stomach's pacemaker cells from being lost later — a reason to intervene sooner rather than wait.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 1.5 — Area-Postrema GLP-1 / Vagal Convergence: Endogenous GLP-1 Excess as a Contributor to ME/CFS Gastroparesis
- **Certainty:** 0.15
- **Mechanistic rationale:** GLP-1 receptor agonists *slow* gastric emptying (documented counterindication here). The cross-ref speculation (spec-area-postrema-gpcr-glp1-gate) already flags GLP-1 as an area-postrema convergence point in ME/CFS. The novel angle: if ME/CFS has chronically elevated *endogenous* GLP-1 (e.g., from ileal stimulation via small-bowel dysmotility/SIBO, or heightened area-postrema sensitivity), that endogenous GLP-1 could itself drive gastric emptying delay and anorexia — making gastroparesis partly a GLP-1-mediated symptom, not purely a vagal-motor failure. This is testable (plasma GLP-1) and pharmacologically tractable (GLP-1-receptor antagonism is investigational), and it links the already-integrated area-postrema speculation to the gastroparesis mechanism.
- **Evidence link:** spec-area-postrema-gpcr-glp1-gate (ch18, already-integrated speculation, notes gastroparesis as a GLP-1 risk); search-log query 28 (GLP-1-RA delayed emptying, 247 hits, not integrated) confirms the emptying-slowing pharmacology; cross-ref vagal-cascade EC-serotonin step (ch34 subsubsec-02).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Fasting and postprandial plasma GLP-1 will be elevated in ME/CFS patients with delayed gastric emptying compared to matched controls, and the emptying delay will correlate with GLP-1 AUC; if GLP-1 is normal and unrelated to emptying, the endogenous-GLP-1 contribution is falsified.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If true, it would mean avoiding treatments that raise GLP-1 and possibly targeting GLP-1 could help restore normal stomach emptying — a lever that today is only treated as a risk, not a cause.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

## Category 2: Research Directions

### Idea 2.1 — Modern-Criteria Gastric-Emptying Scintigraphy Cohort Stratified by Severity (Retiring the Fukuda-Era Anchor)
- **Certainty:** 0.60 (feasibility/design confidence)
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The single direct ME/CFS emptying cohort (Burnet 2004, n=32) is Fukuda-era and not severity-stratified, and no modern-IOM/ICC-criteria cohort exists. The core uncertainty for the urgent severe case is *prevalence and severity-dependence* of delayed emptying under current diagnostic criteria. A pre-registered GES cohort using ICC/IOM criteria, stratified into mild/moderate/severe/very-severe, with standardized 4-hour protocol, would convert the entire gastroparesis-in-ME/CFS claim from a single old finding into a replicable, severity-anchored estimate.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (the cohort to replicate/refute); Wise2021GastricEmptyingScans (protocol non-adherence is a known confound — the design must enforce the standardized protocol); synthesis gap note (no modern-criteria cohort).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Under standardized GES, the prevalence of delayed emptying will (a) replicate Burnet's magnitude in the whole cohort and (b) be higher in severe/very-severe than mild strata; if prevalence is low in severe patients or does not track severity, the severe-case relevance claim weakens.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A modern study would tell patients and clinicians with confidence how common real stomach-slowing is across illness severity, replacing a 20-year-old single result.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 2.2 — GCSI + GES Concordance: Questionnaire vs Objective Emptying as a Dysautonomia-Severity Readout
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Subjective symptom burden (nausea/early-satiety) and objective emptying delay are imperfectly coupled (Burnet shows 23/32 liquid vs 12/32 solid; the afferent/efferent dissociation idea 1.2 predicts discordance). Testing the concordance between a validated symptom index (GCSI) and objective GES in the same modern ME/CFS cohort would clarify whether (a) gastroparesis symptoms in ME/CFS are afferent/dysautonomia-driven (poor GESI-GES concordance) or (b) genuine emptying failure (good concordance). This determines which treatment axis (anti-nausea/afferent vs prokinetic/efferent) is right.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (symptom correlation); Debourdeau2024GastricVolumetry (visceral-hypersensitivity overlap muddies symptom–emptying mapping); search-log query 32 (GCSI validation, not integrated).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In the modern ME/CFS cohort, GCSI symptom scores will correlate only weakly with objective GES retention (r below a pre-specified threshold), indicating afferent/visceral-hypersensitivity dominance; if GCSI and GES correlate strongly, the afferent-dominant dissociation model is falsified and symptom scores can proxy emptying.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Knowing whether reported symptoms track real emptying would tell clinicians whether to trust symptom questionnaires or order an emptying scan before treating.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 2.3 — Gastric Antral Biopsy + Skin Biopsy Paired in the Same ME/CFS Patient (Vagal vs Enteric vs Somatic Origin Discriminator)
- **Certainty:** 0.50 (feasibility)
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The Phase 3 open-question asks whether ME/CFS gastroparesis shares enteric/ICC loss but no histology exists. The prior vagal plan proposed a paired *gastric+skin* biopsy (2.1) resolving visceral-vs-somatic origin. A distinct, sharper angle: combine gastric antral histology (ICC/enteric density, nNOS) with *skin* intra-epidermal nerve-fibre density and, where feasible, with a serological ganglionic-AChR panel in the SAME patient. This three-way readout separates: enteric-structural loss (antral ICC/enteric low, skin normal), generalized SFN (both low), and ganglionic-autoimmune blockade (histology normal, AChR antibodies present). That discrimination directly resolves the Phase 3 open-question and the autoimmune hypothesis 1.1 in one design.
- **Evidence link:** Grover2011CellularChangesGastroparesis + Wang2009ICCLossDiabetes (what antral histology would show if ME/CFS matches diabetic/idiopathic); Montalvo2022LongCovidGIDysmotility (AChR panel target); Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (which patients to select — emptying-confirmed).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Among emptying-confirmed ME/CFS patients, the ICC/enteric-nNOS density on antral biopsy will be either normal (supporting functional/ganglionic origin, falsifying structural-shared-pathology) or reduced (supporting structural loss, falsifying pure-functional origin) — the study cleanly decides the two-armed open question rather than leaving it open.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** One biopsy-and-blood panel could finally tell patients whether their slow stomach is a structural, a generalized-nerve, or an autoimmune problem — each with a different treatment path.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 2.4 — Serial GES + Symptom/PEM Mapping Around a PEM Episode (State vs Trait Gastric Slowing)
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** All ME/CFS emptying data are cross-sectional. Whether gastric emptying delay is a fixed trait or worsens acutely during a PEM episode (a state marker) is unknown. This determines clinical use: a trait marker stratifies chronic severity; a state marker tracks disease flares and might explain post-exertional nausea/feeding collapse. Serial GES (or a non-radioactive breath-test substitute to permit repeated measurement) around a standardized exertion challenge would answer it.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (cross-sectional baseline); Gupta2016GastroparesisDiet (feeding-strategy relevance for flares); synthesis note (no longitudinal emptying data).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** GES retention (or breath-test half-time) will increase significantly during the PEM/post-exertional window relative to baseline in ME/CFS patients; if emptying is stable across the PEM window, gastric slowing is a trait, not a state marker.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If emptying worsens with crashes, feeding adjustments around exertion could prevent a temporary but real drop in how well the stomach handles food.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

## Category 3: Drug/Medication Ideas

### Idea 3.1 — Time-Limited Lowest-Risk Prokinetic Algorithm (Prucalopride-First) Rather Than a Single Agent
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Phase 3 integrated the *safety warning* (metoclopramide TD, domperidone QT, erythromycin tolerance). The distinct novel angle is a *tiered algorithm* for the severe case: select prucalopride (highly selective 5-HT4, no excess QT in pooled RCT meta) as the first-line agent, reserve metoclopramide for short rescue courses, and time-limit all use — explicitly sequencing rather than presenting agents in parallel. This is treatment-design, not a new drug.
- **Evidence link:** Patel2024SHT4Gastroparesis (5-HT4 safety/efficacy pooled data); Ingrosso2023GastroparesisDrugsNMA (comparative adverse-effect hierarchy); Shakhatreh2019Metoclopramide (TD black-box — the sequencing rationale).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In a severe ME/CFS gastroparesis cohort, a prucalopride-first algorithm will yield at least comparable emptying/symptom response with fewer serious adverse events than a metoclopramide-first approach over 12 weeks; if prucalopride is inferior, the sequencing rationale fails.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If the tiered approach works, severe patients could get effective stomach stimulation with a safer drug first, and the riskiest drug reserved for when others fail.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 3.2 — Relamorelin (Ghrelin Agonist) as a Prokinetic That Also Addresses Anorexia, Distinct From 5-HT4/Motilin Routes
- **Certainty:** 0.20
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Relamorelin (ghrelin receptor agonist) is prokinetic and *appetite-stimulating* — uniquely matching the severe ME/CFS gastroparesis phenotype where both emptying and nutritional intake fail (Tseng 2019 nonoral-nutrition risk). It works through a distinct receptor (ghrelin/GHSR-1a) from 5-HT4 and motilin, so it is mechanistically additive and a candidate when 5-HT4/motilin agents fail or are contraindicated. It is in the NMA evidence base but not approved; this flags it as a research target, not a current prescription.
- **Evidence link:** Ingrosso2023GastroparesisDrugsNMA (relamorelin included in NMA evidence base); Tseng2019POTSNutritionalSupport (the nutritional-failure risk it would address).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In a gastroparesis trial subset, relamorelin will improve both GES retention and caloric intake/weight relative to placebo, and the intake benefit will be separable from the emptying benefit; if relamorelin improves emptying without improving intake, its appetite-stimulation niche is not supported.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If relamorelin works, it could be a single drug that both speeds emptying and helps severe patients eat more — two problems treated at once.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 3.3 — Exogenous Cholinergic Support (Pyridostigmine) as a *Mechanism-Directed* Prokinetic Rather Than Symptom-Directed
- **Certainty:** 0.20
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The already-integrated vagal cascade identifies cholinergic efferent denervation as the driver of impaired gastric motility. Most prokinetics act on gastric-specific receptors (5-HT4, motilin, D2). Pyridostigmine (acetylcholinesterase inhibitor) augments *endogenous* cholinergic transmission along the vagal-efferent → enteric-motor pathway — a mechanism-directed approach that treats the denervation itself rather than downstream receptors. It is already in the prior vagal plan (3.3) as a CAP amplifier; the novel gastroparesis-specific angle is using it as a *primary prokinetic* for ME/CFS gastroparesis, with the prediction that it helps precisely the subset whose emptying failure is cholinergic.
- **Evidence link:** cross-ref vagal-cascade (ch34 subsec-02, cholinergic-efferent-mediated GI consequence); Ingrosso2023GastroparesisDrugsNMA (prokinetic context); Montalvo2022LongCovidGIDysmotility (ganglionic-cholinergic axis — where ACh support is most mechanistically apt).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In ME/CFS gastroparesis, GES emptying response to pyridostigmine will be predicted by baseline vagal/cholinergic indices (HRV, or ganglionic-AChR status) — cholinergic-impaired patients respond, others do not; if response is unrelated to cholinergic status, the mechanism-directed claim is falsified.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If correct, some patients with slow stomachs could benefit from boosting their own nerve signal rather than a stomach-specific stimulant — targeting the cause rather than the symptom.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 3.4 — GLP-1 Receptor Agonist as an Explicitly *Counterindicated* Agent in ME/CFS With Gastroparesis (Pharmacovigilance Warning, Not a Treatment)
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** GLP-1 receptor agonists slow gastric emptying and suppress appetite — directly harmful in gastroparesis and in severe ME/CFS with nutritional-failure risk. This is the inverse of a treatment idea: a proactive safety flag for the severe case, who may be on a GLP-1-agonist for weight management or metabolic reasons. It connects to the already-integrated area-postrema/GLP-1 speculation (ch18) and the counterindication logic already noted there, but makes the gastroparesis-specific prescribing risk explicit and actionable.
- **Evidence link:** search-log query 28 (GLP-1-RA delayed emptying, 247 hits, the pharmacology base); spec-area-postrema-gpcr-glp1-gate (ch18, notes gastroparesis as GLP-1 risk + appetite suppression); Tseng2019POTSNutritionalSupport (nutritional-failure risk it aggravates).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** If a GLP-1-agonist-exposed gastroparesis patient discontinues the agent, GES retention will improve or normalize; failure to improve on discontinuation indicates the delay is not (only) GLP-1-driven.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If a patient is taking a GLP-1 weight-loss drug and also has a slow stomach, stopping that drug could be a low-cost way to improve emptying — worth checking before adding a prokinetic.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

## Category 4: Supplement/Nutraceutical

### Idea 4.1 — Ginger as a First-Line Adjunctive Prokinetic for Mild-Moderate ME/CFS Gastroparesis
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Ginger (Zingiber officinale) is a well-tolerated herbal prokinetic that accelerates gastric emptying and reduces nausea with a favourable safety profile — a plausible low-risk first step before escalating to prescription prokinetics in mild-moderate gastroparesis, especially where drug-drug interactions (macrolides, QT) are a concern in the polypharmacy severe case. It is mechanistically distinct (serotonergic 5-HT3 antagonism + prokinetic) and low-cost/accessible.
- **Evidence link:** Gupta2016GastroparesisDiet (dietary/complementary-medicine management, ginger among the complementary approaches); Patel2024SHT4Gastroparesis / Shakhatreh2019Metoclopramide (the safety contrast — ginger has no TD/QT risk).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A ginger intervention will measurably reduce GCSI symptom scores and modestly improve GES retention in mild-moderate ME/CFS gastroparesis relative to placebo; if ginger has no emptying/symptom effect, its adjunctive niche is falsified and it is placebo-equivalent.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Ginger is a cheap, safe thing a patient can try for nausea and slow emptying before prescription drugs — with real, if modest, effect.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 4.2 — Liquid-Nutrient Optimisation as a Feeding-Strategy Intervention for the Very-Severe Case
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** In very-severe ME/CFS, even the effort and volume of a solid meal can exceed what a delayed-emptying stomach can process, causing nausea, vomiting, and caloric failure. Liquid-nutrient formulations (which empty faster and require less gastric grinding) plus small-frequent-meal scheduling are a low-risk, caregiver-implementable nutritional intervention that addresses the nutritional-failure risk documented in autonomic cohorts (Tseng) without any drug. This is distinct from the Phase 3 prokinetic-safety content — it is a feeding strategy.
- **Evidence link:** Gupta2016GastroparesisDiet (small-frequent meals, low-fat/low-fibre, liquid-nutrient optimisation); Tseng2019POTSNutritionalSupport (the nonoral-nutrition escalation this strategy may help avoid).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A liquid-nutrient / small-meal protocol will improve caloric intake and reduce postprandial nausea in severe ME/CFS gastroparesis relative to an unrestricted diet, even absent emptying improvement; if intake does not improve, the strategy does not confer its intended benefit.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Changing how (not just what) a severe patient eats — smaller, liquid, frequent meals — can improve intake and reduce nausea without any medication.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 4.3 — Low-Fibre / Low-Fat Dietary Modification to Reduce Gastric Mechanical Load (Complement to, Not Replacement for, Prokinetics)
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Fibre (mechanically demanding) and fat (delays emptying via nutrient-sensing feedback) both increase the gastric mechanical load a denervated/dysmotile stomach must clear. Dietary low-fat/low-fibre modification reduces emptying workload and is a standard gastroparesis management pillar. The novel framing for ME/CFS: dietary modification is a *first-line, side-effect-free* intervention that should precede or complement (not replace) prokinetics, particularly where the already-integrated SIBO/prokinetic-limitations warning applies.
- **Evidence link:** Gupta2016GastroparesisDiet (low-fat/low-fibre dietary management); Ingrosso2023GastroparesisDrugsNMA (prokinetics as the escalation the diet may reduce need for); cross-ref warn-prokinetic-limitations (ch12, prokinetics best combined with dietary modification).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In ME/CFS gastroparesis, a controlled low-fat/low-fibre diet will improve postprandial symptoms and GES retention relative to a standard diet, with the effect largest in the most mechanically-delayed patients; if the diet has no emptying/symptom effect, the mechanical-load rationale is unsupported.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Adjusting fibre and fat in the diet is a safe, free change a patient can make that reduces the workload on a slow stomach before considering drugs.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

## Category 5: Non-Pharmacological

### Idea 5.1 — Severity-Graded Feeding Strategies With an Explicit Very-Severe/Enteral-Escalation Trigger
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The severe-case context makes nutritional-failure risk the central clinical concern. A severity-graded feeding ladder — oral small-frequent/liquid → calorie-dense oral supplements → nasogastric/enteral → parenteral — with an explicit trigger for escalation (weight loss, dehydration, intractable vomiting) would operationalise care for the very-severe patient who cannot reliably feed orally. Tseng documents that autonomic cohorts require nonoral support; this converts that evidence into a graded, proactive protocol rather than a reaction to crisis.
- **Evidence link:** Tseng2019POTSNutritionalSupport (nonoral-nutrition factors in POTS — the escalation evidence); Gupta2016GastroparesisDiet (the oral tiers); Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (the emptying delay the protocol addresses).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Implementing a graded feeding ladder with an objective escalation trigger will reduce weight loss and unplanned hospitalisation for nutritional failure in severe ME/CFS gastroparesis relative to ad-hoc feeding; if outcomes do not improve, the protocol confers no added benefit over standard care.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A clear step-by-step feeding plan with a "when to escalate" trigger can prevent a severe patient from sliding into dangerous weight loss before help arrives.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 5.2 — Postural Feeding and Activity-Pacing Around Meals to Reduce Autonomic Meal-Time Stress
- **Certainty:** 0.20
- **Mechanistic rationale:** ME/CFS autonomic instability (POTS overlap) can worsen meal-time symptoms — orthostatic pooling and exertion adjacent to meals compound a stomach already struggling to empty. Postural strategies (upright-but-supported feeding, avoiding post-meal recumbency for reflux) and pacing exertion away from the postprandial window are low-cost, non-pharmacological levers that may reduce the autonomic load that aggravates gastroparesis symptoms. This is distinct from the vagal-plan abdominal-massage idea (5.5) — it targets autonomic meal-time context, not enteric stimulation.
- **Evidence link:** Aziz2025AGAGIHyperEhlersDanlos (hEDS/POTS GI dysautonomia management framework); Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (emptying delay being aggravated); Gupta2016GastroparesisDiet (feeding-strategy context).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Postural adjustment and meal-time pacing will reduce postprandial symptom scores and orthostatic symptom burden in ME/CFS gastroparesis relative to usual feeding, even without emptying change; if no symptom reduction occurs, the meal-time-autonomic-load claim is unsupported.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Simple changes to how a patient sits and paces around meals could reduce the nausea and dizziness that make eating miserable, without any drug.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

## Category 6: Combinations + Access

### Idea 6.1 — Caregiver-Implementable Low-Risk Feeding + Ginger Protocol for Home Management of the Severe Case
- **Certainty:** 0.40

## Category 7: Mathematical Model Extensions

### Idea 7.1 — ODE Variable for Gastric Emptying Rate (E_g) Feeding Into the Existing Vagal Cascade Model
- **Certainty:** 0.25
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The already-integrated vagal cascade (ch34 subsec-02) models reduced vagal efferent tone as a multi-system driver, listing impaired GI motility as consequence (c) but without an explicit emptying variable. Adding a gastric-emptying-rate state variable (E_g) whose degradation rate is driven by vagal-efferent-tone deficit, and whose depletion worsens caloric-intake (I) and systemic-energy (S) states, would make the cascade quantitatively predictive of the feeding/nutrition collapse central to the severe case. This is a distinct node from the prior plan's V_d/V_str structural-damage variables (7.1/7.2) — it models the *functional emptying consequence*, not the structural denervation index.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (the quantitative emptying data to parameterise E_g); cross-ref vagal-cascade subsec-02 item (c) impaired GI motility.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** The extended model predicts a monotonic relationship between vagal-tone deficit and E_g degradation such that restoring vagal tone (via the model's treatment variables) partially restores emptying; if observed emptying does not track vagal-tone changes as the model predicts, the E_g coupling is wrong.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A model that links nerve-tone loss to a falling stomach-emptying rate would predict — and test — whether restoring the nerve signal restores normal digestion.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 7.2 — DAG Node for Gastroparesis as an Independent, Mechanistically-Branching Contributor (Not Merely a Vagal-Cascade Corollary)
- **Certainty:** 0.25
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The vagal-cascade treats impaired GI motility as one downstream consequence of low vagal tone. But gastroparesis has multiple independent mechanistic branches (vagal, enteric/ICC structural, ganglionic-autoimmune, nNOS-pyloric, endogenous-GLP-1) that can operate in parallel or compensate for one another. A DAG that models gastroparesis as a multi-input node — with edges from each distinct branch into emptying and from emptying into nutritional/energy states — would let the model represent a patient whose gastric problem is not explained by the vagal cascade alone. This extends, not duplicates, the existing cascade.
- **Evidence link:** Grover2011CellularChangesGastroparesis (enteric/ICC branch); Montalvo2022LongCovidGIDysmotility (autoimmune-ganglionic branch); Debourdeau2024GastricVolumetry (accommodation/visceral-hypersensitivity branch); cross-ref vagal-cascade.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** The multi-branch DAG predicts that gastroparesis can occur in ME/CFS patients with *normal* vagal-tone indices (an enteric/autoimmune/GIP-1-driven branch), and that emptying delay does not always co-vary with vagal dysfunction; if emptying always tracks vagal tone in a modern cohort, the independent-branch DAG is not supported.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Modelling gastroparesis as more than just "low nerve tone" would explain why some patients' slow stomachs do not respond to nerve-targeted treatments, and point at the right branch to treat.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 9.1 — GES as a Dysautonomia-Severity Biomarker in ME/CFS (Beyond the GI Complaint Itself)

### Idea 9.2 — Breath-Test Alternative to GES for Repeatable, State-Dependent Emptying Measurement

The key design issue flagged by Wise is protocol standardization; a validated breath test could be the repeatable tool that GES cannot be for tracking the severe case over time.

- **Falsifiable prediction:** A standardized 13C breath test will correlate with GES retention in ME/CFS patients within an acceptable concordance threshold, allowing repeated measurement; if the breath test does not track GES reliably, it cannot substitute for scintigraphy.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** A safe, repeatable breath test would let clinicians track how well a patient's stomach is emptying over time — around treatments and crashes — without repeated radiation exposure.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 9.3 — GCSI Symptom Index as a Screening Instrument, With GES as the Confirmatory Test (Two-Stage Pathway)
- **Certainty:** 0.35

A two-stage pathway — validated symptom-index (GCSI) as a cheap screen, GES as confirmatory in screen-positive patients — would be a practical diagnostic pathway for the severe case, balancing access (primary-care screening) with accuracy (objective confirmation). - **Evidence link:** Wise2021GastricEmptyingScans (GES as confirmatory standard); Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (symptom–emptying correlation as the screen basis); search-log query 32 (GCSI validation, not integrated).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A GCSI-based screen will have high sensitivity (catch nearly all true GES-delayed patients) at acceptable specificity in ME/CFS; if the GCSI screen misses a substantial fraction of GES-confirmed delay, the two-stage pathway under-diagnoses and fails.

## Category 10: Reasons This Mechanism May NOT Be Relevant to ME/CFS (MANDATORY CRITICAL)

### Idea 10.1 — Delayed Emptying Is a Medication Side-Effect, Not a Primary ME/CFS Feature
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Many ME/CFS medications slow gastric emptying or cause nausea: anticholinergics, tricyclic antidepressants (amitriptyline — used for sleep/pain/fibromyalgia), opioids (used in severe pain), some antihistamines, and (increasingly) GLP-1 agonists. The Burnet 2004 cohort predates modern polypharmacy, but current severe ME/CFS patients are heavily medicated. If the delayed emptying attributed to "ME/CFS" is substantially medication-induced, then it is an iatrogenic, reversible phenomenon rather than an intrinsic disease feature — and the gastroparesis integration would over-attribute to the illness itself.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (cohort emptying — medication status not the focus); the already-integrated prokinetic/drug-interaction caution (Phase 3) documents the polypharmacy context; no ME/CFS emptying study controls for current medications.
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A medication-controlled modern ME/CFS cohort (or a washout/taper study) will show that emptying delay largely resolves when suspect drugs (TCAs, opioids, GLP-1-agonists) are withdrawn; if emptying delay persists in medication-naive patients, the iatrogenic explanation is falsified.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** For some patients, the slow stomach may be caused by a drug they are taking — meaning the fix could be a medication change, not a new prokinetic.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 10.2 — Gastroparesis Symptoms in ME/CFS Are Functional Dyspepsia / Visceral Hypersensitivity, Not True Emptying Delay
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Mechanistic rationale:** The overlap between gastroparesis and functional dyspepsia is large: many patients with nausea/early-satiety have normal objective emptying but abnormal gastric accommodation and visceral hypersensitivity (Debourdeau). If the ME/CFS GI-dominant phenotype is predominantly dyspepsia/hypersensitivity rather than true emptying delay, then (a) prokinetics are the wrong target, (b) symptom-based diagnosis over-attributes to gastroparesis, and (c) the whole "gastric-motility failure" framing would be partly a misclassification of a sensory disorder.
- **Evidence link:** Debourdeau2024GastricVolumetry (accommodation/visceral-hypersensitivity overlap); Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (shows delay is *not* universal — 12/32 solid — consistent with a substantial non-emptying component); Corrado1998NormalGastricEmptying (null — normal emptying with symptoms, supporting the non-emptying explanation).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** In a modern ME/CFS cohort, the majority of patients with GI-dominant nausea/early-satiety will have *normal* GES but abnormal accommodation/hypersensitivity (volumetry), so that true gastroparesis is the minority phenotype; if the majority have objective emptying delay, the dyspepsia-dominance explanation is falsified.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Many patients with nausea may be experiencing a stomach that senses normally but "complains" too much, rather than one that is genuinely slow — a distinction that changes treatment completely.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 10.3 — Reported GI Symptoms and Even Emptying Delay Are Deconditioning / Severe-Illness Effects, Not a Specific Autonomic Lesion
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** Severe illness and bedbound deconditioning, independent of ME/CFS-specific pathology, can impair gastric motility and cause nausea/feeding intolerance through reduced physical activity, altered gut-hormone milieu, and general catabolism. If the emptying delay seen in severe ME/CFS is a non-specific consequence of the severe-illness state (as in any bedbound chronic disease), it does not reflect ME/CFS-specific vagal pathology and would not argue for a dedicated ME/CFS gastroparesis mechanism — it would argue for generic severe-illness nutritional support.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (symptom-correlated delay — could reflect severity itself, not mechanism); synthesis competing-mechanism note (deconditioning named as alternative explanation); Corrado1998NormalGastricEmptying (not universal, consistent with state-dependence).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** ME/CFS patients with objectively documented emptying delay will show the same delay as severity-matched bedbound patients with other chronic diseases (non-ME/CFS); if ME/CFS emptying delay exceeds severity-matched non-ME/CFS patients, the deconditioning-only explanation is falsified.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Some of the slow stomach may simply be the body's general response to being very ill and inactive, in which case the priority is nutrition rather than an ME/CFS-specific nerve treatment.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 10.4 — Subjective Reporting Bias / Ascertainment Inflates the Apparent Prevalence
- **Certainty:** 0.30
- **Mechanistic rationale:** GI symptoms are a frequent complaint in ME/CFS, and the primary ME/CFS evidence (Burnet) enrolled a cohort where GI symptoms were common — potential ascertainment bias toward an emptying-prone subset. Questionnaire-derived symptoms may not reflect objective emptying (idea 2.2). If prevalence is inflated by how patients are selected and asked, the "common gastroparesis in ME/CFS" claim could be a biased estimate rather than a robust disease feature.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (questionnaire-ascertained, symptom-correlated — selection toward GI-dominant); Wise2021GastricEmptyingScans (diagnostic ascertainment heterogeneity); Corrado1998NormalGastricEmptying (an unsampled normal case).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A population-representative ME/CFS cohort (not GI-referral or symptom-ascertained) will show a materially lower GES-delay prevalence than Burnet's; if the prevalence holds in an unselected cohort, the ascertainment-bias explanation is falsified.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If the apparent frequency of slow stomachs is inflated by how patients were chosen, the true number of patients who need emptying treatment may be much smaller.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

## Category 11: Null Hypothesis Assessment (MANDATORY CRITICAL)

### Idea 11.1 — If Gastroparesis Has No Distinct Role, Which Existing Paper Claims Would Need Revision?
- **Certainty:** 0.30 (this is an assessment, not a positive claim)
- **Mechanistic rationale / assessment:** If modern-criteria cohorts showed ME/CFS patients do NOT have emptying delay beyond severity-matched controls, the following would require revision: (a) the Burnet 2004 claim of a genuine ME/CFS emptying phenotype (would collapse to a severity/era artifact); (b) the ch04 gastroparesis subsection's framing of gastroparesis as an intrinsic feature (Phase 3 integration); (c) the attribution of GI symptoms to vagal-efferent gastric-motility failure in the vagal cascade (ch34) — symptoms would need re-attribution to afferent/hypersensitivity or medication; (d) the rationale for prokinetics as an ME/CFS treatment. Crucially, this would NOT touch the core ME/CFS claims (PEM, autonomic dysfunction at other sites, gut-microbiome findings) — gastroparesis is an add-on hypothesis, and its failure is well-contained.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (the claim that would fall); Corrado1998NormalGastricEmptying (the lone null, currently weight-caveated); Debourdeau2024GastricVolumetry (the re-attribution target — dyspepsia/hypersensitivity).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** The null is testable: a modern-criteria, medication-controlled, severity-matched ME/CFS cohort with standardized GES will show emptying delay no different from matched severe controls; if this holds, the gastroparesis integration (Phase 3) and its ch04/ch34/ch12 reflections should be revised to "delayed emptying is a non-specific severe-illness phenomenon, not a distinct ME/CFS mechanism."
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If gastroparesis turns out not to be a distinct feature, the main ME/CFS understanding still stands — but the guidance to treat slow stomachs with prokinetics would need to be walked back.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 11.2 — Gastroparesis Is Real but Epiphenomenal (Present, Yet Not Mechanistically Informative or Therapeutically Distinct)
- **Certainty:** 0.25
- **Mechanistic rationale / assessment:** A weaker null: emptying delay is genuinely present in severe ME/CFS (Burnet) but is an epiphenomenon of the same vagal/autonomic dysfunction already documented elsewhere, contributing no NEW mechanistic or therapeutic information. Under this view, gastroparesis is a redundant downstream symptom of the already-integrated vagal cascade — it neither identifies a new lesion (enteric/ICC/autoimmune) nor warrants gastroparesis-specific treatment beyond what general autonomic support already provides. The practical consequence: the Phase 3 integration overstates the distinctiveness of a symptom that is just another face of known dysautonomia.
- **Evidence link:** cross-ref vagal-cascade (ch34 subsec-02 — gastroparesis already listed as consequence (c)); Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (real delay, but mechanism not isolated); synthesis (vagal-denervation is the leading cross-disease mechanism).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** If gastroparesis-specific treatment (prokinetic, or enteric-targeted) confers no benefit beyond general vagal/autonomic support (i.e., emptying and symptoms respond to the same interventions that improve HRV/orthostatic symptoms), then gastroparesis adds no distinct therapeutic axis; if gastroparesis-specific intervention works where general autonomic support does not, the epiphenomenon view is falsified.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** If the slow stomach is just one more symptom of the same underlying nerve problem, treating the whole nerve problem may suffice — and a separate stomach-specific treatment may add little.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 11.3 — The Entire Integration Rests on a Single Unreplicated Cohort; a Null Replication Would Topple It
- **Certainty:** 0.30 (this is a methodological risk assessment)
- **Mechanistic rationale / assessment:** The entire direct ME/CFS emptying claim rests on Burnet 2004 (n=32, single cohort, never replicated, Fukuda-era). Every downstream claim — ch04 gastroparesis feature, vagal-efferent GI attribution, prokinetic rationale — inherits that single study. There is no independent ME/CFS replication, and the one other direct paper (Corrado) is null. If a pre-registered modern-criteria replication returns null, the entire integration loses its direct-evidence foundation and collapses to cross-disease inference (diabetic/hEDS/post-viral), which is a much weaker basis for asserting ME/CFS-specific gastroparesis. This is the single largest vulnerability in the topic.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (the single unreplicated anchor); Corrado1998NormalGastricEmptying (the existing null); synthesis (no modern-criteria cohort, the identified gap); Wise2021GastricEmptyingScans (protocol heterogeneity makes replication-sensitive).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** A pre-registered, protocol-standardized modern-criteria ME/CFS replication of Burnet will either confirm (proportion ≥ Burnet's) or refute (substantially lower / null) the delayed-emptying finding; if null, the gastroparesis-in-ME/CFS claim downgrades from an observed finding to a cross-disease extrapolation.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Because all the stomach-specific ME/CFS evidence traces back to one 20-year-old study, a single careful modern replication could either confirm or overturn the whole "ME/CFS causes slow stomachs" claim.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

## Category 12: Evidence Quality Concerns (MANDATORY CRITICAL)

### Idea 12.1 — Fukuda-Era Diagnostic Criteria: the Anchor Cohort May Not Be "ME/CFS" by Modern Standards
- **Certainty:** 0.40 (high confidence this is a real limitation)
- **Mechanistic rationale / quality concern:** Burnet 2004 and Corrado 1998 used pre-IOM CDC-1994/Fukuda-era CFS criteria (not precisely stated). The Fukuda criteria are broader and less specific than modern ICC/IOM criteria, so a proportion of the Burnet cohort may not meet current ME/CFS definitions — meaning the direct emptying finding's population generalizability to modern ME/CFS is uncertain. Every downstream claim inherits this. This is not captured by the raw/discounted certainty score (which rates the study itself, not the diagnostic-anchoring gap).
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS / Corrado1998NormalGastricEmptying (Fukuda-era, criteria unstated — per search-log diagnostic-criteria note); synthesis diagnostic-criteria anchor (Doc-Instruction D).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Re-ascertaining the Burnet cohort against modern ICC/IOM criteria (if the original data were available) would change the emptying-prevalence estimate; without such re-analysis, the modern-population estimate is unknowable from this cohort.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** The study that found slow stomachs used an older, broader definition of the illness, so how much it applies to today's diagnosed patients is genuinely uncertain.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 12.2 — n=32 Single Cohort, Never Replicated, With a Single-Investigator/Site Origin
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Mechanistic rationale / quality concern:** The direct ME/CFS finding is n=32 from a single site and era, never independently replicated. Small single-site cohorts are prone to winner's-curse (the positive result that got published may be the one that, by chance, came out strongest) and to site-specific methods. The discounted certainty (0.60) reflects the study's internal design but not the fragility of a single unreplicated positive among a literature that also contains a null (Corrado). Publication bias toward the positive is not captured by the score.
- **Evidence link:** Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (n=32, single site); Corrado1998NormalGastricEmptying (the competing null); synthesis (no replication).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** An independent replication is the only falsification here: if it does not reproduce the effect magnitude, the original effect size was inflated (winner's-curse); if it reproduces, the single-cohort concern is retired.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** One small study, never repeated, is a fragile basis for a treatment decision — a second study could easily change the picture.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 12.3 — Scintigraphy Protocol Heterogeneity Undermines Cross-Study Comparability
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Mechanistic rationale / quality concern:** Wise 2021 documents widespread non-adherence to standardized GES protocols (4-hour imaging, meal standardization). Burnet 2004 used radionuclide liquid+solid emptying against historical controls — a protocol that may not match modern standardized scintigraphy. This means (a) the Burnet delay magnitude may not be directly comparable to modern gastroparesis diagnostic thresholds, and (b) comparisons between Burnet and other emptying studies are methodologically loose. The certainty scores do not capture protocol heterogeneity because they score individual papers, not cross-paper comparability.
- **Evidence link:** Wise2021GastricEmptyingScans (protocol non-adherence); Burnet2004GastricEmptyingCFS (historical-controls protocol).
- **Falsifiable prediction:** Re-measuring a modern ME/CFS cohort with the current standardized 4-hour protocol will produce retention values that align or diverge from Burnet's; if they diverge substantially, the original protocol's incompatibility is confirmed as a methodological limitation.
- **Non-specialist consequence:** Different hospitals and eras measure stomach emptying differently, so reported "delays" may not mean the same thing across studies.
- **Origin:** brainstorm

### Idea 12.4 — Cross-Disease Mechanistic Evidence Is Heavily Discounted Yet Used to Anchor the Mechanism
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Mechanistic rationale / quality concern:** The mechanistic claims (enteric/ICC loss, vagal denervation, autoimmune ganglionic blockade) rest almost entirely on non-ME/CFS evidence: diabetic/idiopathic gastroparesis tissue (Grover 0.52, Wang 0.30 animal), Long-COVID/post-viral (Montalvo 0.38, n=1), POTS/hEDS (Tseng 0.44, Aziz 0.56). These are discounted for population relevance, but the integration's *mechanistic narrative* leans on them heavily because direct ME/CFS evidence is thin.
