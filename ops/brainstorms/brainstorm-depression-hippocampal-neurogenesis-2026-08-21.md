# Brainstorm: Adult Hippocampal Neurogenesis and the Depression–ME/CFS Boundary

**Date:** 2026-08-21
**Topic slug:** depression-hippocampal-neurogenesis
**Origin:** integrate-topic Phase 4 (creative brainstorming)
**Context:** Primary finding (Peng et al. 2026, Nature Medicine, DOI 10.1038/s41591-026-04571-8) reports stalled adult hippocampal neurogenesis in major depressive disorder: more quiescent NSCa stem cells, fewer neuroblasts, reduced DCX/BDNF, elevated interferon/stress signaling across the hippocampal trisynaptic circuit, arguing for molecular reclassification of depression. **Integration decision: PROCEED (cross-disease / differential-diagnostic framing).** There are ZERO direct ME/CFS papers (documented research gap). All ME/CFS relevance is via indirect links: HPA-axis dysfunction in CFS (Papadopoulos 2011), post-viral suppression of neurogenesis after mild COVID (Fernández-Castañeda 2022), neuroinflammation overlap, and the depression–ME/CFS differential boundary. **The Sorrells controversy (does adult human neurogenesis persist?) is unresolved** — all ideas acknowledge both sides.

**Critical framing caps:**
- The ME/CFS extrapolation MUST stay speculative — the science does NOT support a direct ME/CFS neurogenesis claim. Frame ME/CFS-relevant ideas as testable research directions / speculative bridges, NOT established mechanisms.
- All 12 brainstorm categories active (not PARTIAL-capped).
- Every idea tagged `origin: brainstorm`.
- MDD-axis certainty can be moderate-to-high (0.4–0.8); ME/CFS-axis certainty MUST stay below 0.45.

# Constructive Ideas (Categories 1–9), Ranked Most Promising First

## 1. Pattern separation as a cognitive biomarker discriminating molecular depression from reactive low mood in ME/CFS

**Category:** 8 (diagnostic / biomarker)
**Certainty:** 0.40
**Rationale + evidence link:** Pattern separation (discriminating overlapping experiences) is a canonical dentate-gyrus function dependent on newborn neurons; it is impaired in MDD and proposed as a behavioral marker of neurogenesis deficit @Gandy2017PatternSeparation. The core question from the integration (sec-12/subsec-02-depression, ch16) is whether primary molecular depression and reactive post-onset low mood in ME/CFS are separable. A behavioral pattern-separation task could separate the two without post-mortem tissue.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a ME/CFS cohort stratified by depression onset, patients with post-onset reactive low mood will perform normally on a mnemonic-similarity pattern-separation task, while the (expected small) subgroup with primary MDD-like presentations will show selective pattern-separation impairment that correlates with anhedonia severity rather than with fatigue/PEM.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A simple memory test might one day help tell "depression as a reaction to illness" apart from "depression as a brain change," which currently requires invasive tissue analysis.
**origin:** brainstorm
**NEW vs covered:** NEW — extends Gandy's marker proposal into the ME/CFS differential; not currently in the paper.

## 2. Post-infectious suppression of neurogenesis as a shared pathway between long-COVID and post-infectious ME/CFS

**Category:** 6 (cross-disease bridge)
**Certainty:** 0.38 (ME/CFS axis; 0.64 for the long-COVID axis per FernandezCastaneda2022)
**Rationale + evidence link:** Mild respiratory COVID persistently suppresses hippocampal neurogenesis in mice via microglial reactivity and elevated CCL11, and human long-COVID patients with cognitive symptoms have elevated CCL11 @FernandezCastaneda2022MildCOVID. ME/CFS is frequently post-infectious (EBV, Q fever, COVID). If a post-viral state can suppress neurogenesis, post-infectious ME/CFS could share that pathway — a speculative bridge requiring direct testing.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a post-infectious ME/CFS cohort with cognitive symptoms and a matched post-infectious-recovered group, serum CCL11 and microglial-reactivity markers will be elevated only in the group with sustained cognitive/PEM symptoms, and will correlate with memory-task performance.
**Non-specialist consequence:** After an infection, the brain's ability to make new memory cells might be switched off by immune signals — and this could be a testable thread linking post-COVID and post-infection ME/CFS cognitive complaints.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 3. HPA-axis dysfunction as the indirect route linking ME/CFS to neurogenic suppression

**Category:** 6 (cross-disease bridge) / 1 (hypothesis)
**Certainty:** 0.40 (bridge; 0.43 animal + 0.56 CFS-HPA underlying)
**Rationale + evidence link:** Neurogenesis provides hippocampal negative feedback on the HPA axis; neurogenesis-deficient mice show slower glucocorticoid recovery and impaired dexamethasone suppression @Snyder2011BuffersStress. CFS shows HPA-axis dysfunction, typically mild hypocortisolism with blunted responses @Papadopoulos2011HPAaxis. Route: ME/CFS HPA dysfunction → altered glucocorticoid signaling → neurogenic suppression → impaired HPA recovery — a bidirectional loop.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a ME/CFS cohort, the severity of HPA-axis abnormality (dexamethasone-suppression and cortisol-recovery measures) will correlate positively with neurogenesis-linked markers (DCX/BDNF blood levels) and with pattern-separation performance — and this correlation will be absent in matched healthy controls.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The stress-hormone system and the brain's memory-cell factory are linked; if stress-hormone regulation is off in ME/CFS, memory-cell production might follow.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 4. Blood-derived DCX and BDNF as a non-invasive, resolution-agnostic neurogenesis proxy in ME/CFS

**Category:** 8 (biomarker)
**Certainty:** 0.32
**Rationale + evidence link:** Peng 2026 reports reduced DCX and BDNF in MDD neuroblasts @Peng2026NeurogenesisNatureMed. Direct human neurogenesis can only be measured post-mortem, which is unusable in patients. A blood-derived proxy (DCX circulating fragment, BDNF, or both) would let ME/CFS cohorts be studied without tissue. Caveat: BDNF-in-CFS searches returned weak/unrelated results (search log M2/M6), so this is a research proposition, not an established finding.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a ME/CFS cohort, serum BDNF and DCX levels will be lower than matched controls and will correlate with memory-test performance and HPA-axis measures — but this is currently untested and could easily be null given the weak BDNF-in-CFS literature.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A simple blood test could someday estimate the brain's new-cell production, replacing the need for brain-tissue samples.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 5. Antidepressant class as a neurogenesis-restoration differential probe in ME/CFS-depression overlap

**Category:** 3 (drug / medication idea)
**Certainty:** 0.28
**Rationale + evidence link:** Antidepressant exposure is associated with increased hippocampal progenitor proliferation and angiogenesis in MDD post-mortem @Boldrini2012AntidepressantAngio. If a subset of ME/CFS patients with low mood is actually neurogenesis-deficient (a hypothesis, not established), an antidepressant that restores neurogenesis might show selective cognitive/affective benefit in that subset — providing a pharmacodynamic differential probe. This is a hypothesis-generating observation only; no ME/CFS dosing or response data exists.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a ME/CFS cohort with depression, a neurogenesis-promoting antidepressant will improve pattern-separation performance and BDNF/DCX proxies only in patients who also show pre-treatment neurogenesis-linked deficits, and will not improve fatigue or PEM. A negative result (no improvement despite depression relief) would argue that ME/CFS low mood is reactive rather than neurogenesis-driven.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Which depression medication helps might reveal the cause of the low mood — but this is untested in ME/CFS and could easily go the other way.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 6. Neurogenesis as a moderator of treatment response, not a target, in ME/CFS

**Category:** 4 (combinations / access)
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Rationale + evidence link:** Neurogenesis is proposed as a mechanism by which antidepressants work (Boldrini 2012, Tartt 2022 review @Tartt2022HippocampalPlasticity). In ME/CFS, where depression is largely reactive, whether a patient's low mood responds to treatment may depend on whether neurogenesis is intact. Framing neurogenesis as a *response moderator* rather than a *treatment target* keeps the claim modest and testable.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Among ME/CFS patients receiving antidepressant treatment for comorbid low mood, the response trajectory (mood, anhedonia) will track the baseline neurogenesis-linked proxy (pattern-separation or BDNF/DCX), independent of fatigue severity.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Whether a depression treatment works for a given patient might depend on a brain cell process rather than just the dose.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 7. Aerobic exercise dose as a neurogenesis stimulant — but bounded by PEM safety

**Category:** 3 (non-pharmacological intervention)
**Certainty:** 0.30 (exercise→neurogenesis mechanism 0.4; the ME/CFS application 0.20 given PEM)
**Rationale + evidence link:** Exercise is the most robust non-drug stimulus for adult hippocampal neurogenesis in animals (covered in Anacker2017 review @Anacker2017CognitiveFlex). In ME/CFS, exercise is constrained by post-exertional malaise. This idea is a *boundary guardrail*: any neurogenesis-oriented exercise intervention must be delivered within PEM-safe parameters, or the neurogenic benefit is moot.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A PEM-safe (paced, graded) exercise intervention in ME/CFS will increase pattern-separation or BDNF/DCX proxies only if it does not trigger PEM; patients who experience PEM will show the opposite (proxy decline).
**Non-specialist consequence:** Exercise may help the brain make new cells, but in ME/CFS it must stay within the "no-crash" limit or it can do the opposite.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 8. Sleep and circadian rhythm as neurogenesis-relevant modulators in ME/CFS

**Category:** 3 (non-pharmacological intervention)
**Certainty:** 0.28
**Rationale + evidence link:** Sleep disturbance is near-universal in ME/CFS, and hippocampal neurogenesis is regulated by sleep and circadian glucocorticoid rhythms (part of the HPA coupling in Snyder 2011). If sleep disruption suppresses neurogenesis, sleep repair could be a neurogenesis-relevant lever. This is speculative and indirect — sleep's effect on *adult human* neurogenesis is itself unresolved given the Sorrells controversy.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a ME/CFS cohort, sleep-consolidation improvement over a fixed period will correlate with improvement in pattern-separation and HPA-axis recovery measures.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Fixing broken sleep might indirectly support the brain's memory-cell production.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 9. Vagal / anti-inflammatory access route to neurogenesis via microglial reactivity

**Category:** 3 (non-pharmacological) / 4 (combination)
**Certainty:** 0.26
**Rationale + evidence link:** Neurogenesis suppression in the post-viral and MDD contexts is driven by microglial reactivity and elevated interferon signaling @Peng2026NeurogenesisNatureMed @FernandezCastaneda2022MildCOVID. Interventions that dampen neuroinflammation (vagal stimulation, anti-inflammatory approaches) could indirectly protect neurogenesis. This is a speculative extension of documented neuroinflammation in ME/CFS, not an established ME/CFS neurogenesis claim.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a ME/CFS cohort, an intervention that measurably reduces microglial-reactivity markers (e.g., CCL11) will be associated with stable-or-improved pattern-separation performance, while those with persistent reactivity show cognitive decline.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Calming brain immune cells might protect the brain's new-cell factory.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 10. Mathematical model extension: neurogenesis as a homeostatic buffer node in the HPA loop

**Category:** 7 (mathematical model extension)
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Rationale + evidence link:** Snyder 2011 establishes a neurogenesis→HPA-negative-feedback link in animals @Snyder2011BuffersStress. The paper's formalization layer could add neurogenesis as a slow homeostatic node: reduced neurogenesis slows HPA recovery, sustaining stress-driven suppression (a positive-feedback trap). This is a modeling extension to test the loop dynamics, not a claim about ME/CFS.
**Falsifiable prediction:** In a coupled neurogenesis–HPA ordinary-differential-equation model, removing the neurogenesis buffer node produces a bifurcation (sustained glucocorticoid elevation / depression-like steady state) that reproduces the stalled-neurogenesis pattern in Peng 2026 — a falsifiable structural prediction about the loop's stability.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A computer model could show how a broken memory-cell buffer turns a temporary stress response into a lasting one.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 11. Hippocampal volumetric imaging as a cross-check, not a neurogenesis surrogate

**Category:** 8 (diagnostic / imaging)
**Certainty:** 0.25
**Rationale + evidence link:** Depression is associated with reduced hippocampal volume (meta-analysis @Videbech2004HippocampalVolume). However, volume is a poor proxy for neurogenesis (structural MRI cannot resolve new neurons, and the Sorrells controversy makes the cellular interpretation contested). This idea is a *scope warning*: hippocampal volume in ME/CFS should not be read as neurogenesis evidence.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Hippocampal volumetric differences, if found in ME/CFS, will not correlate with any neurogenesis-linked proxy (DCX/BDNF/pattern-separation), confirming volume and neurogenesis are independent axes.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Seeing a smaller brain region on a scan does not mean the brain's cell factory is broken — the two are different measurements.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 12. Depression GWAS + epigenetic partitioning as a roadmap for ME/CFS neurogenesis risk

**Category:** 1 (hypothesis) / 2 (research direction)
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Rationale + evidence link:** Peng 2026 found some dysregulated neurogenesis genes carry MDD GWAS variants while others are epigenetically regulated (chromatin accessibility) @Peng2026NeurogenesisNatureMed. The same "genetic vs epigenetic" split is a testable template for whether any ME/CFS-associated neurogenesis-relevant marker is heritable or environmentally (post-infectious) driven.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If ME/CFS ever shows a neurogenesis-relevant signal, partitioning its genes into germline-variant vs accessible-chromatin categories will reveal whether it is more genetic (like MDD) or more environmentally induced (like post-viral suppression) — currently entirely untested.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Asking whether a brain-cell problem is inherited or switched on by the environment helps guide whether treatment should target genes or triggers.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 13. Resolution of the Sorrells controversy as a prerequisite gate for ME/CFS integration

**Category:** 2 (research direction)
**Certainty:** 0.35 (that this is the correct gate)
**Rationale + evidence link:** The field disputes whether adult human neurogenesis persists at meaningful levels: positive (Eriksson1998, Boldrini2018, Peng2026) vs null (Sorrells2018, Sorrells2021). No ME/CFS neurogenesis claim can be validated until a reproducible human measurement method exists. This idea is a *prerequisite guardrail*: register an open-question that ME/CFS neurogenesis work is blocked on method resolution.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If a consensus human neurogenesis assay is established, the validity of the depression (and any future ME/CFS) neurogenesis finding can be re-tested; until then all such findings remain conditional.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Scientists must first agree on how to measure new brain cells before claiming that an illness changes them.
**origin:** brainstorm

# Critical Ideas (Categories 10–12, MANDATORY)

## 14. The null hypothesis: ME/CFS low mood is purely reactive, with no neurogenesis component

**Category:** 10 (null hypothesis assessment)
**Certainty:** 0.55 (that this is the leading null)
**Rationale + evidence link:** 78.1% of ME/CFS patients develop depression *after* onset, and 96% attribute it to disease severity (subsec-02-depression). The reactive hypothesis predicts NO primary hippocampal neurogenesis defect in most ME/CFS patients. Given ZERO direct ME/CFS neurogenesis studies, the default, best-supported position is that ME/CFS-associated low mood is reactive and neurogenesis is normal.
**Falsifiable prediction:** A ME/CFS cohort will show normal neurogenesis-linked proxies (pattern separation, BDNF/DCX) relative to controls once fatigue/PEM and illness duration are controlled — i.e., no independent neurogenesis deficit beyond what reactive depression would predict.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Most ME/CFS low mood is likely a normal emotional reaction to severe illness, not evidence of a broken brain-cell process.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 15. Post-mortem artifact risk undermines the depression-neurogenesis premise

**Category:** 11 (evidence quality — post-mortem artifacts, fixation, marker validity)
**Certainty:** 0.45
**Rationale + evidence link:** The Sorrells group attributes positive findings to fixation and marker artifacts (DCX/Ki67/PCNA), showing neurogenesis markers drop to undetectable in adults with optimized fixation @Sorrells2018NeurogenesisDrops @Sorrells2021PositiveControls. Peng 2026 is a single-nucleus (snRNA/ATAC) + spatial atlas on post-mortem tissue — a different method from Sorrells, but still post-mortem. The field has not converged on which markers are valid.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If fixation/tissue-processing fully explains the positive findings, a standardized fixation protocol across labs will eliminate the reported adult neurogenesis signal; if neurogenesis truly persists, standardized protocols will retain it. This experiment would settle the controversy.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Some scientists argue the "new brain cells" seen in depression studies may be an artifact of how the brain tissue was stored and stained.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 16. Single, unreplicated primary study — replication required before any ME/CFS relevance

**Category:** 11 (evidence quality — unreplicated primary study)
**Certainty:** 0.50
**Rationale + evidence link:** Peng 2026 is newly published (2026-08-21), its DOI was not yet PubMed-indexed at search time, and it is not independently replicated. Some DEG analyses used permissive thresholds (unadjusted P<0.05 with low cell numbers) — flagged in appendix-h. A single unreplicated multimodal study, however large, is weak ground for the strongest claim (stalled neurogenesis in MDD).
**Falsifiable prediction:** Independent replication with the same single-nucleus + spatial protocol in a separate MDD cohort will either confirm the stalled-neurogenesis pattern or fail to reproduce it; until then the ME/CFS extrapolation is doubly speculative.
**Non-specialist consequence:** One big study is not proof; scientists need an independent group to get the same result before trusting it.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 17. Marker and lineage validity: are the "neuroblast" stages real or computational?

**Category:** 11 (marker validity)
**Certainty:** 0.40
**Rationale + evidence link:** Peng 2026 defines a lineage (NSCa, NSCb, INP, NB, ImGC) via single-nucleus clustering validated by fetal-hippocampus integration and spatial transcriptomics @Peng2026NeurogenesisNatureMed. Single-nucleus RNA cannot directly prove *proliferation* — a newborn neuron is defined functionally, and a "quiescent stem cell" vs "inactive cell" distinction is a computational inference, not direct observation. This is a methodological weakness shared with the broader field.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If the lineage stages are real, spatial/lineage-tracing methods (or functional assays) will confirm a progressive NSC→NB→immature-granule transition with matched protein (DCX, Ki67, nestin) co-localization; if they are largely computational, the protein validation will not co-localize to the assigned stages.
**Non-specialist consequence:** A "stalled cell-factory" claim may rest partly on computer analysis of cell types, not on directly watching cells divide.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 18. Animal-to-human translation gap weakens the mechanism, not just the application

**Category:** 12 (evidence quality — translational gap)
**Certainty:** 0.40
**Rationale + evidence link:** The strongest mechanistic links (neurogenesis buffers stress @Snyder2011BuffersStress; neurogenesis drives pattern separation and cognitive flexibility @Anacker2017CognitiveFlex; post-viral suppression @FernandezCastaneda2022MildCOVID) are primarily rodent-derived, with the human component mostly post-mortem or small samples. Whether rodent neurogenesis dynamics translate to humans is exactly what the Sorrells controversy questions.
**Falsifiable prediction:** If human neurogenesis is materially slower/lower than rodents (Sorrells null), then rodent-derived predictions (e.g., exercise or antidepressant neurogenesis effects) will fail to translate in human trials — a testable cross-species divergence.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Much of what we know about new brain cells comes from mice; mice and humans may not behave the same way.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 19. Publication-bias and same-group-confirmation risk in the positive literature

**Category:** 12 (evidence quality — bias)
**Certainty:** 0.35
**Rationale + evidence link:** The positive literature is heavily same-group: Boldrini, Tartt, Hen, and Peng share overlapping authorship (flagged in appendix-h), and the positive reviews (Anacker2017, Tartt2022) have senior-author overlap with the primary groups. The null side is concentrated in the Sorrells/Alvarez-Buylla group. This two-camp structure increases the risk of confirmation bias on both sides and of publication bias against genuinely mixed or null results.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Independent, non-overlapping research groups attempting either direction will produce results that are less extreme than either current camp's published position (regression to the mean) — a prediction of the two-camp bias hypothesis.
**Non-specialist consequence:** When two rival research teams each keep finding what they expect, the truth is often somewhere between them.
**origin:** brainstorm

## 20. Evidence quality: CFS-HPA link is weak and indirect, so the bridge to neurogenesis is doubly soft

**Category:** 10/12 (evidence quality — indirect link)
**Certainty:** 0.45
**Rationale + evidence link:** Papadopoulos 2011 notes CFS HPA abnormality is mild, heterogeneous, and confounded (sampling, cortisol method) @Papadopoulos2011HPAaxis, and the neurogenesis-HPA coupling is animal-derived @Snyder2011BuffersStress. The chain "ME/CFS → HPA dysfunction → neurogenesis suppression" is therefore two speculative links stacked, not one.
**Falsifiable prediction:** Correcting for the documented confounders (cortisol sampling method, age, sex, illness duration) will reduce or abolish any apparent ME/CFS HPA-axial association, and the residual will not correlate with any neurogenesis proxy.
**Non-specialist consequence:** The connection between ME/CFS, stress hormones, and brain cell production is built on indirect links, so it is easy to overstate.
**origin:** brainstorm

# Low-usefulness / Boundary-flag items (for completeness, NOT recommended for integration)

## L1. Direct neurogenesis drug-target hunt in ME/CFS (low-usefulness)

**Category:** 3 (drug idea)
**Certainty:** 0.15
**Rationale + evidence link:** Pursuing neurogenesis-restoring drugs (BDNF modulators, etc.) as a ME/CFS therapy is premature — there is zero direct ME/CFS neurogenesis evidence, the Sorrells controversy undermines the premise, and the field has not validated a human assay. **Flagged low-usefulness** until items 13/15/16 (method resolution + replication) are settled. Included only to preempt an unjustified treatment idea.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Treating ME/CFS by boosting new brain cells would be guessing without the science to back it.
**origin:** brainstorm

## L2. Serial invasive CSF neurogenesis monitoring (low-usefulness)

**Category:** 8 (biomarker)
**Certainty:** 0.10
**Rationale + evidence link:** Measuring neurogenesis directly would require repeated lumbar punctures for CSF-based lineage markers — impractical, ethically heavy for a severe ME/CFS population, and without a validated marker. Blood proxies (idea 4) are the only feasible route. **Flagged low-usefulness** for the near term.
**Non-specialist consequence:** Checking brain-cell production would require repeated spinal taps — not practical for patients already severely unwell.
**origin:** brainstorm
