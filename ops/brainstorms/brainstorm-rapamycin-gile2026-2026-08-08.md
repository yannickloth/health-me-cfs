## Category 1 — Mechanistic Hypothesis Extensions

### 1.1 IMPDH as mTOR-Independent Drug Target in ME/CFS
**Certainty:** 0.30 → if validated
**Description:** Gile 2026 shows mTORC1→IMPDH→purine metabolism→mitochondrial dysfunction in ME/CFS PBMCs. IMPDH is pharmacologically tractable (mycophenolate mofetil, an FDA-approved IMPDH inhibitor) and could bypass mTORC2-related immunosuppression. Mycophenolate's risk profile (GI toxicity, myelosuppression, pregnancy Category D) differs from rapamycin's (hyperlipidemia, impaired wound healing, pneumonitis). If the therapeutic benefit of mTORC1 inhibition is mediated through IMPDH suppression, IMPDH inhibitors could offer more selective targeting. This must be tested empirically before the claim is made — rapamycin could work through ATG13/autophagy and IMPDH/purine pathways simultaneously, and blocking only IMPDH may be insufficient.
**Status:** ⬜ pending — purely inferential, no clinical data

### 1.2 Purine Metabolism as Modular Validation Target for mTOR Models
**Certainty:** n/a (methodological)
**Description:** The mTORC1→IMPDH→purine→mitochondrial axis is modular — each step has established assays (pS6K for mTORC1 activity, IMPDH enzyme activity assay, LCMS purine metabolomics, Seahorse OCR). This makes the purine pathway an attractive "validation module" for the broader mTOR hypothesis: if mTORC1 inhibition fails to normalize purine metabolism in a well-controlled trial, the pathway connection is falsified independent of clinical outcomes. Modular falsification bypasses the power problem of clinical endpoints in underpowered trials.
**Status:** ⬜ pending

### 1.3 P2X7→NLRP3 as Purine-Mediated Neuroinflammatory Link
**Certainty:** 0.25
**Description:** Extracellular purines (ATP, ADP) accumulate when IMPDH overactivity produces purine excess. P2X7 receptors on microglia are ATP-gated cation channels that trigger NLRP3 inflammasome assembly. The chain: mTORC1↑ → IMPDH↑ → purine accumulation → extracellular ATP/ADP↑ → microglial P2X7 activation → NLRP3 → IL-1β → neuroinflammation. This connects mTOR to NLRP3 via a purine intermediate that is mechanistically distinct from the mitochondrial ROS→NLRP3 route. P2X7 is already druggable (P2X7 antagonists in clinical trials for inflammatory diseases).
**Status:** ⬜ pending

### 1.4 Purine-Metabolism Stratified Rapamycin Responders
**Certainty:** 0.30
**Description:** If mTORC1→IMPDH→purine metabolism is a causal pathway, then baseline purine profile (IMP/XMP ratio, uric acid, xanthine) should predict rapamycin response. Gile 2026 data show differential regulation of IMP→XMP and IMP→hypoxanthine — patients whose primary purine dysregulation is through IMPDH overactivity (high IMP/XMP ratio) should respond better to rapamycin than those with different purine pathway abnormalities. This is a testable prediction that existing Gile 2026 data could partially address.
**Status:** ⬜ pending

## Category 2 — Research Directions

### 2.1 Replicate Gile 2026 Purine Findings in Independent Cohort
**Priority:** HIGH
**Description:** The entire mTORC1→IMPDH→mitochondrial axis rests on a single uncontrolled study from one consortium (Simmaron). Independent replication — ideally with a placebo control — is the single highest-priority research direction.
**Feasibility:** Moderate (LCMS purine metabolomics + Seahorse OCR + flow cytometry = well-established assays; clinical trial infrastructure needed)
**Status:** ⬜ pending

### 2.2 Distinguish Purine-Mediated vs ATG13-Mediated Rapamycin Effects
**Priority:** HIGH
**Description:** Measure IMPDH activity, purine metabolites, pSer258-ATG13, and LC3-II turnover in the same PBMC samples before and after rapamycin. Within-patient correlation between purine normalization and clinical improvement vs autophagy restoration and clinical improvement would distinguish which pathway mediates benefit (or whether both do).
**Feasibility:** High (add purine panels to existing autophagy biomarker protocols)
**Status:** ⬜ pending

### 2.3 Test IMPDH Inhibitor (Mycophenolate) in ME/CFS In Vitro
**Priority:** MEDIUM
**Description:** Expose ME/CFS and control PBMCs to mycophenolate mofetil at clinically relevant concentrations. Measure IMP/XMP ratio, mitochondrial OCR, and microglial inflammatory markers. Compare to rapamycin-treated aliquots from same patients. If mycophenolate replicates rapamycin's metabolic effects, IMPDH is confirmed as a mechanistic node; if not, rapamycin's benefit flows through ATG13/autophagy or another pathway.
**Feasibility:** Moderate (in vitro study; mycophenolate is clinically available but not risk-free for patient trials)
**Status:** ⬜ pending

## Category 10 — Limitations (documented)

### 10.1 Gile 2026 Uncontrolled Design
Already documented in text and registry entry.

### 10.2 Single-Consortium Risk
All rapamycin/ATG13/purine ME/CFS data come from the Simmaron consortium (Ruan 2025, Drosen 2025, Toriola 2026, Gile 2026). This creates correlated bias risk: methodological choices, patient selection, and assay protocols are shared across all four papers. An independent laboratory must replicate any single finding before the corpus can be treated as validated.

### 10.3 IMPDH→P2X7→NLRP3 Cascade Is Entirely Inferred
No single experiment has demonstrated consecutive steps in the same cell type. The cascade assembles separate findings from cancer biology (mTOR→IMPDH), neuroinflammation (P2X7→NLRP3), and ME/CFS (Gile 2026 purine data). Each link is plausible but the full chain has never been tested.

## Category 11 — Falsifiability (documented in section falsifiability checklist)

- IMPDH activity should be elevated in ME/CFS PBMCs and correlate with mTORC1 activity
- Rapamycin should reduce IMPDH activity and normalize IMP/XMP ratio dose-dependently
- Purine supplementation at ME/CFS-relevant concentrations should impair mitochondrial OCR in control PBMCs
- P2X7 link falsified if microglial inflammation reduction is independent of purine changes

## Category 12 — Evidence Gaps

### 12.1 No RCT Data for Rapamycin in ME/CFS
The paper already documents this — the ~34% responder rate is from an uncontrolled pilot. Placebo response in ME/CFS treatment trials can be substantial.

### 12.2 No Independent Purine Replication
The IMPDH→purine→mitochondrial axis is from a single study. Independent replication with placebo control is the rate-limiting evidence gap.

### 12.3 No Pediatric mTOR Biomarker Data
FronticelliBaldelli2025 proposes pS6 as a pediatric mTOR biomarker but provides zero human data. The pediatric mTOR→BBB→neuroinflammation framework is entirely review-level.

### 12.4 Purine Metabolism Not Measured in ME/CFS Cohorts Outside Rapamycin Context
No baseline purine metabolomics in ME/CFS vs healthy controls has been published outside the Gile 2026 cohort (where baseline was pre-treatment, not truly "untreated"). A cross-sectional purine metabolomics study in treatment-naive ME/CFS vs controls would establish whether purine dysregulation is a disease feature or a treatment effect.
