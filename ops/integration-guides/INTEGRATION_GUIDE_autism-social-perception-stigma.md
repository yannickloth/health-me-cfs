## 1. Topic and dual framing

This topic integrates the autism social-perception / first-impression / thin-slice stigma literature,
anchored on Sasson 2017, for two framing purposes that the paper wants developed in parallel:
(a) **Full ME/CFS relevance** — how the anchor's mechanism (first impressions formed from thin slices of
    presentational "style" — facial affect, prosody, movement, gaze — independent of conversational
    "substance") maps onto ME/CFS patients. ME/CFS is an invisible illness; patients often present with
    flat affect, slowed movement/speech, and reduced expressivity (especially when severe/bedbound).
    This mechanism explains why clinician/peer first-impression bias toward "style" (not the medical
    "substance" the patient reports) can drive diagnostic dismissal and reduced willingness to provide
    care/support.
(b) **Fully-developed autism-domain knowledge** — the autism first-impression/stigma field developed with
    the same evidentiary rigor the pipeline applies to an ME/CFS mechanism, cross-referenced to ME/CFS
    wherever autism and ME/CFS genuinely intersect (e.g. co-occurring autism + ME/CFS = compounded
    first-impression bias).

## 5. Certainty notes
Raw certainty 0.55–0.85 (per pipeline scale). ALL papers concern general-population perceivers of another
condition (autism / Parkinson / schizophrenia), so population weight = 0.75 → discounted 0.41–0.64. No
paper studies an ME/CFS cohort. Therefore the ME/CFS application is mechanism transfer BY ANALOGY.

## 6. Null / contradicting / nuance evidence (MUST be integrated for balance)
1. In-group dissociation (DeBrabander 2019): autistic raters show trait judgment but not reduced
   willingness-to-interact.
2. Disclosure reduces bias (Norris 2024): informing perceivers improves employment-interview ratings.
3. Disclosure is condition-specific (Parisi 2026): schizophrenia disclosure WORSENS, autism-label
   IMPROVES impressions → do not present disclosure as a universal intervention.
4. Transcript exception (Wanigasekera 2025): no bias on verbal content alone → confirms "style not
   substance."
5. Relational reversal (Morrison 2020): same-neurotype dyads show better rapport.

## 7. Phase 3/5 integration guardrails (MANDATORY for downstream phases)
- **NO ME/CFS-specific first-impression mechanism may exceed `#speculation`.** No dedicated
  ME/CFS-cohort or chronic-fatigue first-impression study exists (documented gap). Do NOT write
  `#hypothesis-box`/`#fhypothesis`/`#clinical-finding` for a ME/CFS-specific first-impression claim.
- The transdiagnostic "style (flat affect / reduced facial expressivity / slowed movement) drives
  negative first impressions" claim (Sasson + Foster + Hemmesch + Parisi + Wanigasekera) is the strongest
  defensible cross-condition statement.
- The autism-domain content itself (double-empathy, relational model, in-group evidence) may be written
  as `#achievement`/`#hypothesis-box` at its own certainty, since it is autism-field evidence, but must
  keep the ME/CFS transfer explicit as analogy.
- Hypothesis H3 (autistic + ME/CFS double first-impression bias) is hypothesis, not demonstrated. Do not
  present compounded comorbid-autism+ME/CFS stigma as established fact — no dedicated study exists.
- Register linkage: `stigmatisation` subtree node 8.7 "Autism Stigma → Neurodiversity Reversal" (spec
  0.30, not integrated) may be strengthened; cross-ref in Phase 6/7.
