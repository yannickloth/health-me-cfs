---

## Headline

Unrefreshing sleep in ME/CFS has no objective polysomnographic substrate (Mariman 2013) but a candidate biological correlate — impaired glymphatic clearance (Thapaliya 2026). Behavioural sleep intervention (CBT-I) helps fatigue/cognitive symptoms in a related multisymptom illness (Chao 2021), but no direct ME/CFS trial exists. Mindfulness/meditation improves secondary outcomes (sleep, distress, QoL) without touching PEM or energy envelope (Schmidt 2011 null-primary; Meng 2024 meta-analysis). Pacing trial evidence is partial or null (Kos 2015; Meeus 2010), despite a stronger theoretical/energy-envelope case already in corpus.

## Framing to preserve (rival framings, not a single narrative)

- **F2 (mechanism-skeptic)** must be stated: unrefreshing sleep is disease-driven (glymphatic/subjective-objective), so sleep hygiene is unlikely to touch core symptoms.
- **F3 (narrow efficacy)** must be stated: mindfulness helps secondary outcomes, not PEM/energy envelope.
- **F4 (pacing-debate)** must be stated: pacing trial evidence is partial/null despite energy-envelope theory.
- **F1 (consensus-adjacent)** is the counterweight: CBT-I (cross-disease) and HRV-BF (feasibility) are promising adjuncts.

---

## GET/BPS hygiene

- Do NOT bin mindfulness/pacing papers as "GET literature". GET appears only as a comparator arm in @Windthorst2017HRVBFCFS (German pilot), which is not a BPS-promotion study.
- Distinguish GET-therapy (graded exercise as primary — already criticized in exercise-pem.bib/general.bib) from activity-management/pacing (@Kos2015ActivityPacing, @Meeus2010PainPhysiologyEducation, energy-envelope Jason stream).
