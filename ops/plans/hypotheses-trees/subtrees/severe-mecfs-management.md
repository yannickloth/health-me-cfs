### Node 1.1: Care-Delivery Model as a Modifiable Determinant (single-contact vs MDT)
**Environment:** `#open-question` (parked). **Certainty:** 0.22. **Status:** parked.
**Content:** Single-point-of-contact (specialist-nurse-led) care may outperform multidisciplinary-team care for severe patients via an *energy-coupling* mechanism: each interface with a new clinician imposes a discrete sensory+cognitive+postural cost; an MDT multiplies these cost-events and fragments continuity, increasing care-triggered PEM risk. Supported indirectly by the FINE nurse-delivery model @Wearden2010FINETrial and the MDT harm-monitoring gap @McPhee2019TreatmentHarm. Framed in the integrated ch40 clinical-finding `@cf:single-point-of-contact-severe` as an unresolved severity-stratified tension.
**Falsifiability:** In a severity-matched comparison, single-contact care yields a lower per-session interface-cost index and fewer care-triggered PEM episodes than MDT care at equal clinician-hours.
**Citations:** @S4ME2026ManagementSevereVerySevere @Wearden2010FINETrial @McPhee2019TreatmentHarm

### Node 11.2: Off-Label Drug "Little/No Benefit" as Absence-of-Evidence
**Environment:** `#open-question` (parked). **Certainty:** 0.40. **Status:** parked.
**Content:** The factsheet's appraisal that antihistamines, naltrexone, fludrocortisone, and ivabradine "probably produce little or no benefit and are more likely to produce adverse effects" @S4ME2026ManagementSevereVerySevere rests on absence of severe-ME/CFS-specific RCTs rather than a demonstrated null. Existing corpus harm entries (Rowe2001, Marchetta2025, Bolton2019, NaculLDN2025) support caution but do not establish true ineffectiveness. Surfaced in the integrated ch24 limitation `@lim:off-label-drug-expert-caution`.
**Falsifiability:** A severe-ME/CFS-specific RCT of any listed agent returning a meaningful efficacy signal would reject the caution-as-null; a well-powered null would confirm it.
**Citations:** @S4ME2026ManagementSevereVerySevere @Bolton2019NaltrexoneSAE

### Node 12.3: Rehab-Null Severity-Transfer Gap + Missing Harm Data
**Environment:** `#limitation` (parked). **Certainty:** 0.40. **Status:** parked.
**Content:** The factsheet's most confident claim (rehabilitation has no place) rests on paediatric/ambulatory RCT nulls (Gaunt2024, Wearden2010) @Gaunt2024PediatricGET @Wearden2010FINETrial that are least directly applicable to the severe/very-severe population the document addresses. Separately, McPhee2019 @McPhee2019TreatmentHarm shows adverse events are not systematically monitored, so harm from any severe-care intervention may be under-reported.
**Falsifiability:** A severe-ME/CFS-specific rehabilitation trial would test the severity-transfer assumption; systematic adverse-event monitoring would test the harm-reporting gap.
**Citations:** @Gaunt2024PediatricGET @Wearden2010FINETrial @McPhee2019TreatmentHarm
