# Integration Plan: ADHD → Late-Life Cognitive Impairment and the Energy-Deficit Link

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether ADHD's association with late-life cognitive impairment (MCI/dementia) — and the shared "chronic energy deficit" characteristic of ADHD/autism — has sufficient evidence to inform ME/CFS pathophysiology and cross-disease framing. ME/CFS itself is defined by a chronic energy deficit (impaired energy production, post-exertional malaise). The user's question: how is chronic energy deficit related to dementia, given that ADHD and autism share energy deficit as a characteristic?

**Topic slug:** `adhd-dementia-energy-deficit`

**Source paper (driving):** Bauer-Negrini G, Leffa DT, Ferreira PCL, et al. "Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder and Late-Life Cognitive Impairment." *JAMA Psychiatry*, published 2026-10-07. DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.3165. All of Us cohort, n=187 341 (≥50 y), mean follow-up 10.26 y. ADHD (n=3 026, 1.6%) → incident cognitive impairment HR 4.60 (95% CI 3.99–5.29); ADHD PRS highest-vs-lowest quintile HR 1.35 (1.25–1.46). Convergent clinical + genetic evidence.

**Target chapters (provisional):**
- `ch07-energy-metabolism` (energy-deficit mechanism)
- `ch09-neurological` (cognitive impairment / dementia)
- `ch15-integrative-models` (cross-disease energy-deficit framework)
- `ch16-comparative-nosology` (ADHD / autism / dementia parallels)
- `ch19-causal-hierarchy` (energy deficit as trigger vs amplifier)
- `ch20-universal-mechanisms` (if the energy-deficit→neurodegeneration mechanism generalizes)
- `hypothesis-registry.typ` (new hypothesis/speculation entries)

**Pre-identified hypotheses (preliminary certainty pending Phase 1–2):**
- ADHD's elevated dementia risk is partly driven by a shared cerebral/neuronal energy deficit (hypometabolism, mitochondrial dysfunction) rather than purely by diagnostic ascertainment or stimulant exposure — certainty pending
- The "chronic energy deficit" phenotype (shared by ADHD, autism, and ME/CFS) is a candidate common upstream factor linking neurodevelopmental conditions to later neurodegeneration — certainty pending
- ME/CFS's energy deficit is mechanistically analogous to the energy-deficit pathway linking ADHD to dementia — cross-disease speculative, certainty pending
