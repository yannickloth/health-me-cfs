## Part II — New Research Ideas to Develop (not in any existing plan or chapter)

### Research Ideas (for research-ideas/)

#### R1. The Perioperative ME/CFS Management Protocol
**Trigger:** PMID 42464211 (first empirical anesthesia data) + paper's ch24 medication sections + ch20 severe-urgent
**Core idea:** A preoperative risk-stratification + intraoperative drug-selection + postoperative crash-prevention protocol. Anesthetic agents differ in mitochondrial toxicity (propofol inhibits Complex I), autonomic impact (volatile anesthetics → vasodilation in already-dysautonomic patients), and immune effects. Paper already has all the mechanistic pieces; they need to be assembled for the operating room context.
**Testable prediction:** ME/CFS patients receiving total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) with propofol show longer postoperative PEM duration than those receiving sevoflurane-based maintenance, mediated by Complex I inhibition.
**Target:** New research-idea chapter + ch20 augmentation + ch24 anesthetic pharmacology subsection
**Difficulty:** Low (literature: anesthesia pharmacology + ME/CFS case reports; paper's mechanisms readily applicable)
**Software potential:** Pre-op risk calculator app (anesthesia type × severity × surgery invasiveness → predicted recovery time)

#### R2. Handgrip Strength as Pediatric PEM Biomarker
**Trigger:** PMID 42458481 (handgrip in CYP ME/CFS) + paper's ch23-pediatric (many empty stubs)
**Core idea:** Repeated handgrip dynamometry — 3 maximal contractions at baseline, then repeat at t+1h, t+24h post-exertion. The decay slope in maximal voluntary contraction is a quantitative, child-friendly, zero-cost PEM readout. Unlike CPET, it doesn't trigger PEM severely; unlike questionnaires, it's objective. Paper's ch23 has activity/PE stubs; this fills them with a validated protocol.
**Testable prediction:** Handgrip strength decline from baseline to 24h post-school-day correlates with self-reported PEM severity (r > 0.6) in pediatric ME/CFS.
**Target:** Fill ch23 stubs (subsec-safe-activity-maintenance, subsubsec-signs-of-overexertion) + new research-idea chapter + ch15 PEM
**Difficulty:** Low (literature: handgrip dynamometry normative data + the new paper; protocol is straightforward)

#### R3. The Immunosenescence-ME/CFS Interaction Model
**Trigger:** Two Comprehensive Physiology perspectives (2025, 2026) + paper's ch07 (immune) + ch09 (endocrine — HPA aging)
**Core idea:** ME/CFS may accelerate immunosenescence (thymic involution, CD8+ clonal expansion, reduced naive T-cell repertoire) while immunosenescence independently worsens ME/CFS via reduced viral control, impaired tissue repair, and chronic low-grade inflammation. This creates a vicious cycle that explains progressive deterioration with age and duration. Paper has the pieces (immune dysfunction, EBV, T-cell exhaustion) but not the age-immune interaction.
**Testable prediction:** ME/CFS patients show a thymic-involution signature (sjTREC levels, CD4/CD8 ratio) equivalent to healthy controls 15-20 years older, and the delta correlates with illness duration.
**Target:** ch07 augmentation + ch05-prognosis + ch14d (cross-disease aging/immune parallels) + new research-idea chapter
**Difficulty:** Medium (literature: immunosenescence biomarkers, TREC assay, thymic output literature)

#### R4. The Neurovascular Unit as a Unified Brain-Fog Model
**Trigger:** J Transl Med 2026 (neurovascular & synaptic milieu of brain cells in ME/CFS cognitive dysfunction) + paper's separate ch08 (neuro) and ch10 (vascular)
**Core idea:** The neurovascular unit (endothelial cell + pericyte + astrocyte endfoot + basement membrane + neuron) is the interface where cerebral blood flow meets neuronal demand. In ME/CFS, simultaneous endothelial dysfunction (ch10), microglial activation (ch08), and autonomic dysregulation (ch10) converge on NVU failure: neurovascular uncoupling → activity-dependent hypoperfusion → "brain fog on demand." This integrates three paper chapters into a single mechanistic node that explains why cognitive symptoms are effort-triggered and recover with rest.
**Testable prediction:** Functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) during an n-back task shows delayed/disrupted hemodynamic response in ME/CFS vs healthy controls, with the delay proportional to subjective brain fog severity.
**Target:** ch08 + ch10 cross-reference synthesis + ch15 (symptom-producing mechanisms) + new research-idea chapter
**Difficulty:** Low (integration of existing paper content + well-established NVU literature)

#### R5. The GLP-1 Receptor Axis in ME/CFS
**Trigger:** Solve ME/CFS Catalyst Award (Semaglutide trial) + vagal-gastric-denervation plan (VIP loss → GLP-1) + paper's ch09 (endocrine/metabolic)
**Core idea:** GLP-1 receptor agonists have pleiotropic effects relevant to ME/CFS beyond glucose control: anti-inflammatory (NLRP3 suppression, reduced NF-κB), neuroprotective (crosses BBB, reduces microglial activation), metabolic (mitochondrial biogenesis via AMPK), and autonomic (slows gastric emptying → reduces postprandial OI). The vagal-gastric-denervation plan shows VIP+ neuron loss in gastric mucosa → reduced GLP-1 secretion → metabolic + autonomic consequences. Semaglutide/tirzepatide may be the first drug class that simultaneously addresses metabolic, immune, neuroinflammatory, and autonomic domains — i.e., matches the multi-system nature of ME/CFS.
**Testable prediction:** ME/CFS patients with comorbid overweight/obesity show 30%+ improvement in COMPASS-31 composite autonomic score after 12 weeks of semaglutide, with greater improvement in those with lower baseline GLP-1 (postprandial).
**Target:** ch24 (new GLP-1 section) + ch09 + ch06 (metabolic) + vagal-gastric-denervation plan cross-reference + new research-idea chapter
**Difficulty:** Medium (literature: GLP-1 pharmacology, GLP-1 in neuroinflammation, Semaglutide trials in non-diabetic conditions)

#### R6. The Infection-Associated Chronic Condition and Illness (IACCI) Policy Framework
**Trigger:** PMID 41967005 (CID perspective) + health-system roadmap paper (Front Health Serv 2026) + paper's Part 4 (research infrastructure)
**Core idea:** The CID paper proposes "IACCI" as a unifying category for ME/CFS, Long COVID, PTLDS, PANS/PANDAS, and post-Ebola/chikungunya syndromes. The paper's cross-disease comparisons (ch14d) and health-system content (none currently) should adopt this framing. Add a section in Part 4 describing what an IACCI-prepared health system requires: cross-trigger surveillance, shared biomarker platforms, staged rehabilitation protocols, integrated specialty clinics, and a research infrastructure that studies these conditions together rather than in silos.
**Testable recommendation:** Countries with integrated post-infectious clinics (e.g., Germany's Charité Fatigue Center) show shorter diagnostic delay than countries with disease-siloed care.
**Target:** Part 4 new section + ch35-controversies (health-system failures) + ch14d augmentation
**Difficulty:** Low (descriptive/policy — the paper's existing cross-disease content adapts easily)

#### R7. Mitochondrial Stabilizers as a New Drug Class
**Trigger:** Solve ME/CFS Catalyst Award (IVO-21) + paper's ch06 (mitochondrial dysfunction) + ch24 (mitochondrial support — currently only supplements)
**Core idea:** Distinguish "mitochondrial support" (CoQ10, carnitine, NADH — substrate-level support) from "mitochondrial stabilization" (IVO-21, SS-31/elamipretide, MitoQ — membrane-level protection). The former provides fuel to a damaged engine; the latter repairs the engine. Paper's ch24 covers supplements extensively but has no section on mitochondrial-stabilizing drugs in the pipeline. This is a distinct pharmacotherapeutic category that should be separated from nutraceuticals.
**Testable prediction:** Mitochondrial-stabilizing agents show greater effect size on PEM recovery time than substrate-level mitochondrial support in a network meta-analysis of ME/CFS trials.
**Target:** Split ch24 sec-mitochondrial-support into "substrate support" (existing) and "membrane stabilizers" (new) + ch06 + ch27 (emerging therapies)
**Difficulty:** Low (few papers; mainly compound pharmacology + mitochondrial bioenergetics)

#### R8. T-Cell Antigen Discovery as the Critical Open Question
**Trigger:** Solve ME/CFS Catalyst Award (Selin — T-cell antigen discovery) + paper's ch07 (T-cell dysfunction) + cynaera-gaps plan (T-cell mitochondrial exhaustion)
**Core idea:** The paper describes CD8+ T-cell exhaustion, dysfunction, and skewing extensively but doesn't explicitly state the elephant in the room: we don't know what antigen(s) the T-cells are responding to. Is it EBV lytic proteins? HHV-6? A self-antigen (autoimmunity)? A persistent COVID-derived peptide? Answering this determines whether ME/CFS is a chronic infection, an autoimmune disease, or both — and determines whether antivirals, immunosuppression, or both are appropriate. The paper should frame this as the single highest-leverage open question.
**Target:** ch07 augmentation + ch38 (proposed studies) + ch32 (clinical trials — antigen-specific immunotherapy)
**Difficulty:** Low (no new literature — it's a framing exercise for the paper's existing content)

### Software Ideas (for software-ideas/)

#### S1. Perioperative ME/CFS Risk Calculator
**Related to:** R1
**Core function:** Input: surgery type, anesthetic plan, patient severity level, medication list. Output: predicted PEM recovery time, anesthetic agent recommendations, prophylactic measures (IV fluids, steroid stress-dose, antiemetic choice). Uses paper's ch30 cascade-tracing logic to identify which anesthetic mechanism→symptom pathway is most likely to be triggered.
**Users:** Anesthesiologists, surgeons, ME/CFS patients planning surgery
**Novelty:** No preoperative ME/CFS decision-support tool exists anywhere.

#### S2. Handgrip PEM Tracker (Pediatric)
**Related to:** R2
**Core function:** Bluetooth handgrip dynamometer paired with phone app. 3 baseline squeezes in the morning. Repeat after school/activity. Delta plotted over weeks → individual PEM threshold and recovery curve. Gamified for children (animals, achievements, "beat your baseline" framing).
**Users:** Pediatric ME/CFS patients, parents, pediatricians
**Novelty:** First objective pediatric PEM monitoring tool. Fills the IOM-identified gap of "no brief in-office PEM test for children."

#### S3. Drug-Interaction Energy Accounting Tool
**Related to:** earlier brainstorm discussion
**Core function:** Input medication list + doses. Computes CYP450 ATP cost, hepatic conjugation load, renal clearance energy cost. Compares against estimated PEM energy budget (derived from ch06 metabolic reserve estimates). Flags regimens where total daily ATP cost of drug metabolism exceeds predicted reserve.
**Users:** Physicians, pharmacists, ME/CFS patients on polypharmacy (typical is 5-15 drugs)
**Novelty:** No tool exists that sums the *energetic* cost of drug metabolism. Standard DDI checkers only check for pharmacokinetic interactions, not total metabolic load.

#### S4. The CCHS Breath-Stimulus App
**Related to:** earlier brainstorm discussion
**Core function:** Phone microphone detects respiratory pauses > 15s during sleep. Delivers a rising-intensity vibratory stimulus until breathing resumes. Uses the CCHS (Ondine's curse) paradigm — central hypoventilation — applied to ME/CFS patients with documented central sleep apnea or "forgetting to breathe."
**Users:** ME/CFS patients with sleep-disordered breathing, especially those with autonomic failure
**Novelty:** CCHS ventilator paradigm repurposed for phone-based respiratory support. No hardware required.
