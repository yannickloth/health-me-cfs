# Integration Plan: Pramipexole (IR/ER) in ME/CFS — RLS-dose Use, LDA Combo, Co-prescription Combos

**Purpose:** Integrate pramipexole (immediate-release = Mirapexin; extended-release = Sifrol) as a treatment topic: (1) use in ME/CFS at RLS-relevant doses (dopaminergic augmentation probe), (2) IR-vs-ER pharmacokinetic differences, (3) the pramipexole + low-dose aripiprazole (LDA) combination — partial-agonist attenuation of full agonism — and (4) combinations with common ME/CFS co-prescriptions (fludrocortisone, midodrine, LDN, pyridostigmine, beta-blockers, antihistamines, gabapentin/pregabalin, trazodone, amitriptyline, NSAIDs, modafinil, IVIG). Must engage (not contradict) the existing paper stance: pramipexole's D2/D3 full agonism "can worsen ME/CFS symptoms even while treating RLS" by suppressing the D2-brake without D1-go activation.

**Clinical motivation:** patient aeiuno — RLS confirmed by treating physician, pramipexole (Mirapexin) titration started 2026-11-18 (0.5–3 mg/day range, Dr. Pincherle); first-dose reaction at ~0.25 mg: dizziness, weakness, visual disturbance, sickness leave. This first-dose vasomotor/CNS reaction at RLS dose is the paper's predicted DA-supersensitivity signature — a real-world data point the new content must address without individualizing the paper.

**Pre-identified rival framings (Phase 1 must search these, not just confirmatory):**
1. Full D2/D3 agonists WORSEN ME/CFS (existing sec-12 clinical note; D2-brake suppression) — find direct evidence for/against.
2. Dopamine-agonist nulls/negatives in CFS (historical bromocriptine/pergolide RCTs in CFS).
3. RLS in ME/CFS: treatment evidence (pramipexole vs alpha-2-delta vs iron) and whether RLS treatment changes ME/CFS core outcomes.
4. LDA + full-agonist co-administration: PD literature (aripiprazole as adjunct for DA-agonist-induced impulse control disorders / hallucinations / dyskinesia) — partial agonist attenuates full agonist.
5. Harm: sudden sleep onset / somnolence, nausea, orthostatic hypotension, ICD, augmentation, DAWS (withdrawal).
