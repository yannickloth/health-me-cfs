# Literature Summary: L-Theanine-Caffeine Combination on Selective Attention in ADHD

**Topic slug:** `theanine-caffeine-adhd`
**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Primary paper:** Nawarathna et al. 2026 (Nutr Neurosci, DOI 10.1080/1028415X.2026.2659148, PMID 42096377, SLCTR/2023/004)

## Scope

Phase-1 literature research for a treatment/nutraceutical topic. The primary paper tested a high-dose L-theanine-caffeine combination against methylphenidate and placebo for selective attention in adolescents with ADHD (n=21, three-way crossover). This topic is indirect to ME/CFS: the population is an "other disease model" (ADHD), and relevance to ME/CFS runs through (a) caffeine's adenosine-receptor antagonism and stimulant/metabolic effects, and (b) L-theanine's glutamate/GABA modulation and neuroprotection. Full harm-search and indirect-biochemical-link mandate applied.

## Primary Paper Summary

- **Nawarathna et al. 2026** (n=21 adolescents with ADHD, 10-19 y, 18 boys; double-blind placebo-controlled counterbalanced three-way crossover).
- Combo (P=0.038) and methylphenidate (P=0.035) reduced false alarms vs placebo; hits and A' unchanged.
- Only methylphenidate improved reaction time (43.89 ms, P=0.018).
- Both increased P3b amplitude and decreased P3b latency (P<0.05); both counteracted reaction-time slowing over task duration.
- Interpretation: combo enhances neural resource allocation/speed of selective-attention deployment but does not match methylphenidate on processing speed.

## Findings by Search Mandate

### 1. Direct topic (L-theanine, caffeine, combination, ADHD attention)
- Kahathuduwa 2020 (n=5) — proof-of-concept; combination (not components) improved sustained attention/cognition; caffeine alone *worsened* inhibitory control.
- Gerolymos 2026 meta-analysis (31 RCTs, n=1168) — L-theanine improves choice reaction time (SMD 0.51) but has NO effect on fatigue.
- Anas Sohail 2021 — mechanistic review: caffeine = adenosine A1/A2A antagonism; theanine = glutamate reuptake inhibition + GABA-A neuroprotection.
- Al Shahab 2025 — systematic review: caffeine alone NO benefit in ADHD; combination may help.

### 2. Null/negative results
- **Caffeine monotherapy null** (Al Shahab 2025): caffeine alone showed no significant ADHD benefit; combination-dependent.
- **L-theanine fatigue null** (Gerolymos 2026): no effect on fatigue across 31 RCTs — the critical null for ME/CFS applicability.
- **Caffeine-mechanism dissociation from ME/CFS** (Palacios 2023): caffeine inversely associated with *severe fatigue* but NOT with ME/CFS.

### 3. Competing mechanisms
- Becker 2022 (n=48 healthy) — methylphenidate, modafinil, caffeine all enhance memory via FPN-DMN functional-connectivity decoupling (shared dopamine/network mechanism). Methylphenidate's DAT blockade is the mechanistic comparator.

### 4. Harm (treatment topic — MANDATORY)
- Carbone 2025 — caffeine use disorder: adenosine-receptor down-regulation → tolerance + withdrawal (headache, irritability, **fatigue**); excessive use → anxiety, sleep disturbance, cognitive/motor impairment.
- Gerolymos 2026 — no serious adverse events for L-theanine across 31 RCTs (safety signal).
- Weigel 2021 — ME/CFS patients self-report *elevated* caffeine intake (self-medication), no HRQoL benefit demonstrated.

### 5. Indirect biochemical/systemic links to ME/CFS (MANDATORY)
- **Adenosine/purinergic signaling:** Srinivasan & Joseph 2026 — ATP-P2X2/P2X3 purinergic dysregulation hypothesis for ME/CFS dysgeusia/PEM. Caffeine antagonizes adenosine A1/A2A receptors — the same downstream purinergic axis. Documented reasoning: *"Caffeine antagonizes adenosine A1/A2A receptors; searched for adenosine/purinergic signaling AND ME/CFS → 1 hypothesis paper (Srinivasan 2026) + no direct clinical trials."*
- **Caffeine metabolism / energy:** Erasmus 2019 (n=576 chronic-fatigue women) — caffeine as CYP1A2 phase-I hepatic probe; increased phase II glucuronidation in fatigue. Documented reasoning: *"Caffeine is a metabolic stimulant and phase-I probe; searched for caffeine AND biotransformation/chronic fatigue → 1 study (Erasmus 2019)."*
- **Post-exertional malaise:** No direct results ("caffeine AND post-exertional malaise" = 0 hits; "adenosine AND post-exertional malaise" = 5 hits, only Srinivasan 2026 relevant).
- **GABA/glutamate modulation (theanine):** No direct ME/CFS link found; theanine's GABA/glutamate mechanism documented via Anas Sohail 2021 and Han 2024 (tea components review, not integrated — general psychiatric, no ME/CFS).

No cohort overlap detected. Srinivasan 2026 (Front Med, hypothesis) and Palacios 2023 (Nurses' Health Study) and Weigel 2021 (Griffith University, Australia) and Erasmus 2019 (North-West University, South Africa) draw on distinct, non-overlapping cohorts. ## Bottom Line for Integration (Phase 3 note only — no chapter edits here)

The combination's selective-attention benefit is real but ADHD-specific, small-sample, and the component that matters for ME/CFS fatigue (L-theanine) is null for fatigue. Caffeine's adenosine-receptor antagonism is the most mechanistically relevant axis to ME/CFS (via purinergic signaling), but the only bridge is a hypothesis paper (Srinivasan 2026). Caffeine carries harm signals (withdrawal-fatigue via adenosine-receptor down-regulation) that caution against recommending it for ME/CFS fatigue, despite patient self-medication patterns (Weigel 2021). Recommend integration framed as speculation/open-question, not as a supported treatment.
