## Exclusions

- Papers on immune cell mitochondrial dysfunction in conditions other than ME/CFS (e.g., cancer, HIV, general immunology)
- Papers on NK cell mitochondrial function without T-cell data (Brenu/Marshall-Gradisnik NK-focused studies)
- General ME/CFS immunology reviews without mitochondrial/metabolic component
- Papers on autoantibody profiling without T-cell metabolism data
- Papers on EBV in ME/CFS without T-cell metabolic component
- Papers focused exclusively on B cell metabolism without T-cell data
- Papers focused on serum metabolomics without immune cell isolation
- Single-cell RNA-seq papers (Sun 2024) with n=4: too small, findings not independently replicated

## Key Discoveries

1. **Convergent evidence for CD8+ T-cell exhaustion:** Mandarano 2020 → Iu 2024 (PNAS) → Eaton-Fitch 2024 (JCI Insight) → Shahbaz 2026 show progressive refinement from metabolic phenotype → transcriptional state → clinical validation

2. **Shared pathology across ME/CFS and Long COVID:** Shankar 2025 (PNAS), Gil 2024, Eaton-Fitch 2024, Petrov 2026 all demonstrate comparable (but not identical) T-cell dysfunction in both conditions

3. **Sex differences:** Shankar 2025 identifies sex-specific ROS patterns — females show total ROS elevation + T-cell hyperproliferation; males show normal ROS but pronounced lipid oxidative damage

4. **Laboratory convergence:** Hanson lab (Cornell) leads with 4 key papers (Mandarano 2020, Iu 2024, Maya 2023a, Maya 2023b); Marshall-Gradisnik lab (Griffith) adds exhaustion gene expression panel; Cliff/Nacul lab (LSHTM) adds large-scale immunophenotyping

5. **Gap:** No study has directly imaged T-cell mitochondrial morphology (fission/fragmentation) in ME/CFS. The mitochondrial fragmentation evidence in ME/CFS comes from neuronal (Hennig 2022) and fibroblast/lymphoblast (Missailidis 2020) studies, not primary T cells.
