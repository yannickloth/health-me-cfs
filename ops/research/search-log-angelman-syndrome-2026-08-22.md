## Indirect Biochemical / Systemic Link Logic (documented)

Topic mechanism (what Angelman syndrome does biochemically):
- UBE3A encodes E6AP, an E3 ubiquitin ligase; loss disrupts proteostasis (target-protein degradation).
- UBE3A loss impairs GABAergic signaling — reduced tonic GABA-A receptor inhibition.
- UBE3A loss impairs mitochondrial function — reduced ATP, impaired oxidative phosphorylation, elevated ROS.
- AS presents with prominent sleep disturbance and (variably) autonomic features.
- These map to ME/CFS domains: mitochondrial/energy deficit, oxidative stress, excitatory/inhibitory imbalance, sleep pathology.

Indirect search pattern (map these mechanisms into ME/CFS):
- UBE3A itself → "UBE3A AND fatigue/CFS" (M1 = 0) — no direct protein-level link to ME/CFS in the literature.
- Mitochondrial → "UBE3A AND mitochondria" (M11 → Panov, Su), "AS AND mitochondria" (M4/M5).
- GABAergic → "GABAergic AND CFS" (M3 → Wirth 2026, pre-existing), "UBE3A AND GABA" (G2 → Watanabe).
- Oxidative stress → "AS AND oxidative stress" (M5 → Simchi).
- Sleep → "AS AND sleep disturbance" (M7 → Qu).
- Proteostasis → "UBE3A AND proteostasis" (C1), none directly ME/CFS-linked.
- Autonomic → "AS AND autonomic" (M8, G3) — no direct hits; AS autonomic overlap with ME/CFS is unestablished.

Result:
- Direct AS + ME/CFS: 0 PubMed-indexed papers (D1, D2, N1) — genuine research gap; no evidence of co-occurrence or direct clinical overlap.
- Strongest mechanistic bridges: UBE3A → mitochondrial/energy (Su 2011, Panov 2020) and UBE3A → oxidative stress (Simchi 2023), and UBE3A → GABAergic disinhibition (Watanabe 2026), the latter mirroring the E/I imbalance proposed in ME/CFS (Wirth 2026, pre-existing).
- Autonomic and neuroinflammation overlap with ME/CFS: no supporting literature found (M6, M8, G1, G3 screened, excluded).
- Net assessment: overlap is plausible mechanistically (mitochondrial/GABA/sleep) but unproven; no AS-ME/CFS co-occurrence or direct clinical data exists. This is a cross-disease analogy, not an established shared pathophysiology.
