# Literature Summary: Adult Red-Flags & Safety-Netting Protocol (Medical-Emergency Decompensation)

**Topic slug:** adult-red-flags-safety-netting-protocol
**Date:** 2026-08-14
**Agent:** literature-integrator
**Research stream:** adult-red-flags-safety-netting-protocol

---

## Headline finding (the gap under test)

**A consolidated adult MEDICAL-EMERGENCY decompensation protocol for ME/CFS is NOT present in the
literature.** ME/CFS-specific emergency literature is sparse: only one paper directly addresses
CFS in the emergency department (Timbol & Baraniuk 2019). No ME/CFS-specific paper quantifies
sudden-cardiac-death risk in POTS, and no new severe-ME mortality cohort exists beyond the corpus
(Roberts 2016; McManimen). The evidence base for the eight safety domains is therefore largely
**transferable** from adjacent at-risk populations (anorexia nervosa for refeeding; prolonged
immobility/catatonia for VTE + pressure-ulcer + aspiration; general primary care for serious-disease
red flags; general stroke/TIA for focal-neurology red flags).

**Clinical implication:** the protocol must be assembled from (a) ME/CFS-specific anchors
(ED presentation, orthostatic decompensation, differential diagnosis) plus (b) general-medicine
safety-netting for the complications of severe bedbound illness, with population-relevance
discounts applied throughout. The "red-flag triggers to go to ED now" are evidence-transferred,
not ME/CFS-specific.

## Per-paper evaluation

Certainty scale: High 0.7–1.0 (n>100, top journal, replicated); Medium 0.4–0.69 (n=20–100,
peer-reviewed, single); Low 0.0–0.39 (n<20, preprint, flawed).
Population weight: ME/CFS 1.00; Long COVID 0.85; POTS/FM/comorbid 0.80; anorexia-restricted-intake 0.80;
general 0.75; animal 0.50; in vitro 0.40; in silico 0.30.
Discounted certainty = raw certainty × population weight.

### Domain 1 — Malignancy & serious organic disease presenting as ME/CFS-like illness

#### 1. Stadje 2016 — Differential diagnosis of tiredness: systematic review (BMC Fam Pract)
- **Design/sample:** SR + meta-analysis of 26 primary-care studies of patients presenting with tiredness.
- **Findings:** Prevalence among those with tiredness: anaemia 2.8%; malignancy 0.6%; serious somatic disease 4.3%; depression 18.5%. Somatic-disease prevalence identical in controls without tiredness; depression more frequent among the tired. Conclusion: extensive investigation only warranted on specific history/exam findings.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.70 (systematic review/meta-analysis, BMC peer-reviewed, reproducible).
- **Population weight:** 0.75 (general primary care).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.53.
- **Limitations:** Heterogeneity across studies; CFS not pooled; primary-care (not severe ME/CFS) population. **Competing interpretation:** low malignancy yield argues AGAINST blanket cancer screening in chronic fatigue absent red flags.

#### 2. Levine 1992 — Does CFS predispose to non-Hodgkin's lymphoma? (Cancer Res)
- **Design/sample:** Population-based cancer-incidence analysis (Nevada SEER-linked), NHL time trends before/after the 1984–86 CFS outbreaks.
- **Findings:** No statistically significant increase in NHL attributable to the CFS outbreak at state level; trends consistent with national SEER.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.45 (ecological/time-trend, small-scale, dated).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS population).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.45.
- **Limitations:** Ecological design, state-level (not individual), single region, 1992. **Competing interpretation:** does not support malignancy as a common ME/CFS outcome — supports the differential being a red-flag search, not an automatic malignancy work-up.

#### 3. Kobayashi 2022 — Mistaken Identity: diagnoses misattributed to Lyme (Am J Med)
- **Design/sample:** Retrospective cohort, 1261 patients referred for possible Lyme; 1061 (84%) had no active Lyme.
- **Findings:** 690 (65%) received other diagnoses; 405 newly diagnosed. Leading: anxiety/depression 21%, fibromyalgia 11%, CFS 7%, migraine 7%. Non-syndromic newly diagnosed: MS n=11, malignancy n=8, Parkinson n=8, sarcoidosis n=4, ALS n=4.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.60 (single-center retrospective, Am J Med, n=1261).
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (ME/CFS-adjacent — CFS is one misattributed diagnosis; differential-relevant).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.48.
- **Limitations:** Referral-center selection, Lyme-specific framing, descriptive. **Competing/utility:** documents that serious organic disease (incl. malignancy, MS) can present as chronic multi-system symptoms and be missed — supports safety-netting that a red-flag search must not be foreclosed by a syndromic label.

### Domain 2 — Refeeding syndrome in severe/very-severe ME/CFS

#### 4. Kohn 2011 — Refeeding in anorexia nervosa: pathophysiology of protein-calorie malnutrition (Curr Opin Pediatr)
- **Design/sample:** Narrative review of refeeding physiology and outcomes.
- **Findings:** "Start low and go slow" calorie prescription is unlikely to prevent refeeding syndrome; can prolong starvation. Carbohydrate proportion (>40% calories from CHO) is more important than total calories; continuous feeding with <40% CHO reduces refeeding risk; postprandial hypoglycemia is a key risk.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.60 (peer-reviewed review, specialist author team).
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (anorexia restricted-intake → transferable to bedbound ME/CFS).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.48.
- **Limitations:** Narrative review, paediatric/AN focus, no RCT. **Competing:** carbohydrate-driven refeeding physiology is general; applies to any starved/bedbound patient.

#### 5. Proulx-Cabana 2022 — Initial inpatient management of severe anorexia nervosa (Nutrients)
- **Design/sample:** Narrative review + proposed admission protocol for severe AN (BMI<12).
- **Findings:** Higher initial caloric intake supported; continuous NG feeding for BMI<12; 72-h hypoglycemia monitoring; consider continuous cardiac monitoring (bradycardia <30) and systematic phosphate supplementation.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (narrative review + expert protocol, peer-reviewed).
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (severe restricted-intake AN → bedbound ME/CFS).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.44.
- **Limitations:** AN-specific, adolescent, protocol not validated in a trial. **Transferable:** monitoring of phosphate/hypoglycemia/bradycardia during refeeding of a severely malnourished bedbound patient.

#### 6. Ridout 2016 — Daily laboratory monitoring is of poor value in eating disorders (J Adolesc Health) — **NULL/negative**
- **Design/sample:** Retrospective chart review, 196 admissions for eating disorders; guideline-recommended daily lab monitoring.
- **Findings:** Zero cases of refeeding syndrome; 3960 labs, 1.9% below normal, 0.05% critical; 0.28% prompted supplementation, none changed management; cost $269,250 with value 1.04×10⁻⁸ outcomes/dollar.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (single-center retrospective, n=196, peer-reviewed).
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (anorexia restricted-intake).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.44.
- **Limitations:** Single center, retrospective, low baseline risk (mostly non-severe AN). **Competing/negative:** directly challenges aggressive daily-lab-monitoring dogma in refeeding — the very monitoring some safety protocols mandate. In severe ME/CFS where lab access is difficult, this supports risk-stratified (not routine-daily) monitoring.

#### 7. Araujo Castro 2018 — The refeeding syndrome: importance of phosphorus (Med Clin Barc)
- **Design/sample:** Narrative review of refeeding syndrome (RS) pathophysiology and management.
- **Findings:** Hallmark hypophosphatemia, plus hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, thiamine deficiency, sodium/fluid imbalance. RS is potentially fatal but preventable; NICE 2006 guideline is the only treatment framework but based on low-quality evidence; underdiagnosed due to no universal definition.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (peer-reviewed review).
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (malnourished/restricted-intake population).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.44.
- **Limitations:** Review, NICE guidance cited as low-evidence. **Key for safety:** establishes refeeding as a real, fatal-but-preventable risk in any malnourished patient started on feeding.

#### 8. Kwiatkowska 2025 — Comprehensive care of the patient with refeeding syndrome (Pol Przegl Chir)
- **Design/sample:** Narrative review of current RS diagnosis/management.
- **Findings:** RS arises from overly aggressive/inadequate adaptive feeding, exacerbated by malnutrition; causes respiratory failure, neurological disorders, circulatory failure, seizures. Risk factors mostly modifiable. Guidance: gradual energy increase + regular monitoring of phosphorus, potassium, magnesium.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.50 (peer-reviewed review, Polish surgical journal).
- **Population weight:** 0.75 (general critically-ill/malnourished population).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.38.
- **Limitations:** Perioperative/surgical framing; review not systematic. **Transferable:** monitors electrolytes during refeeding; documents systemic (including neuro/cardiac/seizure) consequences of RS.

#### 9. Norrington 2012 — Medical management of acute severe anorexia nervosa (Arch Dis Child Educ Pract Ed)
- **Design/sample:** Review of medical management of acute severe AN, system-based complication approach, feeding + refeeding-syndrome guidance.
- **Findings:** Identifies at-risk patients; system-based approach to complications; guidance on feeding and refeeding-syndrome management.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.50 (peer-reviewed practice review, paediatric focus).
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (acute severe restricted-intake AN).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.40.
- **Limitations:** Paediatric, not validated. **Transferable:** risk identification and feeding approach for the severely malnourished bedbound patient.

### Domain 3 — VTE risk from immobility in severe chronic illness

#### 10. Hull 2010 — EXCLAIM: extended VTE prophylaxis in acutely ill medical patients with reduced mobility (Ann Intern Med)
- **Design/sample:** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial, n=5963; acutely ill medical patients ≥40 y with reduced mobility (bed rest ± bathroom privileges); open-label enoxaparin 10 d then enoxaparin 40 mg/d vs placebo 28 d.
- **Findings:** Extended enoxaparin reduced VTE 2.5% vs 4.0% (ARD −1.53%); increased major bleeding 0.8% vs 0.3%. Benefit concentrated in women, >75 y, and level-1 immobility. Eligibility amended mid-trial (added VTE-risk factors) — a trial limitation.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.70 (large RCT, Ann Intern Med, multi-center).
- **Population weight:** 0.75 (general acutely-ill immobile patients → bedbound ME/CFS).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.53.
- **Limitations:** Immobility was acute/recent, not chronic; trial amended; bleeding risk in real bedbound ME/CFS (often anticoagulation-sensitive) is a safety concern. **Transferable:** establishes that even short immobilization carries measurable VTE risk and that prophylaxis is a benefit/bleed trade-off — directly relevant to safety-netting for immobile severe ME/CFS.

### Domain 4 — Sepsis / pressure injury / aspiration risk from immobility

#### 11. Clinebell 2014 — Preventing medical complications of catatonia: DVT/PE, pressure ulcers, contractures, nutrition (J Clin Psychiatry)
- **Design/sample:** Case report + literature review of preventive guidelines for 4 immobility complications in catatonia (a severe immobile inpatient state).
- **Findings:** DVT/PE prophylaxis with anticoagulants for low-acute-bleeding-risk patients; pressure-ulcer prevention via frequent skin evaluation, support surfaces, repositioning; early enteral nutrition for prolonged immobility. Contracture data extrapolated from neurologic-injury studies.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (review + case report, peer-reviewed).
- **Population weight:** 0.75 (general immobile/institutionalized → bedbound ME/CFS).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.41.
- **Limitations:** Catatonia-specific, review not systematic, single case. **Transferable:** a framework for pressure-ulcer (→ infection/sepsis) and aspiration-risk prevention in any prolonged-immobility patient; DVT prophylaxis as a benefit/bleed trade-off.

### Domain 5 — POTS / autonomic / cardiac-syncope emergency risk — **COVERAGE GAP, largely in corpus**
- No new ME/CFS-specific paper found quantifying sudden-cardiac-death risk or defining when POTS collapse is a cardiac emergency vs benign vasovagal/NMH. Existing corpus covers the substrate (BouHolaigah1995NMH, Novak2026, Jason2024TiltTable, Razumovsky2003CerebralHemodynamics, Rowe2001FludrocortisoneNMH). **Flagged:** the cardiac-vs-neurally-mediated syncope distinction must be transferred from general cardiology; ME/CFS-specific sudden-cardiac data absent.

### Domain 6 — Focal neurology / TIA / stroke-like presentation

#### 12. Vigen 2018 — Stroke risk is low after urgently treated TIA (J Stroke Cerebrovasc Dis)
- **Design/sample:** Retrospective, n=261 TIA patients, urgent Stroke-Unit assessment + immediate prevention; follow-up to 2 y.
- **Findings:** Stroke incidence 1.5% at 1 month, 3.4% at 1 year, 4.2% at end of follow-up; median 90 d to recurrence; ABCD2 score poorly predictive.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (single-center retrospective, peer-reviewed).
- **Population weight:** 0.75 (general TIA/stroke).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.41.
- **Limitations:** Single center, no control. **Transferable:** supports that NEW focal symptoms must be treated as possible TIA/stroke with urgent assessment — urgent referral is the safety-netting message, not benign attribution to ME/CFS.

#### 13. El Husseini 2013 — "Code stroke": hospitalized vs ED patients (J Stroke Cerebrovasc Dis)
- **Design/sample:** Retrospective, 93 in-hospital + 204 ED code-stroke activations, 1 y.
- **Findings:** Hospitalized patients less likely to have stroke/TIA (26.8% vs 51.4%) and less often thrombolyzed; 63.4% of in-hospital code strokes were mimics; "altered mental status" was the sole symptom in 48% of in-hospital vs 10% of ED patients and independently predicted stroke mimic (OR 63.5).
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (single-center retrospective, peer-reviewed).
- **Population weight:** 0.75 (general stroke).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.41.
- **Limitations:** Single center, retrospective. **Transferable:** in an already-ill/hospitalized (or bedbound, symptom-heavy) population, acute focal neurologic deficits are more likely true stroke than in the ED — i.e., a bedbound ME/CFS patient with sudden focal symptoms should NOT be presumed to have a "mimic"; the default must be urgent stroke work-up.

#### 14. Uehara 2014 — Guidelines for management of patients with TIA (Front Neurol Neurosci)
- **Design/sample:** Review of evidence-based TIA management guidelines.
- **Findings:** TIA is a medical emergency with high early-stroke risk; guidelines mandate urgent referral/expert evaluation/immediate treatment; TIA and minor ischemic stroke share pathophysiologic and preventive approaches.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (peer-reviewed guideline review).
- **Population weight:** 0.75 (general TIA).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.41.
- **Limitations:** Review; guideline currency. **Transferable:** TIA = emergency; sudden focal neurologic symptoms require urgent care regardless of background diagnosis.

### Domain 7 — Mortality & co-morbidity in severe ME/CFS — **covered by existing corpus**
- Roberts 2016 (Lancet; SLAM CRIS; no overall excess mortality vs general population, but suicide risk elevated) and McManimen (2016/2018 mortality-in-ME/CFS series) are already in `epidemiology.bib`/`general.bib`. No new severe-ME mortality cohort found. **Flagged:** severe/very-severe ME/CFS mortality (cachexia/failure-to-thrive) is clinically recognized but not robustly quantified — a documentation gap.

### Domain 8 — Emergency department / acute care experience of ME/CFS patients

#### 15. Timbol & Baraniuk 2019 — Chronic fatigue syndrome in the emergency department (Open Access Emerg Med)
- **Design/sample:** Anonymous online survey, n=282 physician-diagnosed CFS; scored core CFS symptoms, ED reasons, attitudes; open-text thematic analysis.
- **Findings:** 59% had gone to an ED; 1/3 of ED presentations consistent with orthostatic intolerance; 42% dismissed as psychosomatic; ED staff not knowledgeable about CFS; encounter favorability 3.6/10; non-attendees avoided ED (nothing could be done / not taken seriously).
- **Raw certainty:** 0.45 (self-report survey, no control, single center).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.45.
- **Limitations:** Self-report, selection/social-desirability bias, n=282, US. **Key for safety:** the most frequent acute ED driver in CFS is orthostatic intolerance; a large fraction are dismissed as psychosomatic — safety-netting must arm patients/caregivers to communicate medical-emergency triggers credibly.

#### 16. Bowden 2026 — ME/CFS health/labour/social outcomes on a benefit: NZ nationwide cross-sectional (BMC Public Health)
- **Design/sample:** Nationwide IDI registry cohort, n=1902 working-age ME/CFS on health/disability benefit, propensity-score-matched comparisons.
- **Findings:** ME/CFS had significantly higher ED visits (18.8% vs 12.8%) and polypharmacy (>10 meds 32.8% vs 14.2%) vs general population; lower hospitalization (11.2% vs 20.9%) than other benefit recipients; low employment (18.3%).
- **Raw certainty:** 0.60 (nationwide registry, BMC peer-reviewed, n=1902).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.60.
- **Limitations:** Benefit-recipient subset only (not all ME/CFS), NZ-specific, cross-sectional, diagnostic-coding dependent. **Relevant:** elevated ED utilization in ME/CFS confirms the ED is a real contact point where safety-netting knowledge matters.

## Safety-relevant harm/adverse findings (must be flagged explicitly)
- **Refeeding syndrome (Domains 2):** potentially fatal but preventable; hallmark hypophosphatemia + hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia/thiamine deficiency; consequences include respiratory failure, circulatory failure, seizures, neurological disorders (Kwiatkowska2025; AraujoCastro2018). Bedbound severe ME/CFS is a high-risk refeeding population; carbohydrate proportion and electrolyte monitoring are the key mitigations (Kohn2011; ProulxCabana2022).
- **VTE (Domain 3):** even recent immobilization carries measurable VTE risk (2.5% vs 4.0% in EXCLAIM); prophylaxis is a benefit-vs-bleed trade-off — of note in ME/CFS where bleeding risk and anticoagulation tolerance vary (Hull2010).
- **Sepsis / pressure-ulcer / aspiration (Domain 4):** immobility predisposes to pressure ulcers (→ local infection/sepsis), aspiration, contractures, and malnutrition; prevention (skin evaluation, repositioning, support surfaces, early enteral nutrition) is the safety-netting standard (Clinebell2014).
- **ED dismissal (Domain 8):** 42% of CFS patients reporting ED use were dismissed as psychosomatic; most common acute driver was orthostatic intolerance (Timbol2019) — a communication/safety risk.
- **Focal neurology (Domain 6):** sudden focal symptoms in an ill/bedbound patient are more likely true stroke than a "mimic" (ElHusseini2013); TIA carries high early-stroke risk — the safe default is urgent assessment (Uehara2014; Vigen2018).

## Bottom line for Phase 2
- **The consolidated adult medical-emergency protocol is a genuine gap** — it must be assembled from ME/CFS anchors (Timbol2019 ED; differential-dx; corpus POTS/orthostatic) plus transferred general-medicine safety-netting (refeeding, VTE, pressure/aspiration, focal-neuro, TIA).
- **Population-relevance discounting is mandatory:** most safety evidence is transferable (0.75–0.80 weight), not ME/CFS-specific (1.00).
- **Direction:** INTEGRATE, as a safety-netting/red-flag protocol built on transferred evidence, with explicit uncertainty flags on the ME/CFS-specific gaps (POTS cardiac-emergency threshold, severe-ME mortality, ED misdiagnosis rates).
