# Literature Summary: Management of Severe and Very Severe ME/CFS (S4ME 2026 Factsheet)

**Topic:** `severe-mecfs-management`
**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Type:** Clinical-management factsheet. Source: Science for ME, "Management of severe and very severe ME/CFS" (Prof. Jonathan Edwards + S4ME members, 2026). PDF: `Literature/severe-care/S4ME2026ManagementSevereVerySevereMECFS.pdf`.

## Bottom Line

**This is a clinical-management/advocacy factsheet, not a primary-evidence paper.** Its bibliography cites 5 papers that were **not** in the corpus and are now verified and added (7 bib entries total, including the factsheet itself as `@misc`). The factsheet's core claims — (1) severity grading tied to care needs with ~1/1000 housebound, (2) environmental-stimuli reduction as a medical necessity to prevent deterioration, (3) single-point-of-contact care over multidisciplinary teams, (4) rehabilitation-based approaches having "no place in management," (5) critical appraisal of off-label drugs (antihistamines, naltrexone, fludrocortisone, ivabradine), and (6) enteral/parenteral feeding for severe nutritional failure with MUST screening — are each supported by a mix of **newly-added** and **already-present** corpus evidence. **The mandatory harm/safety searches returned no genuinely-new ME/CFS-relevant papers**; all relevant safety evidence was already in the corpus, so no harm papers were added (anti-padding). Overall certainty is **moderate** (0.60–0.80); the factsheet's prescriptive guidance rests on advocacy/consensus and indirect harm evidence rather than severe-ME/CFS-specific RCTs, which do not exist.

## Evidence for the Factsheet's Core Claims

- **Severity grading tied to care needs (~1/1000 housebound):** supported by existing corpus (Nacul 2011/2020/2021, DecodeME 2025, Montoya 2021). Moderate certainty; prevalence estimate from advocacy/consensus sources rather than a dedicated severe-prevalence study.
- **Environmental-stimuli reduction as medical necessity:** supported indirectly by corpus severe-care literature (Montoya 2021); consensus/clinical guidance, not primary evidence.
- **Single-point-of-contact (specialist nurse) over multidisciplinary teams:** consensus/advocacy position; indirect support from nurse-delivery data in FINE (Wearden 2006/2010) showing the delivery model, and from McPhee 2019 on harm-monitoring gaps in multidisciplinary centres.
- **Rehabilitation-based approaches "have no place in management":** strongly supported by Gaunt 2024 (GET null), Wearden 2010 (no sustained benefit), and Wilshire 2018 (reanalysis of PACE). This is the **best-evidenced** claim (cert ~0.75–0.80).
- **Off-label drugs (antihistamines, naltrexone, fludrocortisone, ivabradine) little/no benefit, more harm:** supported by existing corpus harm entries (Rowe2001FludrocortisoneNMH, Marchetta2025ivabradine, Bolton2019NaltrexoneSAE, mast-cell antihistamine set, NaculLDN2025) — **no new harm papers justified**. Moderate-to-low certainty; reflects absence of severe-ME/CFS-specific RCT safety data.
- **Enteral/parenteral feeding for severe nutritional failure; MUST screening; not delayed on "functional" grounds:** supported by Baxter2021malnutrition (very severe ME/CFS malnutrition) + Page2019 (enteral safety). Moderate certainty; nutrition-harm anchor is Baxter2021.

## Null / Contradicting / Competing Evidence

- **Null / competing — rehabilitation:** Gaunt 2024 and Wearden 2010 are the key nulls (GET/rehab no sustained benefit) — these *support* the factsheet's rejection of rehab, and *contradict* any claim that rehab is effective.
- **Harm — off-label drugs:** no new ME/CFS-specific safety RCTs; harm rests on indirect/general evidence already in corpus. No paper contradicts the factsheet's "little/no benefit, likely more harm" appraisal.
- **Harm — feeding:** Page 2019 + Baxter 2021 document real risks (aspiration, infection, refeeding) — these argue for *safe, planned* feeding, not delay.
- **No genuinely-new harm paper was found** in either mandated harm search; adding padding papers would misrepresent evidence quality.

## Implication for Phase 2 (Integrate / Reject)

**INTEGRATE.** The factsheet fills a real gap in practical severe/very-severe clinical management (aids & ADL, environmental-stimuli reduction as medical necessity, single-point-of-contact care, enteral/parenteral support, hospital admission adjustments, off-label drug appraisal). The claims are all at least indirectly supported by corpus evidence, and the rehab critique is directly supported by newly-added RCT evidence. Disposition: **partial-integrate** — present severity-grading, environmental-stimuli reduction, feeding, and hospital-admission adjustments as consensus/clinical guidance (moderate certainty, flag advocacy basis); present the rehab rejection and off-label drug appraisal with the direct null/harm evidence; flag the absence of severe-ME/CFS-specific safety RCTs for the off-label drugs.

## Research Gaps

- No severe/very-severe-ME/CFS-specific RCT of any off-label drug (antihistamines, naltrexone, fludrocortisone, ivabradine).
- No ME/CFS-specific prospective enteral/parenteral feeding safety study (relies on Baxter 2021 + Page 2019).
- No dedicated study of severe/very-severe prevalence beyond advocacy/consensus estimates.
- No RCT of single-point-of-contact vs multidisciplinary care in severe ME/CFS.
