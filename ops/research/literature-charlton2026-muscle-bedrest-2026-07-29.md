# Literature Summary: charlton2026-muscle-bedrest
**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Agent:** literature-integrator (Phase 1)

## Primary Paper

### Charlton BT et al. (2026) — Skeletal Muscle Properties in Long COVID and ME/CFS Differ from Those Induced by Bed Rest
- **Journal:** Nature Communications (in press)
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41467-026-75725-y
- **Design:** Cross-sectional comparison: 60d strict head-down tilt bed rest (n=24) vs long COVID (n~25), ME/CFS (n~26, pre-2020 dx), age/sex-matched healthy controls (n=30)
- **Key findings:**
  - VO2max similarly reduced in both patient groups and post-bed rest
  - Bed rest: muscle atrophy without fiber type change
  - Both patient groups: more glycolytic (type IIa/IIx) fibers, lower type I
  - ME/CFS: type I-specific atrophy (not seen in bed rest or LC)
  - OXPHOS capacity correlated with VO2max in pre/post bed rest AND healthy controls -- this correlation ABSENT in both patient groups
  - Bed rest increased capillary density (atrophy > capillary loss)
  - ME/CFS: lower capillary-to-fiber ratios and capillary density
  - Both patient groups: lower capillary supply for given fibre size (downshifted C:F~FCSA relationship)
  - Intrinsic mitochondrial dysfunction in patients (lower E/L coupling efficiency, lower normalized OXPHOS/SDH)
  - Ventilatory responses differed: bed rest altered VE/VCO2 slope + VEmax; patients showed higher HR-VO2 slope (suggesting impaired O2 extraction or stroke volume)
- **Conclusion:** Physical inactivity/deconditioning cannot solely explain skeletal muscle alterations in long COVID and ME/CFS. Patients show disease-intrinsic mitochondrial dysfunction, fibre type shift, and capillarization deficits.
- **Limitations:** Cross-sectional in patients; self-selection bias (milder patients); bed rest cohort younger; head-down tilt ≠ ambulatory deconditioning; no longitudinal biopsies in patients; cannot distinguish disease duration from disease pathophysiology.

## Tier 1 Supporting Papers (Same Wüst Lab / AGBRESA Bed Rest Cohort)

### Hendrickse PW et al. (2022) — Capillary Rarefaction During Bed Rest
- **Journal:** J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle
- **Design:** AGBRESA 55d bed rest; n=19; vastus lateralis + soleus biopsies at baseline, 6d, 55d
- **Key findings:** Rapid early fibre atrophy (-23% at 6d) with proportional capillary loss; by 55d, capillary supply in relative excess of oxidative capacity (+42%). Artificial gravity failed to prevent changes.
- **Relevance:** Establishes the bed rest comparator's capillarization phenotype -- bed rest uncouples capillary supply from oxidative capacity, opposite to what patients show. Foundational to the Charlton 2026 design.

### Eggelbusch M et al. (2024) — Impact of Bed Rest on Skeletal Muscle Metabolism
- **Journal:** Cell Reports Medicine
- **Design:** Same AGBRESA 60d bed rest cohort; multi-omics integrative analysis
- **Key findings:** Intracellular glycogen accumulation at 6d with GLUT4 downregulation; by 55d: intramyocellular lipid + ceramide accumulation, fragmented mitochondria, reduced OXPHOS. Temporal dissociation: carbohydrate defect early, lipid defect late.
- **Relevance:** Establishes the metabolic timeline of bed rest; provides the intracellular nutrient overload mechanism that differentiates bed rest from disease.

### Joseph P et al. (2021) — Invasive CPET in ME/CFS
- **Journal:** Chest
- **Design:** Invasive CPET (radial + PA catheters) in n=20 ME/CFS (Fukuda) vs n=10 controls
- **Key findings:** Marked reduction in peak systemic O2 extraction despite normal cardiac output; preload failure (low biventricular filling pressures); normal pulmonary gas exchange.
- **Relevance:** Direct evidence that exercise limitation in ME/CFS is peripheral (O2 extraction, preload), not central. Cited as ref 40 in Charlton 2026.

## Tier 2 Supporting Papers

### Singh I et al. (2022) — Invasive CPET After COVID-19
- **Journal:** Chest
- **Design:** Invasive CPET in n=10 post-COVID patients with unexplained dyspnea
- **Key findings:** Peak VO2 71% predicted; reduced peak systemic O2 extraction despite normal cardiac output. Preload failure in subset.
- **Relevance:** Replicates the impaired peripheral O2 extraction finding in post-COVID. Cited as ref 41 in Charlton 2026.

### Germain A et al. (2025) — Plasma Proteomics of PEM in ME/CFS
- **Journal:** Mol Cell Proteomics
- **Design:** SomaScan 7K longitudinal plasma proteomics: n=79 ME/CFS vs n=53 age/BMI-matched sedentary controls over 2-day CPET
- **Key findings:** ME/CFS showed persistent immune/metabolic/neuromuscular dysregulation in recovery (PEM); upregulated glycolysis/gluconeogenesis suggestive of mitochondrial stress; disrupted proteomic-VO2max associations. Sex-stratified: distinct female vs male responses.
- **Relevance:** Corroborates Charlton 2026 thesis: PEM proteomic signature ≠ deconditioning. Demonstrates mitochondrial stress markers at proteomic level.

### Toriola MA et al. (2026) — ATG13 Depletion and Muscle Autophagy
- **Journal:** Inflamm Res
- **Design:** Murine model: ATG13 hemizygous deletion
- **Key findings:** ATG13 deficit impairs autophagy, disrupts mitochondrial OXPHOS, increases ROS, polarizes M1 macrophages. M1 infiltration into muscle vasculature, myelin compromise, reduced muscle strength post treadmill.
- **Relevance:** Provides a candidate upstream mechanism (autophagy failure) for the mitochondrial dysfunction and PEM Charlton describes. First evidence that impaired muscle autophagy → PEM-like exercise intolerance.

## Tier 3 Foundational

### Behan WMH et al. (1991) — Mitochondrial Abnormalities in Postviral Fatigue
- **Journal:** Acta Neuropathol
- **Design:** EM of vastus lateralis biopsies, n=50 PVFS
- **Key findings:** 80% had abnormal mitochondria (branching, elongation, cristae compartmentalization, paracrystalline inclusions). Type II fibre atrophy.
- **Relevance:** Seminal structural evidence that mitochondrial pathology in post-viral fatigue is disease-intrinsic. Cited as ref 8 in Charlton 2026. Low certainty due to era and diagnostic criteria, but historically foundational.
