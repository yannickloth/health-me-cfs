## Inclusion Criteria

- Peer-reviewed journal article (Cochrane Database Syst Rev, BMJ, J Gen Intern Med, Lancet Infect Dis, Am J Hematol, BMC Med, Muscle Nerve).
- Directly addresses quinine (or its derivative class) for muscle/leg cramps — efficacy, meta-analysis, or systematic review.
- OR harm/safety of quinine relevant to off-label cramps use (thrombocytopenia, thrombotic microangiopathy, cardiotoxicity/QT, hemolysis in G6PD deficiency, cinchonism).
- OR a competing/alternative treatment for cramps that ME/CFS patients would encounter (magnesium), or the drug-class mechanism context (sodium-channel blockers for myotonia/cramps).
- OR indirect-biochemical: skeletal muscle sodium-channel (Nav1.4/SCN4A) dysfunction causing painful cramps/myotonia — maps to ME/CFS muscle-ion-channel hypothesis (ch30 sec-01).
- Published 1995–2025 (efficacy core 1995–2010; harm 2007–2018; competing 2020–2025).
- Metadata (PMID, DOI, author, volume/page) verified via esummary/efetch.

## Exclusion Criteria

- Quinine for malaria as sole context (efficacy focus is off-label cramps use, not antimalarial dosing).
- Case reports of quinine toxicity where a systematic review already captures the class signal (Liles2016QuinineAdverse, Haeusler2018QuinolineCardiotoxicity supersede individual case reports).
- Target-organ Kv7/ion-channel papers with no cramp/myotonia relevance (taste physiology, blood-vessel histamine Kv7).
- Duplicate PMIDs across queries.
- Non-quinine/cramp carbamazepine indications (vestibular paroxysmia, trigeminal neuralgia).
- 1974 experimental combination (quinine + lithium + salicylic acid) fibromyalgia paper — outdated, non-clinical.

**Safety-gate summary for Phase 3b:** Quinine carries FDA boxed warning against off-label leg-cramp use (2006; restricted 2010). Serious risks: immune thrombocytopenia, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura/hemolytic-uremic syndrome, QT prolongation/TdP (esp. with concurrent QT-prolonging drugs), hemolysis in G6PD deficiency, cinchonism. Contraindications: G6PD deficiency, pregnancy (quinine abortifacient/teratogenic), prolonged QT, hypersensitivity. Bedbound/severe patients: hypotension/orthostatic caution (cross-ref ch14a).

**Reasoning for indirect link:** Quinine's anti-cramp efficacy is attributed to its membrane-stabilizing action on skeletal-muscle sodium channels (Nav1.4) and potassium channels (Kv7/KCNQ), prolonging the muscle-fiber refractory period and reducing abnormal hyperexcitability. Torbergsen2015 demonstrates that pathological gain-of-function/alteration in Nav1.4 (SCN4A mutation) itself produces painful cramps + myotonic discharges. This intersects the ME/CFS muscle-ion-channel / neuromuscular-excitability hypothesis (ch30 sec-01 ion-channel domain) and cramp/PEM overlap. No direct ME/CFS quinine study exists — the link is biochemical/mechanistic only (low-certainty indirect).

## Key Negative / Research-Gap Findings

- **Zero direct ME/CFS (or CFS) studies of quinine** (M1/M2 = 0 hits). Quinine has never been studied in ME/CFS for cramps.
- **Marginal/unreliable efficacy:** ElTawil2010CochraneCramps (23 trials, 1586 patients) found limited evidence of benefit; risk-benefit unfavorable. ManSonHing1998 (with unpublished data) reduced the effect estimate from 8.83 to 3.60 fewer cramps/4-weeks — publication-bias-corrected benefit is small.
- **FDA boxed warning** against off-label leg-cramp use (2006/2010). Harm is the dominant signal.
- **Magnesium (the common alternative) also lacks proof** — Garrison2020CochraneMagnesium found no consistent benefit.
- **Non-drug cramp therapies** (Hawke2021Cochrane) — limited quality evidence, so no clear effective non-drug replacement either.
- **Indirect ion-channel → cramp link exists** (Torbergsen2015) but no direct channel-dysfunction ↔ ME/CFS-cramp study.
