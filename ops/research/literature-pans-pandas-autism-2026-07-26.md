## Executive Summary

PANS/PANDAS is an **existence proof** for the paper's Architecture B (infection → autoantibody → neuropsychiatric phenotype → reversible with immunomodulation): abrupt-onset OCD/tic disorders causally linked to group A streptococcal (GAS) infection, mediated by anti-basal ganglia autoantibodies that cross-react via molecular mimicry and activate CaM kinase II in dopaminergic neurons. The PANDAS mechanism (GAS → neuronal autoantibodies → CaMKII activation → neuropsychiatric phenotype) has been demonstrated at molecular level (Kirvan 2003, *Nature Medicine*). The treatment response — some PANDAS patients improve dramatically with IVIG or plasmapheresis — is direct evidence for acquired, immune-mediated neuropsychiatric disease.

PANS broadens PANDAS beyond streptococcus, covering any abrupt-onset neuropsychiatric deterioration with an infectious/inflammatory trigger. The Stanford PANS clinic experience (Frankovich 2015, Masterson 2025) describes a recognizable syndrome with clinical heterogeneity that mirrors ME/CFS in its diagnostic challenges.

**Key finding for the paper:** The controversy surrounding PANDAS — whether it "really exists" as a distinct disease — is structurally identical to the historical debate about ME/CFS as a biological vs psychosomatic condition. In both cases, syndromic labels obscure mechanism-based subgroups. PANDAS/PANS patients who respond to immunomodulation are an immune-mediated subgroup that syndromic classification cannot distinguish. This is the paper's Architecture B argument in microcosm.

The autism convergence is less direct but still significant: ~20% of mothers who have a child with ASD carry anti-fetal brain autoantibodies (Meltzer 2017, Braunschweig 2013), defining a prenatally-acquired immune-mechanism ASD subgroup. Some children with "autism" have treatable autoimmune encephalitis (Whiteley 2021). A subset of ASD children with autoimmune features respond to IVIG (Connery 2018), while unselected ASD cohorts do not (Plioplys 1998) — reinforcing the stratification imperative.

## Key Direct Evidence Highlights

1. **Molecular mimicry mechanism is established** — Kirvan 2003 (Nat Med): GAS N-acetyl-beta-D-glucosamine cross-reactive with neuronal lysoganglioside. Antibodies activate CaMKII → dopaminergic neuron dysregulation. This is a complete mechanistic chain (trigger → antibody → target → pathway → phenotype).

2. **Immunotherapy responsiveness** — Latimer 2015: 89% of 35 severe PANDAS improved with TPA. Kovacevic 2015: 83% of 12 improved with IVIG. But Williams 2016 RCT: IVIG not significantly superior to placebo overall (subgroup analysis showed GAS+ benefit). Together: some patients clearly respond, not all — exactly the pattern that demands stratification.

3. **PANS broader than PANDAS** — PANS covers ANY infection/inflammatory trigger, not just GAS. This mirrors ME/CFS heterogeneity (multiple triggers → similar phenotype).

## Key Convergence/Overlap Evidence

4. **Maternal autoantibodies define an ASD subgroup** — Braunschweig 2013: ~23% of ASD attributable to maternal anti-fetal brain antibodies targeting 7 specific neuronal proteins. This is prenatal Architecture B (immune mechanism → neurodevelopment → behavioral phenotype).

5. **IVIG in ASD** — Connery 2018: 62% of screened ASD patients with autoimmune features responded to IVIG. But Plioplys 1998: *null* result in unselected ASD. The difference demonstrates the stratification imperative: treat the mechanism, not the syndrome.

6. **"Regressive ASD" and immune features** — Aslan 2021 found no anti-neuronal antibody difference between regressive and classic ASD, but ~20% had elevated antibodies regardless — an immune subgroup may not align neatly with regression.

## Key Null/Negative Evidence

7. **Plioplys 1998** — IVIG in unselected ASD: no benefit. Reinforces that immune treatments only work in immune-mediated subgroups.
8. **Williams 2016 RCT** — IVIG not significantly superior to placebo in PANDAS. Underpowered (n=35). GAS+ subgroup better.
9. **Sigra 2018 systematic review** — Overall evidence quality for PANDAS/PANS treatments rated low.

## Key Indirect Biochemical Links

10. **CaMKII pathway** — Activated by PANDAS autoantibodies; overlaps with ME/CFS calcium signaling dysfunction literature. The same kinase implicated in cardiac failure, memory, and fatigue pathways.

11. **Dopamine receptor autoantibodies** — Found in PANDAS (D1/D2). GPCR autoantibodies (including dopamine receptors) increasingly documented in ME/CFS (see @Sotzny2021).

## Evidence Gaps

- **No direct PANS/PANDAS-ME/CFS overlap studies** found
- **No adult-onset PANS equivalents** described as distinct entity
- **No head-to-head cytokine profiling across PANS vs ASD vs ME/CFS**
- **No basal ganglia imaging in ME/CFS** with autoimmune focus
- **No CaMKII studies in ME/CFS specifically**
- **No studies examining whether PANDAS/PANS resolves into an ME/CFS-like state**

## Integration Assessment for ME/CFS Paper

**Signal strength: Moderate-High (topical). Subject weight: Medium (other disease model).**

PANS/PANDAS provides the cleanest existing example of Architecture B neuropsychiatric disease: a well-characterized molecular mimicry pathway with known autoantibody targets and documented immunotherapy responsiveness. The controversy literature is structurally identical to ME/CFS history — biology hard to see through syndromic labeling. The autism convergence adds depth but is one step further removed (prenatal vs postnatal).
