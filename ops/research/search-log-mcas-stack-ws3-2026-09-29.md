## Inclusion Criteria
- Human, animal, or in vitro studies directly addressing one of E1–F2.
- Chronopharmacology/circadian studies of histamine, leukotrienes, or H1/H2 antihistamines (E1).
- Pyridostigmine dose-response, side-effect threshold, or POTS/ME/CFS use (E2).
- H1/H2 receptor upregulation, antihistamine tolerance, or withdrawal rebound (E3).
- Estrogen/sex-hormone effects on mast-cell degranulation or allergy (E4).
- Acid-suppression–microbiome interaction and bacterial histamine production (F1).
- H2-receptor negative feedback on mediator release (F2).

## Exclusion Criteria
- Non-histamine/mast-cell drug chronobiology (unrelated drugs).
- Myasthenia-gravis-specific pyridostigmine dosing (different disease mechanism).
- Antihistamine efficacy trials without tolerance/rebound/timing angle.
- Histamine in seafood/food safety (scombroid) — off-target for F1 gut-microbiome chain.
- Papers superseded by a later version of the same cohort.

## Honest nulls / gaps (see literature summary for detail)
- **E1:** No head-to-head morning-vs-evening montelukast efficacy trial found; PM dosing is a label recommendation rationalised by nocturnal CysLT peak (Kurokawa 2001, Tanaka 2003).
- **E4:** No direct clinical study of cycle-synchronised MCAS dosing; no MCAS-menstrual-flare trial.
- **F1:** No famotidine-specific microbiome→histamine→harm study; chain is PPI-microbiome + bacterial-histamine, applied to H2RAs by analogy (Vich Vila 2020 is the only paper explicitly noting an H2RA effect).
- **F2:** No human/clinical demonstration of compensatory mediator synthesis; all support is classic in vitro work.
