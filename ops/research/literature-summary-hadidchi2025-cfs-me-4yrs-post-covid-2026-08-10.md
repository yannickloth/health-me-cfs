## Key Themes

### 1. Post-COVID ME/CFS Risk is Robust
The primary finding (Hadidchi 2025: HR 1.46–1.56, n=147,377) is replicated by three independent studies using three different methods:
- **RECOVER 2025** (n=11,785, prospective, IOM criteria): 2.8-fold increased risk
- **Unger 2024/INSPIRE** (n=4,376, prospective, IOM criteria): CDC-led replication
- **Tack 2026** (n=221, CCC clinical assessment): 3.2% lower-bound incidence

No null/negative studies found challenging this association.

### 2. Risk Horizon is Sustained (4+ Years)
Hadidchi 2025 is the first large-cohort study to demonstrate ME/CFS risk persisting up to 4 years post-infection. This is unprecedented in the post-infectious ME/CFS literature — previous studies (Hickie 2006) assessed risk at 6–12 months. The 4-year horizon challenges the assumption that post-infectious risk declines to baseline after the first year.

### 3. Historical Precedent Exists
- **Moldofsky 2011**: SARS-CoV-1 (2003 Toronto outbreak) caused chronic post-infectious fatigue/pain syndrome lasting 1–3 years. This directly foreshadows SARS-CoV-2 findings.
- **Hickie 2006**: Dubbo cohort — 11% post-infectious CFS after EBV, Ross River virus, Q fever at 6 months.
- **Poomkudy 2024**: EBV remains a key post-infectious ME/CFS trigger, with joint hypermobility as risk modifier.

### 4. Acute Biomarkers Do Not Predict ME/CFS
A key null finding from Hadidchi 2025: none of the acute-phase blood biomarkers (ferritin, D-dimer, LDH, CRP) predicted who would develop CFS/ME. This is methodologically important — it distinguishes acute-phase inflammation markers from potentially predictive post-acute markers (e.g., ferritin at 6–12 months per Yamamoto2023/Kedor2022).

### 5. Risk Modifiers Identified
- Female sex, pre-existing autoimmune disease, liver disease, and anxiety disorders increase risk (Hadidchi 2025)
- Joint hypermobility may predispose (Poomkudy 2024)
- GPCR autoantibodies and EBV reactivation are common in post-COVID ME/CFS (Tack 2026: 66.6% and 86.7% respectively)
- Re-infection not additive — risk is concentrated in first infection

## Null/Negative/Skeptical Findings
- **No studies** found reporting zero or non-significant ME/CFS risk after COVID-19
- **No studies** found challenging the post-infectious ME/CFS etiology model
- **Hadidchi null**: acute ferritin not predictive (not a challenge to the risk association, but to the biomarker prediction model)
- **Yang 2026 (Rev Med Virol)**: commentary on post-vaccination syndromes, not original ME/CFS data — excluded

## Replication Status of Hadidchi 2025
| Replication Type | Study | Direction | Certainty |
|-----------------|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Direct (large EHR cohort) | None yet | — | — |
| Direct (prospective, IOM) | RECOVER 2025 | Consistent | 0.78 |
| Direct (prospective, IOM) | Unger 2024 | Consistent | 0.78 |
| Indirect (fatigue, EHR) | Vu 2024 | Consistent | 0.58 |
| Indirect (cluster analysis) | Cornelissen 2024 | Consistent | 0.65 |
| Partial (prospective, CCC) | Tack 2026 | Consistent | 0.55 |

**Replication assessment:** Partially replicated. The post-COVID ME/CFS risk direction is consistently supported by 3 large independent cohorts (RECOVER, INSPIRE, Vu). No direct replication of the 4-year sustained risk exists yet — other studies have shorter follow-up (6 months–2 years). The 4-year horizon is currently unique to Hadidchi 2025 and requires future replication.

## Gaps Identified
1. **No direct replication of 4-year risk horizon**: All comparator studies have shorter follow-up
2. **No studies with pre-COVID ME/CFS baseline**: All post-COVID ME/CFS incidence estimates assume pre-infection absence
3. **EHR diagnosis underascertainment**: ICD codes are known to underestimate true ME/CFS prevalence
4. **Vaccination-era confounding**: Risk estimates for unvaccinated vs. vaccinated populations differ by era
5. **No metabolic/biomarker trajectory studies**: Timing of biomarker measurement (acute vs. post-acute vs. chronic) matters critically for prediction
