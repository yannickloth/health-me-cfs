# Literature Summary: Prolactin Response to Buspirone Challenge in ME/CFS

**Topic slug:** prolactin-buspirone
**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Bib file:** `src/main/typst/mecfs/bib/endocrine-reproductive.bib`
**Papers included:** 14

## Executive Summary

The direct literature on buspirone-stimulated prolactin in ME/CFS is small but shows a consistent, replicated finding from 1992–2001: ME/CFS/CFS/PVFS patients mount a **higher prolactin response to buspirone** than healthy controls, despite normal baseline prolactin (Bakheit 1992; Sharpe 1996). A **depression comparator** shows the opposite direction — depressed patients have the *lowest* prolactin responses, so the challenge probe discriminates ME/CFS from depression (Cleare 1995, d-fenfluramine). The buspirone signal is probe-specific: a purely serotonergic releaser (fenfluramine) gave a **null result** in CFS (Yatham 1995) and an attenuated response in another cohort (Bearn 1995), consistent with the mechanistic interpretation that the exaggerated prolactin rise reflects **altered dopamine-D2 (dopaminergic brake) tone** rather than simple 5-HT1A hypersensitivity. The dopamine–prolactin brake and estradiol regulation of prolactin are both canonical, well-replicated physiology (Ben-Jonathan 2001; Fitzgerald 2008; Qi-Lytle 2023), providing mechanistic plausibility for both candidate mechanisms (a) altered dopamine system and (b) estradiol-mediated regulation. No modern (post-2010) replication study exists — the line was abandoned, confirming the topic's premise and the rationale for a proposed replication study.

**Certainty note:** The direct ME/CFS studies are high *relevance* (ME/CFS cohort, weight 1.00) but small (n=11–20) and single-center, yielding discounted certainty in the 0.35–0.62 range. The mechanism reviews are high *quality* (0.80–0.85 raw) but general-population/physiology (weight 0.75), yielding discounted certainty 0.60–0.64. No paper reaches discounted certainty ≥ 0.70, but several reach ≥ 0.60, supporting a PARTIAL-to-PROCEED integration decision pending Phase 2 synthesis.

## Direct buspirone→prolactin ME/CFS studies

### 1. Bakheit1992Buspirone — Original report
- **Citation:** Bakheit AMO, Behan PO, Dinan TG, Gray CE, O'Keane V. Possible upregulation of hypothalamic 5-hydroxytryptamine receptors in patients with postviral fatigue syndrome. BMJ. 1992;304(6833):1010–1012.
- **PMID/DOI:** 1586780 / 10.1136/bmj.304.6833.1010
- **Design:** Small neuroendocrine challenge (oral buspirone → prolactin) in postviral fatigue syndrome (PVFS)
- **Population:** ME/CFS (postviral fatigue) vs controls
- **Diagnostic criteria:** custom (PVFS clinical criteria; pre-Fukuda era)
- **Cohort overlap:** no known biobank; single-center (UK, Dinan/Behan group)
- **Key findings:** Buspirone-stimulated prolactin elevated in PVFS; interpreted as hypothalamic 5-HT receptor upregulation
- **Null findings:** none reported
- **Limitations:** small n; no depression control; buspirone also blocks D2 (5-HT interpretation ambiguous)
- **Certainty:** raw 0.55 × ME/CFS 1.00 = **0.55**

### 2. Sharpe1996Buspirone — Key replication
- **Citation:** Sharpe M, Clements A, Hawton K, Young AH, Sargent P, Cowen PJ. Increased prolactin response to buspirone in chronic fatigue syndrome. J Affect Disord. 1996;41(1):71–76.
- **PMID/DOI:** 8938208 / 10.1016/0165-0327(96)00075-4
- **Design:** Challenge study; 11 male CFS vs matched healthy controls; buspirone 0.5 mg/kg PO
- **Population:** CFS (Fukuda) vs healthy
- **Diagnostic criteria:** Fukuda
- **Cohort overlap:** independent of Bakheit; Cowen group (Oxford)
- **Key findings:** Significantly higher plasma prolactin and more nausea after buspirone in CFS; GH response did not distinguish groups
- **Null findings:** GH response null (argues against pure postsynaptic 5-HT1A hypersensitivity)
- **Limitations:** small, male-only; no depression group; single-center
- **Certainty:** raw 0.62 × ME/CFS 1.00 = **0.62**

### 3. Sharma2001Buspirone — Recovery signal
- **Citation:** Sharma A, Oyebode F, Kendall MJ, Jones PW. Recovery from chronic fatigue syndrome associated with changes in neuroendocrine function. J R Soc Med. 2001;94(1):26–27.
- **PMID/DOI:** 11220065 / 10.1177/014107680109400107
- **Design:** Brief case/small-series; buspirone challenge across recovery
- **Population:** CFS (Fukuda)
- **Diagnostic criteria:** Fukuda
- **Cohort overlap:** unknown; independent group
- **Key findings:** Neuroendocrine change (buspirone/prolactin) associated with clinical recovery — state-dependence
- **Null findings:** n/a
- **Limitations:** very small; brief-report format
- **Certainty:** raw 0.40 × ME/CFS 1.00 = **0.40**

### 4. Dinan2001Buspirone — Cortisol modulation (mechanism, general pop)
- **Citation:** Dinan TG, Scott LV, Thakore J, Naesdal J, Keeling PW. Impact of cortisol on buspirone stimulated prolactin release. Psychoneuroendocrinology. 2001;26(7):751–756.
- **PMID/DOI:** 11500255 / 10.1016/S0306-4530(01)00029-4
- **Design:** Double-blind placebo-controlled; healthy volunteers (n=50)
- **Population:** Healthy volunteers
- **Diagnostic criteria:** N/A (healthy volunteers)
- **Cohort overlap:** Dinan group (same PI as Bakheit) — note: PI overlap with primary report
- **Key findings:** Buspirone consistently raises prolactin; prolactin AUC correlates highly with baseline cortisol
- **Null findings:** n/a
- **Limitations:** general population; no ME/CFS
- **Certainty:** raw 0.65 × general 0.75 = **0.49**

## Indirect biochemical / systemic link analysis (MANDATORY)

**Logic:** Buspirone is a 5-HT1A agonist that also **blocks dopamine D2**. Dopamine is the **main brake on prolactin** (tonic inhibition via lactotroph D2 receptors). Estradiol **regulates prolactin** (modulates both dopamine delivery and lactotroph function). Therefore the topic's biochemistry intersects documented ME/CFS pathophysiology through the dopaminergic system and through sex-hormone regulation — even though prolactin itself is often not the named variable.

### Dopamine–prolactin brake (mechanism candidate a)
- **BenJonathan2001ProlactinDopamine** (Endocr Rev 2001;22:724–763; PMID 11739329; DOI 10.1210/edrv.22.6.0451). Canonical review: dopamine via portal blood reaches pituitary, binds lactotroph **D2 receptors** to inhibit prolactin; estrogens regulate both dopamine delivery and prolactin synthesis. Raw 0.85 × general 0.75 = **0.64**.
- **Fitzgerald2008ProlactinDopamine** (J Psychopharmacol 2008;22(2 Suppl):12–19; PMID 18477617; DOI 10.1177/0269216307087148). Dopamine holds lactotrophs at low basal tone via D2; PRL feedback short-loop. Raw 0.80 × general 0.75 = **0.60**.
- **QiLytle2023TIDA** (Int J Mol Sci 2023;25(1):110; PMID 38203281; DOI 10.3390/ijms25010110). Modern TIDA review: TIDA → median eminence → portal D2 → prolactin inhibition; activity modulated by **estradiol**, PRL feedback, metabolic signals. Raw 0.80 × general 0.75 = **0.60**.

**Interpretation for candidate (a):** The exaggerated prolactin response to buspirone in ME/CFS is mechanistically consistent with **reduced dopaminergic inhibitory tone** (hypothalamic dopamine → D2 brake), because buspirone's D2 blockade would then produce a larger prolactin release. This ties directly to documented ME/CFS dopaminergic findings (basal-ganglia dopamine reward-processing deficit; dopamine-reuptake-modulator trials). The null fenfluramine results (Yatham 1995; Bearn 1995) support this: a pure 5-HT releaser does not reproduce the signal, localizing the abnormality to the D2/dopamine pathway rather than global 5-HT.

### Estradiol regulation of prolactin (mechanism candidate b)
- Estradiol modulates both the dopamine brake and lactotroph prolactin synthesis (Ben-Jonathan 2001; Qi-Lytle 2023). Estradiol regulation of prolactin is canonical physiology. This provides a mechanistic route by which **~80% female predominance** in ME/CFS could arise via sex-hormone-modulated prolactin/prolactin-adjacent signaling, but **no direct ME/CFS prolactin-by-sex study** was found — this remains a hypothesis requiring direct testing (a gap, not evidence).

### Dopamine in ME/CFS (indirect systemic link)
- **Haghighi2021OSU6162** (Brain Behav 2021;11(4):e02040; PMID 33528911; DOI 10.1002/brb3.2040). Open-label trial of the monoamine (dopamine) stabilizer (-)-OSU6162 in ME/CFS; tests dopaminergic-dysfunction hypothesis. Raw 0.45 × ME/CFS 1.00 = **0.45**. Supports the general premise of dopaminergic dysregulation in ME/CFS.

**Indirect-link verdict:** The indirect evidence (3 canonical dopamine-prolactin mechanism reviews + 1 ME/CFS dopamine-modulation trial) is strong enough (discounted 0.60–0.64 on the mechanism reviews) to support a testable mechanistic model, even though the direct challenge-prolactin ME/CFS literature is small.

## Contradiction / consistency analysis
- **Yatham 1995 (null, fenfluramine)** vs **Sharpe 1996 / Bakheit 1992 (positive, buspirone)** — **resolvable**: different probes. Fenfluramine is a 5-HT releaser; buspirone also blocks D2. The probe-specificity supports the D2/dopamine mechanism rather than a fundamental contradiction.
- **Bearn 1995 (attenuated hypoglycemia PRL; no fenfluramine PRL enhancement)** — consistent with probe-dependent prolactin regulation; not a contradiction of the buspirone finding.
- **Cleare 1995 (CFS prolactin HIGHEST, depression LOWEST)** — the depression-vs-ME/CFS discriminator; central to the topic's value as a diagnostic/biomarker probe.
- **No fundamental contradiction** among included papers; the different probe results are mechanistically interpretable.

## Missing / gap evidence
- **Zero modern (post-2010) replication** of the buspirone→prolactin challenge in ME/CFS. This is the central research gap motivating the proposed replication study.
- **No ME/CFS prolactin-by-sex (estradiol-stratified) analysis** found — candidate (b) remains hypothesis-level.
- **No depression-control arm in the buspirone studies** (Sharpe 1996 had healthy controls only); only the d-fenfluramine study (Cleare 1995) included a depression arm. A replication study should include both ME/CFS and depression groups with buspirone.
- Google Scholar and preprint servers not searched (environment limitation) — noted in search log.

## Cohort-overlap notes
- Dinan is a shared author on Bakheit1992Buspirone and Dinan2001Buspirone (same PI group) — mechanism study confirms the probe's cortisol-dependence but is not an independent replication.
- Sharpe1996Buspirone (Cowen, Oxford) is independent of Bakheit/Dinan — genuine replication.
- Bearn1995, Cleare1995, Cleare1996 share the Wessely/Cleare King's College London group — the depression-vs-CFS contrast and the null fenfluramine finding are from overlapping cohorts.
- Yatham1995 is an independent Canadian group (Dalhousie) — independent null.
- No shared biobank identified across groups beyond the KCL cluster.
