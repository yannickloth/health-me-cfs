# Literature Summary: Menstrual Cycle as Endogenous Pharmacodiagnostic Probe

**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Scope:** Menstrual cycle phase × drug response interactions; oestrogen/progesterone modulation of receptor systems
**Papers found:** 9 (all new to bib)
**New papers added to bib:** 9

---

## Conceptual Framework

The core hypothesis: oestrogen and progesterone fluctuate predictably across the menstrual cycle, differentially modulating receptor expression and function (NMDA, D2, 5-HT1A, GABA-A). By tracking a patient's response to a given drug across follicular vs. luteal phases, one can infer which receptor system the drug acts through — the menstrual cycle serves as an endogenous pharmacodiagnostic probe without requiring additional biomarkers.

**Key receptor-modulation evidence by hormone/receptor:**

| Receptor | Oestrogen Effect | Progesterone/Allo Effect |
|----------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| NMDA | Positive allosteric modulator; enhances LTP (Sellers 2015) | — |
| D2 | ↑ striatal availability in animals; NULL in human PET (Petersen 2021) | — |
| 5-HT1A | ↑ synthesis, release, receptor sensitivity (Shadani 2024); NULL binding change by PET (Jovanovic 2009) | — |
| GABA-A | Minimal direct effect | Potent PAM via allopregnanolone; δ-subunit preference (Locci 2017) |
| CYP450 | Some isoforms modulated (Bosch 2025 review); NULL in rat estrous (Lee 2012) | Negligible |

## Key Null Findings (Essential Context)

Three null findings constrain the strength of the probe hypothesis:

| Study | What was null | Implication |
|-------|---------------|-------------|
| Jovanovic 2009 | 5-HT1A binding unchanged across cycle | Receptor *density/availability* stable; functional sensitivity may still vary |
| Petersen 2021 | D2 binding unchanged across cycle | D2 receptor availability is cycle-stable in striatum |
| Lee 2012 | CYP expression stable across estrous | PK (metabolic) pathway unlikely to confound PD probe inference |

**Interpretation:** Null receptor binding results do NOT falsify the probe hypothesis. Binding potential (BP_ND) measures receptor availability, not functional output. Oestrogen and progesterone may modulate intracellular signalling cascades downstream of receptor binding (G-protein coupling, second messenger efficacy, gene transcription) without changing receptor number. The probe concept depends on functional differences, not density differences.

## What's Missing From the Literature (Identified Gaps)

1. **No human study directly testing cycle-phase-dependent drug response** as a receptor-system probe in any disease
2. **No ME/CFS study with drug administration tracked by menstrual cycle phase**
3. **No PET study measuring functional receptor output** (vs. binding) across menstrual cycle
4. **No clinical trial design** that stratifies drug response by cycle phase at enrollment
5. **Paucity of dense-sampling designs**: most menstrual cycle studies use only 2 timepoints (follicular vs luteal), which may miss the periovulatory estrogen peak

## Overall Assessment

The literature provides **plausible mechanistic support** for the menstrual cycle as endogenous pharmacodiagnostic probe, but **no direct clinical validation**. The strongest evidence is neurobiological (oestrogen modulation of NMDA and serotonin systems; progesterone/allopregnanolone modulation of GABA-A), while the weakest is direct clinical demonstration (no human study has tested the probe concept prospectively). The null findings on receptor *binding* (5-HT1A, D2) do not refute the hypothesis because functional output (signalling efficacy, downstream gene expression) can differ without binding changes. The Thomas 2026 review provides the most directly relevant framework, explicitly calling for menstrual-cycle-aware sampling in ME/CFS research.
