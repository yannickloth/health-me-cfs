## Paper 1 (ANCHOR): Mughal et al. 2025 — Metabolic Adaptation & Fragility in 3D Skeletal Muscle

**Citation:** Mughal S, Andújar-Sánchez F, Sabater-Arcis M, et al. Metabolic adaptation and fragility in healthy 3D in vitro skeletal muscle tissues exposed to chronic fatigue syndrome and Long COVID-19 sera. *Biofabrication*. 2025;17(4). doi:10.1088/1758-5090/adf66c. PMID: 40744071.

**Study Design:** Healthy 3D biofabricated skeletal muscle tissues (human-derived) exposed to sera from ME/CFS (n=?) and Long COVID (n=?) patients for 48h, 96h, and 144h. Transcriptomics, structural analysis, mitochondrial imaging, contractile force measurement.

**Key Findings:**
- Short exposure (48h): Significant reduction in contractile strength; transcriptomic upregulation of protein translation, glycolytic enzymes, calcium homeostasis disruption, hypertrophy, mitochondrial hyperfusion
- Structural confirmation of myotube hypertrophy and elevated mitochondrial oxygen consumption in ME/CFS-exposed tissues
- Prolonged exposure (96–144h): Muscle fragility and weakness, mitochondrial fragmentation into toroidal conformation
- Proposed model: skeletal muscle in ME/CFS/LC progresses through **hypermetabolic state** → severe muscular and mitochondrial deterioration
- First study to demonstrate transient metabolic adaptation in 3D muscle tissue model

**Certainty:** Raw 0.65 × Pop weight 0.40 = **0.26**  
**Bib key:** `Mughal20253dMuscleSera`  
**Bib file:** `musculoskeletal.bib`

## Paper 2: Appelman et al. 2024 — Muscle Abnormalities Worsen After PEM in Long COVID

**Citation:** Appelman B, Charlton BT, Goulding RP, et al. Muscle abnormalities worsen after post-exertional malaise in long COVID. *Nature Communications*. 2024;15(1):17. doi:10.1038/s41467-023-44432-3. PMID: 38177128.

**Study Design:** Longitudinal case-control study (NCT05225688). n=25 Long COVID patients, n=21 healthy controls. Skeletal muscle biopsies before/after maximal exercise test. Metabolomics, histopathology, electron microscopy.

**Key Findings:**
- Skeletal muscle structure associated with lower exercise capacity in LC
- Local and systemic metabolic disturbances in LC muscle
- Severe exercise-induced myopathy after PEM induction
- Tissue infiltration of **amyloid-containing deposits** in skeletal muscle — novel finding
- Muscle fiber type shift toward glycolytic phenotype

**Certainty:** Raw 0.80 × Pop weight 0.85 = **0.68**  
**Bib key:** `Appelman2024MusclePEM`  
**Bib file:** `musculoskeletal.bib`

## Paper 3: Fluge et al. 2016 — Impaired PDH Function in ME/CFS

**Citation:** Fluge Ø, Mella O, Bruland O, et al. Metabolic profiling indicates impaired pyruvate dehydrogenase function in myalgic encephalopathy/chronic fatigue syndrome. *JCI Insight*. 2016;1(21):e89376. doi:10.1172/jci.insight.89376. PMID: 28018972.

**Study Design:** Serum metabolomics n=200 ME/CFS + 102 HC. Myoblasts grown in patient serum. PDH kinase mRNA expression. Amino acid profiling.

**Key Findings:**
- Specific reduction of amino acids fueling TCA cycle (mainly female patients)
- Elevated 3-methylhistidine (endogenous protein catabolism marker) in males
- Functional impairment of PDH — increased PDK1/2/4, SIRT4, PPARδ mRNA
- **Myoblasts grown in severe ME/CFS serum showed:** increased mitochondrial respiration, excessive lactate secretion → metabolic adaptation
- This is the methodological precursor to Mughal 2025 — first serum-transfer-to-muscle-cell experiment in ME/CFS

**Certainty:** Raw 0.65 × Pop weight 0.80 = **0.52**  
**Bib key:** `Fluge2016PDH`  
**Bib file:** `musculoskeletal.bib` and `energy-metabolism.bib`

## Paper 4: Charlton et al. 2025 — Skeletal Muscle Adaptations & PEM (Review)

**Citation:** Charlton BT, Goulding RP, Jaspers RT, Appelman B, van Vugt M, Wüst RCI. Skeletal muscle adaptations and post-exertional malaise in long COVID. *Trends in Endocrinology & Metabolism*. 2025;36(7):614–622. doi:10.1016/j.tem.2024.11.008. PMID: 39694730.

**Study Design:** Opinion/review article from Wüst lab (Amsterdam group).

**Key Findings:**
- Intrinsic mitochondrial dysfunction, endothelial abnormalities, and glycolytic fiber type shift as main contributors to reduced exercise capacity in LC
- Rapid skeletal muscle tissue damage and intramuscular immune cell infiltration contribute to PEM
- Synthesizes evidence that metabolic shift toward glycolysis (away from OXPHOS) is central to post-exertional symptom worsening
- Bridges Appelman 2024 findings with Mughal 2025 metabolic adaptation concept

**Certainty:** Raw 0.45 × Pop weight 0.80 = **0.36**  
**Bib key:** `Charlton2025MusclePEMReview`  
**Bib file:** `musculoskeletal.bib`

## Paper 5: Schreiner et al. 2020 — HHV-6 Reactivation, Mitochondrial Fragmentation

**Citation:** Schreiner P, Harrer T, Scheibenbogen C, et al. Human Herpesvirus-6 Reactivation, Mitochondrial Fragmentation, and the Coordination of Antiviral and Metabolic Phenotypes in ME/CFS. *Immunohorizons*. 2020;4(4):201–215. doi:10.4049/immunohorizons.2000006. PMID: 32327453.

**Study Design:** In vitro: U2-OS cells with chromosomally integrated HHV-6A, ± reactivation. Adoptive transfer of cell supernatants to naive A549 cells. **Adoptive transfer of serum from 10 ME/CFS patients** → mitochondrial fragmentation + antiviral state in naive cells.

**Key Findings:**
- HHV-6 reactivation → mitochondrial fragmentation, PDH inhibition, 1-carbon metabolism induction
- Supernatant adoptive transfer → antiviral state preventing influenza/HSV-1 superinfection
- **ME/CFS patient serum adoptive transfer** produced same mitochondrial fragmentation + antiviral state in naive cells
- Cell danger response (CDR) hypothesis: antiviral protection at cost of energy metabolism

**Certainty:** Raw 0.55 × Pop weight 0.75 = **0.41**  
**Bib key:** `Schreiner2020HHV6MitoME`  
**Bib file:** `immune.bib` and `musculoskeletal.bib`

## Paper 6 (NULL): Nilsson et al. 2020 — No Anti-Mitochondrial Antibodies

**Citation:** Nilsson I, Palmer J, Apostolou E, et al. Metabolic Dysfunction in ME/CFS Not Due to Anti-mitochondrial Antibodies. *Frontiers in Medicine*. 2020;7:108. doi:10.3389/fmed.2020.00108. PMID: 32296708.

**Study Design:** Cross-sectional antibody screening. n=161 ME/CFS, 15 PBC (positive control), 14 fibromyalgia, 29 MS, 44 HC.

**Key Findings:**
- Only 1/161 ME/CFS positive for anti-PDC antibodies (vs 15/15 PBC patients)
- Anti-mitochondrial antibodies (inner/outer membrane) negative in ME/CFS
- Anti-cardiolipin levels not significantly different from HC
- **Important negative result:** Impaired mitochondrial/metabolic dysfunction in ME/CFS cannot be explained by circulating anti-mitochondrial autoantibodies
- Implies the serum factor is something else: cytokines, metabolites, miRNA, exosomes, viral particles

**Certainty:** Raw 0.55 × Pop weight 0.85 = **0.47**  
**Bib key:** `Nilsson2020NoMitoAb`  
**Bib file:** `musculoskeletal.bib`

## Paper 7: Fernandez-Guerra et al. 2021 — PBMC Bioenergetics & Proteomics

**Citation:** Fernandez-Guerra P, Gonzalez-Ebsen AC, Boonen SE, et al. Bioenergetic and Proteomic Profiling of Immune Cells in ME/CFS Patients: An Exploratory Study. *Biomolecules*. 2021;11(7):961. doi:10.3390/biom11070961. PMID: 34209852.

**Study Design:** n=6 ME/CFS patients vs matched controls. PBMC bioenergetics (Seahorse extracellular flux analysis) + quantitative proteomics.

**Key Findings:**
- PBMCs from ME/CFS patients showed significantly lower mitochondrial coupling efficiency
- Proteome alterations centred on **pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH)** and coenzyme A metabolism
- Decreased capacity to provide adequate intracellular ATP
- Reinforces Fluge 2016 PDH impairment finding in a different cell type (immune cells vs myoblasts)
- Links systemic metabolic defect to immune cell dysfunction

**Certainty:** Raw 0.30 × Pop weight 0.80 = **0.24**  
**Bib key:** `FernandezGuerra2021PBMCmito`  
**Bib file:** `energy-metabolism.bib`

## Paper 8 (METHOD): Fernández-Garibay et al. 2022 — Xeno-Free 3D Muscle Platform

**Citation:** Fernández-Garibay X, Gómez-Florit M, Domingues RMA, Gomes ME, Fernández-Costa JM, Ramón-Azcón J. Xeno-free bioengineered human skeletal muscle tissue using human platelet lysate-based hydrogels. *Biofabrication*. 2022;14(4). doi:10.1088/1758-5090/ac8dc8. PMID: 36041422.

**Study Design:** Methodological paper from the same IBEC/Barcelona group (Ramón-Azcón lab) that produced Mughal 2025. Establishes the xeno-free human platelet lysate hydrogel (HUgel) platform used for 3D muscle bioengineering.

**Key Findings:**
- Human platelet lysate-based hydrogels as xeno-free scaffold for human skeletal muscle tissue engineering
- Allows tunable mechanical, structural, and biochemical properties
- Formation of highly aligned, long myotubes expressing sarcomeric proteins
- Electrical stimulation and non-invasive contractile force measurement
- **This is the platform paper describing the technology used in Mughal 2025**

**Certainty:** Raw 0.70 × Pop weight 0.40 = **0.28**  
**Bib key:** `FernandezGaribay2022XenoMuscle`  
**Bib file:** `musculoskeletal.bib`

## Integrated Model (Cross-Paper Synthesis)

The converging evidence pattern:

```
Patient serum/plasma (contains unknown factor(s))
    │
    ├── Mughal 2025: 3D muscle exposed → short-term: glycolytic shift, hypertrophy, mitochondrial hyperfusion
    │                                       long-term: fragility, toroidal mitochondrial fragmentation
    │
    ├── Fluge 2016: Myoblasts in ME/CFS serum → ↑mitochondrial respiration, ↑lactate, PDH impairment
    │
    ├── Schreiner 2020: Serum transfer to naive cells → mitochondrial fragmentation + antiviral state
    │
    ├── Appelman 2024: Muscle biopsy post-PEM → amyloid deposits, metabolic disturbances, myopathy
    │
    ├── Nilsson 2020: NOT anti-mitochondrial antibodies → serum factor is something else
    │
    └── Fernandez-Guerra 2021: PBMCs → PDH proteome alterations, ↓mitochondrial coupling

Uncertain: What IS the serum factor?
    Candidates: cytokines (TNFα, IL-6), miRNA, exosomes, viral particles, metabolites
    NOT: anti-PDC or anti-mitochondrial antibodies
    Relevant: HHV-6 miRNA (miR-aU14) shown to fragment mitochondria (Schreiner/Hennig)
```
