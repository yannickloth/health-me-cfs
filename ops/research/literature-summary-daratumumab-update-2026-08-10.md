# Literature Summary: Daratumumab Anti-CD38 Plasma-Cell Update

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Search Agent:** literature-integrator
**Scope:** Gap-fill / update — find NEW evidence on daratumumab, anti-CD38, plasma-cell-directed therapy in ME/CFS / post-viral fatigue

---

## Executive Summary

**Verdict: Genuinely new direct evidence is very sparse.** No ResetME trial results have been published. No follow-up or subgroup analysis of the Fluge 2025 daratumumab pilot has appeared. No isatuximab studies in ME/CFS exist. The field has not moved substantially since the Fluge 2025 pilot was published in July 2025.

Three context papers (2 new, 1 previously unintegrated) are recommended for addition. None are direct daratumumab-in-ME/CFS evidence; all strengthen the mechanistic rationale.

## Paper 1: Ray et al. 2026 — NK Cell Dysregulation in Long COVID

**PMID:** 41878441 | **DOI:** 10.3389/fimmu.2026.1720551 | **Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology (2026)

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Study design | Cross-sectional; scRNA-seq + flow cytometry + antibody profiling |
| Sample | n=94 LTCS, n=24 convalescents, n=66 HC (antibody/cytokine); n=23/6/9 (flow); n=32/6/8 (scRNA-seq) |
| Relevance | NK cells are the on-target population depleted by daratumumab (anti-CD38); Fluge 2025 found low baseline NK count predicted daratumumab non-response |
| Quality | HIGH — comprehensive multi-omic design, adequate n, peer-reviewed in Frontiers in Immunology |
| Certainty (raw) | 0.75 |
| Population weight | 0.60 (Long COVID, not ME/CFS; similar post-viral fatigue syndrome) |
| Discounted certainty | **0.45** |

### Key Findings
- LTCS patients had elevated anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG titers but REDUCED systemic cytokines (IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-10)
- Flow cytometry: marked depletion of CD56+CD16+ NK cells and CD56+CD3+ NKT cells
- scRNA-seq confirmed NK type I cell loss with broad transcriptional reprogramming
- Functional assays: reduced NK-cell inflammatory capacity

### Relevance to Daratumumab Integration
Fluge 2025 identified low baseline NK-cell count as a significant predictor of NON-response to daratumumab. Ray 2026 provides mechanistic depth: long COVID features quantitative and functional NK deficiency. If daratumumab further depletes already-compromised NK cells (on-target CD38 effect), this could paradoxically worsen immune surveillance. The NK finding bridges the daratumumab response prediction with broader post-viral NK dysfunction.

## Paper 2: Cox et al. 2022 — EBV/HHV-6A dUTPases → TFH → LLPC in ME/CFS

**PMID:** 35482424 | **DOI:** 10.1172/jci.insight.158193 | **Journal:** JCI Insight (2022)

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Study design | Cross-sectional human cohort + in vitro mechanistic + in vivo mouse model |
| Sample | ME/CFS cohort (n not reported in abstract) vs controls |
| Relevance | Directly links viral triggers to long-lived plasma cell (LLPC) generation in ME/CFS — the exact cell population targeted by daratumumab |
| Quality | HIGH — JCI Insight (high-impact), multi-level evidence (human serum, in vitro, mouse) |
| Certainty (raw) | 0.75 |
| Population weight | 0.85 (direct ME/CFS cohort) |
| Discounted certainty | **0.65** |

### Key Findings
- ME/CFS patients: increased activin A and IL-21 serum levels
- These cytokines correlated with antibodies against EBV/HHV-6 dUTPases
- IL-21 elevated but CXCL13 NOT elevated → impaired germinal center function
- ME/CFS serum sufficient to drive TFH cell differentiation via activin A
- EBV dUTPase induced splenic marginal zone B and invariant NKT-FH cells in vivo

### Relevance to Daratumumab Integration
This paper provides the most direct mechanistic bridge from viral trigger → aberrant TFH activity → long-lived plasma cell generation in ME/CFS. It explains WHY plasma-cell targeting (daratumumab) could succeed where B-cell targeting (rituximab) failed: the pathogenic autoantibodies may come from LLPCs generated during a dysregulated germinal center response, and these LLPCs are CD20-negative but CD38-positive.

**Pre-existing status:** NOT currently in any bib file. Already mentioned indirectly through Williams/Cox/Ariza 2025 paper (appendix-h line 2222), but this earlier JCI Insight paper contains the core mechanistic evidence.

## Paper 3: Chiarenza et al. 2025 — Combined Rituximab + Daratumumab in Pediatric Lupus Nephritis

**PMID:** 39935484 | **DOI:** 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1525892 | **Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology (2025)

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Study design | Case report (n=2) |
| Sample | 2 pediatric patients with refractory lupus nephritis |
| Relevance | Demonstrates safety of combined B-cell (rituximab) + plasma-cell (daratumumab) targeting; single-infusion protocol reduced cost/toxicity |
| Quality | LOW — n=2, case report, different disease |
| Certainty (raw) | 0.30 |
| Population weight | 0.20 (pediatric LN, not ME/CFS) |
| Discounted certainty | **0.06** |

### Key Findings
- Single infusion of rituximab + daratumumab was safe and effective in 2 pediatric cases
- Combined CD20 + CD38 targeting may reduce adverse effects and costs vs prolonged daratumumab
- Provides proof-of-concept for dual B-cell/plasma-cell depletion strategy

### Relevance to Daratumumab Integration
Peripheral context only. Demonstrates that the combination approach (anti-CD20 + anti-CD38) is feasible in autoantibody-driven disease. The Fluge 2019 rituximab null + Fluge 2025 daratumumab positive pattern suggests plasma cells (not B cells) are the relevant target; combination therapy could theoretically capture both compartments.

**Certainty discounted to near-zero** due to n=2 and different disease. Include as contextual footnote, not as direct evidence.

## Research Gaps Identified

1. **ResetME results unpublished** — n=66 RCT, recruiting per appendix I. No interim/final results in PubMed or clinicaltrials.gov as of 2026-08-10.
2. **No daratumumab long-term follow-up** — Fluge 2025 followed patients 12-24 months. No extension data published.
3. **No responder biomarker analysis** — Fluge 2025 noted low NK counts predicted non-response but no deeper immunophenotyping of responders vs non-responders published.
4. **No isatuximab in ME/CFS** — Different anti-CD38 antibody with different binding epitope and potentially different NK-depleting profile; not studied in ME/CFS.
5. **BC007 Phase II results unpublished** — Appendix I lists n=114 RCT as highest-priority; no results posted.
6. **No CAR-T cell or bispecific engager studies in ME/CFS** — Despite the revolution in autoimmune disease treatment with CAR-T and bispecific T-cell engagers (CD19, BCMA), none tested in ME/CFS.

## Integration Recommendation

**Add 2 papers to bib (Ray 2026 and Cox 2022).** Chiarenza 2025 is too peripheral at n=2 and different disease — add as appendix-only reference or skip.

Both additions strengthen the existing daratumumab narrative in the paper:
- **Cox 2022** explains WHY plasma cells matter in ME/CFS (viral dUTPase → TFH → LLPC axis)
- **Ray 2026** explains a potential constraint on daratumumab efficacy (pre-existing NK deficiency → poor response)
