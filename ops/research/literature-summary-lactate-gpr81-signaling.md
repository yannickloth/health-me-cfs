## Evidence landscape

**Strong (≥0.45 discounted):** Stacpoole2006DCA (RCT, DCA lowers lactate but neurotoxic), Kuratsune1998 (ME/CFS acylcarnitine deficiency, cross-nationally replicated), Bager2021 (thiamine RCT for fatigue), Haunhorst2025 (PEM immunometabolic framing), Halestrap2012 (MCT transport gate).

**Weak (<0.40 discounted):** Teitelbaum2006 (open-label pilot), Colgan2026 (protocol/pilot), and all six foundational signaling papers (animal/in-vitro) at 0.26–0.38.

**Null/competing:** Forsythe2000 (bicarbonate is not supported — harm/competing arm). Corpus reuse for null anchors: `Godlewska2025MRS` resting-muscle lactate null, `Ryback2026myoblastNull` serum-transfer null.

**Missing:** No study measures GPR81/HCAR1 expression or signaling in ME/CFS patients (gap G6 remains a *mechanistically-plausible* but *unmeasured* hypothesis). No ME/CFS RCT of DCA, D-ribose, thiamine, or MCT/ketogenic therapy exists.

## What the evidence establishes vs. what must stay speculative

**Establishes (high confidence, general biology):** lactate is a GPCR ligand (GPR81/HCAR1) with adipose, immune (macrophage, T cell), and brain (plasticity, vascular) signaling functions; its transport is gated by MCT1–4. Lactate is not inert waste.

**Speculative (translation gap):** that *dysregulated GPR81/HCAR1/MCT signaling* contributes to ME/CFS symptoms. This is a mechanistically-plausible but unmeasured hypothesis (population discount 0.50 for all direct signaling evidence).

**Speculative (intervention):** that lowering/clearing lactate (DCA, thiamine, carnitine, ketogenic/MCT) improves ME/CFS. Evidence is either a different disease (DCA, thiamine) or low-tier pilot (D-ribose, ketogenic). Bicarbonate is actively unsupported.

**Does not support:** buffering lactate with bicarbonate (Forsythe2000); treating DCA as safe (neuropathy).
