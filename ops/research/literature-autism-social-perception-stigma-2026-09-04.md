## Null / contradicting evidence found
1. **In-group dissociation (DeBrabander 2019):** autistic raters share trait-inference tendency but NOT reduced social-interaction willingness — the stigmatizing behavioral component is strongest in cross-neurotype judgment.
2. **Disclosure reduces bias (Norris 2024):** informing perceivers of the diagnosis improves ratings — bias is partly reducible by information.
3. **Disclosure is condition-specific (Parisi 2026):** disclosing schizophrenia WORSENS impressions (stigma), while mislabeling as autism IMPROVES them — no universal disclosure benefit.
4. **Meta-analytic transcript exception (Wanigasekera 2025):** no impression difference for transcript modality — confirms "style not substance" and that verbal content alone does not drive the bias.
5. **Relational reversal (Morrison 2020):** same-neurotype pairs show better rapport — the "deficit" is relational, not intrinsic.

## Evidence landscape
- **Strong (≥0.60 raw):** anchor + meta-analysis + mechanism papers + clinical thin-slice review — but all by-analogy to ME/CFS.
- **Weak/moderate:** independent single studies, moderate n.
- **Missing:** any direct ME/CFS or chronic-fatigue first-impression study; any autism+ME/CFS comorbidity-stigma study.
- **Null:** no uniform-null finding; the field robustly shows the effect, with the null being modality-specificity (transcript) and in-group non-replication of the willingness component.

## Integration implications
- NO direct ME/CFS evidence → any ME/CFS causal first-impression claim is capped at `#speculation`. Decision will likely be **PARTIAL or PROCEED-on-analogy** at Phase 2 (need ≥2 papers ≥0.40 OR ≥1 ≥0.60 discounted; present: many at 0.41–0.64 discounted). But all are cross-condition analogy, so ME/CFS-specific environments must stay speculation/open-question.
- The transdiagnostic "style (flat affect / reduced expressivity) drives negative first impressions" claim is the strongest defensible cross-condition statement.
