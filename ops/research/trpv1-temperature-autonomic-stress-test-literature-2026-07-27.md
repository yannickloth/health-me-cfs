*VanCampenEtAl2021 (already in bib):* Post-tilt cerebral blood flow recovery delayed in ME/CFS — severity-graded, with severe patients showing CBF still -25% after 5 min supine recovery. Independent of HR/BP normalization.
*Nelson2021HRVrecoveryMECFS:* Lower HR recovery after exercise in ME/CFS (HRR ≤34.5 bpm; ROC AUC 74.8%). Same group had non-significant peak VO2 change.
*Ruijgt2026:* Wearable HRV lower for 24h post-exercise in Long COVID. Nighttime HRV decreases with intense exercise suggesting PEM threshold above VT1.
*Oosterwijck2017:* Reduced parasympathetic reactivation during recovery associated with failure of exercise-induced analgesia in ME/CFS — baroreceptor role in PEM.
*Cherneva2025:* Chronotropic insufficiency in 81.5% of moderate-severe PCS vs 12.5% controls. Abnormal HRR in 33%. Autonomic dysfunction limits exercise via non-cardiorespiratory pathways.

### Dimension 4: CPET null finding (PEM threshold measurement)

| Bib Key | First Author | Year | Design | n | Raw Cert | Pop | Pop Weight | Discounted |
|---------|-------------|------|--------|---|----------|-----|-----------|------------|
| Mancini2026CPETrecoveryNull | Mancini DM | 2026 | Human ME/CFS (Fukuda) | 58 CFS, 25 HC | 0.70 | ME/CFS | 1.00 | 0.70 |

*Mancini2026:* Largest 2-day CPET replication. NO significant Day 1→Day 2 decline in peak VO2 or VO2@VT in ME/CFS. This null result challenges the 2-day CPET as definitive PEM measurement — creates a need for alternative PEM threshold methods (thermal stress test fills this gap).

*Evengard1998:* Key null — CSF substance P levels NORMAL in CFS (unlike FM where they are elevated). Limits direct SP pathology relevance to CFS specifically.

## Key Gaps

1. **No direct human study of post-thermal autonomic recovery in ME/CFS.** The hypothesis is mechanistically supported by separate lines (TRPV1→autonomic, cold pressor→CFS, HRV recovery→ME/CFS) but never directly tested as a combined protocol.
2. **No PEM threshold correlation with thermal recovery.** Ruijgt2026 shows HRV predicts PEM in Long COVID but not ME/CFS.
3. **No controlled thermal challenge (sauna→recovery→HRV) study in ME/CFS.** All thermal therapy studies measured subjective outcomes (fatigue, mood); none measured autonomic recovery time.
4. **Substance P in CFS is normal (Evengard1998)** — TRPV1→SP→neuroinflammation pathway may not be the operative route in CFS (unlike FM). TRPV1→CGRP→vasodilation or TRPV1→sympathetic outflow → baroreflex may be more relevant.
