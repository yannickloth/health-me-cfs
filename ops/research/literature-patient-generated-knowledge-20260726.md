## Competing/Alternative Views

- **Data quality concerns:** Schmitz 2018 (JAMIA) documents challenges: low retention, uncertain data quality, need for validation, privacy concerns. Same concerns in Wicks 2018 (self-experimentation bias).
- **Biomedicalization risk:** de Boer 2021 warns that biomedicalized patient participation may undermine non-biomedical patient knowledge and sustain reductive ideas about who patients are.
- **Expertise tension:** Byrne 2020 explicitly analyzes the tension between protecting against epistemic injustice and maintaining clinical expertise — warns that uncritical acceptance of patient testimony can also produce harm.
- **Citizen science limitations:** Afshinnekoo 2016 (Br Med Bull) — selection bias, limited sample sizes, difficulty enforcing standardized protocols for non-scientist data collectors.
- **Not all patient-led is equal:** Cousins 2026 acknowledges that many patient involvement frameworks are criticized for tokenism.

---

## Null/Negative Findings

- **Patient-generated data may be biased:** Wicks 2018 acknowledges that patient self-experimentation generates "relatively biased data" and that translation to generalizable decisions is challenged.
- **No evidence of militancy:** Blease & Geraghty 2018 found "no compelling evidence that the vast majority of patients with ME/CFS, or the POs representing them, have adopted any such militant political policies."
- **Spirulina N-of-1 negative:** Baicus & Baicus 2007 (PMID 17335116) — 4 N-of-1 RCTs showed spirulina did not ameliorate idiopathic chronic fatigue. Demonstrates N-of-1 methodology can produce null results.
- **mHealth data quality:** Schmitz 2018 systematic review of 36 ResearchKit apps found low retention as common challenge — data quality uncertain.
