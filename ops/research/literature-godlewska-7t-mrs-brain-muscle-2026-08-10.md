# Literature Summary: Godlewska et al. 2025 — Brain and Muscle Chemistry in ME/CFS and Long COVID (7T MRS)

**Topic slug:** `godlewska-7t-mrs-brain-muscle`
**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Mode:** MIXED

## Primary paper

**Godlewska et al. 2025, Molecular Psychiatry** (7T MRS; PMID 40652046; DOI 10.1038/s41380-025-03108-8)
- 7T MRS in 24 ME/CFS, 25 long COVID, 24 healthy controls.
- Brain regions: pregenual (pgACC) + dorsal anterior cingulate (dACC); plus calf muscle.
- **ME/CFS:** lactate elevated in BOTH pgACC (p=0.004) and dACC (p=0.006) vs HC. Consistent with energetic stress + mitochondrial dysfunction.
- **Long COVID:** total choline (tCho) LOWERED in dACC vs HC (p=0.0002). Author links to blood-clot/`brain fog` association and choline's anti-coagulative role.
- **Calf muscle at rest:** no significant between-group differences.
- **Central implication:** ME/CFS and long COVID show DIFFERENT neurochemical profiles despite overlapping clinical symptoms → should NOT be studied as a single combined group until mechanisms are understood.
- Conflict of interest: BRG has received consultancy fees from Tiefenbacher Pharmaceuticals.
- Cohort overlap: Oxford (University of Oxford / Oxford Health NHS Foundation Trust). Independent lab.

## Supporting — brain lactate elevation in ME/CFS (replication cluster)

- **Murrough 2010 (Mount Sinai, NMR Biomed):** Ventricular CSF lactate significantly elevated in CFS vs HC AND vs major depressive disorder (MDD); correlated with severity of mental fatigue specific to CFS. Replicates prior Shungu study. → **Diagnostic/differential value: MRS lactate distinguishes CFS from depression.** (n=17 CFS, 19 HC, 21 MDD). Already in bib as Natelson/Murrough context.
- **Natelson 2017 (Mount Sinai, J Neurol Sci):** CFS: lower glutathione (GSH), lower CBF, higher ventricular lactate, more spinal-fluid abnormalities vs HC. Replicates prior. (Already in bib: Natelson2017Lactate.)
- **Mueller 2020 (UAB, Brain Imaging Behav):** Whole-brain MRS: elevated CHO/CR left anterior cingulate; elevated LAC/CR (insula, thalamus, cerebellum); elevated brain temperature. Suggests neuroinflammation. (Already in bib: Mueller2020MRS.)

## Supporting — cross-disease lactate and therapy-response biomarker

- **Natelson 2015 (J Pain):** Fibromyalgia patients have elevated ventricular lactate vs HC; 8-week milnacipran (SNRI) lowered lactate AND pain vs placebo; proposss MRS lactate as a therapy-response biomarker. (Already in bib: Natelson2015milnacipran.)

## Supporting — Gulf War Illness (chronic fatigue overlap)

- **Jones 2025 (UAB, Exp Brain Res):** 20 GWI veterans vs 20 healthy: widespread DECREASES in brain choline, NAA, creatine; regional elevations in lactate and temperature. Same UAB methodology as Mueller. Parallels the long COVID choline-lowering pattern and ME/CFS lactate elevation. Cross-disease consistency of brain metabolite shifts in chronic fatigue conditions.

## Supporting — long COVID brain MRS (heterogeneous choline; energy shifts)

- **Sklinda 2021 (Pol J Radiol):** Brain-fog long-haul COVID (n=11): routine MRI normal, but Glx and Lac concentration changes on MRS; probable ischaemic background. Supports energy-metabolite change in long COVID brain.
- **Bravi 2025 (Neuroscience):** COVID-19 recovery (n=64 vs 33 HC): HIGHER glutamate and NAA with better cognition; interpreted as repair/remyelination (opposite to energy-deficit reading — a competing interpretation for long COVID NAA).
- **Pajuelo 2024 (MAGMA):** Post-COVID (n=58 vs 23 HC): choline INCREASED in corpus callosum splenium (CCS) in OLDER (>40y) patients only. → Choline direction is region- and age-dependent; the Godlewska dACC-choline-lowering finding may not generalize to all regions/ages. Important contradiction framing.

## Supporting — muscle energy metabolism (contextualizing the resting-muscle null)

- **Naegel 2023 (NMR Biomed):** Dynamic 31P-MRS exercise/recovery: reduced muscle oxidative capacity (prolonged τPCr, reduced Vmax) in COVID-19 and MS vs controls. → The muscle deficit in post-viral fatigue is EXERCISE-PROVOKED (recovery dynamics), consistent with Godlewska's null finding in RESTING muscle. Reconciles resting-null with exercised-muscle bioenergetic deficit literature.
- **Sharma 2007 (Magn Reson Imaging):** Postpolio (background): reduced PCr/Pi (oxidative phosphorylation impairment) via in vivo 31P-MRS at rest. Background only — population weight low.

## Strengths / weaknesses / missing / null

- **Strong:** Independent-lab replication of brain-lactate elevation in ME/CFS (3 labs: Mount Sinai, UAB, Oxford). Primary study uses ultra-high-field 7T (best resolution). Directly measures target tissue (bridges blood-to-brain compartment gap).
- **Weak:** n=24/25/24 modest; 7T single cohort not yet independently replicated at 7T; muscle finding is a null at rest (may miss exercise-provoked deficits). Choline direction heterogeneous (↓ dACC in Godlewska vs ↑ CCS older in Pajuelo vs ↑ cingulate in Mueller ME/CFS).
- **Missing:** No CSF/NAD+ assay to confirm the mitochondrial interpretation of lactate. No perfusion measure in Godlewska to separate hypoperfusion vs mitochondrial cause of lactate.
- **Null:** Resting muscle metabolites did NOT differ — important negative result for the muscle-bioenergetics-at-rest assumption.
