# Literature Summary: Satellite Cell Depletion and Impaired Muscle Regeneration in ME/CFS and Long COVID

**Topic slug:** `satellite-cell-regeneration`
**Source:** Charlton BT et al., 2026 AMS Conference Abstract C14
**Date:** 2026-07-26

---

## Executive Summary

**This is the first-ever measurement of satellite cells (SCs) in ME/CFS or Long COVID muscle.** PubMed returns zero direct satellite cell/ME/CFS papers. Charlton 2026 AMS C14 reports significantly reduced Pax7+ satellite cells (P<0.001), trending reduced PDGFRα+ FAP cells (P=0.061), and a significant correlation between SC count and OXPHOS capacity (r=0.43, P=0.0035) in 51 patients (26 ME/CFS + 25 LC) vs 30 controls. The finding is novel, un-replicated, and comes from a single cohort (Wüst lab, VU Amsterdam). Eight supporting papers provide mechanistic plausibility (SC biology, prior muscle biopsy evidence, oxidative stress-impaired regeneration) but no independent replication exists.

**CRITICAL CAVEAT:** All Wüst-lab publications (Appelman 2024, Charlton 2025 medRxiv, Slaghekke 2026 F5, Charlton 2026 C14) are analyses of the same biobank (26 ME/CFS + 24--25 LC + 30 HC). These are not independent replications.

## 1. Index Paper: Charlton et al. 2026 — AMS Abstract C14

### Study Design
Vastus lateralis biopsies from a single longitudinal cohort. IHC for Pax7 (satellite cells) and PDGFRα (FAP cells). High-resolution respirometry for OXPHOS capacity. Metabolomics.

### Samples

### Key Findings
1. **Reduced Pax7+ satellite cells (P<0.001)** — primary finding, both ME/CFS and LC groups
2. **Trending reduced PDGFRα+ FAP cells (P=0.061)** — niche support cells for SCs
3. **SC count correlated with OXPHOS capacity (r=0.43, P=0.0035)** — mechanistically links mitochondrial dysfunction to impaired regenerative capacity
4. **Metabolic shift away from oxidative metabolism** — consistent with prior Wüst-lab findings of reduced OXPHOS
5. **Reduced antioxidative metabolites** — suggests oxidative stress may drive SC depletion
6. **Conference abstract only** — full methods, any subgroup analyses, and adjustment for confounders unavailable

- **Population weight:** 0.925 (mixed ME/CFS=1.00 + LC=0.85)
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.37
- **Limitations:** Conference abstract format; single biobank; no independent replication; no adjustment for age/physical activity/menopause which all affect SC count; no functional SC assays (ex vivo differentiation); no longitudinal data

## 2. Corroborating Evidence (Indirect)

### Prior Muscle Biopsy Studies Showing Regeneration/Degeneration Pathology
| Study | Year | Population | n | Key finding relevant to SC depletion |
|-------|------|-----------|----|--------------------------------------|
| Vecchiet 1996 | 1996 | CFS | 21 | Fatty degeneration, fibrous regeneration, mitochondrial monstrosity — earliest CFS muscle pathology |
| Hejbøl 2022 | 2022 | LC | 14 | 43% showed regeneration signs (split fibers, central nuclei); fiber atrophy in 71% — indirect evidence of incomplete repair |
| Colosio 2023 | 2023 | PASC | 22 | Reduced type IIx fiber CSA; preserved mitochondrial respiration — early functional impairment |
| Soares 2022 | 2022 | COVID | review | Impaired regenerative capacity as recognized post-COVID sequela |

### Satellite Cell Biology — Mechanistic Plausibility
- **SC-OXPHOS dependency (Bhattacharya 2024):** SC activation and differentiation require a metabolic switch from glycolysis to OXPHOS. Impaired mitochondrial function directly blocks myogenic differentiation. The Charlton 2026 r=0.43 correlation fits this established pathway.
- **SC niche regulation (Yin 2013):** FAP cells (PDGFRα+) are essential niche support cells for SCs. The trending FAP depletion (P=0.061) suggests the SC niche itself may be compromised.
- **SC depletion mechanisms (Dumont 2015):** Chronic inflammation (TNFα, IL-6), oxidative stress, and metabolic dysfunction are established drivers of SC exhaustion and impaired self-renewal. All three are documented in ME/CFS.
- **Gerwyn & Maes 2017:** Reviewed chronic oxidative/nitrosative stress in ME/CFS muscle as a mechanism impairing muscle repair — consistent with the SC depletion finding 9 years before it was measured.

## 5. Key Finding Summary

The Charlton 2026 AMS C14 abstract reports the first direct measurement of satellite cell depletion in ME/CFS and Long COVID — significantly reduced Pax7+ SCs (P<0.001) correlated with OXPHOS capacity (r=0.43) in 51 patients vs 30 controls. This is a novel, un-replicated finding from a single biobank (Wüst lab, VU Amsterdam). The known metabolic dependency of SC activation on OXPHOS (Bhattacharya 2024) provides mechanistic plausibility: impaired mitochondrial function in ME/CFS muscle would be expected to block SC differentiation and self-renewal, leading to progressive SC pool depletion. Prior muscle biopsy studies (Hejbøl 2022, Vecchiet 1996) showed signs of incomplete regeneration (split fibers, central nuclei, fatty degeneration), consistent with a failed regenerative response. The trending FAP cell depletion (P=0.061) suggests the SC niche itself may be compromised. However, independent replication from a different cohort/lab is essential: all Wüst-lab publications on this topic are analyses of the same biobank. The finding is high-impact if replicated, but currently rests on a single cohort in conference-abstract form.

## 6. Research Gaps

1. **Independent replication** — satellite cell quantification in a non-Wüst ME/CFS/LC cohort
2. **Functional SC assays** — ex vivo differentiation capacity, colony-forming assays
3. **Longitudinal data** — does SC depletion progress with disease duration? Is it reversible?
4. **Causal mechanism** — is SC depletion driven by OXPHOS failure, inflammation, oxidative stress, or disuse?
5. **Confounder adjustment** — age, sex, menopause, physical activity, BMI all affect SC count
6. **Full paper publication** — conference abstract lacks methods detail, subgroup analyses, and confounder-adjusted models
