## Stream A — Nrf2 pathway measurement in ADHD

### Key finding: the "no study has measured Nrf2 in ADHD" claim is now FALSE

**Gürbüzer N, Ozkaya A, Mercantepe F. "The SIRT-1/Nrf-2/HO-1 antioxidant defense axis in adult attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder." *Metab Brain Dis*. 2026;41(1):85.**
- PMID: 42012587 (verified) · DOI: 10.1007/s11011-026-01845-5 · PMCID: PMC13099689
- **Design:** cross-sectional, human. 60 adults with ADHD + 60 age/sex-matched healthy controls (SCID-5/CV + DIVA 2.0 diagnosis; ASRS severity; Turkey).
- **Measured:** serum Nrf2, HO-1, SIRT-1, TNF-α, CRP, ferritin (ELISA).
- **Result:** Nrf-2, HO-1, SIRT-1 significantly LOWER in ADHD (all p < 0.001), each negatively correlated with symptom severity; ROC AUC 0.756 (Nrf-2), 0.797 (HO-1); TNF-α not different.
- **Relevance:** first direct measurement of Nrf2 and its downstream effector HO-1 in ADHD patient blood. Directly relevant to the dopamine-Nrf2-NLRP3 axis hypothesis.
- **Caveats:** measures circulating serum protein, NOT Nrf2 nuclear translocation or NQO1 target-gene expression; adult ADHD only; single study, no replication.

**Raw cert 0.55 · population weight 0.75 (general population, ADHD model) → discounted 0.41.**

### Sulforaphane RCT in ADHD (did NOT measure Nrf2)
- **Ghannadi F, et al. 2025. "Sulforaphane Adjunct to Methylphenidate for ADHD." *Clin Neuropharmacol* 48(6):181-187.** PMID: 41604557 (verified). RCT, 70 children 6–11 y, methylphenidate ± sulforaphane 30 mg/d, 8 wk. Improved ADHD-RS symptom scores; measured NO Nrf2/HO-1/NQO1 biomarkers. Does NOT contradict the "never measured" claim → but an Nrf2-activating drug was tested in ADHD without measuring the pathway.

### Animal / in vitro (do NOT measure ADHD patients)
- Mokhtar 2024 *Int J Mol Sci* (PMID 39273230): MSG mouse ADHD model, hesperetin → Nrf2/Keap1/HO-1/SIRT1.
- Zang 2025 *Eur J Med Res* (PMID 41366813): MSG mouse, dihydromyricetin → SIRT1/Keap1/Nrf2/HO-1.
- Ding 2025 *Free Radic Biol Med* (PMID 40023299): SHR rat, baicalin → Nrf2/Keap-1/HO-1, nuclear translocation.
- Carreón-Trujillo 2023 *Cell Biochem Biophys* (PMID 36346546): in vitro SH-SY5Y, atomoxetine → Nrf2/NQO1 unchanged.

## Stream B — Adult-onset ADHD consensus (past Moffitt 2015)

### Verified consensus: "adult-onset ADHD as a distinct syndrome" is NOT the 2026 consensus

**Sibley MH, et al. 2018. "Late-Onset ADHD Reconsidered With Comprehensive Repeated Assessments Between Ages 10 and 25." *Am J Psychiatry* 175(2):140-149.**
- PMID: 29050505 (verified) · DOI: 10.1176/appi.ajp.2017.17030298 · PMCID: PMC5814300
- MTA normative comparison group, n=239, ages 9.89→24.40, eight comprehensive assessments.
- **~95% of individuals who screened positive on symptom checklists were excluded from a late-onset ADHD diagnosis.** Among impairing late-onset symptoms, the most common exclusion was symptoms occurring exclusively in the context of heavy substance use; most late-onset cases were adolescence-limited. **No evidence for adult-onset ADHD independent of a complex psychiatric history.** Strongly supports the measurement-artifact / missed-childhood / comorbidity view against Moffitt's distinct-syndrome reading.
- Raw cert 0.65 · weight 0.75 → 0.49.

**Asherson P & Agnew-Blais J. 2019. "Annual Research Review: Does late-onset ADHD exist?" *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 60(4):333-352.**
- PMID: 30843223 (verified) · DOI: 10.1111/jcpp.13020
- King's College London; Agnew-Blais co-author (key figure in late-onset debate). Balanced middle position: a significant proportion of young adults meeting ADHD criteria did not meet full criteria as children, BUT **most late-onset cases develop the disorder between ages 12–16 (adolescent-onset)**, many with childhood symptoms or an externalizing disorder (e.g., ODD). Insufficient data clarify whether early/late onset share neural mechanisms; impairing ADHD can emerge after 12 "although perhaps rarely in the context of a complete absence of precursors."
- Raw cert 0.62 · weight 0.75 → 0.47.

**Breda V, et al. 2020. "Revisiting ADHD age-of-onset in adults: to what extent should we rely on the recall of childhood symptoms?" *Psychol Med* 50(5):857-866.**
- PMID: 30968792 (verified) · DOI: 10.1017/S003329171900076X
- 1993 Pelotas Birth Cohort, n=3,810 at age 22. Retrospective recall of childhood ADHD symptoms: accuracy only **55.4%**, sensitivity **32.8%**, PPV **40.7%** vs age-11 assessments. Supports that much apparent adult-onset reflects missed/misrecalled childhood symptoms; cautions against retrospective recall to establish neurodevelopmental trajectory.
- Raw cert 0.58 · weight 0.75 → 0.44.

### NOTE on subagent misattribution (corrected against verified records)
- PMID 35034501 is **Riglin L, et al. 2022** ("'Late-onset' ADHD symptoms in young adulthood", *J Atten Disord*, Agnew-Blais as co-author) — NOT a standalone Agnew-Blais paper. Not used as a citation.
- PMID 30968792 is **Breda V, et al. 2020** (Caye is co-author, not first author). Cited as Breda2020adhdrecall.

## Haplogroup U confirmation (unchanged)

- **Chang2020haploADHD** (Transl Psychiatry 10:370, PMID 33139694): 2,076 ADHD cases vs 5,078 controls; haplogroups U (OR 0.77) and K (OR 0.69) protective. Sole haplogroup-ADHD source. Already in bib.
- **Giannoulis2024sysrevmtADHD** (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry 33:1675, PMID 35796884): systematic review; confirms Chang 2020 as the haplogroup-assignment study within scope; no newer haplogroup-ADHD cohort. Already in bib.
- **BillingRoss2016mtDNA** (J Transl Med 14:19, PMID 26791940): ME/CFS; haplogroups J, U, H associated with symptom severity/type (joint pain, bloating, post-exertional "feeling dead"); U associated with attenuated symptom severity. Anchors the cross-study ME/CFS overlap. Already in bib.
- Confirmed: NO study combines haplogroup + ADHD comorbidity + chronic fatigue in one cohort (exhaustive search, zero results). Gap remains open.
