# Literature Summary: Dementia Neuroinflammation (Kuring 2026)

**Topic slug:** dementia-inflammation
**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Primary paper PDF:** `Literature/reviews/Kuring2026_dementia_inflammation_meta.pdf`

## Overview

A PRISMA/MOOSE-conforming systematic review and meta-analysis (41 studies, 3895 participants,
2256 dementia / 1639 controls) testing the **inflammatory-mediated neurodegeneration hypothesis**:
mental illness → inflammatory response → neurodegenerative changes → dementia. It is distinctive
because it **excluded comorbid mental and physical health conditions with a known inflammatory
response**, isolating dementia-specific (not comorbidity-driven) inflammation.

## Key Findings by Paper

### Kuring 2026 (primary)
- AD associated with inflammation independent of comorbid inflammatory conditions. **IL-17A (g=0.74),
  IL-1α (0.57), IL-10 (0.59)** elevated (each 4 studies); **G-CSF (0.80), GM-CSF (0.44), IL-3 (1.39)**
  elevated (2 studies each).
- **IL-3 and G-CSF were NOT examined in prior Alzheimer meta-analyses** — novel markers elevated in AD.
- Excluding comorbid inflammatory conditions **removed the IL-6 and TNF-α elevations** that prior
  meta-analyses reported in AD → the comorbidity, not dementia per se, may drive those classic signals.
- VaD/DLB/FTD show **tentative, divergent** profiles; IL-1α, IL-3, CCL18 higher in AD but lower in
  VaD/DLB/FTD → dementia subtypes may have discrete inflammatory processes.
- **Peripheral vs CSF discordance** for IL-1β and TNF-α in AD (lower in CSF, no peripheral difference)
  → blood may not be a reliable proxy for CNS neuroinflammation (parallels Document-Specific
  Instruction C compartment gap).
- Disease-subtype heterogeneity: minimal overlap in immune profiles across AD/VaD/DLB/FTD.

### Kuring 2023 (companion)
- From the mental-illness side of the hypothesis: depression, anxiety, PTSD each associated with
  inflammation independent of comorbid inflammatory conditions.
- IL-17A elevated in AD AND in depression; G-CSF elevated in AD, depression, AND PTSD → shared
  inflammatory pathway candidate linking mental illness to dementia.

### Strawbridge 2019 (ME/CFS parallel)
- ME/CFS: TNF-α (0.274), IL-2, IL-4, TGF-β (0.967), CRP (0.622) elevated vs controls (42 studies).
- The **specific elevated markers differ** between CFS (TNF-α/IL-4/TGF-β/CRP) and AD (IL-17A/IL-1α/
  IL-10/G-CSF/IL-3) → both conditions show low-grade inflammation, but the signature is not identical.
- Heterogeneity across ME/CFS studies → inflammation not uniform across all patients (subgroup caveat).

## Strongest Mechanistic Claim Supportable

The **inflammatory-mediated neurodegeneration template** (a chronic low-grade inflammatory state that
can be dissociated from psychiatric comorbidity, with subtype/disease-specific marker profiles) is a
viable cross-disease model. This is the transferable concept: ME/CFS, like dementia, (a) shows
low-grade inflammation independent of psychiatric comorbidity, (b) has subtype/severity
heterogeneity in its inflammatory profile, (c) has a plausible mental/physiological-stress →
neuroinflammation → CNS-pathology causal chain (Kuring's hypothesis and the paper's own ME/CFS
sickness-behavior models).

## What Must Remain Speculative

- Whether inflammation is causal, prodromal, or epiphenomenal — cross-sectional included studies
  cannot order cause and effect (Kuring 2026 itself states this).
- Whether the specific AD dementia markers (IL-17A, IL-1α, IL-10, G-CSF, GM-CSF, IL-3) translate to
  ME/CFS — they do NOT match the ME/CFS signature (TNF-α/IL-4/TGF-β/CRP), so transfer of the *specific
  markers* is NOT supported; only the *general template* transfers.
- The mental-illness → inflammation direction of causality (reverse causation / prodrome not excluded).

## What the Evidence Does NOT Support

- **IL-6 and TNF-α as dementia-specific (independent of comorbidity)** — Kuring 2026 found them null
  once comorbid inflammatory conditions were excluded.
- Transferring the *specific* AD dementia cytokine signature to ME/CFS — the ME/CFS profile differs.
- A fully causal dementia model — unresolved.

## Null / Contradicting Evidence

- Kuring's own nulls: IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β not elevated in AD once comorbidity excluded (contradicts
  several older AD meta-analyses). This is a methodological caution, not a dementia refutation.
- Blundell 2015 (CFS): only TGF-β reproducibly elevated in CFS → weak/mixed peripheral cytokine
  signal in ME/CFS, consistent with Strawbridge's heterogeneity caveat.
- ME/CFS-specific neuroinflammation via TSPO-PET: Nakatomi 2014 (in bib) is the primary evidence;
  replication is limited — noted as the null/under-replicated angle.

## Cohort-Overlap Check (Document Instruction B)

- Kuring 2026 and Kuring 2023 are the SAME research group (Kuring/Mathias/Ward/Tachas, University
  of Adelaide) testing the SAME hypothesis from two disease angles. They are NOT overlapping-cohort
  re-analyses — 2023 is mental-illness inflammatory meta, 2026 is dementia inflammatory meta. They are
  **methodologically parallel companion papers, not duplicated evidence**. Treat as 2 independent
  evidence sources for Phase 2 weighting, but note same-group provenance in the claim-fidelity check.
- Strawbridge 2019 independent group (King's College London). No overlap.

## Study-Date Anchoring (Document Instruction D)

- Kuring 2026 final search 2024-10-01; dementia diagnosed per published clinical criteria (DSM
  era-dependent, mostly post-1990). The dementia population is clinical, not biomarker-confirmed.
- Strawbridge 2019, Kuring 2023 use per-study case definitions (heterogeneous ME/CFS diagnostic
  criteria, mostly Fukuda era) — diagnostic-era heterogeneity noted.
