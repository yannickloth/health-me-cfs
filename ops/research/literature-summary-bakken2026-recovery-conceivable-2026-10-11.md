# Literature Summary: Bakken 2026 — Recovery Conceivable (HCP BPS Perspectives)

**Topic slug:** `bakken2026-recovery-conceivable`
**Date:** 2026-10-11
**Question:** What does the evidence show about the value *and* the harm of psychologically/behaviourally informed (BPS) approaches to CFS recovery, the "recovery conceivable" framing, and the epistemic-trust/alliance mechanism that Bakken et al. (2026) identify as the operative ingredient?

## Headline finding

Bakken et al. (2026) is a **qualitative HCP-perspective study, not an efficacy study** — it documents *how* HCPs who already endorse BPS approaches operationalise a recovery "map", not *whether* it works. The surrounding evidence splits cleanly along the four rival framings:

1. **Efficacy is modest and bounded (F1).** The best recent synthesis (Ingman 2022) finds ~44% "better" and ~11% "worse" after CBT, ~43%/14% after GET; Knoop 2007 puts full recovery at 23%. So "recovery conceivable" is true, but full recovery is a minority outcome.
2. **Harm is documented (F2).** Geraghty & Blease 2019 enumerates seven modalities of potential iatrogenesis under the BPS frame; Thoma 2023 argues psychosomatic attribution is inconsistent with evidence and harmful.
3. **The biomedical counter-narrative is historically and clinically grounded (F3).** Thoma 2023 (organic/ICD status), Devendorf 2019 (physicians define recovery as full remission — a high bar the BPS "map" rarely meets), and Cross 2026 (uncertainty is sociohistorically *produced*).
4. **The alliance/trust mechanism is the study's most defensible contribution (F4).** Flückiger 2018 (alliance r≈.28, >30,000 patients) and Heins 2013 (alliance explains ~25% of post-CBT fatigue variance in CFS) give the epistemic-trust reading empirical backing that the BPS *content* reading lacks.

## Evidence landscape

### Strong (discounted certainty ≥ 0.50)
- **Ingman2022RecoveryRCTReview** (0.60) — systematic review, 1990 participants; the current CBT/GET prognosis evidence.
- **Heins2013TherapeuticRelationship** (0.55) — n=217 CFS; alliance/expectations predict CBT outcome (the only CFS-specific alliance evidence).
- **Fluckiger2018AllianceMeta** (0.53 discounted, raw 0.70) — meta-analysis, >30,000 patients; alliance robustly predicts outcome (general population, discounted).
- **Knoop2007FullRecoveryCBT** (0.50) — n=96; 23% full recovery after CBT.
- **Geraghty2019BPSHarm** (0.50) — seven harm modalities under BPS.
- **Thoma2023PsychosomaticHarmful** (0.50) — psychosomatic attribution inconsistent/harmful.

### Weak (discounted < 0.50)
- **Ingman2025RecoveryThematic** (0.40) — patient-side recovery (qualitative).
- **Devendorf2019RecoveryPhysician** (0.40) — n=10 physicians; recovery = full remission.
- **Bakken2026RecoveryConceivable** (0.35) — primary; qualitative, purposively biased sample.
- **Cross2026BiomedicalUncertainty** (0.30) — historical analysis (population 0.75).
- **Fisher2025EpistemicTrustPanoramic** (0.30) — epistemic trust still under-validated.
- **Bordin1979WorkingAlliance** (0.26), **Fonagy2014EpistemicTrust** (0.26) — foundational theory, general population.

### Null / negative
- No direct null trial is added (the topic is not a treatment-efficacy question); the negative signal is carried by the *harm* strand (F2) and by Ingman 2022's "11–14% worse" tail, which must be held alongside the "44% better" head.

### Missing
- No RCT/cohort directly testing whether the *epistemic-trust/alliance* component — as opposed to BPS content — drives CFS recovery (Heins 2013 is the closest proxy).
- No patient-side study of non-improvers/deterioration under BPS approaches (Bakken 2026 itself flags this gap).
- No trial-registry check performed (declared gap).

### Indirect biochemical/systemic links (MANDATORY column)
This is a non-mechanistic, non-treatment topic (sociology/nosology/psychologisation). There is **no biochemical pathway** to map; the "indirect link" analogue is the *mechanism-of-the-mechanism*: BPS/psychobehavioural approaches claim to act on (a) stress/autonomic regulation and (b) maladaptive-learning/predictive-processing. Neither claim is validated as the causal driver of CFS in this search — the predictive-processing account (Kube 2020; Van den Bergh 2017; Henningsen 2018b, all cited by Bakken but not added here as they fall outside the 5–15 cap) is a model, not an established CFS mechanism. Recorded as: **no indirect biochemical link established; the "map" is orientation-for-change, not a validated causal model** (Bakken 2026's own characterisation).

## Contradictions detected

- **F1 vs F2 (same intervention, opposite valences).** Ingman 2022 / Knoop 2007 (some patients improve) vs Geraghty 2019 / Thoma 2023 (some patients harmed). **Resolvable in principle:** they measure different tails of the same distribution — but the F2 papers argue the harm is *intrinsic to the framing*, not just a poor-outcome tail. This is the central tension the integration must surface, not dissolve.
- **F1 vs F3 (attribution).** BPS "map" (recovery via relearning/regulation) vs biomedical (organic disease; psychosomatic attribution harmful). **Fundamental at the level of causal attribution** — same population, opposite explanatory models. Per the band rule, the supporting papers are qualitatively similar in certainty (0.50–0.60), so Phase 2 should present both sides rather than weight one over the other.
- **F4 vs F1/F3 (where the contribution lives).** The alliance/trust evidence (Heins 2013, Flückiger 2018) supports *relational* factors independent of BPS *content* — implying the paper's defensible claim is the trust mechanism, not the psychobehavioural model itself. This is a framing decision for the main session, not a data contradiction.

## Clinical relevance

- **Direct patient-care implication:** LOW-to-MEDIUM. The topic is interpretive/sociological, not a treatment claim. The actionable residue is: (a) the therapeutic relationship and *embodied* improvement (not explanation alone) are what make change credible — consistent with the healing-norms stance that validation precedes any reappraisal; (b) BPS "maps" carry a documented harm risk when they override patient narrative (Geraghty 2019).
- **Severity applicability:** unknown — none of the qualitative/HCP studies stratify by severity; Ingman 2022 explicitly notes findings may not generalise to severe CFS.
- **Healing relevance (KPI):** `pacing·management` — bears on how any psychobehavioural "recovery" framing is delivered versus a pacing/energy-management stance, and on trust-building in the clinical encounter.
