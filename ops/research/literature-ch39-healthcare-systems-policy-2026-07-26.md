## Key Themes and Gaps

### 1. Medical Education Deficit

The literature consistently documents that ME/CFS is severely undertaught in medical education:
- **Muirhead2021**: 41% of UK medical schools do not teach ME/CFS at all; of those that do, NO school shared their syllabus — teaching content is unknown. Two-thirds of respondents wanted better teaching aids. No CME requirement exists for ME/CFS.
- **Bateman2021Mayo**: "Many clinicians lack the knowledge to appropriately diagnose or manage ME/CFS." Up to 91% of U.S. patients remain undiagnosed.
- **Hussein2024barriers**: "Inadequate or inconsistent healthcare provider education and training on ME" identified as a core barrier category in Canada.
- **Policy gap**: No national medical education standard for ME/CFS exists in any country. No CME requirement.

### 2. Diagnostic Delay and Healthcare Access Disparities

- **Samms2025unequalaccess** (already in epidemiology.bib): N=100,055; White patients ~5× more likely diagnosed than ethnic minorities; 10× variation across English ICBs. Estimated lifetime prevalence 0.6% UK-wide.
- **Dimmock2024disparities** (already in general.bib): U.S. racial disparities documented; CDC NHIS data analysis.
- **Hussein2024barriers**: Consistent Canadian evidence of access barriers but evidence base is sparse (21 eligible records only).
- **Cornelis2026KCE420/420S** (already in general.bib): Belgian HTA; N=749 survey + 19 interviews; patient-reported needs analysis.
- **Policy gap**: No research on interventions to reduce diagnostic inequality; no equity-focused implementation studies.

### 3. Guideline Inconsistency

- **Kingdon2022NICEprimary**: NICE NG206 (2021) marked a paradigm shift (no GET; PEM-centered; diagnosis at 3 months). But implementation in primary care is unmonitored.
- **Smith2014AHRQ**: Pre-IOM systematic review documenting heterogeneity across 8 case definitions. Oxford criteria (least restrictive) inflates heterogeneity. Treatment evidence rated low-to-moderate. Foundational but now dated.
- **Existing** — NICE2021mecfs, Nacul2021EUROMENE, DEGAM2022fatigue, GBA2023LongCOVRL: Multiple national/regional guidelines exist but diverge significantly on GET, CBT, diagnostic latency.
- **Policy gap**: No systematic comparison of guideline impact on patient outcomes. NICE 2021 is the most evidence-based but uptake by clinicians is unknown.

### 4. Disability Systems

- **Podell2020**: 75% of ME/CFS patients unable to work. Practice guide for U.S. disability claims. Key: PEM latency undermines standard functional capacity evaluations; 2-day CPET and objective impairment evidence are critical but underused.
- **Policy gap**: No cross-national comparison of disability determination for ME/CFS; no studies of disability appeal outcomes; no research on employment retention interventions.

### 5. Healthcare Invalidation and Stigma

- **Bontempo2025invalidation**: Highest-quality evidence in this domain. Meta-synthesis of 151 qualitative reports across 11 contested illnesses. Four classes of consequences: emotional states, healthcare trauma, system avoidance, diagnostic delay. Establishes a mechanistic model of how invalidation causes harm.
- **Chu2021suicidal**: Elevated suicide risk in ME/CFS. Psychosomatic misattribution history creates barriers to mental health engagement.
- **Fennell2021suffering**: Theoretical framework applied to severe ME/CFS. Healthcare-engendered trauma is a recognized consequence.
- **Policy gap**: No intervention studies testing de-stigmatization strategies in healthcare settings.

### 6. Specialist vs Primary Care Models

- **Bateman2021Mayo** outlines what specialist clinicians recommend but is consensus-based, not comparative.
- **Parslow2017paediatric**: Specialist services identify four domains for pediatric assessment. But no comparison with primary care management.
- **Thornton2025energy**: Co-production identifies patient preferences (multidisciplinary teams, mHealth), but n=8.
- **Critical gap**: Zero RCTs or controlled comparisons of specialist ME/CFS clinic models vs usual primary care. No evidence on whether specialist clinics improve outcomes, reduce diagnostic delay, or are cost-effective. This is the single largest evidence gap in the domain.

### 7. Pediatric Health Services

- **Parslow2017children**: Meta-ethnography of children's experiences. Biographical disruption profound. Need for health-education communication.
- **Parslow2017paediatric**: Health professionals identify child-specific outcome domains. Call for child-specific PROMs.
- **Policy gap**: Pediatric-to-adult transition services not studied; school accommodation research absent.
