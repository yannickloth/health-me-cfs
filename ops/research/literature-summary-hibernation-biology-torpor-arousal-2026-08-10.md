## Summary

Twelve papers were identified and evaluated. The corpus spans four functional domains: (1) arousal-initiation mechanisms, (2) energy-metabolic signaling (AMPK/mTOR), (3) thermogenic effector pathways (BAT UCP1), and (4) translational/cross-species evidence. All are model-system/zoology studies with no ME/CFS cohorts — population-relevance discount is substantial (weight 0.45–0.55, producing discounted certainty of 0.31–0.47).

## Thematic Synthesis

### Arousal Initiation: Non-Neuronal Metabolic Sensing
The dominant recent finding is that spontaneous arousal from torpor is NOT initiated by the hypothalamic thermoregulatory centers (dorsomedial hypothalamus, preoptic area). Instead, the choroid plexus, tanycytes, and pars tuberalis — brain-barrier structures in contact with blood and CSF — show peak activation (c-fos) at arousal initiation (Markussen 2024). This suggests a metabolic-feedback model: circulating factors (lipids, adenosine metabolites) signal the need for arousal, and the hypothalamus executes (not initiates) the rewarming. This is complementary to, but mechanistically distinct from, the EP3R preoptic "two-way switch" for fever/torpor (Machado 2025, already in bib).

### Arachidonic Acid as Arousal Lipid Signal
The Wang 2026 T2T genome study identifies arachidonic acid (20:4 n-6) as a key regulator of hepatic lipid metabolism during hibernation, acting through PPARα and TRPV-Ca2+ signalling. This directly implicates the eicosanoid/prostaglandin cascade in the arousal-lipid signalling pathway. González-Riaño 2025 shows complementary brain lipidome shifts: decreased phosphatidic acid with increased DAG (PKC activation) and elevated methylated LPA during torpor. Together, these suggest an arachidonic-acid → prostaglandin → cAMP/PKC → arousal signalling axis.

### Adenosine A1 Receptor: Conserved Torpor-Entry Signal
Shimaoka 2018 demonstrates that central A1AR activation (icv CHA) induces deep hypothermia with maintained cardiac rhythm in non-hibernating rats. This is the strongest translational bridge: the A1 receptor is a conserved torpor-entry signal across species. Uchino 2024 (identified in search, not included as it is partially redundant with the existing Machado 2025) extends this by showing A1-agonist-activated neurons overlap with torpor-tagged preoptic neurons. The adenosine A1→A2A shift hypothesis (A1 downregulation permits arousal, A2A upregulation drives microglial activation) is indirectly supported but no single paper tests it directly in a hibernation context.

### mTORC1: Conserved Metabolic Depression/Reactivation Switch
Wu & Storey 2021 establish mTORC1 suppression as a conserved feature of metabolic depression across hibernation, estivation, anoxia tolerance, and dauer diapause. mTOR reactivation accompanies arousal. Jobava 2021 demonstrates that non-hibernating mammalian cells possess a latent "adaptive pausing response" (APR) — a hibernation-like translational shutdown with mRNA poised at initiation codons, reversible upon ISR signalling. Dias 2021 connects this to stem cell quiescence, identifying HIF-1α/mTOR/autophagy as a convergent dormancy-regulatory axis.

### BAT UCP1: Primary Thermogenic Effector of Arousal
Hunstiger 2023 provides detailed seasonal protein-expression data: BAT UCP1 peaks in winter (interbout arousal period) and tapers after terminal arousal. Muscular NST (SERCA, sarcolipin) shows the opposite temporal pattern — it is a post-hibernation recovery mechanism, not the arousal driver. This clarifies the thermogenic hierarchy: BAT → interbout arousal; muscle NST → post-hibernation reactivation.

### Primate Translation: Dwarf Lemur Model
Blanco 2024 reviews the Duke Lemur Center's achievement of induced hibernation in captive dwarf lemurs (Cheirogaleus spp.) — the only obligate primate hibernators. This is the closest available translational model for human torpor biology. The ability to experimentally manipulate torpor depth (via temperature and food availability) provides a primate-specific platform for testing arousal-modulating interventions.

### Cross-Species Transcriptomic Resource
Fu 2021 provides 6,505 differentially expressed/processed transcripts across three brain regions in 13-lined ground squirrels, with deconvolution of seasonal vs. temperature-dependent effects. This is the primary dataset for benchmarking ME/CFS brain expression patterns against a natural hypometabolic state.

### Caution: Arousal Oxidative Cost
Duffy 2022 demonstrates that interbout arousal increases oxidative damage in ground squirrel tissues. This is critical balance evidence: arousal is costly, and hibernators have evolved protective adaptations (antioxidant upregulation) that ME/CFS patients may lack. Pharmacological arousal without concurrent antioxidant protection could be harmful.

## Highest-Value Papers for Integration

1. **Shimaoka 2018** (certainty 0.40): Strongest translational bridge — A1AR activation induces torpor-like state in non-hibernator.
2. **Jobava 2021** (certainty 0.47): Cellular mechanism for hibernation-like state in mammalian cells — closest to human biology.
3. **Wang 2026** (certainty 0.35): T2T genome + multi-omics validation of arachidonic acid signalling — most comprehensive molecular dataset.
