# Literature Summary: Skeletal Muscle Oxygenation & Microvascular Dysfunction in Long COVID and ME/CFS

**Topic slug:** `slaghekke2026-muscle-microvascular`
**Source:** Slaghekke A et al., 2026 AMS Conference Abstract
**Full paper:** Charlton BT et al., 2025 medRxiv preprint (same group, same data)
**Date:** 2026-07-26

---

## 1. Index Paper: Slaghekke/Charlton et al. 2025/2026

### Study Design
Maximal cycling test + VO₂max + NIRS muscle oxygenation + vastus lateralis biopsy with IHC + EM. Unique feature: comparator group of healthy volunteers after strict 60-day bed rest — tests the deconditioning hypothesis head-to-head against the microvascular pathology hypothesis.

### Samples
| Group | n | Notes |
|-------|---|-------|
| Long COVID (LC) | 24 | Post-SARS-CoV-2; fatigue + PEM |
| ME/CFS | 26 | Pre-2020 dx (no pandemic confound) |
| Healthy controls (HC) | 30 | Age/sex matched |
| Bed rest | 10 | Healthy volunteers after strict 60d bed rest |

### Key Findings
1. **Lower VO₂max (p<0.0001) and peak power output (p<0.0001)** in both LC and ME/CFS vs HC
2. **Lower tissue O₂ uptake (p=0.001) and vasodilatory capacity (p=0.011)** in both patient groups (NIRS)
3. **Lower capillarization only in ME/CFS (p<0.0005)** — Long COVID capillarization was preserved
4. **Both groups: decreased capillary contact lengths (p<0.0001), decreased capillary tortuosity (p<0.0001)**
5. **Substantial capillary basement membrane thickening (p<0.0001)** — cutoff observed at ~62.7% in HC vs ~63.2%+ in patients
6. **EM confirmed:** BM thickening, microvacuolization, endothelial hypertrophy, degeneration signs
7. **Bed rest comparator:** Bed rest produced muscle atrophy + reduced OXPHOS (expected detraining). Patients: no atrophy, but less capillaries + more glycolytic fibers — neither associated with VO₂max. Bed rest altered CV/respiratory response at both submaximal AND maximal exercise; patients showed alterations only at submaximal.

### Central Argument
Physical inactivity alone does not explain lower exercise capacity in Long COVID and ME/CFS. Microvascular pathology — particularly capillary BM thickening, reduced tortuosity, and reduced contact length — is a primary contributor to impaired peripheral oxygen extraction.

## 2. Corroborating Evidence

### Direct Replication — Capillary BM Thickening

**Aschman et al. 2023** (Acta Neuropathologica Communications, n=11 PCS, Charité Berlin):
- Fewer capillaries, thicker capillary basement membranes, increased CD169+ macrophages
- SARS-CoV-2 RNA **NOT** detected in muscle → argues against viral persistence as direct cause
- Complement proteins elevated in serum, matching transcriptomic signature in muscle
- Proposes immune-mediated structural changes of microvasculature explain exercise-dependent fatigue
- **Key:** Independent group (Berlin vs Amsterdam) with same core finding — capillary BM thickening in post-viral fatigue

**Agergaard et al. 2023** (Clinical Neurophysiology, n=18 Long COVID, Aarhus):
- Reduced capillary-to-fiber ratio (p=0.009) and increased capillary BM thickness
- Mitochondrial changes + terminal nerve/motor endplate damage + ACh receptor loss
- 73% of patients had myopathic EMG findings
- **Key:** Capillary injury documented as *separate* from mitochondrial pathology — two parallel pathological processes

### Functional Confirmation — Impaired O₂ Extraction

**Joseph et al. 2022** (Chest, n=45 ME/CFS + 18 HC, Brigham):
- **Invasive CPET with arterial/venous catheters** — gold-standard exercise hemodynamics
- Reduced peak systemic O₂ extraction (0.69 vs 0.77, p<0.001) — normal cardiac output, normal pulmonary gas exchange
- **Problem is peripheral extraction, not delivery**
- Pyridostigmine increased peak VO₂ by +0.9 mL/kg/min (p=0.002) — neurovascular dysregulation confirmation

**Squires et al. 2026** (ATS 2026 abstract, n=35 ME/CFS + 10 LC, Brigham/Harvard):
- iCPET + frozen vastus lateralis biopsy
- Median CS activity = 66% of reference; mtDNA = 55.5% of reference
- Lower Complex II+III activity correlated with worse systemic O₂ extraction (ρ = −0.33, p = 0.028)
- CS activity and mtDNA did NOT correlate with SOE → functional, not quantitative, mitochondrial defect
- **Bridging paper:** Connects the capillary-level O₂ *delivery* problem (Charlton, Aschman) to downstream O₂ *utilization* defect

### Meta-Analytic Confirmation — Reproducibility

**Franklin & Graham 2022** (Fatigue: Biomedicine, n=5 studies pooled):
- Systematic review + meta-analysis of 2-day CPET studies
- Clinically significant test-retest reduction in work rate at AT (-21W, 95% CI -38 to -4)
- 78% probability future study would show clinically significant AT decline
- Confirms objective, reproducible exercise impairment consistent with capillary-level O₂ delivery problem

## 3. Mechanistic Context

### Capillary BM Thickening → Exercise Intolerance Pathway

```
Capillary BM thickening (Aschman 2023, Charlton 2025)
  → Increased O₂ diffusion distance from capillary to mitochondria
  → Reduced capillary tortuosity (Charlton 2025) → reduced capillary surface area
  → Decreased capillary contact length (Charlton 2025) → reduced exchange surface
  → Impaired peripheral O₂ extraction (Joseph 2022, Squires 2026)
  → Functional mitochondrial impairment (Complex II+III ↓) (Squires 2026)
  → Reduced VO₂max (Charlton 2025, Keller 2024, Franklin 2022)
  → Exercise intolerance + PEM
```

### The Deconditioning Counter-Argument — Rebutted

The Charlton/Slaghekke paper directly addresses the claim that exercise intolerance reflects deconditioning:
- **If deconditioning only:** Muscle atrophy + reduced OXPHOS (bed rest pattern)
- **If disease pathology:** Capillary BM thickening, reduced tortuosity, glycolytic shift WITHOUT atrophy
- The bed rest comparator produced the deconditioning phenotype
- Patients produced the microvascular pathology phenotype
- **These are different patterns** → deconditioning alone is excluded as the explanation

### Connective Tissue Hypothesis

**Wirth 2026** (Preprints, hypothesis paper):
- Proposes capillary BM thickening in ME/CFS may be mechanistically linked to connective tissue laxity/hypermobility
- Hyaluronan overproduction → CD44/TSG-6 pathway → excess ECM → capillary BM thickening
- Impaired pericyte recruitment contributes to microvascular pathology
- Provides testable biochemical pathway — needs validation

## 4. Contradictions and Discordant Findings

### Capillarization: ME/CFS vs Long COVID

| Study | ME/CFS capillarization | Long COVID capillarization |
|-------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Charlton/Slaghekke 2025 | Reduced (p<0.0005) | Preserved (NS) |
| Agergaard 2023 | — | Reduced (p=0.009) |
| Aschman 2023 | — | Reduced (vs historical controls) |

**Assessment:** Charlton finding of preserved Long COVID capillarization is contradicted by both Agergaard and Aschman. Possible explanations:
1. **Different muscles biopsied:** Agergaard biopsied biceps brachii + anterior tibialis; Charlton biopsied vastus lateralis — fiber type differences
2. **Disease duration/time from infection:** Charlton's LC patients may be earlier in disease course; Agergaard/Aschman biopsies may have been done at different time points
3. **Different criteria:** Agergaard/Aschman didn't use formal ME/CFS diagnostic criteria for their PCS patients
4. **Replication limitation:** Charlton is a preprint; peer review may address this

**Consensus finding** (all three studies agree): **Capillary BM thickening is present and significant** — this is the more reliable and replicated finding.

### Mitochondrial vs Vascular Primacy

- **Scheibenbogen & Wirth 2024/2025** (J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle) proposes: microvascular disturbance comes first, mitochondrial damage follows → temporal progression
- **Bizjak et al. 2024** (IJMS): ME/CFS-specific mitochondrial morphology (subsarcolemmal damage) that differs from post-COVID pattern; this is an independent pathological process from capillary changes
- **Squires 2026:** Functional (not biomass) mitochondrial defect correlates with O₂ extraction — the functional impairment may be *secondary* to reduced O₂ delivery

**Assessment:** The evidence is converging on a dual-process model: capillary-level O₂ delivery impairment + downstream functional mitochondrial impairment. They are not mutually exclusive; the temporal sequence (microvascular → mitochondrial) is plausible but not proven.

## 5. Null / Negative Evidence

### Normal Muscle Biopsies in ME/CFS — Limited

- **Pietrangelo et al. 2018** (Eur J Transl Myology) — "Old muscle in young body" — found altered fiber phenotype and oxidative balance, but did not specifically assess capillary BM thickness. Not a null finding per se, but not confirmatory of microvascular findings either.
- **Bizjak et al. 2024** found "relatively normal mitochondrial structure" in ME/CFS overall (specific SS damage pattern present but total mitochondrial content not different from controls). However, they did not assess capillary morphology.
- **Gusnanto et al. 2020** (medRxiv) — cytokine profiles predict muscle function but reported "normal" blood count and questionnaire scores; no biopsy EM/IHC.

**Gap:** No paper explicitly reporting "normal capillary BM thickness" in ME/CFS. The null hypothesis is untested — we have only positive findings, which could reflect publication bias.

## 8. Key Gaps

1. **No direct ME/CFS study of capillary BM thickening without Long COVID comparator:** Charlton's is the first ME/CFS-specific biopsy for capillary morphology. Need independent replication.
2. **No longitudinal biopsy data:** Cross-sectional — cannot establish whether capillary BM thickening precedes or follows mitochondrial dysfunction.
3. **Mechanism of BM thickening unknown:** Is it immune-mediated (complement, macrophages — Aschman), HA-driven (Wirth), or something else? No interventional studies.
4. **Severity stratification:** None of the biopsy studies stratified by ME/CFS severity — severe/bedbound patients not represented.
5. **Biomarker potential untested:** Capillary BM thickness requires biopsy — cannot be a routine diagnostic. Need to identify serum/imaging surrogates.
6. **No pediatric data:** All studies in adults.
7. **Publication bias risk:** No paper reports normal capillary BM thickness in ME/CFS — could reflect publication bias or genuine pathology.
8. **Charlton/Slaghekke paper is a preprint:** Not yet peer-reviewed. Findings are provisional until journal acceptance. The AMS 2026 abstract is a conference presentation of the same data in abridged form.

---

## 9. Summary Statement

The hypothesis that skeletal muscle microvascular dysfunction — specifically capillary basement membrane thickening, reduced capillary tortuosity, and decreased capillary contact length — impairs peripheral oxygen extraction and contributes to exercise intolerance in Long COVID and ME/CFS is supported by:

- **3 independent biopsy cohorts** across 3 countries with consistent core finding (BM thickening)
- **1 gold-standard invasive CPET study** confirming peripheral O₂ extraction impairment
- **1 meta-analysis** confirming the exercise limitation is objective and reproducible
- **1 direct deconditioning comparator** (bed rest cohort) definitively excluding inactivity as the sole explanation

The capillary BM thickening finding is independently replicated. The functional consequence — impaired peripheral O₂ extraction — is independently demonstrated. A mechanistic pathway from capillary pathology → O₂ delivery impairment → functional mitochondrial defect → exercise intolerance is supported but not yet causally proven.

**Strength:** Convergent evidence from multiple independent groups using different methodologies. **Weakness:** No longitudinal or interventional data. Core paper (Charlton 2025) is a preprint.

**Integration recommendation:** PROCEED — evidence supports capillary/microvascular pathology as a primary contributor to exercise intolerance. The deconditioning counter-argument is directly addressed by the bed rest comparator. Integrate as a mechanistic hypothesis with the caveat that Charlton/Slaghekke is a preprint awaiting peer review.
