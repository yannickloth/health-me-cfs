## Inclusion Criteria
- Peer-reviewed primary research or review
- Direct relevance to ME/CFS, chronic fatigue, or energy depletion
- Social cognition, social withdrawal, social behavior, irritability, or conservation-withdrawal

## Exclusion Criteria
- Articles about other diseases (MS, cancer, depression, schizophrenia, COPD, aHUS)
- Infant/child development studies (for conservation-withdrawal — pediatric focus, not chronic illness)
- Scoping review protocols (no results)
- Articles about internet usage behavior (not social interaction mechanisms)

## Conclusion
No new papers found beyond the 13 already added to general.bib and the existing bibliography entries. This is a genuine research gap — social cognition, social withdrawal, emotional synchrony, and psychosocial consequences of energy depletion are understudied in ME/CFS specifically. The existing evidence base consists of:
- Sleep-deprivation studies in healthy populations (generalizable to fatigue/energy depletion)
- Sickness-behavior framework (evolutionary/conceptual, well-established)
- Ego depletion / self-control literature (general psychology, replication-caveated)
- Emotional suppression in ME/CFS (Rimes 2016 — one direct study)
- One recent social-interaction-in-ME/CFS search hit (Shortland 2024 — protocol only, no results published)

The gap is itself a finding: the blog article's claim that "no published study has directly measured emotional synchrony or emotional contagion in ME/CFS" is confirmed by this search.
