## E1 — Circadian timing of antihistamine/mast-cell drugs

**Verdict: SUPPORTS (mechanistic, no direct ME/CFS data).**

Nocturnal histamine/leukotriene/mast-cell peaks are well documented, and chronotherapy of antihistamines + leukotriene antagonists is an established principle. Montelukast PM dosing is a label recommendation with a mechanistically sound basis (nocturnal cysteinyl-leukotriene peak), but no head-to-head AM-vs-PM efficacy trial was found. No ME/CFS-specific circadian-timing study exists.

## E2 — Cholinergic reserve / pyridostigmine therapeutic window

**Verdict: SUPPORTS (direct ME/CFS signal present).**

The pyridostigmine benefit/side-effect gap is real and dose-limited by muscarinic side effects across POTS, orthostatic-hypotension, and now a direct ME/CFS pilot (Schlömer 2025). This supports a "cholinergic reserve index" concept. The strongest discounted certainty comes from the direct ME/CFS study (0.55).

## E3 — Antihistamine tachyphylaxis / tolerance / rebound

**Verdict: SUPPORTS (tolerance + rebound documented; H1 upregulation is the molecular substrate).**

Tolerance to H1-antihistamine sedative effects and rebound pruritus/urticaria on chronic-antihistamine discontinuation are documented. Receptor upregulation (H1 and H2, via inverse agonism) provides the molecular mechanism. No ME/CFS-specific data.

## E4 — Menstrual-cycle / estrogen mast-cell timing

**Verdict: SUPPORTS (mechanistic; no direct cycle-timing clinical trial).**

Estrogen drives mast-cell degranulation/histamine release (in vitro Zaitsu 2007; in vivo Xu 2020), and sex-hormone modulation of mast-cell-mediated disease is clinically recognised (urticaria premenstrual worsening). **No direct study of cycle-synchronised MCAS dosing exists** (`mast cell activation syndrome menstruation flare` = 0 hits).

## F1 — Famotidine gastric pH → microbiome → histamine (HARM) **[HARM HYPOTHESIS]**

**Verdict: PARTIAL / mechanistically plausible, clinically UNSUBSTANTIATED.**

Three links each have evidence — (a) acid suppression alters gut flora (PPI strong; H2RA noted but weaker), (b) gut bacteria produce histamine, (c) microbial histamine drives symptoms — but the full chain *famotidine → histamine-producing overgrowth → increased histamine load → harm* has **no direct demonstration**. No famotidine-specific microbiome→histamine study exists (`famotidine mast cell adverse degranulation` = 0 hits).

## F2 — Compensatory mediator synthesis on receptor blockade (HARM) **[HARM HYPOTHESIS]**

**Verdict: SUPPORTS mechanistically (classic in vitro evidence), clinically UNSUBSTANTIATED.**

H2-receptor negative feedback on mast-cell/basophil histamine release is a classic, well-established in vitro mechanism. H2 antihistamines demonstrably *augment* antigen-induced histamine release from human basophils in vitro (Tung 1982). **No human/clinical study demonstrates clinically significant compensatory mediator synthesis on famotidine/H2 blockade.**

Frame as a mechanistically grounded, unproven safety concern — not an established adverse effect.

1. **E2 is the strongest idea** — it has a direct ME/CFS pilot (Schlömer 2025) and consistent dose-limited cholinergic side-effect evidence; the "cholinergic reserve index" is the most supportable construct in this workstream.
