# Literature Summary: Sleep, Mindfulness, and Resting in ME/CFS (2026-10-10)

**Topic slug:** `sleep-mindfulness-resting`
**Phase 1 output** — 13 papers (3 sleep.bib, 10 treatments.bib)

---

## Evidence landscape

### Strong (certainty >= 0.60, discounted >= 0.50)

- @Schmidt2011MBSRFibromyalgia (0.75 raw / 0.60 disc) — top-tier 3-arm RCT, n=177; mindfulness did NOT beat active control on the primary HRQoL endpoint (null-on-primary), but MBSR arm improved 6/8 secondary outcomes. The cleanest high-quality signal: mindfulness has narrow (secondary) efficacy.
- @Chao2021CBTIGulfWar (0.70 / 0.56) — RCT, n=85; CBT-I improved both sleep and non-sleep fatigue/cognitive symptoms in Gulf War Illness (chronic multisymptom illness), maintained 6 months. The strongest behavioural-sleep-intervention evidence for a ME/CFS-adjacent illness.
- @Meng2024MindfulnessFM (0.65 / 0.52) — meta-analysis, 10 RCTs n=818; mindfulness reduces FIQ/PSQI/BDI/PSS short-term but FIQ and PSQI benefit attenuates (non-significant) medium-term.
- @Thapaliya2026GlymphaticMECFS (0.55 / 0.55) — first direct DTI-ALPS study in ME/CFS; glymphatic index reduced and correlates with sleep disturbance (r=-0.47) and concentration (r=-0.43). Provides a biological substrate for unrefreshing sleep.

### Weak (certainty < 0.40)

- @Xie2024QigongCFSProtocol (0.35) — protocol only, no results.
- @Porter2022MindfulnessLongCOVID (0.45 / 0.38) — narrative review, no new data.

### Null / negative

- @Carta2024HRVBFFibromyalgia — HRV-BF produced NO between-group difference in any outcome (fibromyalgia phase II RCT). Direct null.
- @Schmidt2011MBSRFibromyalgia — null on primary HRQoL outcome.
- @Mariman2013SleepCFS — no objective PSG correlate of unrefreshing sleep; insufficient evidence that treating sleep disorders improves CFS fatigue.
- @Kos2015ActivityPacing — pacing showed no advantage on the primary participation endpoint (only Satisfaction favored pacing, ES 0.74).
- @Meeus2010PainPhysiologyEducation — pacing education was the weaker comparator vs pain-physiology education.

### Missing

- No direct ME/CFS mindfulness RCT with a PEM or energy-envelope primary endpoint exists (the gap that framings F3/F4 point at).
- No direct ME/CFS CBT-I trial; the behavioural-sleep-intervention evidence is cross-disease (Gulf War Illness).
- No DMN/neuroimaging meditation-mechanism study in ME/CFS.

### Indirect biochemical / systemic links

- Sleep -> glymphatic clearance -> ME/CFS: @Thapaliya2026GlymphaticMECFS (direct DTI-ALPS). Cross-links to the existing glymphatic stream in brain-clearance.bib (Wostyn2018GlymphaticCFS, Tu2023DTIALPSFibromyalgia, Tang2025COVIDSleepDTIALPS).
- Meditation/slow-breathing -> parasympathetic/vagal tone -> HRV: @Windthorst2017HRVBFCFS, @Cossu2025HRVBFLongCOVID, @Carta2024HRVBFFibromyalgia (null). Cross-links to existing autonomic-cardiovascular.bib HRV and central-noradrenergic streams.

## What the evidence establishes

1. **Unrefreshing sleep is disease-driven, not primary insomnia.** Mariman 2013 (review) documents the subjective-objective discrepancy — no PSG substrate. Thapaliya 2026 now supplies a candidate biological substrate: impaired glymphatic clearance during sleep. Together these support framing F2: behavioural sleep hygiene targets a secondary symptom, not the disease process.
2. **Behavioural sleep intervention (CBT-I) can still help fatigue/cognitive symptoms** in a closely-related multisymptom illness (Chao 2021, Gulf War Illness) — the F1 counterweight. Whether this transfers to ME/CFS is untested.
3. **Mindfulness has narrow, secondary-outcome efficacy.** The best evidence (Schmidt 2011 null-primary; Meng 2024 meta-analysis) shows benefit for sleep, distress, mood and quality of life, but no robust effect on the core fibromyalgia FIQ, and no PEM/energy-envelope endpoint has ever been tested. Framing F3 is best supported.
4. **Pacing is contested (F4).** Kos 2015 shows only partial pacing benefit (satisfaction, not performance); Meeus 2010 shows pacing education is weaker than pain-physiology education. These sit beside the already-integrated energy-envelope literature (Jason 2009/2012; O'Connor 2019) which supports pacing conceptually — the trial evidence for pacing-as-intervention is weaker than the theoretical case.

## Strongest supportable mechanistic claim

Impaired glymphatic clearance during sleep is a measurable correlate of unrefreshing sleep and cognitive impairment in ME/CFS (Thapaliya 2026), consistent with the subjective-objective sleep discrepancy (Mariman 2013). This is a correlation, not a demonstrated causal mechanism.

## What must remain speculative

- That meditation/vagal retraining reverses ME/CFS pathophysiology (no direct mechanism study; HRV-BF is null in fibromyalgia).
- That pacing "works" beyond symptom avoidance — the trial evidence is partial or null.
- That treating sleep improves the core ME/CFS fatigue (insufficient evidence; cross-disease only).

## Primary integration targets (suggested)

- ch17 non-pharmacological interventions (meditation, HRV-BF, pacing, rest, sleep)
- ch04/ch05 symptoms (unrefreshing sleep — glymphatic substrate, subjective-objective discrepancy)
- Part 2 mechanisms (glymphatic/sleep architecture) — cross-reference brain-clearance stream

## GET/BPS hygiene note

- @Windthorst2017HRVBFCFS uses graded exercise training as a COMPARATOR arm (German exploratory pilot), not as a BPS-promotion intervention. It is not part of the GET literature targeted for criticism.
- Pacing papers (@Kos2015ActivityPacing, @Meeus2010PainPhysiologyEducation) are activity-management/pacing interventions — correctly distinguished from graded-exercise therapy. PACE-trial adaptive-pacing is separate and already covered in the corpus.
