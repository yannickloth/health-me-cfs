## Paper 1: Gile et al. 2026

**Citation:** Gile B, Bulbule S, Toriola MA, et al. Association of rapamycin treatment with the modulation of purine metabolism, reduced microglial inflammatory responses, improved mitochondrial energy metabolism, and alleviation of fatigue symptoms in ME/CFS subjects: pilot findings from phase-II observational study. *J Transl Med*. 2026;24(1):921. PMID 42432754.

**Design:** Phase II multicenter observational pilot (n=86 enrolled; 70 completed day 36; 40 completed 90 days; 53% attrition). Simmaron/Bateman Horne/Mayo/CCD consortium. Compounded low-dose rapamycin. No placebo group. NCT06257420.

**Key findings:**
1. LCMS purine metabolomics: rapamycin differentially regulated IMP→XMP and IMP→hypoxanthine conversion — reduced flux through IMP dehydrogenase (IMPDH)
2. Flow cytometry confirmed IMPDH activity reduction in rapamycin-treated PBMCs
3. Purine supplementation induced mitochondrial oxidative stress in PBMCs; rapamycin partially mitigated this
4. Seahorse OCR: mitochondrial respiration (basal, ATP-linked, maximal, spare capacity) improved in responder PBMCs after 90 days
5. Reduced microglial inflammatory markers by flow cytometry
6. Clinical symptom improvement consistent with prior Ruan 2025 pilot (fatigue, PEM reduction)

**Novel contribution:** First mechanistic biomarker evidence beyond clinical outcomes. Establishes purine metabolism as a rapamycin-affected pathway in ME/CFS — mTORC1→IMPDH→purine biosynthesis→mitochondrial respiration→microglial inflammation axis.

**Quality assessment:**
- Raw certainty: 0.50 (sample size adequate n=86, peer-reviewed J Transl Med, unreplicated, uncontrolled design reduces certainty)
- Population weight: 1.00 (ME/CFS cohort)
- Discounted certainty: 0.50
- GRADE-equivalent: Low (uncontrolled observational; single consortium)

**Limitations:** Uncontrolled (no placebo); molecular results responder-biased; 53% attrition at 90 days; same consortium as Ruan2025/Drosen2025/Toriola2026 → potential correlated bias across all rapamycin/ATG13 papers from this group; no blinding.

## Paper 2: Fronticelli Baldelli and Buonsenso 2025

**Citation:** Fronticelli Baldelli G, Buonsenso D. Proposed Mechanistic Axis of Infections and mTOR Hyperactivation: A Multidisciplinary Review of Immune, Rheumatologic, and Psychiatric Links. *Children*. 2025;12(12):1603. PMID 41462744.

**Design:** Narrative review — clinical, translational, and experimental literature synthesis.

**Key findings:**
1. Proposes mTOR as the mechanistic link between peripheral infection-driven inflammation and CNS dysfunction
2. Stepwise cascade: infection→sustained mTOR activation→T-cell/macrophage pro-inflammatory skew→BBB compromise (endothelial mTOR weakens tight junctions, increases vesicular transport)→microglial/neuronal mTOR activation→neuroinflammation, impaired synaptic plasticity, neurotransmitter disruption
3. Framework spans Long COVID, ME/CFS, and PANS/PANDAS in children
4. pS6 as candidate mTOR pathway activation biomarker
5. Motivates mTOR-targeted interventions in pediatric post-infectious neuropsychiatric syndromes

**Novel contribution:** Extends mTOR hyperactivation framework to pediatrics. Reinforces infection→mTOR→BBB→neuroinflammation cascade from a pediatric standpoint.

**Quality assessment:**
- Raw certainty: 0.35 (review only, no primary data, single-proponent, MDPI Children journal)
- Population weight: 0.85 (includes ME/CFS in pediatric context but review only)
- Discounted certainty: 0.30
- GRADE-equivalent: Very Low (narrative review; no empirical data)

**Limitations:** Review only — no primary human data. Pediatric focus with adult ME/CFS inference. Many steps lack pediatric human evidence. Single-proponent model. Journal impact factor ~2.5.

## Indirect Biochemical Links

The Gile 2026 paper provides direct biochemical evidence for a pathway previously only inferred:

1. **mTORC1→IMPDH→purine metabolism→mitochondrial respiration:** mTORC1 is known to regulate IMPDH expression (Ben-Sahra 2013, 2016 — de novo purine synthesis via mTORC1→ATF4→MTHFD2). Gile 2026 provides the first demonstration of this axis in ME/CFS PBMCs with rapamycin intervention. The purine metabolism link was searched for in Phase 1 of the original mtor-autophagy cycle but no papers existed — this fills a gap.

2. **Purine-driven microglial inflammation:** Extracellular purines (ATP, ADP, adenosine) signal through purinergic receptors (P2X7, P2Y) on microglia. Purine accumulation from IMPDH overactivity could drive microglial P2X7→NLRP3 inflammasome activation — connecting to the existing NLRP3/PEM framework in the paper.

3. **Gile 2026 strengthens the mTOR chain:** The paper adds a specific biochemical node (IMPDH/purines) between mTORC1 activation and mitochondrial dysfunction, replacing a black-box arrow with a named enzymatic step.
