## Scope
Clinical-safety content. Documents the MEDICAL-EMERGENCY triggers ("go to ED now" red flags)
and safety-netting for severe/very-severe ME/CFS — when ME/CFS decompensates into a
different, dangerous disease. Eight clinical-safety domains (malignancy, refeeding, VTE,
sepsis, POTS/cardiac-syncope, focal-neurology, mortality, ED experience).

## Inclusion Criteria
- Peer-reviewed journal article (original study, systematic review, RCT, or evidence-based guideline review).
- Population: ME/CFS OR a population with a directly transferable risk profile for severe bedbound
  ME/CFS (anorexia nervosa restricted-intake for refeeding; prolonged immobility/catatonia for VTE +
  pressure-ulcer + aspiration; general primary care for serious-disease red flags).
- Addresses one of the eight clinical-safety domains (medical-emergency decompensation triggers,
  safety-netting, complication prevention).

## Exclusion Criteria
- Case reports of unrelated single pathologies (e.g., purple urine bag, ECMO pressure injuries).
- Population-specific surgical/ICU/oncologic papers with no transferable signal to bedbound chronic illness.
- XMRV retrovirus malignancy papers (contamination/de-discovery noise; not the differential question).
- Review/practice content in non-English languages not indexed with an abstract (Danish, Czech).
- Duplicates of papers already in the bib corpus (mortality: Roberts2016, McManimen2016mortality,
  McManimen2018SevereUnderResearch already present; POTS/cardiac: BouHolaigah1995NMH, Novak2026,
  Jason2024TiltTable, Razumovsky2003, Rowe2001 already present).
- Papers already in corpus (referenced, not re-added).

## Key negative / coverage gaps
1. **POTS → sudden cardiac death / cardiac-emergency in ME/CFS:** no ME/CFS-specific paper
   quantifies sudden-cardiac-death risk or defines when POTS collapse is a true cardiac emergency
   versus benign vasovagal/NMH. Domain largely covered by existing corpus entries
   (BouHolaigah1995, Novak2026, Jason2024, Razumovsky2003, Rowe2001). The cardiac-vs-neurally-mediated
   syncope distinction is transferred from general cardiology, not ME/CFS-specific.
2. **Severe ME/CFS mortality:** no new Norwegian/German severe-ME mortality cohort found beyond
   existing corpus (Roberts2016 Lancet; McManimen). Severe-ME mortality remains a documented but
   under-powered evidence base.
3. **ED misdiagnosis in ME/CFS:** Timbol2019 is the sole ED-specific study; it is a self-report
   survey (n=282, 59% ED attendees), not a registry/claims cohort.
