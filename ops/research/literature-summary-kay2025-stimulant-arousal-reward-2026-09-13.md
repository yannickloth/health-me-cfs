## 1. Primary paper

### Kay et al. 2025 — Stimulant Medications Affect Arousal and Reward, Not Attention Networks
- **Key:** `Kay2025stimulantarousalreward` (verified)
- **Citation:** Kay BP, Wheelock MD, Siegel JS, et al. *Cell*. 2025;188(26):7529–7546.e20. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2025.11.039. PMID 41448140.
- **Design:** ABCD Study resting-state fMRI (n=11,875; n=337 stimulant-on-scan-day) + precision imaging drug trial (n=5 healthy adults, methylphenidate 40 mg, 165–210 min each). Power ≥95% for previously reported attention-network effects.
- **Findings:** Stimulant FC differences in somato-cognitive action/motor (arousal) and salience/parietal-memory (reward) networks; **none** in DAN/VAN/FPN attention-control networks. Stimulant pattern matched sleep-duration and NE-transporter maps; reversed sleep-deprivation effects. Cognition improved only in ADHD or sleep-deprived children.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.80 (Cell; very large discovery sample + independent controlled replication; rigorous methods) — tempered slightly because the causal arm is n=5 and the discovery arm is cross-sectional.
- **Population weight:** 0.75 (general population + ADHD; no ME/CFS).
- **Discounted certainty:** **0.60**.

## 2. Supporting human evidence — objective null / subjective-objective dissociation

### Ilieva, Hook & Farah 2015 — Prescription Stimulants' Effects on Healthy Cognition
- **Key:** `Ilieva2015StimulantMetaAnalysis` (verified)
- **Citation:** *J Cogn Neurosci*. 2015;27(6):1069–1089. doi:10.1162/jocn_a_00776. PMID 25591060.
- **Design:** Meta-analysis, 48 studies, n=1,409 healthy.
- **Findings:** Only small enhancement of inhibitory control/short-term memory; working-memory effect fragile; publication bias qualified long-term/working-memory effects. Authors: effect is "probably modest overall"; users may seek energy/motivation more than cognition.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.75 (meta-analysis in a strong journal; heterogeneous primary studies; publication-bias concern).
- **Weight:** 0.75 (healthy).
- **Discounted:** **0.56**.

### Ilieva, Boland & Farah 2013 — Objective vs Subjective Amphetamine Effects
- **Key:** `Ilieva2013ObjectiveSubjective` (verified)
- **Citation:** *Neuropharmacology*. 2013;64:496–505. doi:10.1016/j.neuropharm.2012.07.021. PMID 22884611.
- **Design:** Double-blind crossover placebo-controlled, healthy adults, 13 cognitive measures.
- **Findings:** No objective enhancement; an illusory perception of cognitive improvement was produced.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.70 (rigorous double-blind crossover; modest n; single formulation/dose).
- **Weight:** 0.75.
- **Discounted:** **0.53**.

### Roberts et al. 2020 — Cognitive Enhancement in Healthy Adults (Meta-analyses)
- **Key:** `Roberts2020CognitiveEnhancementMeta` (verified)
- **Citation:** *Eur Neuropsychopharmacol*. 2020;38:40–62. doi:10.1016/j.euroneuro.2020.07.002. PMID 32709551.
- **Design:** PRISMA series of meta-analyses, k=47.
- **Findings:** Limited/inconsistent enhancement in rested healthy adults; benefits mainly in sleep-deprived/low-baseline states.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.75.
- **Weight:** 0.75.
- **Discounted:** **0.56**.

### Repantis et al. 2010 — Modafinil/MPH Neuroenhancement Systematic Review
- **Key:** `Repantis2010ModafinilMPHenhancement` (verified)
- **Citation:** *Pharmacol Res*. 2010;62(3):187–206. doi:10.1016/j.phrs.2010.04.002. PMID 20416377.
- **Design:** Systematic review.
- **Findings:** Inconsistent cognitive effects; mood/arousal/wakefulness effects more consistent — early statement of the dissociation.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.65 (older review, pre-modern methods).
- **Weight:** 0.75.
- **Discounted:** **0.49**.

### Marraccini et al. 2016 — Prescription Stimulant Neurocognitive Meta-analysis
- **Key:** `Marraccini2016StimulantMetaAnalysis` (verified)
- **Citation:** *Exp Clin Psychopharmacol*. 2016;24(4):269–284. doi:10.1037/pha0000079. PMID 27454675.
- **Design:** Systematic meta-analysis.
- **Findings:** Small, domain-specific effects with evidence of both enhancement and impairment.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.60.
- **Weight:** 0.75.
- **Discounted:** **0.45**.

## 3. Supporting human evidence — reward/effort mechanism

### Chong, Fortunato & Bellgrove 2023 — Amphetamines Improve Motivation to Invest Effort in ADHD
- **Key:** `Chong2023AmphetamineMotivation` (verified)
- **Citation:** *J Neurosci*. 2023;43(41):6898–6908. doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0982-23.2023. PMID 37666665.
- **Design:** Within-subject effort-based decision task (20 ADHD on amphetamine; 24 controls).
- **Findings:** Amphetamines increased willingness to invest effort for reward (reward-effort invigoration) without improving ability.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.70.
- **Weight:** 0.75 (ADHD population).
- **Discounted:** **0.53**.

### Bowman et al. 2023 — Smart Drugs Increase Level but Decrease Quality of Effort
- **Key:** `Bowman2023NotSoSmart` (verified)
- **Citation:** *Sci Adv*. 2023;9(28):eadd4165. doi:10.1126/sciadv.add4165. PMID 37315143.
- **Design:** Placebo-controlled, healthy adults, knapsack optimization task.
- **Findings:** MPH/dextroamphetamine/modafinil decreased attained task value (effort quality) while increasing decision time/steps (effort level); strategies became more random.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.70.
- **Weight:** 0.75.
- **Discounted:** **0.53**.

### Greenhouse-Tucknott et al. 2025 — Perceived Fatigue, Effort, and Confidence
- **Key:** `GreenhouseTucknott2025FatigueEffort` (verified)
- **Citation:** *Physiol Behav*. 2025;291:114792. doi:10.1016/j.physbeh.2024.114792. PMID 39730065.
- **Design:** Within-subject rewarded effort task (22 healthy males).
- **Findings:** Perceived fatigue did not alter effort cost-benefit valuation but reduced confidence in ability — a boundary condition on reward models of fatigue.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.60.
- **Weight:** 0.75.
- **Discounted:** **0.45**.

## 4. Competing mechanism (retained and flagged)

### Rubia et al. 2014 — Stimulants Modulate Cognitive-Control Regions (Meta-analysis)
- **Key:** `Rubia2014StimulantBrainMeta` (verified)
- **Citation:** *Biol Psychiatry*. 2014;76(8):616–628. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2013.10.016. PMID 24314347.
- **Design:** Systematic review + meta-analysis of task-fMRI stimulant studies in ADHD.
- **Findings:** Stimulants most consistently increased right inferior frontal cortex/insula activation (cognitive control) — the leading competing account. Kay 2025 notes its power analysis covered the effect sizes (d≈0.89) reported by this class of ROI study, and attributes the discrepancy to design/sample differences.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.65.
- **Weight:** 0.75 (ADHD/clinical).
- **Discounted:** **0.49**.
- **Band note:** Kay2025 (0.60) vs Rubia2014 (0.49) differ by 0.11 discounted — within the ±0.10 measurement uncertainty. Per the Phase 2 band rule this is a candidate for both-sides framing (`#open-question`) if the conflict cannot be resolved by methodological differences; Kay 2025 offers a methodological resolution (large BWAS vs small ROI studies), which the synthesis should weigh.

## 5. Harm / safety

### Oliva et al. 2025 — Safety of Stimulants Across Patient Populations (Meta-analysis)
- **Key:** `Oliva2025StimulantSafety` (verified)
- **Citation:** *JAMA Netw Open*. 2025;8(5):e259492. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.9492. PMID 40343695.
- **Design:** Meta-analysis of stimulant safety across populations beyond ADHD (incl. off-label).
- **Findings:** Quantifies cardiovascular, psychiatric, sleep, and appetite adverse-event profile — the harm ceiling for off-label ME/CFS use.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.75.
- **Weight:** 0.75 (mixed/general).
- **Discounted:** **0.56**.

### Nourredine et al. 2026 — ADHD Pharmacological Interventions Network Meta-analysis
- **Key:** `Nourredine2026ADHDNetworkMeta` (verified)
- **Citation:** *Lancet Psychiatry*. 2026;13(6):485–495. doi:10.1016/S2215-0366(26)00091-X. PMID 42134365.
- **Design:** Systematic review + dose-effect network meta-analysis.
- **Findings:** Modern comparative efficacy/tolerability and dose-response structure for stimulant and non-stimulant ADHD drugs.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.80.
- **Weight:** 0.75 (ADHD).
- **Discounted:** **0.60**.

## 9. Gap-fill statement

The workspace already cites extensive stimulant pharmacology and ME/CFS stimulant-response evidence (ch33 sec-16, ch34 sec-12, ch07 sec-28). **Kay 2025 was not cited anywhere.** The specific claim it establishes — that stimulant benefit is arousal/reward-salience, not attention-capacity, so a positive response indexes low arousal/reward rather than augmented attention — is genuinely absent from the document. This integration is therefore a true gap-fill, not a duplicate. No additional ME/CFS-specific campaign literature was found that the corpus did not already hold.
