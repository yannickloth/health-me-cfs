## Inclusion Criteria
- Direct study of AhR, tryptophan/indole metabolites, or the AhR-gut-microbiome axis in ME/CFS, chronic fatigue, or related post-infectious/chronic conditions
- Mechanism papers establishing AhR biochemistry (ligand sensing, Th17/Treg, IL-22, intestinal barrier, immune regulation) relevant to the AhR hypothesis
- Null/negative and competing/contradicting evidence (mandatory)
- Harm/adverse-effect evidence on AhR agonists (mandatory)
- Peer-reviewed preferred; recent + relevant preprints accepted (flagged)
- Each paper: topic-appropriate bib file, annotated appendix-h entry

## Exclusion Criteria
- Oncology-focused AhR/kynurenine papers (T-cell exhaustion in cancer; not relevant to ME/CFS mechanism)
- General-population metabolomics without fatigue/CFS relevance
- Pure pharmacology of AhR agonists in non-immune contexts
- Non-English abstracts without full text
- Papers already in the corpus under another research_stream

## Notes
- Direct AhR×ME/CFS literature is sparse (5 PubMed hits, overlapping). The strongest direct study is Esteban2026 (MicrobiologyOpen), which embeds a critical PARTIAL-NULL result: AhR activation did not differ between ME/CFS and controls overall, but was elevated in people with neurocognitive symptoms.
- IL-22×ME/CFS and Th17/Treg×ME/CFS both returned 0 direct hits — the AhR mechanism's downstream immune readouts have no direct ME/CFS studies, so these are covered via mechanism reviews (Rothhammer2019, Thirugnanam2026) and the direct microbiome papers (Esteban2026, Chojnacki2025).
