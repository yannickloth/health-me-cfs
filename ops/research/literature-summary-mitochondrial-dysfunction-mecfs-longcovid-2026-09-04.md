## Positive / mechanistic reinforcement

### 1. Tasoula2026multiomicsLCmito — Multi-omics of long COVID reveals persistent mitochondrial dysfunction
- **Bib key:** `Tasoula2026multiomicsLCmito` (added to `bib/energy-metabolism.bib`)
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (secondary multi-omics re-analysis of public datasets)
- **Population weight:** 0.70 (LC human tissue/PBMC/serum + Syrian-hamster animal + transcriptomic re-analysis)
- **Discounted certainty:** ~0.39
- **One-line finding:** Across species/tissues, sustained suppression of OXPHOS, mitochondrial-stress-response activation, and immune activation persist to ≥12 months post-infection; skeletal-muscle mitochondrial repression is the most pronounced.
- **Quality notes:** Front Immunol (peer-reviewed, OA); authors affiliated with commercial Guarnieri Research Group LLC (conflict-of-interest disclosed); integrative re-analysis, not a single prospective cohort.
- **Cohort overlap:** None with corpus (first Guarnieri-line entry). Re-uses prior public LC datasets.

### 2. WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine — Reduced ATP/PCr ratios in neuro-psychiatric post-COVID (³¹P MRS)
- **Bib key:** `WeberFahr2026ATPphosphocreatine` (added to `bib/energy-metabolism.bib`)
- **Raw certainty:** 0.72
- **Population weight:** 0.85 (Long COVID; subgroup also met ME/CFS criteria)
- **Discounted certainty:** ~0.61
- **One-line finding:** Whole-brain ³¹P-MRSI in 27 PCCo vs 23 recovered controls shows reduced ATP/PCr centered on cingulate cortex; lower ACC ATP/PCr correlates with poorer cognition; similar alterations in the PCCo subgroup meeting ME/CFS criteria.
- **Quality notes:** Biological Psychiatry (top-tier); first in-vivo human brain-energy evidence in PCCo; moderate n but controlled.
- **Cohort overlap:** None with corpus.

### 3. Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation — Temporal plasma proteomics reveals maladaptation after exertion in ME/CFS
- **Bib key:** `Germain2025plasmaProteomicMaladaptation` (added to `bib/energy-metabolism.bib`)
- **Raw certainty:** 0.72
- **Population weight:** 1.0 (ME/CFS)
- **Discounted certainty:** ~0.72
- **One-line finding:** Longitudinal Somascan 7K proteomics in 132 subjects (79 ME/CFS vs 53 sedentary controls, two maximal CPETs 24 h apart) shows persistent immune/metabolic/neuromuscular dysregulation during PEM-recovery, with upregulation of glycolysis/gluconeogenesis "suggestive of mitochondrial stress," and decoupling of protein abundance from VO₂max/anaerobic threshold.
- **Quality notes:** Molecular & Cellular Proteomics; sedentary-control arm explicitly distinguishes disease effects from deconditioning (competing-axis neutralizer).
- **Cohort overlap:** **FLAG** — same Cornell (Hanson/Giloteaux) ME/CFS cohort as existing corpus `Germain2020metabolic`, `Germain2017metabolomics`, `Germain2022exercise`. Distinct new proteomic data + new n, but shared site/biobank.

### 4. Keele2026mapMECFSmito — Gene-expression/proteomic evidence across tissues supports mitochondrial dysregulation in ME/CFS
- **Bib key:** `Keele2026mapMECFSmito` (added to `bib/energy-metabolism.bib`)
- **Raw certainty:** 0.45 (in-silico re-analysis)
- **Population weight:** 0.30 (in silico) — supporting only
- **Discounted certainty:** ~0.14
- **One-line finding:** mapMECFS + DecodeME GWAS gene/protein data show lower MT-RNR1/MT-RNR2 mitochondrial-gene expression in ME/CFS in two studies, supporting mitochondrial dysregulation; maps ME/CFS genes to approved drug targets.
- **Quality notes:** IJMS; transparent negative-consistency caveat (little cross-study gene overlap). Weak reinforcement of the general mitochondrial-dysregulation claim; no mechanism.
- **Cohort overlap:** Uses DecodeME + mapMECFS repositories (shared biobanks with other corpus entries).

### 5. Magawa2026TRPM3NKmitoCa — Deficient TRPM3-linked mitochondrial Ca²⁺ influx in ME/CFS NK cells
- **Bib key:** `Magawa2026TRPM3NKmitoCa` (added to `bib/energy-metabolism.bib`)
- **Raw certainty:** 0.50
- **Population weight:** 0.60 (ME/CFS population, but ex-vivo/in-vitro cellular readout)
- **Discounted certainty:** ~0.30
- **One-line finding:** Fluorescence imaging (n=10 ME/CFS, CCC, vs 10 HC) shows altered TRPM3-mediated cytosolic and mitochondrial Ca²⁺ mobilization in NK cells, with reduced TRPM3-dependent mitochondrial Ca²⁺ influx in ME/CFS.
- **Quality notes:** BMC Immunology; small n; CCC diagnosis; extends the TRPM3/Ca²⁺ line to the mitochondrial-Ca²⁺ compartment (mechanistically adjacent to the F3 mito-Ca²⁺ cascade).
- **Cohort overlap:** **FLAG** — Griffith University / Marshall-Gradisnik (single-lab TRPM3-NK line, already extensively in corpus `ion-channels-lithium.bib`). Retained as representative ME/CFS entry; its Long-COVID companion (PMID 42484498, Eur J Immunol 2026, same lab) deliberately NOT separately added to avoid same-lab redundancy.

### 6. Groysman2026fragileMitophagy — Fragile mitophagy as a recovery-failure endotype for post-exertional malaise
- **Bib key:** `Groysman2026fragileMitophagy` (added to `bib/energy-metabolism.bib`)
- **Raw certainty:** 0.30 (single-author hypothesis/opinion; no primary data)
- **Population weight:** 0.40 (Long-COVID reasoning; in-silico/conceptual)
- **Discounted certainty:** ~0.12 (concept only)
- **One-line finding:** Proposes "fragile mitophagy" — mitochondrial injury with inadequate lysosomal completion of mitophagy — as a PEM recovery-failure endotype; predicts PEM severity tracks impaired lysosomal completion/danger signaling more than baseline ATP deficit.
- **Quality notes:** Front Med hypothesis article. No data. Included solely because the recovery-failure-endotype framing is a novel extension of the F4 (mitochondrial damage → mitophagy impairment) cascade not otherwise present in corpus.
- **Cohort overlap:** None.

## Null / negative

### 7. Ryback2026myoblastNull — Indistinguishable mitochondrial phenotypes after ME/CFS vs control serum (failed replication)
- **Bib key:** `Ryback2026myoblastNull` (added to `bib/energy-metabolism.bib`)
- **Raw certainty:** 0.75 (large preregistered replication)
- **Population weight:** 0.40 (in-vitro mechanistic test; ME/CFS sera)
- **Discounted certainty:** ~0.30 (as a negative)
- **One-line finding:** Pre-registered replication of Fluge 2016 with sera from 67 ME/CFS and 53 controls and >1,700 Seahorse mitochondrial stress tests found NO difference in maximal respiratory capacity of healthy myoblasts exposed to ME vs control serum — strong evidence against the hypothesis that ME serum factors differentially alter healthy-myoblast mitochondrial phenotype in vitro.
- **Quality notes:** PLoS One; larger and pre-registered vs the original; UK (Edinburgh) + Action for ME involvement. Quality limitations: PLoS One tier; in-vitro model only.
- **Cohort overlap:** None with corpus (original Fluge2016 IS in corpus).
- **Implication:** Limits the "serum-factor → transferable in-vitro myoblast mitochondrial defect" layer only. Does NOT contradict in-vivo muscle-mitochondrial data (Charlton 2026 bed-rest, Germain 2025, Weber-Fahr 2026 brain MRS) or the muscle-biopsy WASF3/supercomplex/PDH findings, which rest on tissue, not serum-cell transfer.
