# Literature Summary: Lifetime Energy-Level Problems → ME/CFS (Mild / Constitutional Cases)

Date: 2026-09-01 | Stream: `lifetime-energy-mild-cases`

## Purpose

Evaluate whether patients with lifetime energy-level problems (constitutional tiredness, "wired-tired" ADHD/autism energetic profiles, "just more tired than expected" at baseline) — i.e. MILD cases — represent a distinct predisposing/prodromal trajectory that precedes (or is continuous with) a later moderate/severe ME/CFS development. This phase specifically targets evidence NOT already covered by the integrated ADHD/ASD predisposition streams (`adhd-energy-mecfs`, `adhd-asd-comorbidity-mecfs`, `brain-energy-asd-mecfs`, `lower-baseline-predisposition`).

## Evidence Landscape

### Strong (certainty ≥ 0.60 raw)
- **Harvey 2009 (0.68)**: Prospective MRC birth cohort (N=5362) — persistent fatigue WITHOUT comorbid psychiatric disorder is a real population construct (7.4% new-onset at 43). Specific premorbid risk factor = EXCESSIVE childhood energy (OR 2.63), NOT low baseline energy. This is a critical counterpoint to the low-baseline premise.

### Medium (0.40–0.60 raw)
- **Sunnquist 2015 (0.55)**: The pivotal case-definition paper. Lifelong fatigue is an exclusionary criterion in CFS/ME definitions, but it is hard to operationalize, few meet it rigorously, and those with vs without it show few symptom/functional differences. Recommendation: remove lifelong fatigue as exclusion. **This is the strongest NEW evidence that a lifetime-tiredness constitution is continuous with (not categorically opposed to) ME/CFS, and that mild/lifetime cases should not be excluded.**
- **Collin 2015 (0.60)**: ALSPAC birth cohort — childhood-onset chronic disabling fatigue (1.9% at age 13), predicted by maternal anxiety/depression and child psychological factors; defined without PEM, i.e. a mild persistent low-functioning state.
- **Brown 2013 (0.48)**: Energy-envelope clusters; a low-daily-energy-reserve cluster (Cluster 3) is severely limited even without acute overexertion.
- **Jason 2010 (0.45)**: Fatigue-type severity clusters; includes a low-severity ('mild') cluster within ME/CFS.

### Weak (certainty < 0.40)
- **Smith 1991 (0.40)**: Small historical pediatric case series; raises the prodrome question (depression vs discrete psychosomatic vs infectious/immunologic).

## Key Findings Summary

1. **Lifelong fatigue should NOT exclude ME/CFS.** Sunnquist 2015 is direct evidence that a lifetime-tiredness constitution is not categorically distinct from the illness and that mild/lifetime cases are being excluded by case-definition criteria. This is the strongest novel support for integrating a mild/lifetime-end presentation.

2. **The premorbid profile may be HIGH energy, not low energy.** Harvey 2009 (population) and the existing corpus (Kato2006 emotional instability, @Jason2015CFSvsME sudden-onset) point to premorbid overactivity/emotional-instability more than low-baseline energy. This challenges the "constitutional low-baseline-energy" premise and suggests the more defensible framing is a **continuous energy-constraint spectrum** (lifetime low-reserve OR overactivity-then-crash), with PEM as the discriminator between constitution and disease.

3. **Childhood-onset fatigue is measurable and distinct.** Collin 2015 establishes a chronic-disabling-fatigue trajectory in adolescence (defined without PEM) — evidence that a mild/lifetime low-functioning phenotype predates adult ME/CFS.

4. **Mild-severity cases exist within ME/CFS.** Jason 2010 and Brown 2013 show severity-based clustering includes a mild/low-severity subset and a low-baseline-reserve phenotype.

## Contradictions / Competing Mechanisms

- **Low-baseline vs high-baseline premorbid energy:** Harvey 2009 finds EXCESSIVE childhood energy predicts fatigue-without-psychiatric-disorder; the topic's low-baseline premise predicts LOW energy. These are not necessarily contradictory — the "energy constraint" may be a low *reserve ceiling* (a low energy-envelope ceiling that both overactivity and low-baseline daily energy interact with), but this needs explicit framing as an open question, not asserted as resolved.
- **Constitution vs disease:** Sunnquist 2015 argues lifelong fatigue should not exclude ME/CFS (continuous), while the topic's premise (a) asks whether "just tired" without PEM is distinct. The evidence supports BOTH: continuous for low-baseline-reserve risk, but PEM remains the discriminator (Sunnquist does not argue the no-PEM state is ME/CFS).

## Null / Negative Evidence Found

- No paper found showing a **distinct** "lifelong mild ME/CFS" diagnostic entity; the population evidence (Harvey, Collin) shows persistent fatigue as a general-population construct, not a validated ME/CFS prodrome.
- No strong evidence of mild-ME/CFS *underdiagnosis* quantified in the literature searched (searches returned no dedicated underdiagnosis-of-mild papers; the angle is implied by Sunnquist's exclusion-criterion critique but not directly measured).

## What Is NOT Covered (gaps)

- No direct cohort showing mild/lifetime-energy patients later developing moderate/severe ME/CFS (longitudinal transition data absent from this pool).
- No quantitative estimate of mild-ME/CFS underdiagnosis prevalence.
- No ME/CFS-specific study isolating a "constitutional low-baseline-energy" biomarker (mitochondrial reserve in mild cases unaddressed here — that biochemical angle is already partly in `mitochondrial-predisposition`/`adhd-energy-mecfs`).

## Integration Implication (preliminary — Phase 2 decides)

- **New evidence WAS found for the mild/lifetime/constitutional angle beyond the ADHD/ASD predisposition streams**: the Sunnquist lifelong-fatigue case-definition finding is novel and directly on-topic; Collin (childhood trajectory) and Jason/Brown (mild-severity clustering) add severity/continuum support. Harvey 2009 adds a critical counterpoint (high-energy premorbid) that must frame the low-baseline premise as an open question.
- Expected Phase 2 decision: **PARTIAL** (mild-severity nuance + case-definition critique; not a categorical new trajectory). The low-baseline-energy premise is only weakly supported (no direct ME/CFS mild-cohort data) and is partly contradicted by Harvey's high-energy finding → likely #open-question / #limitation framing, not a new #hypothesis-box.
- Cohort overlap: 4 of 6 papers are DePaul University / Jason group (Sunnquist2015, Jason2010, Brown2013, + existing Jason corpus). Harvey/Collin are independent (MRC/ALSPAC). No biobank or recruitment-year overlap beyond DePaul.
