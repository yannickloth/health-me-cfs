# Literature Summary: Longitudinal Mild/Moderate → Severe Transition in Established ME/CFS

Date: 2026-09-02 | Stream: `years-mild-moderate-then-severe`

## Purpose

Determine whether any longitudinal cohort study reports the rate and/or predictors of
progression across a severity boundary (mild→moderate, moderate→severe, toward
housebound/bedbound) over years, in patients with ESTABLISHED ME/CFS who were mild or
moderate at baseline. This tests the "Progressive Worsening" arm of the slow,
years-long trajectory already described in ch06 sec-03/sec-04. The document currently
anchors the deterioration arm on a pooled, uncited "10–20% worsen during follow-up"
figure (sec-04) plus cross-sectional housebound prevalence (Pendergrast2020housebound,
~25%), and flags a prospective severity-transition cohort as an open evidence gap
(`pred-mild-lifetime-transition-cohort`).

## Result

**N=0 clean inclusions.** No NEW cohort was found that (a) follows established
(non-conversion) mild/moderate ME/CFS, (b) stratifies baseline by severity category, and
(c) reports a quantified rate/predictors of crossing a severity boundary over years,
that is not already covered by the corpus.

## Evidence Landscape / Certainty Assessment

### The one clean severity-boundary shift is ALREADY covered
Van Campen 2023 (PMID 38138257) is the only surfaced study that reclassifies ME/CFS
patients across ICC mild/moderate/severe tiers longitudinally (51/45/4% → 1/72/27% in
patients re-tested because of worsening). It is already in the corpus
(`VanCampenEtAl2023`, stream `autonomic-cardiovascular`) and is framed as a CBF/severity
correlation paper. It CANNOT supply a population mild/moderate→severe transition rate
because its worsening arm is selected by re-presentation for worsening, not a natural
cohort. Raw certainty for the *transition-rate* claim from this paper: low (population
selection bias); population-discounted: low. Not re-added.

### Partial worsening proportions (continuous scales)
- Jones/Newton 2011 (PMID 21073560): specialist CFS clinic, 74 followed 2005→2009
  (4 yr). FIS worsened ≥10 points in 45%, improved ≥10 in 26–39%. Quantifies a
  deterioration majority on a continuous fatigue scale but does NOT report
  mild/moderate→severe boundary crossings. Raw certainty for a *worsening-proportion*
  claim: moderate (0.50); population-discounted for the *severity-boundary* question:
  low (no severity tiers). Direct relevance to the specific gap: partial.
- Tomić 2026 (PMID 42356126): 16-yr FU of 40 (20 analysed) women with established
  CDC-criteria ME/CFS; reports 15% with "progression of ME/CFS," but the study's primary
  outcome is new somatic/psychiatric comorbidity (RA 15%, cancer 10%, depression 20%).
  Small n, women-only, fatigue-scale severity, no per-patient baseline mild/moderate/
  severe classification. Raw certainty for a *severity-boundary-transition* claim: low;
  population-discounted: low.

### No clean cohort → the gap stands
Across ~30 query families (PubMed) and a Google Scholar construct sweep, no
population-representative prospective cohort was found that classifies established
mild/moderate ME/CFS at baseline and reports a mild/moderate→severe transition rate over
years. The pooled "10–20% worsen during follow-up" figure currently in sec-04 rests on
older clinic-based systematic reviews (Cairns2005prognosis and comparable), none of which
present clean severity-tier transition data either.

## Contradictions / Competing (null) Evidence

- **Severity can be relatively stable / improve in the majority at the symptom level.**
  Jones/Newton 2011 (39% improved on FIS), van der Werf 2002 (46% self-reported
  improvement at 1 yr in short-duration disease), and HUNT general-population
  trajectories (Glette 2024) collectively indicate that a large share of chronic-fatigue
  and short-duration patients do NOT worsen over 1–4 years. This is the honest competing
  arm: over typical clinical follow-up horizons the modal course is stability or modest
  improvement, and clear mild/moderate→severe progression is a minority that these
  cohorts do not isolate by severity boundary.
- **Direction of the severe-severity effect is persistence, not necessarily new onset.**
  Jason/Katz 2026 (post-IM) shows severe-at-onset persists (~81%) whereas moderate
  largely resolves — consistent with severity at onset dominating outcome, not a steady
  mild→severe drift that a transition-rate cohort would capture.

## Null / Negative Evidence Found

- No study found that stratifies ESTABLISHED mild/moderate ME/CFS at baseline and reports
  a quantified mild→moderate or moderate→severe transition rate over years.
- No cohort found that reports predictors of progression TO housebound/bedbound from a
  mild/moderate baseline (only cross-sectional housebound prevalence, Pendergrast2020,
  already cited).

## What Is NOT Covered (residual gap)

- A clean longitudinal severity-boundary transition cohort in established (non-conversion)
  ME/CFS remains absent. The document's own `pred-mild-lifetime-transition-cohort`
  framing of this as the open evidence gap is correct.
- No quantified rate of progression from mild/moderate to severe/housebound over years in
  a general (non-specialty-selected) mild/moderate population.

## Integration Implication

- **Verdict: (b) — no NEW clean cohort exists → honest gap stands → DEFER/REJECT.**
- No bib entries added; no research_stream keys produced; no changes to ch06 sec-03/sec-04.
- Optional note (not acted on this cycle): if a future edit wishes to nuance the uncited
  "10–20% worsen during follow-up" figure in sec-04, Jones/Newton 2011 (PMID 21073560;
  45% worsened ≥10 FIS points over 4 yr in a specialist clinic) is the strongest available
  continuous-scale worsening estimate and could be cited with an explicit caveat that it
  measures fatigue-scale change, not severity-boundary transition. That addition would be
  a prose/citation nuance, not resolution of the severity-transition gap.
