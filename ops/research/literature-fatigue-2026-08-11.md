# Literature Summary: Fatigue as a Core Symptom of ME/CFS

**Topic slug:** fatigue-core-symptom
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Phase:** Phase 1 — Literature Search
**Papers included:** 10

---

## Scope

Fatigue as a standalone core symptom of ME/CFS — definition, prevalence by fatigue domain, severity estimation, objective vs subjective measurement, fatigability as a distinct construct, symptom clustering/phenotyping, differentiation from other fatigue conditions (idiopathic chronic fatigue, depression, Sjögren's fatigue).

**Exclusions:**
- Fatigue-PEM relationship (already covered in `sec-01-pem/`, committed)
- Fatigue-CNS-inflammation mechanism (Omdal plan, ch06/ch15)
- FSS/MFI instrument validation (Krupp 1989, Smets 1995 already in corpus)
- Pure mechanism papers (energy metabolism, immune)

---

## Papers by Theme

### 1. Fatigue Definition & Construct Validity

#### Tankisi et al. 2024 (@Tankisi2024fatigueFatigability)
- **What:** Narrative review of clinical neurophysiology in defining fatigue vs fatigability
- **Key distinction:** Fatigue = subjective perception; fatigability = objective performance decline measurable by neurophysiological tools
- **Tools:** Central fatigability (twitch interpolation, TMS, EEG/MEG, readiness potentials); peripheral fatigability (EMG, SFEMG, nerve conduction)
- **Relevance:** Provides the conceptual framework for distinguishing ME/CFS fatigue (subjective) from ME/CFS fatigability (objective); essential for new sec-00-fatigue section
- **Certainty:** 0.60 (narrative review, not systematic)

#### Bileviciute-Ljungar et al. 2020 (@BileviciuteLjungar2020ICF)
- **What:** Preliminary ICF Core Set for ME/CFS; n=100 patients assessed by rehabilitation team
- **Key:** ICF distinguishes b1300 (energy level/fatigue) from b4552 (fatigability) as separate body-function categories; fatigue impairment in 100% of patients
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates fatigue and fatigability are formally distinct constructs in the WHO ICF framework — ME/CFS affects both
- **Certainty:** 0.55 (preliminary core set; single-center; team consensus, not Delphi)

#### Campos et al. 2022 (@Campos2022PVFassessment)
- **What:** Review of post-COVID fatigue assessment methods across subjective (questionnaires) and objective (performance) domains
- **Key:** Multi-domain framework: subjective fatigue + objective fatigability + cognitive impairment
- **Relevance:** Assessment framework directly applicable to ME/CFS; bridges Long COVID fatigue to ME/CFS fatigue measurement
- **Certainty:** 0.55 (narrative review, not systematic; COVID-19-specific)

### 2. Fatigue Severity Estimation

#### Park et al. 2024 (@Park2024fatigueSeverity)
- **What:** Systematic review + meta-analysis of fatigue severity across 60 ME/CFS RCTs (n=7,088)
- **Key:** Pooled fatigue severity 77.9/100; physical fatigue 74.3, cognitive 74.2, mental 70.1; severity varies by diagnostic criteria (ICC 54.2 → Canadian 83.6) and assessment tool (MFS 54.2 → CIS 88.6)
- **Relevance:** Provides quantitative benchmarks for fatigue severity — the most comprehensive severity estimate available
- **Certainty:** 0.75 (systematic review, large pooled n, but heterogeneous trial populations)

### 3. Fatigue Phenotypes & Subgroups

#### Habermann-Horstmeier & Horstmeier 2025 (@HabermannHorstmeier2025symptomClusters)
- **What:** n=748 ME/CFS; symptom clusters tested via EFA, CFA, SEM
- **Key:** Brain factor (brain fog, sensory hypersensitivity, etc.) excellent fit (CFI=0.996); Gut-Immune two-factor structure; Autonomic higher-order latent factor; clusters align with biological systems
- **Relevance:** Fatigue is embedded in coherent multi-system symptom clusters — supports the idea that fatigue is not a unitary construct in ME/CFS
- **Certainty:** 0.60 (large n, strong methodology, but self-report and cross-sectional)

#### Eaton-Fitch & Marshall-Gradisnik 2026 (@EatonFitch2026registryQoL)
- **What:** n=2,873 ME/CFS vs n=797 controls; largest Australian registry
- **Key:** K-means clustering → 4 symptom-severity clusters; HRQoL significantly impaired; ICC patients worst outcomes; symptom clustering differs by case definition
- **Relevance:** Empirical evidence for fatigue-severity-based subgroups; case definition matters for patient stratification
- **Certainty:** 0.65 (large registry, but voluntary self-selection bias)

#### Lee et al. 2025 (@Lee2025fatigueDominantLC)
- **What:** n=100 fatigue-dominant Long COVID; cross-sectional clinical/lab characterization
- **Key:** Fatigue scores strongly inter-correlated across instruments; cortisol negatively correlated with fatigue (mental > physical); no sex/age/duration differences
- **Relevance:** Post-viral fatigue is a coherent and measurable construct; cortisol association suggests endocrine contribution without explaining physical dimensions
- **Certainty:** 0.60 (single time point; no healthy controls; Korean population)

### 4. Objective Correlates of Fatigue

#### Liu et al. 2025 (@Liu2025geneticFatigueActigraphy)
- **What:** UK Biobank: n=295 CFS vs n=63,133; actigraphy + genetic analysis
- **Key:** CFS patients had decreased overall movement (d=0.220), lower activity amplitudes (d=-0.377), lower wrist temperature amplitudes (d=-0.173); genetic overlap between CFS and population fatigue
- **Relevance:** Actigraphy provides objective correlates of fatigue; genetic architecture supports fatigue continuum model
- **Certainty:** 0.70 (large population cohort, but self-reported CFS; small CFS n)

#### Bedard et al. 2026 (@Bedard2026centralFatigability)
- **What:** n=15 ME/CFS vs n=19 HC; fMRI + EEG + EMG during grip force fatigability task
- **Key:** ME/CFS patients develop fatigue earlier; healthy controls upregulate brain activity pre-fatigue — ME/CFS brains fail to increase motor drive (central, not peripheral origin)
- **Relevance:** Direct neuroimaging evidence for central fatigability — the brain fails to sustain motor output. Distinguishes ME/CFS from peripheral muscle fatigue.
- **Certainty:** 0.60 (small sample, NIH intramural, but rigorous multimodal design)

### 5. Differential Diagnosis of Fatigue

#### Eguchi et al. 2026 (@Eguchi2026EVmiRNAfatigue)
- **What:** EV-miRNA profiling: n=6 ME/CFS vs n=6 idiopathic chronic fatigue vs n=8 depression
- **Key:** 62 EV-miRNA signature with 87% sensitivity / 94% specificity for ME/CFS vs ICF/depression; ME/CFS EVs had unique size/fluorescence profile; top miRNAs: miR-21-5p, let-7f-5p, miR-26b-5p, miR-20a-5p
- **Relevance:** Molecular evidence that ME/CFS fatigue is biologically distinct from idiopathic chronic fatigue and depression — supports construct validity of ME/CFS fatigue
- **Certainty:** 0.50 (very small discovery cohort; preliminary; requires replication)

## Cross-Paper Themes

1. **Fatigue vs fatigability:** Tankisi 2024 and Bileviciute-Ljungar 2020 both formalize this distinction; Bedard 2026 provides neuroimaging evidence for central fatigability specifically.

2. **Measurement heterogeneity:** Park 2024 quantifies how much fatigue severity varies by assessment tool (54.2–88.6 on 100-point scale); Campos 2022 and Lee 2025 both show multi-domain assessment is essential.

3. **Fatigue is not unitary:** Habermann-Horstmeier 2025 shows fatigue embeds within coherent multi-system clusters; Eaton-Fitch 2026 shows 4 severity-based subtypes; Eguchi 2026 shows molecular differentiation.

4. **Objective correlates exist but are underutilized:** Liu 2025 (actigraphy), Bedard 2026 (fMRI/EEG/EMG), Tankisi 2024 (neurophysiology tools) all provide objective measurement frameworks rarely used in clinical practice.

---

## Gaps Identified

- No systematic review of subjective-vs-objective fatigue discordance in ME/CFS specifically (Novak 2024 did this for dysautonomia, not fatigue)
- No large-scale latent class analysis of fatigue alone (all cluster analyses include multi-system symptoms)
- Limited data on fatigue trajectory/fluctuation (daily/within-day variability)
- No validated ME/CFS-specific fatigue instrument (all instruments borrowed from other diseases)
