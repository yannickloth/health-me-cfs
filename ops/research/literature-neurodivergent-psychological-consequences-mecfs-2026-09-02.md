# Literature Survey: Neurodivergent-Specific Psychological Consequences of ME/CFS

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Research stream:** `neurodivergent-psychological-consequences-mecfs`
**Complement to:** `literature-adhd-energy-mecfs-predisposition.md` (2026-04-20) and ch07 sec-28 / ch14d / ch17 — which cover ADHD/ASD as ME/CFS *predisposition* and ME/CFS energy loss producing ADHD-like executive dysfunction.

This search targets the **neurodivergence-specific psychological-consequence** angle — what ME/CFS's energy loss does to mental health and identity *specifically* in autistic / ADHD / neurodivergent people, including overlap with autistic burnout, masking + social-energy limits, and diagnostic overshadowing. It does NOT re-litigate the predisposition or brain-energy material.

---

## Direct Intersection (the whole point of the topic) — why it is thin

The single most important structural finding: **few studies measure psychological consequences in individuals who carry BOTH a formal ME/CFS diagnosis AND an autism/ADHD diagnosis.** Evidence instead clusters into four resolvable sub-claims, each with different strength:

| Sub-claim | Strength | Anchor papers |
|---|---|---|
| A. Autistic burnout's exhaustion phenotype overlaps ME/CFS-like energy loss (target 3) | Strong | Raymaker 2020, Higgins 2021, Clarey 2026, Ali 2026, Pagán 2025 |
| B. Masking / camouflaging predicts central-sensitivity (fatigue/energy) symptoms + ND illness burden (target 6) | Good | Grant 2025, Hodge 2026 |
| C. Diagnostic overshadowing: autistic distress/fatigue misattributed, incl. CFS listed as perceived misdiagnosis (target 5) | Good–direct | Kentrou 2024, (Grant 2025) |
| D. ADHD × chronic fatigue shares transdiagnostic psychological characteristics (double burden) (targets 1,7) | Sparse–moderate | Rogers 2017 |
| E. Loss of special interests / cognitive identity (target 4) | Very thin | Clarey 2026 (identity disruption, proximate only) |

## Papers (included)

### 1. Raymaker et al.\ 2020 — Defining Autistic Burnout (foundational)
**PMID 32851204 · PMC7313636 · DOI 10.1089/aut.2019.0079 · Autism in Adulthood 2020;2(2):132–143**
**Population:** n=196 autistic adults (qualitative interviews + an online survey), community-engaged CBPR design.
**Design:** Participatory mixed-methods; proposed definition of autistic burnout.

**Key findings:**
- Autistic burnout defined as "a highly debilitating state characterized by chronic, often extensive exhaustion, reduced tolerance to stimuli, and reduced ability to perform everyday tasks" — a **chronic-exhaustion phenotype that is phenomenologically continuous with the energy loss of ME/CFS** (target 3 core).
- Drivers: cumulative sensory/social/cognitive overload + masking + unmet support needs + life transitions.
- Exhaustion and functional collapse, not primary mood disorder — key for distinguishing ND burnout from secondary depression.

**Quality:** High. Foundational, multi-method, community-engaged; the most-cited definitional source.
**Certainty (raw):** 0.78. **Discounted (0.90 ND×ME/CFS-exhaustion overlap weight):** ≈ 0.70.
**Relevance:** Defines the ND-specific exhaustion construct that overlaps ME/CFS energy loss; frames the psychological overlap this stream integrates.

### 2. Higgins et al.\ 2021 — Defining autistic burnout by lived experience (Delphi)
**PMID 34088219 · DOI 10.1177/13623613211019858 · Autism 2021;25(8):2356–2369**
**Population:** n=69 autistic lived-experts + 14 professionals/researchers, Grounded Delphi.
**Design:** Expert Delphi consensus on autistic burnout definition and features.

**Key findings:**
- Consensus features: (1) exhaustion, (2) withdrawal, (3) reduced tolerance, (4) reduced functional capacity, (5) skill loss / cognitive regression.
- Consensus that autistic burnout is **distinct from clinical depression** and requires different support; overlaps chronic-fatigue phenomenology.
- Emphasises aetiology in accumulated social/sensory demands + masking + identity, not pathology of the autistic person.

**Quality:** High for a Delphi (lived-experience panel), but expert-opinion (not empirical outcome data).
**Certainty (raw):** 0.68. **Discounted (0.90):** ≈ 0.61.
**Relevance:** Corroborates Raymaker; explicitly separates ND burnout from depression — a key ND psychological-consequence discriminator.

---

### 3. Clarey et al.\ 2026 — Shame, identity disruption, and functional collapse in autistic burnout
**PMID 42109126 · DOI 10.1177/13623613261444797 · Autism 2026;30(6):1519–1531**
**Population:** n=11 autistic adults (semi-structured interviews, online).
**Design:** Qualitative template analysis.

**Key findings:**
- Beyond exhaustion: prominent **shame**, shaping interpretation of functional limits and help-seeking (target 4 identity/psych).
- **"Dual masking"** — concealing both autistic traits AND signs of burnout — intensified strain, delayed recognition, undermined recovery.
- Withdrawal and "gentle re-engagement" via low-demand, people-adjacent activity as recovery strategies.
- Identity disruption central to the burnout experience.

**Quality:** Medium (qualitative, n=11).
**Certainty (raw):** 0.48. **Discounted (0.90):** ≈ 0.43.
**Relevance:** ND-specific shame + identity disruption + dual-masking — the psychological-consequence texture that general ME/CFS psych chapters do not capture.

---

### 4. Ali, Mandy & Happé\ 2026 — How autistic burnout feels (earlier vs later diagnosis)
**PMID 41761756 · DOI 10.1177/13623613261422117 · Autism 2026;30(4):1014–1027**
**Population:** n=20 autistic adults (8 childhood-diagnosed).
**Design:** Qualitative reflexive thematic analysis (KCL / UCL).

**Key findings:**
- Burnout experienced as (1) "powering down" and/or (2) "overactivation" of mind/body, producing (3) craving for sensory/social rest — an **energy-regulatory** reading of ND exhaustion.
- Later-diagnosed adults: burnout more chronic and confusing; used substances to cope; "not knowing why" took a (sometimes dangerous) toll — **a few contemplated suicide** (target 7 suicidality, indirect).

**Quality:** Medium-High (reflexive thematic analysis; established authors).
**Certainty (raw):** 0.52. **Discounted (0.90):** ≈ 0.47.
**Relevance:** Later-diagnosis subgroup = double burden (undiagnosed ND + chronic exhaustion without explanation) — bridges diagnostic overshadowing and suicidality.

---

### 5. Pagán et al.\ 2025 — Autistic burnout and posttraumatic stress
**PMID 41473934 · DOI 10.1016/j.rin.2025.100010 · Research in Autism Spectrum... (Res Neurodivers) 2025;1:100010**
**Population:** n=91 trauma-exposed autistic adults without intellectual disability.
**Design:** Cross-sectional (ABSI, PCL-5, PHQ-9, GAD-7) + exploratory factor analysis.

**Key findings:**
- Strong correlation autistic burnout ↔ PTSS (r=0.70, p<0.001); EFA suggested shared underlying factors (self-image, memory).
- Burnout related to depression (PHQ-9) and anxiety (GAD-7) as well — quantifies the psychological-correlate burden of ND burnout.

**Quality:** Medium (cross-sectional, n=91, novel instrument).
**Certainty (raw):** 0.55. **Discounted (0.85):** ≈ 0.47.
**Relevance:** Quantifies the trauma/psychiatric correlate of the ND exhaustion phenotype — a neurodivergence-specific burden layer.

---

### 6. Kentrou et al.\ 2024 — Perceived misdiagnosis of psychiatric conditions in autistic adults
**PMID 38596613 · PMC11001629 · DOI 10.1016/j.eclinm.2024.102586 · eClinicalMedicine 2024;71:102586**
**Population:** n=1,211 autistic adults (52.6% women, mean 42.3 y), Netherlands register.
**Design:** Register survey (frequency of perceived pre-autism-diagnosis misdiagnosis).

**Key findings:**
- 24.6% (n=298) reported ≥1 previous psychiatric diagnosis perceived as a misdiagnosis.
- Most frequent perceived misdiagnoses: personality, anxiety, mood disorders — **followed by "chronic fatigue syndrome / burnout-related disorders" and ADHD** (target 5 DIRECT).
- Women (31.7%) report perceived misdiagnoses more than men (16.7%).

**Quality:** High (large register, direct to overshadowing).
**Certainty (raw):** 0.72. **Discounted (0.90):** ≈ 0.65.
**Relevance:** Strongest direct evidence that autistic patients' fatigue is misattributed (here psychiatric); CFS/burnout named as a common perceived misdiagnosis → diagnostic overshadowing of physical energy loss.

---

### 7. Grant, Norton & Hoekstra\ 2025 — Camouflaging and illness perceptions in central sensitivity syndromes
**PMID 42383277 · PMC13315306 · DOI 10.1089/aut.2024.0186 · Autism in Adulthood 2025/2026;8(4):646–663**
**Population:** n=504 adults (autism, CSS, both, neither); CSS = fibromyalgia, IBS, migraine etc.
**Design:** Cross-sectional online (CAT-Q, CSI, PHQ-9, GAD-7, BIPQ).

**Key findings:**
- **Camouflaging significantly predicted CSS symptoms** (target 6 DIRECT).
- Autistic people with a CSS had higher camouflaging than CSS-only; autistic people reported **significantly more negative effect of chronic illness on life** despite no more symptoms than non-autistic people with CSS (greater ND illness burden/QoL impact).
- Authors explicitly raise **diagnostic overshadowing** of autism by a CSS label.

**Quality:** Medium-High (n=504, validated instruments; CSS not pure ME/CFS).
**Certainty (raw):** 0.60. **Discounted (0.85):** ≈ 0.51.
**Relevance:** Direct masking + central-sensitivity link; quantifies that autistic people with chronic-fatigue-adjacent syndromes carry disproportionate mental-health/identity burden.

---

### 8. Rogers et al.\ 2017 — Fatigue in adult ADHD: a trans-diagnostic approach
**PMID 27918087 · DOI 10.1111/bjc.12119 · British Journal of Clinical Psychology 2017;56(1):33–52**
**Population:** n=243 adult ADHD, n=86 CFS, n=211 healthy controls.
**Design:** Cross-sectional between-subjects (Maudsley ADHD + CFS services).

**Key findings:**
- 62% of adults with ADHD met fatigue caseness; ADHD group significantly more fatigued than HC.
- ADHD symptoms significantly greater in the CFS group than HC.
- ADHD and CFS groups **did not differ on functional impairment, mood, or self-efficacy** — shared trans-diagnostic psychological characteristics (double-burden reading, target 1/7).

**Quality:** Medium-High (multi-group clinical sample, transdiagnostic).
**Certainty (raw):** 0.60. **Discounted (0.85):** ≈ 0.51.
**Relevance:** Nearest ADHD-specific overlap study — empirical anchor that adult ADHD and CFS share mood/self-efficacy/functional psychological profile, supporting a distinct ND (ADHD) psych-consequence pathway rather than generic deconditioning.

### 9. Hodge & Meltzoff\ 2026 — Autistic camouflaging and mental health: scoping review
**PMID 42311563 · PMC13270291 · DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1701615 · Frontiers in Psychiatry 2026;17:1701615**
**Population:** 48 studies (autistic participants).
**Design:** Scoping review (qualitative/quantitative/mixed), Cochrane-adapted charting.

**Key findings:**
- Positive relationship camouflaging ↔ poor mental health (depression, anxiety, stress, wellbeing, burnout), effect sizes small–large.
- **Bidirectional** in qualitative studies; association stronger with assimilation than compensation/masking subscales.
- Non-autistic people showed similar camouflaging↔mental-health links.

**Quality:** High for a scoping review, but no ME/CFS-specific content.
**Certainty (raw):** 0.62. **Discounted (0.80):** ≈ 0.50.
**Relevance:** Broad support that masking is a psychological-burden mechanism that, combined with ND energy limits, drives the double burden.

---

### 10. Whaling et al.\ 2026 — Health-condition profiles among autistic adults (latent class)
**PMID 42482145 · DOI 10.1186/s13229-026-00732-1 · Molecular Autism 2026 (online ahead of print)**
**Population:** n=640 autistic adults (autism-affirming telehealth), retrospective chart review.
**Design:** Latent class analysis (30 physical+psych indicators).

**Key findings:**
- 3 classes: Mental+Physical Health (17.7%), Mental Health Predominant (56.3%), Minimal (26.1%).
- Mental+Physical class: high mood/anxiety endorsement **alongside elevated chronic pain, chronic fatigue, headaches, dizziness, IBS, hormone problems** — documents autism + chronic-fatigue + psych comorbidity clustering.

**Quality:** Medium (retrospective chart, telehealth sample; chronic fatigue = indicator not ME/CFS dx).
**Certainty (raw):** 0.50. **Discounted (0.55):** ≈ 0.28.
**Relevance:** Population-level indication that autistic adults who carry physical + psych comorbidity (incl. chronic fatigue) are a distinct, higher-need cluster — background context, not a psych-consequence measure.

---

### 11. Quadt et al.\ 2026 — Likely neurodivergence and variant connective tissue in chronic pain/chronic fatigue
**PMID 41774972 · DOI 10.1016/j.jpsychires.2026.02.036 · Journal of Psychiatric Research 2026;197:125–132**
**Population:** n=83 CP/CF adults vs n=91 non-clinical.
**Design:** Online case-control + joint-hypermobility mediation.

**Key findings:**
- CP/CF patients far more likely to screen likely-autism (aOR 14.3) and likely-ADHD (aOR 12.9).
- Joint hypermobility significantly mediated neurodivergence → CP/CF.
- Authors urge routine ND screening in CP/CF "to optimise fair access to appropriate support for improved quality of life."

**Quality:** Medium-High (case-control, mediation), but predominantly a prevalence/mechanism paper.
**Certainty (raw):** 0.62. **Discounted (0.50 for THIS psych-consequence angle — QoL peripheral, overlap with predisposition stream):** ≈ 0.31.
**Relevance:** Reinforces high ND prevalence in chronic fatigue (data) and flags fair-access/QoL need; mostly overlaps the already-integrated predisposition/hypermobility angle. Included as supporting/context, not a primary psych-consequence anchor.

## Negative / Null Results and Caveats

1. **Comorbid-cohort measurement gap (primary).** No study was found that measures psychological consequences specifically in people with formal ME/CFS + formal autism/ADHD co-diagnosis. Evidence is indirect: autistic burnout in autistic people (overlap), masking in CSS, CFS-as-misdiagnosis in autistic adults, ADHD-vs-CFS comparison. This constrains how strongly the stream can claim "measured psych burden in comorbid patients."

2. **ADHD side is sparse.** Direct ADHD × ME/CFS psychological-consequence literature returned 2–6 hits, mostly overlapping autism/overshadowing or case reports; Rogers 2017 is the main usable anchor.

3. **Loss-of-special-interests target unfound.** Primary data on losing special interests / cognitive identity to ME/CFS energy loss was not located in PubMed. Only Clarey 2026 (identity disruption in autistic burnout) is proximate. Google Scholar/preprint follow-up is a stated gap.

4. **Autistic burnout ≠ ME/CFS (measurement overlap caution).** The strongest papers describe autistic burnout's exhaustion phenotype; they do not diagnose ME/CFS. Using them to argue "autistic burnout is ME/CFS" would overreach. They support a *phenomenological overlap and differential-diagnosis* claim, not co-morbidity equivalence.

5. **Sample-quality spread.** The two definitional papers (Raymaker, Higgins) are high-certainty; the most psychologically specific (Clarey n=11; Ali n=20) are small qualitative studies. Confidence is higher on the existence of the phenomenon than on effect sizes.
