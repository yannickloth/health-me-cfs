## Summary of Key Findings by Historical Period

### 1. Early Outbreaks (1934-1959)

**Primary Sources:**
- **Gilliam1938** (USPHS Bulletin) — first documented ME/CFS outbreak at Los Angeles County Hospital, 1934. 198 staff affected with symptoms mimicking poliomyelitis but with negative polio tests. Described as "atypical poliomyelitis." Crucial because: establishes pre-WWII recognition; demonstrates healthcare workers as first documented cohort; shows outbreak pattern (cluster onset, prolonged convalescence, female predominance).
- **Sigurdsson1950** (Am J Hyg) — 1948-49 Akureyri, Iceland outbreak. 1,106 cases. Coined "Iceland disease." Neurologic symptoms + profound fatigue + prolonged recovery. Key: detailed epidemiological description of post-epidemic cluster.
- **RoyalFree1957** (BMJ) — 1955 Royal Free Hospital London outbreak. 292 staff affected over 4 months. Primary source hospital report documenting clinical features: severe muscle pain, paresis, sensory disturbances, emotional lability, prolonged convalescence (weeks to months). Origin of the term "myalgic encephalomyelitis."
- **Acheson1959** (Am J Med) — seminally synthesized 14 epidemics worldwide (1934–1958): Los Angeles 1934, Akureyri 1948-49, Adelaide 1949, New York State 1950, Middlesex 1952, Coventry 1953, Rockville 1953, Royal Free 1955, Durban 1955, Segbwema 1956, Punta Gorda 1956, Gilfac 1956, Northwest London 1957, Athens 1958. Coined "benign myalgic encephalomyelitis." Key observations: all outbreaks had female predominance, prolonged recovery, neurological involvement, and negative polio testing. Established the syndrome as a distinct clinical entity.

**Controversy:**
- **McEvedy1970** (BMJ) — proposed that Royal Free outbreak was "epidemic hysteria," not organic disease. Argued based on female predominance, absence of mortality, and negative laboratory findings. This paper established the psychosomatic framing that dominated ME/CFS medicine for decades. Subsequently refuted by Ramsay and others.

**Book:**
- **Ramsay1986** — "Myalgic Encephalomyelitis and Postviral Fatigue States: The Saga of Royal Free Disease." Definitive rebuttal of McEvedy's hysteria hypothesis. Ramsay was a physician at Royal Free during the 1955 outbreak. Documented long-term follow-up showing organic sequelae inconsistent with hysteria.

### 2. Incline Village / Lake Tahoe (1984-1985)

- **Buchwald1992** (Ann Intern Med) — described an outbreak of chronic illness at Incline Village/Lake Tahoe, Nevada (1984-85). 259 patients, characterized by severe fatigue, cognitive dysfunction, and immunologic abnormalities. Proposed linkage to chronic Epstein-Barr virus infection. Key role: this outbreak catalyzed CDC engagement and led directly to the Holmes 1988 case definition.

### 3. Diagnostic Criteria Evolution (1988-2021)

**Key Papers:**
- **Holmes1988** (Ann Intern Med) — first CDC working case definition of Chronic Fatigue Syndrome. Required: new onset persistent fatigue + 8 of 11 symptom criteria OR 6 of 11 + 2 of 3 physical criteria. Criticism: too broad; did not require PEM; conflated CFS with idiopathic chronic fatigue.
- **Sharpe1991oxford** (JRSM) — UK Oxford criteria. Required fatigue as principal symptom with definite onset, plus impairment. Widely criticized for being overly broad; included mild cases and depression. Used in PACE trial — core methodological criticism.
- **Fukuda1994** (Ann Intern Med) — CDC revised criteria. Required unexplained persistent fatigue ≥6 months + ≥4 concurrent symptoms from 8 categories. Most widely used research criteria for two decades. Criticism: still too broad; did not require PEM; captured heterogeneous populations.
- **Carruthers2003** (JCFS) — Canadian Consensus Criteria (CCC). First criteria to require post-exertional malaise as cardinal feature. Required: fatigue, PEM, sleep dysfunction, pain, ≥2 neurological/cognitive manifestations, ≥1 autonomic/neuroendocrine/immune manifestation. More restrictive than Fukuda. Important shift: recognized PEM as defining feature, distinguishing ME/CFS from idiopathic chronic fatigue.
- **Carruthers2011** (J Intern Med) — International Consensus Criteria (ICC). Required PEM plus neurological impairment, immune/gastrointestinal/genitourinary impairment, and energy production/transport impairment. Separated "ME" from "CFS" diagnostic groups. Controversial for PENE (post-exertional neuroimmune exhaustion) terminology.
- **IOM2015** (National Academies Press) — "Beyond ME/CFS: Redefining an Illness." Coined SEID (Systemic Exertion Intolerance Disease). Required: substantial reduction/impairment in functioning ≥6 months + PEM + unrefreshing sleep + (cognitive impairment OR orthostatic intolerance). Recommended abandoning CFS name as trivializing. Currently used by CDC.
- **NICE2021** — NG206 guideline. Required all four core symptoms: debilitating fatigue, PEM, unrefreshing sleep, cognitive difficulties. ≥3 months persistence. Explicitly recommends against GET. CBT as supportive only.
- **Brurberg2014** (BMJ Open) — systematic review of 20 case definitions. Found: different definitions select different populations; only 2 of 20 had adequate content validity; Fukuda criteria capture 2.5x more patients than CCC. Quantified the heterogeneity problem.

**Nomenclature Evolution Tracked:**
- atypical poliomyelitis → epidemic neuromyasthenia → benign myalgic encephalomyelitis → Iceland disease → Royal Free disease → ME → postviral fatigue syndrome → chronic fatigue syndrome → CFS → ME/CFS → SEID
- WHO ICD-8 (1967): "benign myalgic encephalomyelitis" under disorders of the nervous system
- WHO ICD-10 (1992): G93.3 — "postviral fatigue syndrome / benign myalgic encephalomyelitis"
- WHO ICD-11 (2022): 8E49 — "postviral fatigue syndrome," subclass of "other disorders of the nervous system"

### 4. The PACE Trial Controversy (2011-2023)

- **White2011** (Lancet) — PACE trial primary report (n=641). Compared CBT, GET, adaptive pacing, and specialist medical care. Claimed CBT and GET were "moderately effective" with recovery rates of 22-30%. Used Oxford criteria for inclusion. Key controversy: recovery thresholds were changed mid-trial; the original protocol defined recovery as SF-36 physical function ≥85 and Chalder fatigue ≤18 (bimodal scoring), but published paper lowered thresholds to SF-36 PF ≥60 and Chalder ≤18 (Likert scoring). At original thresholds, recovery was ~6% in all groups. Extensive FOIA requests and re-analysis.
- **Wilshire2018** (BMC Psychology) — re-analysis of PACE data under original protocol thresholds. Found: CBT/GET effects were non-significant or very small at original recovery thresholds. The mid-trial changes inflated efficacy by ~3-4x.
- **Vink2015** — documented harms from GET; showed that PACE's recovery claims were unsupported by objective measures (6-minute walk test showed no improvement).
- **Vink2018cochrane** — analysis showing Cochrane review of exercise therapy was misleading due to inclusion of Oxford-criteria studies that diluted ME/CFS populations.
- **Geraghty2019GET** — PACE trial patient surveys showing 54-74% harm rates from graded exercise.

**Significance:** The PACE controversy was a watershed moment that led to:
- UK tribunal ordering release of raw data (2016)
- Cochrane withdrawing the GET review (2019)
- NICE reversing its recommendation for GET (2021)
- CDC removing GET from its clinical recommendations (2021)

### 5. IOM 2015 Redefinition

- **IOM2015** — comprehensive NIH-commissioned review by Institute of Medicine (now National Academy of Medicine). Systematic review of 9,000+ studies. Key conclusions: ME/CFS is a "serious, chronic, complex, systemic disease"; "ME/CFS is not a psychiatric disorder"; PEM is the cardinal feature; proposed SEID name; estimated prevalence at 836,000-2.5 million Americans with estimated economic cost of $17-24 billion annually.

### 6. Modern Landmark Studies (2024-2025)

- **Walitt2024NIH** (Nat Commun) — NIH intramural study. Deep phenotyping of 17 PI-ME/CFS patients vs 21 controls. Found: altered effort preference, decreased brain activation in TPJ, CSF catechol abnormalities (decreased DOPA, DOPAC, DHPG), reduced peak VO₂, chronotropic incompetence, B-cell abnormalities, sex-specific gene expression differences. Controversial for emphasizing "effort preference" over physiological mechanisms. Triggered widespread patient and researcher critique.
- **DecodeME2025** (preprint) — largest ME/CFS GWAS. 15,000+ participants, 8 million SNPs tested. Found 8 genome-wide significant loci. SNP heritability 9.5%. MAGMA tissue enrichment overwhelmingly brain-focused. Key genes: CA10, SHISA6, SOX6, LRRC7, DCC, UNC13C. Demonstrates ME/CFS has polygenic architecture with brain tissue enrichment.

## Key Evidence Themes

1. **Recurrent outbreak pattern:** clustered onset in institutional settings, female predominance, neurological symptoms, prolonged recovery, negative standard testing — consistent across 14+ epidemics spanning 1934-1985.

2. **Nomenclature as battleground:** Each name shift (atypical polio → ME → CFS → ME/CFS → SEID) reflects underlying epistemological tensions — is this neurological, psychiatric, or "real"?

3. **Diagnostic criteria as gatekeeping:** Each criteria iteration selects different populations; Fukuda captures 2.5x more patients than CCC; this heterogeneity has been the single biggest confound in ME/CFS research.

4. **PACE as watershed:** The PACE trial's methodological failures (mid-trial threshold changes, Oxford criteria, subjective outcomes only, unblinded design) catalyzed institutional reversals by NICE, CDC, and Cochrane.

5. **From psychosomatic to biological:** McEvedy's 1970 "mass hysteria" paper set a 40-year trajectory of psychosomatic framing. The IOM 2015 report explicitly broke with this: "ME/CFS is a biological illness, not a psychologic disorder." The 2024 NIH study and 2025 DecodeME GWAS provide molecular evidence supporting the biological disease model.

6. **Persistent underfunding:** NIH funding for ME/CFS remains <0.03% of budget, and has been declining since 2024, despite evidence of biological basis and post-COVID surge in prevalence.
