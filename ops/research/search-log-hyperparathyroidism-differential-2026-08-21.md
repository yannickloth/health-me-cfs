## Inclusion Criteria
- Peer-reviewed studies on hyperparathyroidism (primary/secondary/tertiary) presenting with fatigue, cognitive symptoms, muscle weakness, or depression mimicking ME/CFS, CFS, or fibromyalgia
- Studies of serum calcium / PTH screening as part of a pre-CFS/ME/CFS/fibromyalgia-diagnosis exclusion workup
- Prevalence / misdiagnosis studies of hyperparathyroidism in fibromyalgia or chronic-fatigue cohorts
- Case reports/series of hyperparathyroidism misdiagnosed as CFS/fibromyalgia
- Reviews of hypercalcemia/PTH neuropsychiatric and neuromuscular symptoms
- Treatment-outcome studies (parathyroidectomy) documenting reversibility of fatigue/cognitive symptoms
- Diagnostic workup guidelines for ME/CFS exclusion (calcium/PTH)

## Exclusion Criteria
- Secondary hyperparathyroidism purely in chronic renal failure / dialysis populations with no relevance to ME/CFS symptom mimicry
- Hyperparathyroidism associated with cancer or other clearly distinct primary disease
- Purely surgical/imaging technique papers with no symptom or diagnostic-yield relevance
- Animal/in-vitro-only mechanistic studies (not population-relevant)
- Papers where the hyperparathyroidism is incidental to an unrelated primary topic

## Indirect Biochemical / Systemic Link Logic (documented)
- Topic mechanism: hyperparathyroidism raises serum calcium (hypercalcemia) and PTH. Documented biochemical/systemic effects: hypercalcemia (fatigue, cognitive impairment, neuromuscular weakness, thirst/polyuria, depression), elevated PTH (bone pain, neuropsychiatric effects).
- Indirect search pattern: "hypercalcemia AND fatigue", "hypercalcemia AND cognitive impairment", "hypercalcemia AND neuromuscular weakness", "PTH AND fatigue" — mapped the downstream consequences of the topic to chronic-fatigue-relevant symptoms.
- Result: the indirect hypercalcemia/PTH searches returned mostly unrelated papers (renal, parathyroid cancer, sarcoidosis). The strongest indirect support came from the neuropsychiatric reviews (Mairinger 2026, Koman 2022, Oberger Marques 2020, Wootton 2026, Liu 2021) that document the fatigue/cognitive/depression symptom overlap of PHPT directly.
