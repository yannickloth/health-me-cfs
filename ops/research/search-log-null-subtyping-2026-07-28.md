# Search Log: Null Subtyping — Absent vs Blocked vs Overwhelmed Receptor Pharmacology in ME/CFS
## Date: 2026-07-28 | research_stream: null-subtyping

---

## Search Strategy

Objective: Distinguish three distinct types of null drug responses in ME/CFS pharmacodiagnostics:
1. **ABSENT** — receptor/enzyme never present (knockout, genetic null, tissue never developed)
2. **BLOCKED** — receptor present but orthosteric site occupied (autoantibody, competitive ligand, PTM locking inactive state)
3. **OVERWHELMED** — receptor present/functional but outcompeted by supraphysiological endogenous antagonist

Queries targeting: (a) foundational receptor pharmacology, (b) GPCR autoantibodies in ME/CFS/POTS/LC, (c) kynurenic acid/NMDA, (d) allosteric bypass probes, (e) negative/null replication studies, (f) indirect biochemical links.

## Evidence Gaps Identified

1. **No direct study** of salbutamol vs isoproterenol response in β2-AR AAb-positive patients — the allosteric bypass probe remains theoretical
2. **No direct study** of concentration-competition probe (increasing drug concentration to overcome KYNA at NMDA) in ME/CFS
3. **No direct study** of bypass agonist strategy (e.g., β3-AR agonist when β2-AR blocked) in ME/CFS
4. **No replication** of the Scheibenbogen immunoadsorption finding with sham-controlled design yet published (IA-PACS-CFS and EXTINCT completed but results pending)
5. **KYNA levels** in ME/CFS CSF not directly compared to control CSF — most studies are serum/plasma
6. **GPCR AAb functional activity** (not just titer) in ME/CFS — Blaes2011 demonstrated this for CRPS but not replicated in ME/CFS context
