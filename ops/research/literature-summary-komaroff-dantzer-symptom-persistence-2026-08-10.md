# Literature Summary: Komaroff & Dantzer 2025 — Symptom Causes & Persistence

**Topic slug:** `komaroff-dantzer-symptom-persistence` | **Date:** 2026-08-10
**Core source:** Komaroff AL, Dantzer R. Causes of symptoms and symptom persistence in long COVID and ME/CFS. Cell Rep Med 2025;6(8):102259.

## Overview

This is a high-authority REVIEW by the two leading exponents of the neuroimmune/sickness-behavior model (Komaroff — dean of ME/CFS researchers; Dantzer — originator of the cytokine-induced sickness-behavior framework). It consolidates the current mechanistic understanding of how ME/CFS and long COVID symptoms arise and persist. It spans the full pathophysiology: shared underlying abnormalities, the sickness-behavior/torpor symptom-generating pathway, downstream neural circuits, genetic vulnerability, and persistence mechanisms (vicious cycles).

## Evidence landscape

### Strong (certainty ≥ 0.60, replicated / high-authority)
- **KomaroffDantzer2025** (0.78): the review itself — authoritative synthesis spanning all domains.
- **Dantzer2001** (0.52 disc): foundational sickness-behavior framework; the conceptual anchor.
- **Baraniuk2024** (0.62 disc): meta-analysis confirming reduced NK-cell cytotoxicity in ME/CFS (Hedges' g 0.96), with heterogeneity caveat.
- **Hrvatin2020 / Machado2025** (0.31 disc each): Nature-level discoveries of torpor and fever/torpor two-way-switch neural circuits — novel, but animal/in-vitro (0.50 weight).

### Medium / supporting (0.40–0.59)
- **Sommen2026** (0.42): deep-immunophenotyping fatigue signature in long COVID.
- **Augustin2026** (0.42): persistent gut-immune dysregulation in post-COVID.
- **Azcue2026** (0.36): GPCR autoantibodies in post-COVID + CFS.

### Null / negative (constrains claims, already in corpus)
- **Omdal2026LCFatigueStress** (existing): no association of persistent long-COVID fatigue with peripheral inflammatory/cellular-stress biomarkers (CRP, TNF-α, IL-6). **Must be reconciled** with the review's "systemic inflammation → symptoms" arm — resolution is that the review specifies *neuroinflammation* (localized/CNS), which serum markers don't detect.
- **Tuomaala2026TSPOLongCovid** (added in audit): global TSPO-PET availability NOT elevated in LC vs HC — first TSPO-PET study in long COVID (n=14). However, limbic TSPO DVR correlated with symptom severity (QoL rho −0.83 to −0.70; depression/anxiety rho 0.75–0.97 in hippocampus/amygdala). Constrains the "global neuroinflammation" claim to a regionally-specific (limbic) pattern that supports the neural-circuit model while narrowing its spatial scope.

### Missing (not found, notable)
- Direct human evidence that the rodent sickness/torpor neural circuits are chronically engaged in ME/CFS/long COVID. (Framed by the review as a hypothesis backed by analogy + LPS-fMRI data in the broader literature.)
- Primary source for the specific 3-loci long COVID GWAS (HLA-DQA1-DQB, ABO, BPTF-KPAN2-C17orf58) cited as ref 31 by Komaroff-Dantzer 2025 — not located in PubMed; may be a preprint or the review's own formulation. The review itself remains the citation source for this claim.
- TSPO-PET studies in ME/CFS beyond Nakatomi (2014 cortical/limbic finding): no new TSPO-PET ME/CFS study found for 2020–2026 (Tuomaala 2026 is LC-only, with partial null).

## Central mechanistic claim supported

Many peripheral abnormalities (autonomic/endothelial dysfunction, autoantibodies, mitochondrial dysfunction, dysbiosis, redox imbalance) can directly produce symptoms AND converge on neuroinflammation, which activates dedicated sickness-behavior and torpor neural circuits that generate the core symptoms (fatigue, cognitive impairment, pain). Symptom persistence stems from persistent antigen/agent, tissue injury, defective resolution, reactivation, dysbiosis, autoimmunity, a defective circuit "switch," and mutually reinforcing vicious cycles.

## What must remain speculative

- The chronic engagement of specific named rodent neural circuits in human ME/CFS/long COVID (translation gap, animal → human).
- The torpor/AMPK-mTOR hypometabolic-switch being *causally* engaged (vs. downstream damage).

## What the evidence does NOT support
- A single lesion / single infectious agent as the cause.
- That serum inflammatory markers fully capture the inflammation driving symptoms (Omdal null; review pivots to localized neuroinflammation).
- Autoimmunity as a uniformly confirmed mechanism (review: "mixed").
