# Literature Summary: ADHD → Late-Life Cognitive Impairment / Energy-Deficit Mechanism

**Topic slug:** adhd-dementia-energy-deficit
**Date:** 2026-10-08
**Driving paper PDF:** `Literature/neurological/BauerNegrini2026ADHDdementia/BauerNegrini2026ADHDdementia.pdf`
**Mechanistic question:** Is a shared cerebral/neuronal ENERGY DEFICIT (glucose hypometabolism, mitochondrial/bioenergetic failure) a plausible common mechanism linking ADHD/autism → dementia, and by analogy → ME/CFS cognitive symptoms?

## Overview

The driving paper (Bauer-Negrini 2026, JAMA Psychiatry) establishes, with convergent EHR-diagnosis and ADHD-PRS genetic evidence, that ADHD is associated with a ~4-fold hazard of incident MCI/dementia in a large diverse US cohort (All of Us, n=187,341). This Phase-1 search assembled the direct epidemiological context (supporting + contradicting), the competing/confounder mechanisms, and — critically — the indirect ENERGY-DEFICIT mechanism the user asked about (cerebral glucose hypometabolism and mitochondrial failure as a shared link across ADHD/ASD → dementia → ME/CFS).

## Key Findings by Paper

### Direct (ADHD → dementia/MCI epidemiology)

- **Bauer-Negrini 2026 (driving):** ADHD HR 4.60 (3.99–5.29) for incident MCI/dementia; ADHD PRS HR 1.35 (1.25–1.46). Attenuated by comorbidity/education/utilization but persistent (fully adjusted 3.08; matched 2.26). Reverse-causation lag ≥9 y → non-significant. Comorbid ADHD worsens mental health/QoL/social satisfaction/utilization.
- **Tzeng 2019:** Taiwan nationwide; adjusted HR 4.01 (2.53–6.36).
- **Dobrosavljevic 2021:** Sweden n=3.59M; dementia HR 2.92, MCI HR 6.21; psychiatric-comorbidity attenuation to 1.62/2.54.
- **Levine 2023:** Israel n=109,218; adjusted HR 2.77 (2.11–3.63); stimulant-treated → no clear risk increase.
- **Golimstok 2024:** 15-y prospective; dementia HR 3.33, LBD HR 54.5 — ADHD maps to Lewy-body (dopaminergic) trajectory, not amyloid-AD.
- **Rast 2025:** All of Us case-control; NDC (ADHD+autism) → ADRD OR 2.68, PD OR 2.09.
- **Leffa 2023:** ADNI; ADHD PRS predicts cognitive decline + AD pathophysiology in cognitively unimpaired older adults (genetic, not EHR).

### Null / negative

- **Becker 2022:** methodological critique; estimates under- or over-stated (ascertainment/representativeness).
- **Becker 2023:** systematic review; only 7 studies, meta-analysis precluded, direct-effect magnitude undetermined.
- Embedded nulls: Levine 2023 (stimulant-treated null), Dobrosavljevic 2021 (psychiatric attenuation), driving-paper ≥9-y lag non-significance.

### Competing mechanisms / confounders

- **Stimulants (harm/confounder angle):** Levine 2023 (no risk increase in treated); Golimstok 2025 (may attenuate). Direction unresolved — NOT a treatment recommendation.
- **Vascular/cardiometabolic:** Du Rietz 2021 (ADHD → physical conditions, partly genetic); Fluegge 2018 (diabetes removes ADHD→AD link).
- **Psychiatric comorbidity:** Dobrosavljevic 2021.
- **Ascertainment bias:** Becker 2022.

### Indirect energy-deficit mechanism (the critical link)

- **Cunnane 2011:** brain glucose hypometabolism precedes cognitive decline in at-risk AD groups; candidate defects = glucose transport, glycolysis, mitochondrial function; self-reinforcing vicious cycle.
- **Swerdlow 2014:** mitochondrial cascade hypothesis — bioenergetic failure gates amyloid cascade in sporadic AD.
- **Mosconi 2013:** FDG-PET hypometabolism is a decades-early, upstream signature of preclinical AD.

## Strongest Mechanistic Claim Supportable

A shared **cerebral/neuronal energy-deficit** (glucose hypometabolism + mitochondrial/bioenergetic failure) is a *plausible* common mechanism. The chain assembles from already-in-corpus ADHD/ASD energy evidence (Zametkin1990, Killeen2013, Almutairi2024, Berthier2025, BlagojevicStokic2026) → the AD-side energy anchors added here (Cunnane2011, Swerdlow2014, Mosconi2013) → ME/CFS cerebral-hypometabolism/mitochondrial literature already in corpus. The epidemiological ADHD→dementia association (this search) supplies the missing clinical bridge: the same population with a characterized brain energy deficit (ADHD) shows elevated late-life dementia incidence. Fluegge 2018 independently supports a metabolic/energy-mediation channel (diabetes removes the ADHD→AD signal).

## What Must Remain Speculative

- **Causal direction** of energy deficit (upstream driver vs downstream of synaptic loss) — Cunnane explicitly notes the vicious-cycle ambiguity; no MR resolves it.
- **Specificity** of the ADHD→Lewy-body (dopaminergic) vs Alzheimer (amyloid) trajectory — Golimstok 2024 favours a non-amyloid path, which is *more* consistent with an energy/catecholaminergic mechanism than with classic amyloid cascade.
- Transfer of the ADHD/ASD/AD energy-deficit signature *specifically* to ME/CFS cognitive symptoms — the ME/CFS-side hypometabolism is documented but mechanistic identity (same pathways) is inferred, not measured head-to-head.

## What the Evidence Does NOT Support

- A claim that ADHD stimulant treatment *causes* dementia (Levine 2023 null; Golimstok 2025 suggests possible attenuation).
- A clean "ADHD → Alzheimer's amyloid" causal claim (Leffa 2023 shows PRS ↔ AD pathophysiology but not causality; Golimstok 2024 shows LBD, not AD, dominance).
- Discounting the confounding literature: psychiatric comorbidity and ascertainment bias materially attenuate the association (Dobrosavljevic 2021, Becker 2022).

## Study-Date Anchoring (Document Instruction D)

- ADHD-dementia cohort studies span 2018–2026; dementia diagnosed per ICD codes (register/EHR), not biomarker-confirmed. ADHD ascertained by EHR diagnosis or PRS (PRS not subject to reverse causation).
- Driving paper: ADHD PRS residualized on genetic ancestry; associations significant in European and African ancestry strata (European HR 1.38, African HR 1.29) — ancestry-dependent magnitude noted.
