# Literature Summary: Hyperparathyroidism as a Differential Diagnosis for ME/CFS

Date: 2026-08-21
Topic slug: hyperparathyroidism-differential
Scope: Phase 1 evidence review for adding hyperparathyroidism to the endocrine-exclusions differential-diagnosis requirement (src/main/typst/mecfs/part1-clinical/ch05-diagnostic-criteria/sec-08-differential-diagnosis/requirements/req-endocrine-exclusions.typ).

## Evidence Landscape

### Strong (certainty >= 0.60 discounted)
- **Adkisson 2014** (discounted 0.56, Surgery, n=2184): Fibromyalgia symptoms respond to parathyroidectomy. 80/2184 PHPT patients had prior FM; 89% improved in >=1 FM symptom after surgery; cognition/memory improved most (80%). Explicit clinical conclusion: "Before diagnosing FM, providers should exclude PHP [primary hyperparathyroidism], which is surgically correctable."
- **Koman 2022** (discounted 0.53, World J Surg, population n=8279 vs 82,790): PHPT patients use more antidepressants/anxiolytics pre-op; some psychiatric excess resolves after parathyroidectomy.
- **Liu 2021** (discounted 0.53, Surgery, prospective n=244 vs 161): Moderate-severe depression fell 27.5% to 8.2% and anxiety 18.0% to 5.3% after parathyroidectomy.

### Medium
- **Costa 2016** (discounted 0.44, n=100 FM women): 6% hypercalcemic HPT and 17% normocalcemic HPT in FM — a high frequency that may represent misdiagnosis.
- **Ferrari 2015** (discounted 0.48, n=125 FM): PHPT prevalence NOT elevated vs general population — a null counterweight.

### Weak
- **Borgia 2012** (discounted 0.24, case series): HPT a forgotten cause of musculoskeletal pain mistaken for fibromyalgia.

### Reviews (discounted 0.38)
- **Mairinger 2026**, **Oberger Marques 2020**, **Wootton 2026**: document fatigue, cognitive, muscle weakness, depression overlap; diagnostic workup = serum calcium + PTH.

### Missing
- No peer-reviewed case series specifically indexed as "hyperparathyroidism misdiagnosed as chronic fatigue syndrome" (search returned 0). The misdiagnosis evidence is strongest in the fibromyalgia literature (Costa, Adkisson, Borgia).

### Null
- Ferrari 2015: PHPT prevalence in FM not different from general population. This does NOT argue against screening — it argues screening yield is low in the general FM population, but the surgically correctable nature and symptom overlap still justify exclusion (NICE NG206 requires calcium/phosphate).

## Indirect Biochemical / Systemic Links
- Topic does (biochemically): raises serum calcium and PTH. Downstream effects: hypercalcemia -> fatigue, cognitive impairment, neuromuscular weakness, thirst/polyuria, depression; elevated PTH -> bone pain, neuropsychiatric effects.
- Searches for these downstream effects in fatigue contexts returned mostly unrelated renal/parathyroid-cancer/sarcoidosis papers. The strongest indirect support came from PHPT-specific neuropsychiatric reviews, which directly document the fatigue/cognitive/depression symptom overlap.

## Internal Contradictions
- **Costa 2016** (high HPT frequency in FM, suggests misdiagnosis) vs **Ferrari 2015** (prevalence not elevated vs general population). Resolvable: Costa screened a small single-centre FM sample and reported higher-than-age-expected rates; Ferrari compared to population prevalence figures. Both are compatible with the clinical reality that HPT is a low-yield but high-consequence differential to exclude. Framing for Phase 3: present both (misdiagnosis case evidence + null prevalence), weight toward "routine exclusion is low-yield but warranted because surgically correctable."

## Strongest Mechanistic/Clinical Claim Supportable
- Primary hyperparathyroidism produces fatigue, cognitive impairment, muscle weakness, and depression that overlap ME/CFS and fibromyalgia; some of these symptoms are reversible after parathyroidectomy; serum calcium (with PTH if indicated) should be part of the pre-ME/CFS-diagnosis exclusion workup.

## Must Remain Speculative
- The proportion of ME/CFS-diagnosed patients who actually have occult primary hyperparathyroidism is unknown (no direct ME/CFS-cohort prevalence study found). Evidence is strongest in fibromyalgia, not ME/CFS per se.

## Not Supported (null)
- Routine screening of all fibromyalgia patients is NOT high-yield (Ferrari null), but this does not negate the exclusion requirement.

## Primary Integration Targets
- ch05 sec-08 differential-diagnosis, req-endocrine-exclusions.typ: add a Hyperparathyroidism requirement block (serum calcium + PTH; distinguishing features; treatment: parathyroidectomy for primary, vitamin D correction for secondary). ## Clinical Relevance Assessment
- Clinical relevance: HIGH for the differential-diagnosis requirement — a missed primary hyperparathyroidism is surgically correctable and would otherwise be mislabelled ME/CFS. Subset: all severity levels (screening is a pre-diagnosis step).
- Off-label clinical experience: N/A (this is a diagnostic exclusion, not a treatment).
