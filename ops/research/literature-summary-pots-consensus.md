# Literature Summary: POTS Consensus and Non-POTS Dysautonomia

Date: 2026-09-01
Topic slug: pots-consensus
Phase: 1 (literature research) of /integrate-topic
Augments: pots-mecfs (existing integration topic)

## Topic Summary

A modified-Delphi international expert consensus (Sivakoti 2026) that (a) critiques the ≥30 bpm (adults) / ≥40 bpm (adolescents) HR-rise diagnostic threshold as too restrictive, (b) recognizes a "non-POTS dysautonomia" population with orthostatic intolerance + autonomic dysfunction + functional impairment who do not meet the HR threshold, and (c) calls for standardized nomenclature and classification. Not a mechanistic or trial study — actionable content is diagnostic-criteria critique, consensus recommendations, and research direction.

## Papers Included (6 NEW — all verified absent from corpus)

### 1. Sivakoti et al.\ 2026 — International Multidisciplinary Expert Consensus (PRIMARY)
- **Citation:** Am J Med, 2026, online ahead of print. doi:10.1016/j.amjmed.2026.08.012. PMID 42665152.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (modified Delphi consensus; adequate-to-rigorous methodology but expert opinion, not replicated empirical data)
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (POTS/dysautonomia patients)
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.55 × 0.80 = 0.44
- **Key contribution:** The anchor for the whole topic. All 31 statements reached >90%–100% consensus (24/31 unanimous); critiques the HR threshold, recognizes non-POTS dysautonomia, calls for standardized nomenclature/classification.

### 2. Parsaik et al.\ 2013 — Orthostatic Intolerance Without Postural Tachycardia
- **Citation:** Clin Auton Res. 2013;23(4):181–188. doi:10.1007/s10286-013-0199-5. PMID 23729158.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.60 (peer-reviewed; Mayo Clinic retrospective, n=184 total [84 POTS + 100 OI]; single study)
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (OI/POTS)
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.60 × 0.80 = 0.48
- **Key contribution:** Empirical basis for the "non-POTS dysautonomia" construct — OI-without-tachycardia patients share clinical presentation, autonomic findings, and deconditioning with POTS, with only mild additional dysautonomia in POTS.

### 3. Chung and Raj 2026 — POTS: A Review (JAMA)
- **Citation:** JAMA, 2026, online ahead of print. doi:10.1001/jama.2026.14809. PMID 42635998.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (authoritative peer-reviewed review; no original data)
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (POTS/general dysautonomia)
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.55 × 0.80 = 0.44
- **Key contribution:** Confirms current consensus criteria and documents underrecognition, ~70% functional impairment, 24-month diagnostic delay, 30–40% post-infectious onset. Provides the "current state" the consensus reacts against.

### 4. Uppal et al.\ 2026 — Randomized Crossover Trial: Ivabradine vs Propranolol vs Placebo
- **Citation:** JACC: Advances. 2026;5(6 Pt 2):102795. doi:10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.102795. PMID 42132710.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.60 (randomized placebo-controlled crossover RCT; but small n=28)
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (POTS)
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.60 × 0.80 = 0.48
- **Key contribution:** Treatment/harm dimension. Both rate-slowing drugs lower HR below the POTS diagnostic threshold and are preferred over placebo; symptom/QoL response is more selective — supports individualized treatment and calls the HR-threshold model into question.

### 5. Mauriello et al.\ 2026 — Answers to Common Questions About POTS and Chronic Orthostatic Intolerance
- **Citation:** Pediatr Ann. 2026;55(3):e108–e113. doi:10.3928/19382359-20260112-04. PMID 41784480.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.40 (peer-reviewed Q&A/review; lower-tier journal; no original data)
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (pediatric POTS)
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.40 × 0.80 = 0.32
- **Key contribution:** Explicitly addresses adolescents who seem to have POTS but do not meet criteria — the exact subthreshold population the consensus seeks to capture.

### 6. Boris et al.\ 2020 — Orthostatic Heart Rate Does Not Predict Symptomatic Burden in Pediatric COI
- **Citation:** Clin Auton Res. 2020;30(1):19–28. doi:10.1007/s10286-019-00622-y. PMID 31385108.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (peer-reviewed; CHOP retrospective; single center)
- **Population weight:** 0.80 (pediatric POTS/OI)
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.55 × 0.80 = 0.44
- **Key contribution:** Null/critique evidence — HR rise 30–39 bpm vs ≥40 bpm shows no clinical difference across 28 symptoms; directly supports the claim that a specific HR threshold is neither predictive nor definitive.

## Cross-Cutting Themes
1. The HR-rise threshold (≥30/≥40 bpm) is questioned as not predictive of symptomatic burden (Boris 2020, Mauriello 2026, Sivakoti 2026)
2. A dysautonomic population exists below the HR threshold and is currently undiagnosed (Parsaik 2013, Sivakoti 2026)
3. Treatment response is individualized and may not correlate with HR lowering (Uppal 2026 RCT)
