## Headline finding (the gap under test)

**Klein et al. 2023 (Nature) — the essay's core long-COVID immune-profiling citation — was
NOT in the paper's bibliography.** It is now added (`@Klein2023longcovidprofiling`), filling the
single largest novel gap. The paper already covers T-cell exhaustion/checkpoints, immune
tolerance, and herpesvirus reactivation (see "already-covered" note), so this search focused
on the long-COVID → ME/CFS **convergence** literature and the **immunotherapy** angle.

**Convergence verdict:** Direct head-to-head comparison shows **comparable immune/inflammatory
profiles** between ME/CFS and long COVID (Petrov 2025: no significant biomarker differences
between groups; both show lower lymphocytes/CD8/NK + higher IL-6/TNF). This supports the paper's
existing convergent-immunopathology framing and adds a novel direct-comparison evidence base.

**Caveats that must travel with the finding (MANDATORY, per Doc-Instruction B):**
1. **Long-COVID immune changes are partly reversible** (Phetsouphanh 2024: PD-1/TIM-3 exhaustion
   and antibody elevation resolve by 24 months in mild-moderate long COVID) — a contrast with
   ME/CFS persistence that the integration must not paper over.
2. **EBV reactivation is NOT causal in mild long COVID** (Hoeggerl 2023 null) and the EBV signal
   in long COVID is **epitope-specific**, not general reactivation or autoimmunity (Lorenz 2026).
3. **Peripheral cytokine/cortisol are inconsistent long-COVID biomarkers** (Fleischer 2024 null),
   conflicting with Klein 2023's lower-cortisol finding.
4. **Immunotherapy (checkpoint blockade/CAR-T) has no ME/CFS evidence base** — the treatment angle
   is restricted to IVIG/rituximab/TPE, which failed in unselected populations (Kaplan 2026).

## Per-paper evaluation

Discounted certainty = raw certainty × population weight.

### Thread 1 — Long-COVID immune profiling

#### 1. Klein et al. 2023 — Distinguishing features of long COVID identified through immune profiling (Nature)
- **Design/sample:** Cross-sectional, n=275 (long COVID vs matched controls), multidimensional
  immune phenotyping + unbiased machine learning (Yale Immunobiology + Mount Sinai, PI Iwasaki/Putrino).
- **Findings:** Reduced naive CD4+/CD8+ T cells; exhausted T cells (PD-1, TIM-3); exhausted B cells;
  elevated nonclassical monocytes. Exaggerated humoral responses against SARS-CoV-2 AND against
  other viruses (esp. EBV, VZV — higher antibody titres). Lower morning cortisol. ML separates
  long COVID from controls.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.85 (Nature, n=275, multi-site, machine learning, top-journal).
- **Population weight:** 0.85 (long COVID).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.72.
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** The essay's exact citation; provides the canonical long-COVID immune
  signature (T-cell exhaustion + EBV/VZV antibody over-reactivity + hypocortisolism) that the
  paper can cite as convergent with ME/CFS. Directly feeds the ch08 checkpoint/exhaustion narrative.
- **Limitations:** Cross-sectional (no causality); self-report symptom grouping; not ME/CFS itself.
- **Cohort-overlap check:** Yale LISTEN / Mount Sinai PASC cohorts, 2020–2022 recruitment.
  PI Iwasaki (Yale), Putrino (Mount Sinai). No overlap with the included ME/CFS studies
  (Bulgaria, Australia, Germany). No shared biobank with other included papers.

#### 2. Phetsouphanh et al. 2024 — Improvement of immune dysregulation in long COVID at 24 months (Nat Commun) — **RESOLUTION/null-direction**
- **Design/sample:** Longitudinal 24-month follow-up, Australian ADAPT cohort (Kirby Institute,
  UNSW Sydney; PI Kelleher/Matthews), mild-moderate long COVID vs recovered controls.
- **Findings:** Early features (elevated nucleocapsid IgG, higher neutralizing capacity, elevated
  PD-1/TIM-3 on CD4+/CD8+ T cells at 3–8 months) RESOLVED by 24 months. scRNA-seq at 24 mo showed
  reconstituted naive T/B subsets, no exhaustion-score difference. 62% reported QoL improvement.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.70 (Nat Commun, longitudinal, scRNA-seq, but mild-moderate only).
- **Population weight:** 0.85 (long COVID).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.60.
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Critical counterweight: long-COVID immune activation is time-limited
  and partly reversible, whereas ME/CFS features persist — the integration must distinguish
  transient long-COVID changes from stable ME/CFS biomarkers.
- **Limitations:** Mild-moderate cohort (may not capture ME/CFS-phenotype long COVID); single site.
- **Cohort-overlap check:** ADAPT cohort (Kirby Institute, Australia), 2020–2021 recruitment.
  Same group as Phetsouphanh2022immuneDysfunction (already in corpus, PMID 35318473 — the ADAPT
  8-month paper). **FLAG: same PI (Kelleher/Matthews) AND same ADAPT biobank as the existing
  Phetsouphanh2022immuneDysfunction entry** — longitudinal extension, not independent replication.

### Thread 2 — Long-COVID → ME/CFS immune convergence

#### 3. Petrov et al. 2025 — Comparable immune alterations in ME/CFS and long COVID (Biomedicines)
- **Design/sample:** Cross-sectional n=190 (65 CFS, 54 long COVID, 70 controls; Plovdiv, Bulgaria;
  Maes-collaborated). Lymphocyte subsets + cytokine profiling.
- **Findings:** Both conditions vs controls: lower lymphocytes, CD8+ T cells, NK cells; higher
  IL-6, TNF, IL-4, IL-10. **No significant biomarker differences between ME/CFS and long COVID.**
- **Raw certainty:** 0.60 (n=190, peer-reviewed, but single-center, Fukuda criteria).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS included).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.60.
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Direct evidence for immune convergence — the paper's convergent-
  immunopathology claim gains a head-to-head comparative anchor.
- **Limitations:** Single center; Fukuda (broad) criteria; limited cytokine panel; cross-sectional.
- **Cohort-overlap check:** Plovdiv, Bulgaria, Medical University of Plovdiv cohort; recruitment
  window not explicitly 2020s-shared with Klein/ADAPT. **FLAG: shares the Plovdiv CFS cohort with
  the existing Petrov2026Immunophenotyping (PMID 42196466) entry** — same PI (Petrov/Taskov/Maes),
  likely overlapping patient base (65 CFS subset). Not independent of the corpus Petrov entry.

#### 4. Ivanovska et al. 2025 — Differential characteristics: long COVID vs ME/CFS (Biomedicines)
- **Design/sample:** Narrative review comparing clinical presentation, pathophysiology, treatment.
- **Findings:** Both involve viral persistence, immune dysregulation, endothelial dysfunction,
  autoimmunity (relative contributions uncertain); immune dysfunction flagged as shared mechanism.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.45 (narrative review, non-systematic).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS included).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.45.
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Confirms immune dysfunction as the shared thread; framing support only.
- **Limitations:** Non-systematic; repeats contested CBT/GET claims without full critical appraisal.
- **Cohort-overlap check:** N/A (review). Same author group as Petrov 2025/2026 (Plovdiv).

### Thread 2b — EBV/VZV reactivation & cortisol/cytokine in long COVID (null/negative + refinement)

#### 5. Hoeggerl et al. 2023 — EBV reactivation is not causative for post-COVID syndrome (BMC Infect Dis) — **NULL**
- **Design/sample:** Prospective cohort of SARS-CoV-2 seropositive blood donors with
  asymptomatic/mild COVID (Salzburg, Austria; PMU Salzburg, PI Laner-Plamberger).
- **Findings:** Determined PCS rate; EBV was NOT reactivated in individuals reporting PCS in this
  mild-disease population (neopterin + EBV markers).
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (prospective, but mild-only donor population, single site).
- **Population weight:** 0.85 (long COVID).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.47.
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Tempering evidence — EBV reactivation may be a severity/dysregulation
  marker rather than causal, cautioning against a causal EBV-reactivation model of post-viral illness.
- **Limitations:** Mild-disease population; may miss severe long COVID where reactivation is more prominent.
- **Cohort-overlap check:** Salzburg blood-donor cohort, 2020–2021; no overlap with other included papers.

#### 6. Lorenz et al. 2026 — Enhanced antibody reactivity to specific EBV EBNA1 epitopes in PCS (Scand J Immunol)
- **Design/sample:** Retrospective cross-sectional n=96 (48 PCS vs 48 convalescents; Rostock, Germany;
  PI Müller-Hilke). Peptide microarray + ELISA for EBV EBNA1 IgG; autoantibody screen.
- **Findings:** Overall EBNA1 IgG binding similar, but PCS showed stronger reactivity to specific
  epitopes (glycine-alanine repeat residues 90–325; central 405–419, distinct from MS epitope).
  No autoantibody increase.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.50 (small, retrospective, explorative).
- **Population weight:** 0.85 (long COVID).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.43.
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Refines the EBV signal to epitope-specific enhanced reactivity — relevant
  to the EBV autoantibody picture in ME/CFS (not general reactivation/autoimmunity).
- **Limitations:** n=96, retrospective, exploratory.
- **Cohort-overlap check:** Rostock University cohort, 2021–2023; no overlap with other included papers.

#### 7. Fleischer et al. 2024 — Cytokines and cortisol may not be reliable PASC biomarkers (Ther Adv Neurol Disord) — **NULL**
- **Design/sample:** Prospective cohort n=178 (91 ongoing PASC, 40 resolved, 34 infected no PASC,
  13 never infected; Essen, Germany; PI Kleinschnitz). Serum IL-1β/IL-6/TNFα + cortisol.
- **Findings:** No cytokine/cortisol differences between groups.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (n=178, prospective, but single-timepoint peripheral blood, ELISA).
- **Population weight:** 0.85 (long COVID).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.47.
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Caution against single-timepoint peripheral cytokine/cortisol as stable
  biomarkers (conflicts with Klein 2023 lower-cortisol); relevant to ME/CFS cytokine-biomarker claims.
- **Limitations:** Single timepoint; the study's own "non-organic genesis" conclusion is not
  warranted by a null biomarker result (interpretive caution flagged).
- **Cohort-overlap check:** Essen University cohort, 2021–2022; no overlap with other included papers.

### Thread 3 — Immunotherapy treatment angle

#### 8. Kaplan 2026 — Therapeutic plasma exchange & immunomodulatory strategies in post-infectious syndromes (Transfus Apher Sci)
- **Design/sample:** Narrative review of immune dysregulation across PTLDS, long COVID, ME/CFS,
  PANS/PANDAS; evaluates IVIG, rituximab, TPE.
- **Findings:** Immune-targeted treatment evidence strongest in biomarker-defined subsets; phase III
  RituxME (ME/CFS) and phase II TPE (post-COVID) FAILED in unselected populations.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.50 (narrative, single-author integrative-medicine affiliation).
- **Population weight:** 0.85 (mixed post-infectious, ME/CFS included).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.43.
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** The treatment angle for the essay's immunotherapy framing — reinforces
  that blanket immunotherapy lacks support without immune-subset stratification; a caution against
  borrowing oncology checkpoint/CAR-T approaches without selection biomarkers.
- **Limitations:** Non-systematic; single author; integrative-medicine affiliation.
- **Cohort-overlap check:** N/A (review).

## Bottom line for Phase 2

- **Direction: INTEGRATE**, focused on the long-COVID → ME/CFS immune-convergence thread.
  Klein 2023 is the anchor (0.72 discounted), Petrov 2025 the direct-comparison support (0.60).
- **Mandatory caveats:** reversibility of long-COVID immune changes (Phetsouphanh 2024),
  non-causal EBV in mild cases (Hoeggerl 2023), epitope-specific EBV signal (Lorenz 2026),
  unreliable single-timepoint cytokine/cortisol (Fleischer 2024), and absence of ME/CFS
  immunotherapy evidence (Kaplan 2026).
- **Immunotherapy/CAR-T/AlphaFold:** NO ME/CFS evidence base found. The essay's checkpoint-inhibitor
  and CAR-T framing must remain speculation gated by the existing autoimmune-risk limitation in ch08;
  AlphaFold/AI is deferred (methodological, not pathophysiology).
