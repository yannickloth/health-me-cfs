## 1. Pharmacodiagnostic / Therapeutic-Diagnostic Literature

### Laragh1988DiagnosisExJuvantibus — Category 1 (FOUNDATIONAL)

- **PMCID:** 3049337 | **Journal:** Hypertension (1988)
- **Design:** Review/conceptual paper
- **Key claim:** Individual response patterns to 5 classes of antihypertensives (diuretics, CCBs, α-blockers, β-blockers, ACE inhibitors) classify hypertensive patients into biologically relevant groups. An agent antihypertensive in one patient may increase BP in another or have no effect in a third.
- **Quality:** High — published in Hypertension; well-cited conceptual paper
- **Certainty:** 0.60 (conceptual, not empirical trial; strongly cited; mechanism-based)
- **Relevance:** Directly establishes the "diagnosis ex juvantibus" principle — using drug response heterogeneity for diagnostic classification. First explicit statement of the pharmacodiagnostic concept in cardiovascular medicine.

### Samuel2019Nof1Hypertension — Category 1

- **PMID:** 30842257 | **Journal:** Pediatrics (2019)
- **Design:** N-of-1 crossover trial; n=32 children with hypertension; compared lisinopril, amlodipine, hydrochlorothiazide via repeated ABPM
- **Key finding:** No single medication preferred for >50% of patients; 49% preferred lisinopril, 24% amlodipine, 12% HCTZ; 4/32 uncontrolled on any monotherapy. Bayesian credible intervals reported.
- **Quality:** High — Pediatrics journal; rigorous crossover design; direct clinical application
- **Certainty:** 0.75
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates N-of-1 methodology for personalizing drug choice using patient-level response data. Methodologically translatable to ME/CFS pharmacodiagnostic matrix.

### Samuel2023Nof1RCT — Category 1

- **PMID:** 36227203 | **Journal:** Am J Hypertens (2023)
- **Design:** RCT comparing n-of-1 trial-informed treatment (n=23) vs usual care (n=26) in pediatric hypertension
- **Key finding:** 69% probability n-of-1 trials increased BP control at 6 months (Bayesian OR 1.24, 95% CrI 0.51–2.97). 93% probability of greater systolic BP reduction in n-of-1 group (mean difference −3.6 mmHg).
- **Quality:** High — RCT design; Bayesian analysis; peer-reviewed
- **Certainty:** 0.70
- **Relevance:** Direct evidence that n-of-1 methodology improves outcomes; Bayesian framework directly applicable to pharmacodiagnostic matrix inference.

### Monini2006SilentReflux — Category 1

- **PMID:** 16846931 | **Journal:** Acta Otolaryngol (2006)
- **Design:** RCT; n=60 patients with suspected laryngopharyngeal reflux; PPI vs immunostimulant
- **Key finding:** PPI treatment used as "ex adjuvantibus" diagnostic criterion; significant improvement in laryngeal features in PPI group vs immunostimulant group
- **Quality:** Medium — small RCT; single center; specialty journal
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Relevance:** Clinical application of diagnosis ex juvantibus: response to therapeutic probe used as diagnostic evidence. Reflux → PPI response confirms diagnosis.

## 2. Constraint-Satisfaction / Multi-Drug Diagnostic Methods

### Friston2023ComputationalPsychiatry — Category 2

- **PMID:** 36056173 | **Journal:** Mol Psychiatry (2023)
- **Design:** Review — computational psychiatry framework
- **Key claim:** Brain as an organ of inference and prediction; generative models underwrite sentient processing and scientific diagnosis. Computational phenotyping as key method. Dysconnection → aberrant belief updating → false inference.
- **Quality:** High — Mol Psychiatry; Karl Friston (leading theorist); comprehensive framework
- **Certainty:** 0.55 (theoretical review, not empirical trial)
- **Relevance:** Provides formal mathematical framework (Bayesian inference, active inference, generative models) for pharmacodiagnostic reasoning. Drug response = new evidence that updates diagnostic belief.

### Schwartenbeck2016ComputationalPhenotyping — Category 2

- **PMID:** 27517087 | **Journal:** eNeuro (2016)
- **Design:** Methods paper with worked example — 2-step maze task, active inference model
- **Key finding:** Demonstrates how computational model parameters can be inferred from behavioral data and used to classify between-subject variables (e.g., diagnosis). Shows cross-validation approach.
- **Quality:** Medium — well-executed methods paper; single worked example
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Relevance:** Concrete demonstration of computational phenotyping pipeline — parameter estimation → classification. Direct analogue for drug-response-based diagnostic classification.

### Strauss2021DiseasePhenotypes — Category 2

- **PMID:** 34314651 | **Journal:** J R Soc Interface (2021)
- **Design:** Population-wide (n=9M) medical claims data; network science + machine learning
- **Key finding:** Higher-order diagnosis features (disease interactions) better characterize patient health states. Data-driven detection of metabolically healthy vs unhealthy obesity phenotypes. Disease network captures synergistic interactions.
- **Quality:** High — large population dataset (n=9M); rigorous methodology; J R Soc Interface
- **Certainty:** 0.70
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates that multi-disease interaction patterns yield phenotypic subtypes invisible to single-disease analysis. Directly analogous: multi-drug response patterns reveal bottleneck subtypes invisible to single-drug analysis.

## 3. ME/CFS-Specific Medication Response Literature

### Scheibenbogen2018Immunoadsorption — Category 3

- **PMID:** 29543914 | **Journal:** PLoS One (2018)
- **Design:** Open-label pilot; n=10 post-infectious ME/CFS with elevated β2AdR autoantibodies; 5-day IA
- **Key finding:** 7/10 reported rapid improvement during IA; 3/10 had long-lasting moderate-to-marked improvement (6–12+ months); 2/10 short improvement only; 2/10 improved after initial worsening. Heterogeneous response pattern.
- **Quality:** Medium — small n; open-label; pilot trial
- **Certainty:** 0.40
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates ME/CFS treatment response heterogeneity — different response trajectories to same intervention → evidence for mechanistic subgroups. Pharmacodiagnostic principle in action.

### Wirth2021SkeletalMuscle — Category 3

- **PMID:** 33882940 | **Journal:** J Transl Med (2021)
- **Design:** Hypothesis paper
- **Key claim:** β2AdR dysfunction → Na+/K+-ATPase impairment → sodium overload → NCX reversal → calcium overload → mitochondrial dysfunction → metabolic/vascular vicious circle → PEM. NHE1 upregulation lowers re-induction threshold.
- **Quality:** Medium — hypothesis paper; rigorous mechanistic chain; no direct trial
- **Certainty:** 0.35
- **Relevance:** Detailed mechanism linking β2AdR dysfunction to skeletal muscle pathology. If correct, patients with this mechanism should respond to β2AdR-targeted interventions — testable via pharmacodiagnostic matrix.

### Kim2023SjogrenFatigue — Category 3

- **PMID:** 37568396 | **Journal:** J Clin Med (2023)
- **Design:** Cross-sectional; n=19 pSS patients assessed by CCC for ME/CFS
- **Key finding:** Only 4/18 pSS fulfilled CCC for ME/CFS. Handgrip strength fully recovered 1h post-exertion in pSS (contrast to ME/CFS). β1-, β2-, M4-receptor autoantibodies correlated with disease activity but not fatigue severity.
- **Quality:** Medium — small n; single center
- **Certainty:** 0.50
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates differential diagnosis via exercise response: recovery pattern distinguishes pSS fatigue from ME/CFS PEM. Exemplifies drug/physiological challenge as diagnostic tool.

### FalagueraVera2020PressurePoint — Category 3

- **PMID:** 33142896 | **Journal:** Int J Environ Res Public Health (2020)
- **Design:** Prospective cohort; manual physiotherapy in fibromyalgia with/without ME/CFS comorbidity
- **Key finding:** Pre-treatment pressure point thresholds inversely correlate with treatment response. ME/CFS comorbidity significantly modifies response — FM+ME/CFS patients respond differently than FM-only. Post-stratification analysis reveals subgroup-specific treatment effects.
- **Quality:** Medium — moderate n; exploratory subgroup analysis
- **Certainty:** 0.45
- **Relevance:** Direct ME/CFS evidence: baseline biomarkers (PPT) + comorbidity status predict differential treatment response. Two-axis constraint on pharmacodiagnostic matrix.

## 4. Analogous Methods in Other Diseases

### Hu2024ParkinsonSubtyping — Category 4

- **PMID:** 39331104 | **Journal:** J Parkinsons Dis (2024)
- **Design:** Review
- **Key claim:** PD subtyping at clinical, genetic, imaging, and molecular levels improves prediction of medication response, clinical endpoints, and progression trajectory. Subtyping reduces confounding heterogeneity in drug trials. Multi-modal classification needed.
- **Quality:** High — J Parkinson's Disease; comprehensive review
- **Certainty:** 0.55
- **Relevance:** PD subtyping directly parallels proposed ME/CFS pharmacodiagnostic subtyping. Medication response is an explicit subtyping dimension.

### Zhang2022RuxolitinibHLH — Category 4

- **PMID:** 35344583 | **Journal:** Blood (2022)
- **Design:** Prospective clinical trial; n=52 newly diagnosed pediatric HLH; ruxolitinib monotherapy with response-based stratification
- **Key finding:** Overall response rate 69.2% at day 28; 42.3% sustained CR. All responders achieved first response within 3 days. Response significantly associated with underlying etiology (EBV-HLH most sensitive, 87.5% ORR). 57.7% stratified to additional chemotherapy based on early response.
- **Quality:** High — Blood journal; prospective trial
- **Certainty:** 0.75
- **Relevance:** Directly demonstrates response-based stratified treatment: early drug response reveals etiology and guides next step. Paradigm for pharmacodiagnostic decision tree.

## 5. Null/Negative/Cautionary Literature

### Schaaf2024ReinforcementLearning — Category 5

- **PMID:** 37684495 | **Journal:** Behav Res Methods (2024)
- **Design:** Test-retest reliability study; n=69+47 across two learning paradigms; 5-week interval
- **Key finding:** Reinforcement learning model parameters showed poor test-retest reliability (ICC 0.02–0.52 bandit task; 0.01–0.71 reversal learning task). Mood (stress, happiness) partly explained within-participant variability. Computational phenotyping parameters are unstable.
- **Quality:** High — large sample for reliability study; rigorous methods; Behav Res Methods
- **Certainty:** 0.70
- **Relevance:** Critical caution: if RL parameters are unstable, drug-response-derived parameters may also be unstable across repeated assessments. Pharmacodiagnostic matrix must account for within-patient variability.

### Ravichandran2024ActiveLearning — Category 5

- **PMID:** 39628837 | **Journal:** Front Artif Intell (2024)
- **Design:** Algorithmic study; 4 active learning algorithms × 2 human heuristics × 3 classifiers × 15 datasets
- **Key finding:** Human labeling heuristics (fast-and-frugal tree, tallying) significantly degrade active learning performance — sometimes below random. Proposed inverse information density algorithm achieved 87% improvement over alternatives.
- **Quality:** Medium — simulation study; not clinical; but rigorous comparison
- **Certainty:** 0.50
- **Relevance:** Caution: clinician heuristics/biases in labeling "responder" vs "non-responder" degrade diagnostic inference. Algorithm must be robust to labeling bias.

## 6. Information-Theoretic / Algorithmic Methods

### Newberry2016DiagnosisGout — Category 6

- **PMID:** 26985540 | **Journal:** AHRQ Comparative Effectiveness Review (2016)
- **Design:** Systematic review of diagnostic tests for gout
- **Key finding:** Clinical algorithms (Diagnostic Rule: sensitivity 88%, specificity 75%; CGD: sensitivity 97%, specificity 96%) can form a diagnostic decision tree, with invasive/imaging tests reserved for ambiguous cases. Evidence strength rated low due to limited validation.
- **Quality:** Medium — AHRQ report; well-conducted review; limited primary validation
- **Certainty:** 0.55
- **Relevance:** Explicit diagnostic decision tree methodology: clinical algorithm → rule in/out → escalate to more definitive test. Paradigm for pharmacodiagnostic decision tree: drug A response → narrows hypotheses → drug B to distinguish remaining.

**Evidence quality assessment:** 5 high-certainty papers (≥0.60), 9 medium-certainty (0.40–0.59), 2 low-certainty (<0.40). Literature supports pharmacodiagnostic methodology as a feasible but underdeveloped approach. The Laragh1988 paper provides a strong conceptual foundation in hypertension. N-of-1 trial methods (Samuel 2019, 2023) demonstrate practical implementation. ME/CFS-specific evidence exists but is limited — treatment response heterogeneity is documented but not systematically leveraged for diagnosis. Computational phenotyping and Bayesian frameworks (Friston 2023, Strauss 2021) provide the mathematical infrastructure. Critical cautions about parameter instability (Schaaf 2024) and labeling bias (Ravichandran 2024) must be addressed in any implementation.

**Key gap:** No paper directly describes a multi-drug, cross-hypothesis pharmacodiagnostic matrix for any disease. The concept is novel and synthetically assembled from the above evidence base.
