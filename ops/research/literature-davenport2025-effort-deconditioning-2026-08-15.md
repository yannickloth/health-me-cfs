## Primary Paper

### Davenport TE et al. (2025) — Altered Effort and Deconditioning Are Not Valid Explanations of ME/CFS
- **Journal:** Nature Communications 16:9176 (Matters Arising)
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41467-025-64538-0
- **Design:** Formal rebuttal (correspondence) of Walitt et al. 2024 (Nat Commun 15:907) "altered effort preference" / deconditioning interpretation of NIH deep-phenotyping data.
- **Key arguments (7):**
  1. Single CPET cannot capture PEM (cannot measure response to an initial exertional provocation); 2-day CPET is the correct paradigm (IOM 2015 cautioned single CPET may be insufficient).
  2. Deconditioning and PEM are not mutually exclusive.
  3. Walitt's samples were not adequately matched with deconditioned controls.
  4. Exercise HR was *lower* in PI-ME/CFS vs healthy volunteers — deconditioning produces *elevated* HR — so data argue for impaired oxidative metabolism + chronotropic incompetence, against deconditioning.
  5. PEM is not "discomfort" but profound exacerbation (IOM 2015 systemic exertion intolerance disease).
  6. EEfRT not validated in ME/CFS; increasing PEM during the test confounds effort interpretation; misattributes symptoms to psychological/motivational factors.
  7. Underpowered: only 8 PI-ME/CFS + 9 HC undertook CPET; possibly mildest subgroup; outcome order omitted from abstract.
- **Conclusion:** Effort-preference/deconditioning framing unsupported; physiology (reduced HR, chronotropic incompetence) fits impaired oxidative metabolism, not deconditioning.
- **Limitations:** Correspondence (no new data); underlying CPET cohort n=17; relies on secondary interpretation.
