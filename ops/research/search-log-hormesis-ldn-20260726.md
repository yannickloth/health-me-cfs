## Inclusion Criteria
- Any study of naltrexone/naloxone dose-response within the clinical LDN range (0.5–6mg)
- Biphasic/hormetic/non-monotonic dose-response involving naltrexone, naloxone, TLR4, or opioid receptors
- LDN microglial immunomodulation with dose-dependent effects
- Calabrese hormesis framework applied to immune/inflammatory pharmacology
- Clinical LDN dose-escalation or dose-comparison studies
- Compensatory endorphin upregulation with naltrexone
- Filamin A binding as ultra-low-dose mechanism distinct from standard LDN

## Exclusion Criteria
- Full-dose naltrexone (50mg+) only, without LDN comparison
- Naltrexone for addiction/opioid use disorder without dose-response or immune relevance
- Not English full text
- Pure manufacturing/formulation papers (PLGA microparticles, compounding)
- Plant/herb hormesis with no naltrexone/TLR4 connection
- Non-mammalian immune developmental studies (medaka fish)

## Key Papers Identified (22)

### Category 1: Direct LDN Dose-Response Studies
1. **Bruun-Plesner2020** (32068870) — First dose-response exploration in FM; ED50=3.88mg, ED95=5.40mg; high interindividual variation
2. **Younger2009** (19453963) — First controlled LDN pilot in fibromyalgia; 4.5mg, 30% symptom reduction; ESR predicted 80% variance in response
3. **Ologunowa2025** (40272382) — Meta-analysis: LDN SMD −1.03 vs baseline, ns vs placebo; I²=91% for pain; null finding note

### Category 2: Biphasic/Hormetic Naltrexone
4. **Dara2023** (37371715) — Naltrexone has "biphasic dose-dependent pharmacodynamic effects"; LDN shows "hormetic effects" for chronic pain
5. **Hammerslag2020** (31862556) — Naltrexone shows inverted-U curve for fentanyl demand in rats; increased initial intake at low doses

### Category 3: Microglial TLR4 Dose-Dependent Mechanism
6. **Kučić2021** (34445130) — LDN shifts BV-2 microglia from M1 to M2; metabolic switch glycolysis→OXPHOS; dose-dependent mTOR/S6K modulation
7. **Kwilasz2021** (32905811) — (+)-naltrexone blocks TLR2+TLR4; prevents EAE memory deficits and hippocampal neuroinflammation
8. **Patel2022** (34821408) — LDN timing differentially affects EAE outcomes; prophylactic vs therapeutic; OGF/IL-17 serum modulation

### Category 4: TLR4 Compensatory/Biphasic
9. **Wadowska2022** (36165203) — MCPIP1 mediates endotoxin tolerance via TLR4 deubiquitination; demonstrates compensatory pathway

### Category 5: Opioid Receptor Compensatory Mechanisms
10. **Boyadjieva2004** (15210757) — Naltrexone disrupts μ/δ opioid feedback in splenocytes; upregulates δOR binding → enhanced NK cytolytic response
11. **Toljan2018** (30248938) — Review: LDN (1-5mg) vs VLDN (1μg-1mg) vs ULDN (<1μg); filamin A binding at ULDN; distinct dose-range mechanisms

### Category 6: Filamin A / TIR Domain
12. **Burns2010** (20726836) — Filamin A pentapeptide as high-affinity naltrexone target; PTI-609 designed from this discovery

### Category 7: General Hormesis Framework (Calabrese)
13. **Calabrese2021Nrf2** (33667690) — Nrf2 as generalized mediator of hormetic dose responses; redox-activated adaptive anti-inflammatory upregulation
14. **Calabrese2021UltraLow** (34157423) — Ultra-low doses (≤1 molecule/cell) trigger hormetic responses via amplification cascades
15. **Calabrese2023NO** (36764605) — NO-induced hormetic effects in immune, cardiovascular, neuronal systems; biphasic dose-response quantification
16. **Calabrese2023PFAS** (42362174) — PFAS hormesis across immune cell types; quantitative features generalize to pharmacologics

### Category 8: Clinical Reviews and Scoping
17. **Rupp2023** (37302106) — LDN scoping review for centralized pain; notes "variability in dosing paradigms and time to response"
18. **Beaudette-Zlatanova2023** (37045708) — Null RCT: LDN 4.5mg not superior to placebo in arthritis pain; small n=23

### Category 9: LDN Anti-Inflammatory Mechanism Details
19. **Choubey2020** (32943552) — LDN binds SIRT1; abrogates hyperinsulinemia-mediated NF-κB translocation; macrophage repolarization
20. **Osman2024** (38850786) — Naltrexone (4mg/kg in rats) as TLR4 inhibitor; reduces MYD88-TRIF pathway; confirms intestinal I/R protection

### Category 10: Endorphin Compensatory Upregulation
21. **Kreek1992** (1346939) — Chronic naltrexone upregulates opioid receptors; normalization of beta-endorphin with chronic methadone
22. **Berrendero2010** (20170672) — Endogenous opioid system in nicotine dependence; naltrexone effective in smoking cessation; opioid receptor upregulation documented

## Key Patterns

### Evidence Strength
- **Strongest direct evidence:** Bruun-Plesner2020 (dose-response ED50/ED95 within LDN range), Dara2023 (explicit "biphasic" and "hormetic" characterization of naltrexone)
- **Strongest mechanistic evidence:** Kučić2021 (microglial M1→M2 metabolic switching, dose-dependent), Boyadjieva2004 (μ/δ receptor feedback disruption → NK enhancement), Calabrese2021Nrf2 (general hormesis mechanism)
- **Strongest behavioral inverted-U:** Hammerslag2020 (naltrexone inverted-U curve for fentanyl demand)
- **Critical null:** Ologunowa2025 (meta-analysis: LDN not superior to placebo), Beaudette-Zlatanova2023 (RCT: no benefit in arthritis)

### Gap Analysis
- **No studies directly comparing 1.5mg vs 3mg vs 4.5mg LDN outcomes** in any disease — the specific within-range dose-response question remains unanswered by direct evidence
- **No within-range dose-response studies in ME/CFS specifically**
- **No studies of chronic/LDN dose microglial adaptation** — Kučić2021 is acute (24h), Patel2022 is closest (8 days treatment)
- **Gold-standard hormetic model (Calabrese) not yet applied specifically to naltrexone** — Dara2023 is the closest
- **LIFT trial (NaculLDN2025)** may provide dose-response data but results pending

### Summary
The hypothesis that LDN shows non-monotonic dose-response within the clinical 0.5–4.5mg range is **plausible and mechanistically grounded** but **not yet directly demonstrated** by head-to-head dose-comparison trials. The hormetic framework (Calabrese) provides a unifying theoretical model: low-dose TLR4 blockade may trigger Nrf2-mediated compensatory anti-inflammatory upregulation while higher doses within range push past the compensatory window into net suppression. Evidence is strongest from: (1) Bruun-Plesner's ED50/ED95 estimation showing wide interindividual variation, (2) Dara's explicit biphasic/hormetic characterization, (3) Kučić's demonstration of dose-dependent microglial metabolic switching, and (4) Boyadjieva's μ/δ receptor feedback disruption.
