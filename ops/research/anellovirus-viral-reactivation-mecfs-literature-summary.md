## Papers

### 1. Briese et al. 2023 — Multicenter Virome Analysis in ME/CFS (@Briese2023MulticenterVirome)
- **Source:** J Med Virol 95(8):e28993, DOI 10.1002/jmv.28993. (Abstract + Crossref metadata; full text blocked by cookie wall.)
- **Design:** Multicenter case–control; PCR + high-throughput sequencing of blood, feces, saliva.
- **Key finding:** No consistent group-specific viral differences. **Lower prevalence of anelloviruses in ME/CFS cases vs controls.**
- **Conclusion:** Future investigation should focus on adaptive immune responses, not viral-gene-product surveillance.
- **Raw certainty 0.60; population weight 1.00 (ME/CFS cohort) → discounted 0.60.**
- **Relevance:** Direct ME/CFS evidence. Null result for productive viral replication; supports immune-dysregulation/adaptive-immune focus.

### 2. Maguire et al. 2026 — Virus Reactivation in Acute and Long COVID (IMPACC) (@Maguire2026ViralReactivation)
- **Source:** Nature, DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10740-z. Full text read via pdftotext.
- **Design:** Longitudinal prospective multi-omic cohort; n=1154 hospitalized COVID-19 patients (IMPACC), up to 12 months.
- **Key findings:**
  - Widespread reactivation of Herpesviridae (EBV, CMV, HSV1) and Anelloviridae in **immunocompetent** patients — NOT explained by immunosuppression.
  - **Anelloviridae transcripts significantly more prevalent in the long-COVID physical-disability PRO group**, controlling for age, sex, immunosuppression, acute severity (adjusted P=0.012).
  - Anelloviridae associated with neutrophil-degranulation gene enrichment; negatively associated with oxidative-stress-protective metabolites.
  - Anelloviridae previously linked to **chronic fatigue syndrome** and multiple sclerosis (cites Grinde; TTV 80–90% population prevalence).
  - Reactivation dynamics differ by virus; external-cohort validation.
- **Raw certainty 0.60; population weight 0.85 (long COVID / post-viral) → discounted 0.51.**
- **Relevance:** Strong post-infectious precedent. Anelloviridae = immune-dysregulation signature associated with persistent physical disability (fatigue, PEM, cognitive dysfunction overlapping ME/CFS symptoms).

## Synthesis of the two papers (cross-paper logic)
- Briese: no productive viral replication signal in ME/CFS → viral-gene-product surveillance null.
- Maguire: viral reactivation (esp. Anelloviridae) is a marker of immune dysregulation (dysvirosis) associated with persistent post-infectious disability, not necessarily productive infection.
- Joint implication: Anelloviridae burden reflects **immune state** (adaptive/innate immune competence), not an ongoing productive viral infection. Briese's lower anellovirus in ME/CFS + Maguire's association of anellovirus with long-COVID physical disability are consistent with an immune-dysregulation interpretation of the virome rather than a direct viral cause.

## Evidence quality
- Strong (≥0.60): Briese (ME/CFS-specific multicenter).
- Strong (≥0.60 raw): Maguire (large n, Nature, externally validated) — discounted 0.51 by long-COVID population.
- Missing: direct ME/CFS cohort measuring both anellovirus burden AND adaptive immune correlates in the same patients.
- Null: Briese (no group viral differences except anelloviruses).

## Competing mechanisms
- Alternative to "immune-dysregulation-mediated virome shift": cohort/technical effects (sequencing platform, PCR primer bias, sampling compartment), medication effects, disease-stage differences.
