# Literature Summary: Crash-Phase Near-Syncopal / Orthostatic-Collapse Phenomenology in PEM

**Date:** 2026-08-14
**Agent:** literature-integrator
**Research stream:** crash-phase-near-syncope-pem
**Anecdote anchor:** single unverifiable social-media post describing "sinking into a black hole, approaching loss of consciousness without losing consciousness, plug pulled / system shutdown." NOT integrable. Only peer-reviewed evidence is in scope.

---

## Headline finding (the gap under test)

**No peer-reviewed paper names a distinct "crash-phase near-syncope / impending loss of consciousness" as a named feature of the PEM crash itself.** Searches for `"post-exertional malaise" AND syncope/presyncope/near-syncope` return **0 PubMed hits** in every direct-phrase variant. The near-syncopal / fainting quality in ME/CFS is uniformly reported in the literature under **orthostatic intolerance (OI) — syncope/presyncope provoked by *upright posture* during tilt-table testing or standing**, not as a quality of the post-exertional crash. The closest crash-adjacent evidence is a submaximal-exercise PEM provocation study (Rayhan & Baraniuk 2021) reporting reduced global cerebral blood flow and symptom exacerbation after exercise.

**Verdict on feature under test: the crash-phase near-syncopal quality is NOT named in the literature.** What IS established:
1. High prevalence of orthostatic syncope/presyncope/NMH/POTS in ME/CFS (Bou-Holaigah 1995; Stewart 1998; Jason 2024).
2. Cerebral hypoperfusion during orthostatic stress (Novak 2026; van Campen deconditioning 2021 — already in corpus) — **but directly contradicted by Razumovsky 2003 (null CBFV difference)**.
3. Orthostatic intolerance correlates with worse PEM (Jason 2024).
4. Treatment null for NMH-targeted fludrocortisone (Rowe 2001).

## Per-paper evaluation

Certainty scale: High 0.7–1.0 (n>100, top journal, replicated); Medium 0.4–0.69 (n=20–100, peer-reviewed, single); Low 0.0–0.39 (n<20, preprint, flawed).
Population weight: ME/CFS 1.00; Long COVID 0.85; POTS/FM/comorbid 0.80; general 0.75; animal 0.50; in vitro 0.40; in silico 0.30.
Type: **(a)** = near-syncopal quality DURING crash phase (feature under test) vs **(b)** = upright orthostatic syncope only.

### 1. Bou-Holaigah 1995 — NMH and CFS (JAMA)
- **Design/sample:** Case-comparison, n=23 CFS + 14 healthy controls, 3-stage tilt-table test.
- **Findings:** Abnormal upright-tilt response in 22/23 CFS (96%) vs 4/14 controls (p<.001); 70% of CFS had abnormal response in stage 1 (no controls).
- **Type:** **(b)** upright OI only — syncope/presyncope on standing/tilt. No crash-phase attribution.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (n=23, JAMA top journal, landmark/foundational concept, single study, not formally replicated at this scale).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS cohort).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.55.

### 2. Stewart 1998 — NMH and autonomic dysfunction in children with CFS (Clin Auton Res)
- **Design/sample:** n=16 CFS children (11–19 y) + 26 syncope patients + 13 controls; HRV during 40-min 80° HUT.
- **Findings:** 13/16 CFS children fainted during HUT; 5/13 pure vasodepressor syncope; abnormal autonomic modulation in those who fainted.
- **Type:** **(b)** upright OI only (fainting on tilt). Pediatric.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.50 (n=16, peer-reviewed, single study, small n).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS cohort, pediatric).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.50.

### 3. Rowe 2001 — Fludrocortisone acetate to treat NMH in CFS: RCT (JAMA)
- **Design/sample:** Double-blind placebo RCT, n=100 randomized (83 with adequate outcome data); CFS + tilt-provoked NMH.
- **Findings:** Fludrocortisone no better than placebo (14% vs 10% ≥15-point wellness improvement, P=.76); no difference in other symptom scores or follow-up tilt result. Confirms NMH is provoked by orthostatic stress in CFS, but NMH-targeted monotherapy is a **treatment null**.
- **Type:** **(b)** upright OI (NMH provoked on tilt). Null/negative for treatment.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.65 (RCT, n=100, JAMA top journal, single RCT).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS cohort).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.65.

### 4. Razumovsky 2003 — Cerebral and systemic hemodynamics during upright tilt in CFS (J Neuroimaging) — **NEGATIVE for mechanism**
- **Design/sample:** n=26 CFS + 23 controls; transcranial Doppler MCA CBFV + BP/HR/ET-CO2 during 3-stage HUT.
- **Findings:** CBFV did NOT differ between CFS and controls in any posture or at termination of tilt; only difference was lower end-tidal CO2 in CFS at termination (P=.002). Conclusion explicitly "not consistent with the hypothesis" that orthostatic CBFV reduction distinguishes CFS.
- **Type:** Neither (a) nor (b) — directly challenges the cerebral-hypoperfusion-during-orthostatic-stress mechanism underpinning the near-syncope hypothesis. **Competing/negative.**
- **Raw certainty:** 0.50 (n=49 total, peer-reviewed, single study, contradicts van Campen CBF findings — unresolved conflict).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS cohort).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.50.

### 5. Rayhan & Baraniuk 2021 — Submaximal exercise provokes PEM + reduced global CBF (Front Neurosci) — **closest to crash-phase**
- **Design/sample:** n=34 ME/CFS + 24 controls; 2 submaximal bicycle exercise tests on consecutive days bracketed by MRI, OI, and symptom assessments before/after exercise.
- **Findings:** Exercise-induced increased spontaneous activity in the anterior default-mode-network node (medial PFC) in ME/CFS (decreased in controls); controls overall had higher BOLD signals → reduced global cerebral blood flow in ME/CFS. Framed as a biomarker of PEM/symptom exacerbation.
- **Type:** **(a)-adjacent** — exercise-triggered PEM symptom exacerbation with reduced global CBF; the only paper where the near-syncopal/cerebral-perfusion change is tied to the post-exertional state rather than tilt. Does NOT explicitly report "near-syncope" or "impending LOC."
- **Raw certainty:** 0.55 (n=34+24, peer-reviewed, single study, novel paradigm not independently replicated).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS cohort).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.55.

### 6. Jason 2024 — Head-up tilt table test as a measure of autonomic functioning in ME/CFS (J Pers Med)
- **Design/sample:** n=193 adults with ME/CFS; single head-up tilt; DePaul Symptom Questionnaire.
- **Findings:** 32.5% demonstrated POTS or OH on tilt. Those with POTS/OH had significantly more problems with **post-exertional malaise** and sleep, and worse physical/health function.
- **Type:** **(b)** upright OI, but establishes a direct **OI ↔ PEM association** — orthostatic intolerance is worse in patients with more severe PEM. Bridges OI to the PEM state.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.60 (n=193, peer-reviewed, single study, established Jason lab).
- **Population weight:** 1.00 (ME/CFS cohort).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.60.

### 7. Novak 2026 — Shared autonomic phenotype of Long COVID and ME/CFS (PLoS One) — **mechanistic, large**
- **Design/sample:** n=170 ME/CFS + 143 Long COVID + 73 HC + 290 hEDS; autonomic tests + transcranial Doppler CBFv + SFN biopsy + ICPET (2018–2023, Brigham & Women's Faulkner).
- **Findings:** Reduced orthostatic CBFv (88% ME/CFS, 92% LC); mild-moderate widespread autonomic failure (89%/95%); SFN (53%/67%); POTS (19%/22%); **neurogenic orthostatic hypotension (15%/15%)**; preload failure (92%/96%). Lab tests did not distinguish conditions.
- **Type:** **(b)** orthostatic cerebral hypoperfusion + orthostatic hypotension — mechanism under tilt/orthostatic stress, not crash-phase. Supports the cerebral-hypoperfusion substrate.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.70 (n=170 ME/CFS, n=313 combined; PLoS One peer-reviewed; large; single center/study).
- **Population weight:** 0.90 (mixed ME/CFS 1.00 + LC 0.85 cohort; ME/CFS dominates). 
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.63.

### 8. Tokumasu 2022 — Clinical characteristics of ME/CFS diagnosed in Long COVID (Medicina)
- **Design/sample:** Retrospective descriptive, n=279 Long COVID patients at Okayama University aftercare clinic; ME/CFS by Fukuda/CCC/IOM.
- **Findings:** ME/CFS prevalence 16.8% among Long COVID patients meeting the three criteria; detailed clinical characteristics including orthostatic/autonomic features.
- **Type:** **(b)** general ME/CFS clinical characterization in a Long-COVID cohort; documents the post-infectious population in whom OI/PEM features cluster. Background population.
- **Raw certainty:** 0.50 (n=279, peer-reviewed/MDPI, single-center retrospective, descriptive).
- **Population weight:** 0.85 (Long COVID cohort).
- **Discounted certainty:** 0.43.

## Indirect mechanistic links
- **Cerebral hypoperfusion substrate:** Novak 2026 (88% ME/CFS reduced orthostatic CBFv), van Campen CBF series (already in corpus), Rayhan 2021 (reduced global CBF post-exercise). Establishes that cerebral blood flow is impaired under orthostatic/exertional stress in a large fraction of ME/CFS — the plausible physiological substrate for a "sinking/impending-LOC" crash sensation.
- **Neurogenic orthostatic hypotension:** Novak 2026 documents 15% of ME/CFS and Long COVID with neurogenic OH — a true blood-pressure-collapse phenotype (not just HR-based POTS).
- **OI ↔ PEM association:** Jason 2024 shows tilt-table OI correlates with worse PEM — links orthostatic instability to the PEM state.

---

## Bottom line for Phase 2
- **Feature under test (crash-phase near-syncope as a named PEM quality): NOT supported by direct literature** — the term is absent.
- **Closest indirect support:** Rayhan 2021 (exercise-triggered PEM + reduced global CBF) is the only paper tying cerebral-perfusion change to the post-exertional state rather than tilt.
- **Well-supported adjacent facts:** upright orthostatic syncope/NMH/POTS is common in ME/CFS; cerebral hypoperfusion during orthostatic stress is common but contradicted by Razumovsky; OI correlates with worse PEM.
- **Direction:** Likely **DEFER or PARTIAL**. If integrated, the defensible claim is limited to: (1) ME/CFS has high upright-orthostatic syncope/presyncope prevalence, and (2) orthostatic cerebral hypoperfusion is a plausible substrate for a crash sensation — but the *specific* "crash-phase near-syncope" experiential feature cannot be cited to any paper. No paper supports a distinctive near-syncopal quality intrinsic to PEM distinct from upright OI.
