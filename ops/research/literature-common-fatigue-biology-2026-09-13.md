## Scope

The claim that pathological fatigue across Long COVID, ME/CFS, SLE, Sjögren's, RA, psoriatic arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis, systemic sclerosis, ANCA-associated vasculitis, sarcoidosis, idiopathic inflammatory myopathies, myasthenia gravis, multiple sclerosis, IBD (Crohn's/UC), coeliac disease, primary biliary cholangitis, cancer, infectious mononucleosis/EBV, and immune-checkpoint-inhibitor (ICI) therapy reflects:

1. **A shared neuroimmune programme** — cytokine-to-brain signalling / sickness behaviour.
2. **An immune-metabolic energetic conflict** — "selfish brain" vs "selfish immune system" (glucose/energy redistribution away from the brain during immune activation).
3. **Mechanistic heterogeneity** — multiple objective subtypes, not one universal biomarker.

**Exclusions:** papers already in the corpus (reused by PMID); GET/CBT trial literature; cohort re-analyses of included cohorts (Sandler 2022 DIOS); theoretical framing papers unrelated to fatigue prevalence or ME/CFS.

## Synthesis by Claim

### Claim (a) — Shared neuroimmune fatigue programme
- `Morris2016NeuroImmuneFatigue` is the explicit unifying review: same cytokine/oxidative/dopaminergic/mitochondrial pathways across RA, SLE, Sjögren's, cancer, MS, stroke and ME/CFS.
- `Arnett2012InflammatoryFatigueSickness` supplies the conceptual bridge from sickness behaviour to ME/CFS.
- Cross-disease prevalence is high and remarkably consistent: SLE 67–90%, MS ~80%, axSpA 56%, PsA 51%, IBD 47% (72% active), MG 42–82%, PBC >50%, cancer 49%.
- The strongest *mechanistic* human support is `Li2025ICIfatigue` (Th1/effector-CD8 signature tracks fatigue onset) and `Hickie2006DubboCohort` (pathogen-nonspecific post-infective fatigue).

### Claim (b) — Immunometabolic energetic conflict ("selfish brain" vs "selfish immune system")
- `Kealy2020InflammationBrainEnergy` (mouse + human CSF): immune activation lowers blood and CSF glucose; spontaneous-activity suppression tracks glucose and is rescued by glucose — the mechanistic core.
- `Titlestad2024BrainGlucoseDelirium` (n=406 humans): impaired brain glucose utilization during an inflammation-associated neuropsychiatric state.
- `Fenckova2024SelfishBrainImmune` names the framing but is only a highlight (cert 0.26).
- ME/CFS-side energy-redistribution links already in corpus (`Lacourt2018InflammationEnergy`, `VanCampenhout2025EnergyImmune`, `KomaroffDantzer2025SymptomPersistence`).

### Claim (c) — Subtype / biomarker heterogeneity
- `Ruiter2020MyastheniaFatigue`: central fatigue dissociated from peripheral fatigability (42–82% prevalence despite controlled neuromuscular transmission).
- `Newton2007PBCautonomic`: a non-inflammatory autonomic axis (40% vs 6% orthostatic) independently predicts fatigue and cognitive symptoms.
- `Harper2021VasculitisFatigue`: fatigue persists in disease remission.
- `deKleijn2009SarcoidosisFatigue`, `Kawka2021SLEfatigue`, `Basta2018SScFatigue`: fatigue dissociated from disease-activity parameters.
- `Lynch2022PBCfatigue`: explicit central ("lack of intention") vs peripheral ("lack of ability") distinction.
- Together these support multiple distinct maintenance mechanisms (cytokine-driven, autonomic, central-motivational) — i.e. heterogeneity, not one universal biomarker.

## Contradictions Found

| Paper A claims | Paper B claims | Nature of tension |
|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Morris2016 / Arnett2012: a *common* inflammatory pathophysiology underlies fatigue across diseases | Kawka2021 / deKleijn2009 / Basta2018: fatigue is dissociated from disease-activity/inflammatory parameters | Universality of the cytokine-driven mechanism vs evidence that inflammation does not track fatigue severity |
| DSilva2022 / Hickie2006: inflammation/acute-illness severity modulates fatigue (active>remission; severity predicts PICS) | Harper2021 / Ruiter2020: substantive fatigue persists in remission / with controlled disease | Inflammation is necessary-but-not-sufficient; a second, inflammation-independent mechanism must exist |
| Kealy2020 (mouse): immune activation reduces brain glucose → suppressed activity (bioenergetic causality) | Li2025ICI (human): fatigue tracks Th1/effector-CD8 *immune activation* without objective tumour response | Immune-activation level, not energy substrate per se, is the human correlate — unresolved whether cytokines act via energy redistribution or directly on brain circuits |
| Fenckova2024 "selfish brain vs selfish immune" energetic-conflict framing | Yamagata2017 (excluded) frames the same conflict in mood disorders, not fatigue | The framing is borrowed across conditions; no study directly tests the allocation conflict in ME/CFS |

## Null / Negative Findings

- **No eligible null/negative meta-analysis** was identified that directly tests the "shared fatigue biology" claim and refutes it. The closest corpus-resident null evidence is biomarker-heterogeneity work (e.g. `Taccori2023` urinary-biomarker SR/MA) — already integrated.
- Multiple disease-specific reviews report fatigue **not** statistically related to disease activity (SLE, sarcoidosis, SSc) — a negative for the strong "inflammation drives fatigue" version, handled above as a contradiction.
- **IFN-alpha 33% persistent-fatigue study:** not verified. No PubMed record matching the thread's cited "33% persistent fatigue" IFN-alpha study was found within 6 screening queries. Closest primary evidence is `Majer2008` (IFN-alpha motor slowing/fatigue, PMID 18258414) and the existing corpus entry `Capuron2012dopamineinterferon`. **Flag: thread's specific IFN-alpha statistic is unverified — treat as unsupported until a source is produced.**
- Prednisone-treated sarcoidosis patients reported *more* fatigue (deKleijn2009) — a possible harm signal for glucocorticoid management of fatigue, observational.
