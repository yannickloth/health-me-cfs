# Literature Summary — Iron Redox Polarity: ME/CFS vs Long COVID Divergence

## Key Finding: Therapeutic Bifurcation Emerging

The literature reveals a critical divergence in iron handling between ME/CFS and Long COVID that has direct clinical implications. The data support a model where ME/CFS involves **hepcidin-dependent functional iron deficiency** (high ferritin, low serum iron, low TSAT — iron present but sequestered) while Long COVID involves a more complex **hepcidin-independent iron dysregulation** with distinct temporal dynamics and a broader pattern of iron maldistribution affecting erythropoiesis and lymphocyte function.

Kavyani2023's low hepcidin finding may reflect chronic hypoxia-driven suppression, compensatory iron mobilization attempt, or a distinct ME/CFS-specific hepcidin biology different from classic inflammatory anemia.

The Hanson2024 single-cell data revealing monocyte iron loading concurrent with lymphocyte iron demand is mechanistically novel.

## Key Findings Relevant to ME/CFS vs LC Iron Polarity

### 1. Ferritin Elevation: Shared Feature, Different Context

Both ME/CFS and post-COVID ME/CFS show elevated ferritin (Baklund2021, Yamamoto2023). However:
- **In ME/CFS:** Ferritin elevation accompanies low serum iron + low TSAT + low hepcidin — functional iron deficiency pattern where iron is present but trapped in macrophages.
- **In Long COVID:** Ferritin can be elevated (hyperferritinaemia in 35% at 60d, Sonnweber2022CovILD) but this coexists with genuine iron deficiency (24%), anemia, and aberrant stress erythropoiesis (Hanson2024). The ferritin is not just an acute-phase reactant — it reflects a multi-compartment iron redistribution problem.
- **Ferritin as predictor:** Yamamoto2023 found ferritin >193 µg/L in post-COVID ME/CFS vs <100 in non-ME/CFS long COVID. But Hadidchi2025 found acute-phase ferritin did NOT predict subsequent ME/CFS. The timing matters: ferritin at follow-up > ferritin during acute infection.

### 2. Hepcidin: Opposite Findings

- **ME/CFS:** Kavyani2023Hepcidin found hepcidin decreased nearly by half. This is unexpected for classic inflammatory anemia (where IL-6 → ↑hepcidin → iron sequestration). Possible explanations: chronic hypoxia suppressing hepcidin, compensatory attempt to mobilize iron, or ME/CFS-specific dysregulation.
- **Long COVID:** Gietl2024PostCOVIDlab found hepcidin was higher in patients who recovered vs those with persistent symptoms at 12 months. This suggests adequate hepcidin response may be protective — or that low hepcidin in persistently ill patients reflects ongoing iron dysregulation.

### 3. Erythrocyte Function and Oxygen Transport

KronsteinWiedemann2024RBC demonstrates a key LC-specific finding: impaired erythrocyte O2 binding with increased CO binding, decreased plasma iron and TSAT, and elevated MCH. This is not described in ME/CFS literature. The RBC dysfunction → tissue hypoxia pathway may be LC-specific and distinct from ME/CFS energy metabolism defects.

### 4. Ferroptosis: Theoretical Target in Both

Sousa2023Ferroptosis and Mantle2025FerroptosisOverlap both identify ferroptosis (iron-dependent lipid peroxidation cell death) as shared mechanism. COVID-19 creates an iron overload + oxidative stress environment that triggers GPX4 inactivation — this is distinct from the functional iron deficiency of ME/CFS. In ME/CFS, the ferroptosis risk may arise from chronic low-grade oxidative stress + impaired antioxidant capacity rather than frank iron overload.

## Evidence Gaps

| Gap | Severity | Description |
|-----|----------|-------------|
| **No head-to-head iron panel comparison** | Critical | No study compares full iron panel (serum Fe, ferritin, TSAT, sTfR, hepcidin, erythropoietin) between ME/CFS and Long COVID patients in the same analysis. All comparisons are indirect across separate studies with different methods, populations, and timepoints. |
| **Hepcidin in Long COVID** | High | Only Gietl2024 measured hepcidin in LC (n=34). No LC study has measured hepcidin, ferritin, TSAT, and sTfR simultaneously to phenotype iron handling. |
| **Hepcidin in ME/CFS** | High | Only Kavyani2023Hepcidin measured hepcidin (n=? not specified for this marker). Needs replication. |
| **Iron intervention trials** | Critical | No RCT of iron supplementation OR iron chelation in ME/CFS or post-viral fatigue. All treatment recommendations are extrapolated from IDWA and anemia literature. |
| **Ferroportin/IRP biology** | Moderate | No direct measurement of ferroportin, IRP1/IRP2, or heme oxygenase-1 in ME/CFS or LC. |
| **Brain iron imaging** | Moderate | MRI iron quantification (QSM, R2*) has not been applied to ME/CFS or LC cohorts. Basal ganglia iron accumulation in neurodegeneration suggests this is relevant. |
| **Sex differences** | Moderate | Yamamoto2023 and Morita2024 note female-predominant ferritin elevation but sex-stratified analysis of iron handling in ME/CFS vs LC is absent. |
| **Null results for iron supplementation** | Low | Existing literature (systematic review in energy-metabolism.bib) found no consistent benefit of iron supplementation in CFS — but none of these studies used functional deficiency criteria (ferritin + TSAT) to select patients. |

## Clinical Implications: Therapeutic Bifurcation

The emerging model creates a dangerous therapeutic fork:

| | ME/CFS Functional Iron Deficiency | Long COVID Iron Dysregulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Pattern** | High ferritin, low Fe, low TSAT, low hepcidin | Variable: hyperferritinaemia + iron deficiency + anemia + RBC dysfunction |
| **Iron supplementation** | **Potentially harmful** — iron is present but trapped; adding more iron may worsen oxidative stress/ferroptosis risk | **May help subset** — genuine iron deficiency (24%, Sonnweber2022CovILD) and anemia (9%) respond to iron in non-ME/CFS populations |
| **Iron chelation** | **Theoretical benefit** — reducing macrophage iron load could improve iron availability; but no trials | **Unclear risk/benefit** — may worsen anemia and O2 delivery in patients with true deficiency |
| **Hepcidin-directed** | **Agonist?** — low hepcidin may contribute to iron maldistribution; hepcidin agonists could restore iron compartmentalization | **Unclear** — recovered patients had higher hepcidin; agonists might help or worsen depending on phase |
| **Anti-ferroptosis** | CoQ10, vitamin E, GPX4 enhancers — already in treatment armamentarium | Same agents likely beneficial; ferroptosis risk from inflammatory iron overload |
| **Key caveat** | **Must rule out absolute iron deficiency first** — ME/CFS patients with low ferritin (<30 µg/L) should be treated with iron regardless of ferritin elevation in population means | **Phenotype before treating** — measure full panel (Fe, ferritin, TSAT, sTfR, Hb, CRP) before deciding iron vs anti-inflammatory vs chelation approach |

The most dangerous error: giving iron to an ME/CFS patient with high ferritin and low TSAT (functional iron deficiency) because "iron is low." This is the standard inflammatory anemia pattern where iron supplementation does not help and may fuel ferroptosis.
