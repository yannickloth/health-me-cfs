## Inclusion Criteria

- Health-services research or health policy analysis focused on ME/CFS
- Studies of healthcare access barriers, diagnostic disparities, clinician education gaps
- Disability system research (SSDI, PIP, work disability)
- Guideline comparisons and policy analyses (NICE, CDC, IOM, Mayo, AHRQ)
- Medical education surveys and curriculum analyses
- Qualitative research on patient experiences of healthcare (when addressing systemic issues)
- Systematic reviews, scoping reviews of healthcare delivery for ME/CFS
- Peer-reviewed, published after 2010 (post-IOM diagnostic shift); exceptions for systematic reviews (pre-2014 AHRQ included as foundational)

## Exclusion Criteria

- Biomedical studies of ME/CFS pathophysiology (already covered in other chapters)
- Treatment efficacy trials without health-services/system component
- Papers on Long COVID only (without explicit ME/CFS healthcare system discussion)
- Papers on general chronic disease healthcare access (without ME/CFS-specific data): used for background only
- Clinical case reports, editorials, letters (unless data-bearing)
- Duplicate entries already in existing bib files

## Null/Negative/Contradictory Evidence

- **Smith2014AHRQ** found no diagnostic method adequately validated for ME/CFS in populations with diagnostic uncertainty. Eight case definitions exist but no reference standard. Treatment evidence rated low to moderate at best.
- **Hussein2024barriers** documented that the Canadian evidence base for ME/CFS healthcare barriers is extremely sparse (only 21 eligible records).
- **Muirhead2021** found 41% of UK medical schools do not teach ME/CFS at all; of those that do, none shared their syllabus for content verification.
- **Samms2025unequalaccess** (already in bib) found 10× variation in ME/CFS diagnosis rates across English ICBs and 5× lower diagnosis rates in minority ethnic groups.
- **Larun 2024 Cochrane review** (already in exercise-pem.bib) continues to claim exercise "probably reduces fatigue" despite all studies having high risk of bias — a policy contradiction.
