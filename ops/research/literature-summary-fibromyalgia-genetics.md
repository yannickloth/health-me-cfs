# Literature Summary: Fibromyalgia Genetics (Kerrebijn 2026)

**Topic slug:** `fibromyalgia-genetics`
**Date:** 2026-08-22
**Primary paper:** Kerrebijn et al. 2026, Nature Medicine, doi:10.1038/s41591-026-04492-6

## Summary

The primary paper (Kerrebijn 2026, Nature Medicine) is the largest multi-ancestry GWAS meta-analysis of fibromyalgia to date (2,563,755 individuals, 54,629 cases / 2,509,126 controls, 11 cohorts). It identifies 26 risk loci, establishes exclusive brain/neural-cell-type heritability enrichment, demonstrates pervasive positive genetic correlation with chronic pain/psychiatric/somatic disorders (rg > 0.7 with low back pain, PTSD, IBS), finds no evidence of a primary autoimmune architecture (no MHC signal, no immune/glial enrichment), and shows no sex difference in genetic architecture. This provides robust genetic evidence that fibromyalgia is a central nervous system (nociplastic) disorder.

The supporting literature independently corroborates and contextualizes these findings:
- **Bright 2026** (Nat Commun) is an independent multi-ancestry GWAS (85k cases) confirming a neural/psychiatric-somatic genetic architecture and providing partial locus replication.
- **Lin 2026** (PLoS Genet) GenomicSEM shows a shared CNS/synaptic genetic factor across fibromyalgia-psychiatric traits.
- **Johnston 2025** (HGG Adv) establishes nociplastic pain as a shared heritable factor underlying chronic overlapping pain conditions.
- **Rahman 2021** (ARD) confirms heritable CWP genetics (RNF123) with a COMT replication warning.
- **Pan 2025** (Mol Pain) provides an independent widespread-pain GWAS signal with sex-specific architecture.
- **Hu 2025** supports a causal CNS/brain-network contribution via MR.
- **Clauw 2024** and **Ablin 2025** frame the autoimmune debate and gene-environment context; **D'Agnelli 2019** documents the pre-GWAS evidence gap.

## ME/CFS relevance

Fibromyalgia co-occurs with ME/CFS (per the primary paper's introduction). This genetic evidence:
1. Establishes fibromyalgia as a CNS disorder genetically — relevant to ch04 (comorbidity), ch14d (cross-disease), ch13 (integrative).
2. rg > 0.7 with IBS, low back pain, PTSD — shared chronic-pain/somatic genetic architecture relevant to ME/CFS comorbidity modeling.
3. Neural genes (HTT/GPR52/DRD2) as candidate shared mechanisms; risk-locus overlap with ME/CFS (OLFM4, RABGAP1L/GPR52) and long COVID (BPTF) per DecodeME/Chaudhary (cited in primary).
4. Existing corpus papers (Steen2026sharedgenetic, Hajdarevic2022gwas, Schlauch2016gwas, DecodeME2025) already establish the ME/CFS genetic side; this topic adds the fibromyalgia-side genetic architecture and the shared-overlap evidence.

- **Counts:** 1 paper ≥ 0.60; 7 papers ≥ 0.40; 3 papers < 0.40 (none — all 10 are ≥ 0.40). 0 null papers. 0 contradictions that are fundamental (the Bright/Kerrebijn partial-locus overlap is complementary; Clauw's autoimmune debate is resolved toward non-autoimmune by Kerrebijn's genetic data).
- **Competing evidence handled:** (1) Autoimmune debate (Clauw 2024) — Kerrebijn 2026 provides genetic evidence against a primary autoimmune architecture, but notes modest correlations with atypical autoimmune subtypes; both sides retained. (2) COMT replication failure (Rahman 2021) — a warning that candidate-gene pain associations often fail to replicate; no COMT locus in Kerrebijn's 26. (3) Pan 2025 sex-specific widespread-pain locus vs Kerrebijn's no-sex-difference finding — different traits (single-item pain vs ICD-10 FM) and different methods; not a fundamental contradiction.
