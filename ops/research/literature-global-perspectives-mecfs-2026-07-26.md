# Literature Summary: Global Perspectives on ME/CFS — 2026-07-26

## Overview
15 papers across 9 search domains covering ME/CFS in low-resource settings, global health equity, cultural variation, traditional medicine, disability recognition, and post-infectious fatigue in infectious-disease-burden settings.

## Key Findings by Domain

### 1. Post-Infectious Fatigue Burden in LMIC/Tropical Settings
- **Condé 2026** (dengue fatigue MA): First systematic review quantifying post-dengue fatigue — establishes that arboviral infections in LMICs produce chronic fatigue syndromes comparable to post-EBV CFS. Dengue endemic in 100+ countries, mostly LMICs.
- **Duvignaud 2018** (chikungunya cohort, Reunion Island): In a tropical island setting, 38% of chikungunya survivors had chronic fatigue at 30-month follow-up. Shows post-arboviral fatigue is a major public health burden.
- **Indirect evidence**: Arboviral disease burden (dengue, chikungunya, Zika) concentrated in LMICs → post-infectious fatigue likely a hidden epidemic unrecognized as ME/CFS.

### 2. Cultural Variation in Symptom Expression & Illness Labelling
- **Ware & Kleinman 1992**: Landmark anthropological comparison of neurasthenia (China) vs CFS (US). Different cultural idioms for similar fatigue syndromes. Somatic vs psychological framing varies by culture.
- **Starcevic 1999**: Neurasthenia-CFS conceptual mapping across cultures. Notes that global diagnostic expansion eliminates neurasthenia as label in some settings while CFS/ME emerge as replacement.
- **Lee 1998**: In China, shenjing shuairuo (neurasthenia) serves as culturally acceptable idiom for fatigue/distress — ME/CFS would be filtered through this culturally shaped lens.
- **Lee 2000** (Hong Kong primary care): Chronic fatigue patients in Chinese primary care show different illness experience patterns from Western CFS cohorts.

### 3. ME/CFS as a Neglected Disease in Global Health
- **Arron 2024**: Argues ME/CFS is a "neglected disease" with biological basis (microclots, amyloid fibrin). Pretorius group in South Africa — notable as one of the few ME/CFS research contributions from the African continent. Global health framing: ME/CFS research concentrated in high-income countries.
- **Miller 2026**: Post-acute sequelae across epidemic history (influenza 1918, polio, Ebola, SARS, COVID-19). Global health relevance — pandemics produce long-term disability disproportionately in settings with poor acute care infrastructure.

### 4. Burden of Disease & Health Economics
- **Vester 2026**: Scoping review of ME/CFS disease burden. Finds consistent severe QoL impairment (EQ-5D scores ~0.40-0.55, lower than MS/stroke). Economic burden substantial ($18-51B in US). Gap: no non-high-income-country burden estimates identified in the review.

### 5. Disability Recognition and ICD Classification
- **Podell 2020**: Practical guide to documenting ME/CFS disability for legal/insurance purposes. ME/CFS coded as G93.3 in ICD-10, 8E49 in ICD-11. Highlights disconnect between ICD coding existence and practical disability recognition — especially relevant where disability insurance systems are weak or absent (most LMICs).

### 6. Traditional Medicine Approaches
- **Wang 2014** (TCM systematic review, 23 RCTs): Limited evidence quality, but TCM herbal formulations show signal for fatigue reduction. Methodological weaknesses preclude firm conclusions.
- **Kong 2023** (TCM mind-body exercises MA): Tai Chi, Qigong, Baduanjin show moderate effect on fatigue. In LMIC settings where pharmacological options limited, mind-body practices may be more accessible.
- **Wang 2017** (acupuncture/moxibustion MA): 31 RCTs; acupuncture shows small-moderate effect. Acupuncture widely available in East Asian settings and in Indian traditional medicine contexts.
- **Gap**: Zero Ayurveda + CFS systematic reviews found. Large gap given Ayurveda's prominence in South Asia.

### 7. Research Capacity in LMICs
- **Sarker 2024** (Bangladesh RCT protocol): First registered ME/CFS clinical trial from Bangladesh. Testing physiotherapy vs drug management in post-COVID ME. Represents emerging research capacity in South Asia.
- **National Academies 2024** (workshop): "Toward a Common Research Agenda in Infection-Associated Chronic Illnesses" — includes post-Lyme, ME/CFS, Long COVID. Global health dimension weak but present — calls for international standardization.

### 8. Iran ME/CFS Data
- **Simani 2021** (Iran post-COVID cohort): 17.5% of hospitalized COVID patients met CFS criteria at 6 months. 75% female. First ME/CFS prevalence estimate from a middle-income Middle Eastern country. Fukuda criteria used.

## Gaps Identified
1. **Zero population-based ME/CFS prevalence studies from Sub-Saharan Africa**
2. **Zero Ayurveda or Siddha medicine ME/CFS studies** despite these being primary healthcare modalities in South Asia
3. **No WHO global initiative for ME/CFS**
4. **No ME/CFS disability weight in GBD study** — post-infectious fatigue burden invisible in global health metrics
5. **No gender-disaggregated ME/CFS analysis from LMIC settings**
6. **Karasz** (South Asian immigrant CFS in UK/Canada), **Bhui** (Somali refugee fatigue syndromes) — known qualitative literature not PubMed-indexed
7. **Zero ME/CFS traditional medicine studies from African traditional medicine systems**

## Cohort Overlap Check
- Condé 2026 and Duvignaud 2018: both include Gérardin (senior author) at Reunion Island but different diseases (dengue vs chikungunya) and different cohorts → no overlap concern.
- Wang 2014, Kong 2023, Wang 2017: all systematic reviews of different TCM modalities; primary RCTs may overlap in review scopes but this is expected and non-problematic for systematic review evidence.
- No other cohort overlaps identified.
