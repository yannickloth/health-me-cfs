# Literature Summary: Long Covid–AD(H)D Neuroimmune Framework

**Topic slug:** `longcovid-adhd-neuroimmune`
**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Anchor paper:** Spanoghe M, Antonacci T, Schneider N, Molmans THJ. Viewpoint: Linking long Covid and AD(H)D through neuroimmune dysfunction. Brain Behav Immun 2026;131:106181. DOI 10.1016/j.bbi.2025.106181.

## Summary of the evidence landscape

The anchor is a **viewpoint** (top journal synthesis, no primary data) proposing four converging neuroimmune mechanisms linking Long Covid and AD(H)D: (1) frontal–striatal–hippocampal dysfunction, (2) neuroimmune dysregulation of catecholamine systems, (3) tryptophan–kynurenine dysregulation, (4) mitochondrial bioenergetic defects — plus off-label ADHD pharmacotherapy in LC and a precision-immunopsychiatry framework.

The newly-added primary sources split into:
- **Corroborating mechanistic (6):** Cysique2023KynureninePASC (kynurenine ↔ PASC cognitive impairment), Rassoulpour2005KynurenicAcid (kynurenic acid lowers striatal dopamine — indirect bridge), Sarkar2010DopamineImmunoreg + Feng2021DopamineImmunomodulatory (dopamine–immune crosstalk), Tanaka2022MitoKynurenine (mitochondrial–kynurenine link), Xu2025MitoComplexesADHD (mitochondrial complex suppression in COVID cognitive decline).
- **Corroborating clinical/off-label (2):** FesharakiZadeh2023Guanfacine (guanfacine+NAC for LC cognitive deficits), Krishnan2022BrainFogMultidisciplinary (multidisciplinary post-COVID brain fog).
- **Corroborating epidemiological (4):** Koonce2024LongCovidStimulant (stimulant prescribing in LC), Merzon2022LongCovidADHDYouth (ADHD → LC risk), Gimbach2024ADHDMedicationEurope (post-COVID ADHD med consumption), Ferrara2023CovidADHD (COVID–ADHD correlation viewpoint).
- **Null (1):** Zemer2024COVIDADHD20Year (20-year national cohort: no independent COVID effect on ADHD diagnosis/treatment).

**Indirect biochemical links (mandatory):** Kynurenine ME/CFS and mitochondrial ME/CFS evidence is already extensive in the corpus (kynurenine-excitotoxicity stream; Verma2016ADHDcybrid, Molnar2024LCmitochondria, Shankar2025oxidativestress) — these were checked and NOT re-added, but they corroborate mechanisms (3) and (4) in the ME/CFS population directly. The dopamine–immune link (mechanism 2) had no ME/CFS-specific entry, so general-dopamine-immune reviews (Sarkar2010, Feng2021) were added.

## Null / negative / competing evidence

- **Zemer2024COVIDADHD20Year (NULL):** High-certainty 20-year national cohort found NO independent effect of COVID-19 on ADHD diagnosis/treatment rates. This is the framework's own cited counterpoint. It suggests reported post-COVID ADHD may reflect diagnosis catch-up, ascertainment, or symptom overlap rather than infection-driven new-onset ADHD.
- **Causal-direction ambiguity (competing interpretation):** Merzon2022 (ADHD → higher LC risk) and SaezFrancas2012/Quadt2024 (pre-existing ADHD/neurodivergence → chronic fatigue) support ADHD as a *predisposing* factor, not solely LC-induced. This competes with the framework's "acquired, infection-triggered" dopaminergic/noradrenergic dysfunction emphasis.
- **Gimbach2024 catch-up vs trend-change (competing):** The European increase in ADHD medication post-COVID is consistent with catch-up diagnosis rather than infection-driven onset — ambiguous on whether infection genuinely causes ADHD.

## Contradictions between papers

- **Zemer2024 (null) vs Koonce2024/Gimbach2024/Merzon2022 (positive epidemiological association):** Directly conflicting. Zemer found no COVID effect on ADHD diagnosis/treatment in a large cohort; Koonce/Gimbach found increased stimulant prescribing, Merzon found ADHD→LC association. Resolvable in part by design: Zemer measures ADHD diagnosis/treatment *rates* (population-level, catch-up-adjusted), whereas Koonce/Gimbach measure stimulant *prescribing* and Merzon measures LC risk among ADHD patients (different outcomes, different populations). This is a **fundamental-frame conflict** (same underlying overlap, opposite conclusions about infection-driven causality). Present as an `#open-question` in Phase 3, not weighted toward one side — the certainty difference between Zemer (0.70) and the positive epidemiology (0.55–0.65) is ≤0.15, within measurement uncertainty (band rule → present both sides).

## Strongest defensible mechanistic claim
Mechanism (3) kynurenine dysregulation → dopamine/cognition is the best-supported arm: direct human PASC evidence (Cysique2023, 0.55) + established biochemistry (Rassoulpour2005, Tanaka2022) + extensive existing ME/CFS kynurenine coverage. Mechanisms (1) and (2) rest on general-dopamine-immune biology and animal data. Mechanism (4) mitochondrial is heavily corroborated in ME/CFS already.

## What must remain speculative
The "acquired neuroimmune-mediated dopaminergic/noradrenergic dysfunction" hypothesis and the causal claim that infection *drives* new ADHD — both contradicted by Zemer2024 and by the catch-up interpretation of the prescribing data.

## What the evidence does NOT support
Infection-driven new-onset ADHD at population scale. The framework's stronger, better-evidenced claim is the *shared mechanism* (convergent neuroimmune/catecholamine/kynurenine/mitochondrial dysfunction), not a large infection→ADHD incidence effect.
