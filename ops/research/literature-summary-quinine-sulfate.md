# Literature Summary: Quinine Sulfate for Muscle/Leg Cramps (Bedtime Use)

**Topic:** `quinine-sulfate`
**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Type:** Treatment/medication (off-label bedtime use for muscle/leg cramps)

## Bottom Line

Quinine's use for leg/muscle cramps is an **off-label practice whose efficacy evidence is marginal and whose safety profile carries an FDA boxed warning**. The strongest efficacy source (ElTawil 2010 Cochrane) found limited benefit across 23 trials / 1586 patients and an unfavorable risk–benefit balance. Harm is dominated by serious immune-mediated hematologic and cardiovascular events (Liles 2016). The FDA restricted quinine for cramps in 2010. There are **zero direct ME/CFS studies** of quinine. The only ME/CFS-relevant pathway is an **indirect, mechanistic** one: quinine's anti-cramp action is attributed to skeletal-muscle sodium/potassium (Nav1.4 / Kv7-KCNQ) channel modulation, and Nav1.4 (SCN4A) dysfunction independently produces painful cramps + myotonia (Torbergsen 2015) — intersecting the ME/CFS muscle-ion-channel hypothesis. This is a low-certainty indirect link only.

## Evidence Landscape

**Strong:** Harm is well-established (Liles 2016 = high-certainty systematic review, 0.80 raw). Efficacy is limited and publication-bias-inflated (ElTawil 2010; ManSonHing 1998). These two arms are internally consistent: modest, unreliable efficacy + real, serious harm = unfavorable net.

**Weak / missing:** No direct ME/CFS or CFS study of quinine (0 hits). No quinine–cramp RCT in any chronic-illness or fatigued population. The ion-channel mechanism in ME/CFS is indirect only (Torbergsen 2015 is a primary-channelopathy family, not ME/CFS).

**Null:** Cochrane efficacy review = limited/unreliable benefit; Cochrane magnesium = no consistent benefit; Cochrane non-drug therapies = limited evidence. So quinine's marginal efficacy exists in a context where the standard alternatives also lack proof.

## Contradiction Detection

- ManSonHing 1995 (published-data-only) reported 8.83 fewer cramps/4wk; ManSonHing 1998 (adding unpublished data) reduced this to 3.60 — a **resolvable** discrepancy (publication bias), not a fundamental contradiction. The Cochrane review (ElTawil 2010) independently concluded limited benefit. These converge: benefit is small and partly publication artifact.
- Haeusler 2018 vs White 2007: consistent on quinoline QT risk; White 2007 nuances that quinine's repolarization effect is modest (quinidine/halofantrine worse). Not a contradiction — both flag cardiovascular risk.

## What This Evidence Does / Does Not Support

- **Establishes:** Quinine has limited, possibly publication-bias-inflated efficacy for leg cramps; it carries serious, well-documented adverse effects (hematologic, cardiovascular); it is FDA-boxed-warned and restricted for off-label cramp use. The anti-cramp mechanism is skeletal-muscle Na/K-channel membrane stabilization.
- **Supports (mechanistic, indirect):** A link between skeletal-muscle sodium-channel (Nav1.4) function and painful cramps/myotonia — relevant to the ME/CFS muscle-ion-channel and cramp/PEM hypotheses. Quinine is one probe of that system (channel blocker), though not a safe one.
- **Does NOT support:** Any claim that quinine is an appropriate or safe bedtime treatment for ME/CFS cramps. There is no ME/CFS-specific evidence, and the general-population safety signal is strongly adverse.
- **Clinical relevance:** LOW for quinine as a therapeutic recommendation (rejected by FDA for this use). HIGH as a **harm/red-flag and mechanistic-context** item for the treatment chapters and the ion-channel chapter — it illustrates both (a) a drug class (channel blockers) that modulates cramp/myotonia excitability, and (b) why off-label quinine is unsafe. Subset applicability: unknown (no ME/CFS data).

## Feed to Later Phases

- **Phase 2 (decision):** Expect PARTIAL or REJECT for quinine as a *recommended treatment* (uniformly adverse + null for ME/CFS). Likely integrated only as a `#limitation`/`#practical-warning` (off-label drug to avoid) and as mechanistic context for the ion-channel chapter — not as a therapeutic endorsement. The strongest discounted cert (0.60, Liles harm) supports a warning, not a treatment claim.
- **Phase 3b (safety gate):** Harm search complete. Contraindications: G6PD deficiency, pregnancy (abortifacient/teratogenic), prolonged QT, hypersensitivity, thrombocytopenia history. Interactions to check against ME/CFS co-prescriptions (esp. QT-prolonging, antiarrhythmics, other channel-active drugs).
- **Competing-treatment context:** Magnesium and non-drug therapies lack proof too — so ME/CFS patients' cramps are an evidence gap across options, with quinine the *least safe* of the commonly used ones.
- **Mechanistic thread (indirect):** Nav1.4/KCNQ channel dysfunction → cramps/myotonia → relevance to ch30 sec-01 (ion-channel domain) and cramp/PEM overlap. Low certainty (0.25 discounted), speculation/limitation only.
