## Inclusion Criteria
- Human studies or authoritative physiology reviews directly relevant to prolactin response to neuroendocrine challenge (buspirone and/or fenfluramine) in ME/CFS / chronic fatigue syndrome / postviral fatigue.
- Depression-vs-ME/CFS comparative neuroendocrine studies (discriminator relevance).
- Null/negative serotonergic challenge studies in ME/CFS (to capture the competing-mechanism and null evidence).
- Canonical physiology reviews of the dopamine–prolactin brake (D2) and estradiol modulation of prolactin (mechanism candidates a and b).
- ME/CFS-specific dopaminergic-modulation studies (indirect dopamine link).
- Peer-reviewed journals (all included); one conference-adjacent open-label (OSU6162, Brain Behav).
- Papers added to `bib/endocrine-reproductive.bib` with `research_stream = {prolactin-buspirone}`.

## Exclusion Criteria
- Papers studying prolactin/prolactinomas/bromocriptine/estradiol without any ME/CFS, CFS, PVFS, or long-COVID relevance.
- Animal models of serotonergic/dopaminergic mechanisms without translational human relevance to the prolactin challenge (e.g., reserpine mouse, CRF-2 knockout).
- Post-polio, fibromyalgia, POTS, or menorrhagia papers with only tangential prolactin/hormone content.
- Case reports of endocrine syndromes (Sheehan's, POEMS, empty sella) without ME/CFS challenge relevance.
- Depression-only buspirone/prolactin studies without ME/CFS comparator (used only as methodology context, not included).
- 2020s prolactin-ME/CFS hits that were case reports or unrelated (verified and excluded).
- Thin BMJ letters (Curtis 1992, Bevan 1992) whose content could not be verified (no abstract; content unknown) — excluded to avoid unverifiable characterization.

## Notes
- **Research gap documented:** Zero modern (post-2010) replication studies of the buspirone→prolactin response in ME/CFS were found. The direct challenge-prolactin literature is entirely from 1992–2001. This confirms the topic's premise that the line was abandoned for years and supports the need for a modern replication study.
- **Null evidence captured:** Yatham 1995 (fenfluramine: no difference) is a genuine null finding for a serotonergic probe; the positive findings are specific to buspirone (a D2 blocker), consistent with the dopamine-D2 mechanism interpretation.
- Google Scholar / preprint servers not programmatically accessible in this environment; noted for reproducibility (a future run should add these). PubMed + Crossref coverage is authoritative for the indexed literature.
