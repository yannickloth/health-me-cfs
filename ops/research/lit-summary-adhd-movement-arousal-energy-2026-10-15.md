## What the included evidence establishes

Two supplied sources anchor the topic. **Sarver 2015** (n=52; 29 ADHD / 23 TD boys, 8–12 y) shows that *higher* observed gross-motor activity predicted *better* — though not normalised — phonological working-memory (WM) performance in children with ADHD, while the same higher activity predicted *worse* WM in typically developing (TD) children. **Orban 2018** (n=62; 32/30) shows that ADHD children's attentive behaviour is intact during a low-WM-demand "preferred-activity" video (d = −0.02) but drops disproportionately during analogue classroom instruction (d = −0.71), and that **59%** of the between-group inattention gap is attributable to central-executive WM impairment (direct 41% + indirect via phonological short-term memory coordination 15%); after accounting for WM the group difference is no longer detectable (d = −0.29, ns).

Together with the rest of the F1 set these establish a coherent programme claim: **the behavioural signature of ADHD during cognitively demanding tasks (excess movement, lapses in attentive behaviour) is at least partly a downstream expression of an effortful-arousal system being recruited to sustain a limited-capacity WM.** Supporting pieces:

- **Rapport 2009** (n=23): activity level tracked central-executive demand, not storage/rehearsal; the elevated ADHD activity *under control conditions* was fully attenuated once central-executive variance was removed — i.e. the "ubiquitous" hyperactivity was WM-linked.
- **Kofler 2015** (n=25): when WM memory-set was unpredictable (sustained central-executive load) activity stayed high; when predictable, activity fell over time.
- **Dekkers 2021** (n=60): activity rose with cognitive demand for *all* children, and children with ADHD were motorically more active under every WM condition — the increase was *not* a graded dose-response but a step up that then stayed relatively stable, which the authors read as arousal-promotion.
- **Wiegers 2024** (n=43, on/off stimulant, actigraphy): a significant medication×activity interaction for visuospatial WM — the medication benefit was largest in the highest-activity category, implying stimulant and endogenous movement act on a shared arousal/energetics substrate.

## Strongest mechanistic claim supportable
Hyperactivity during cognitive work functions, in ADHD, as an **arousal/energetics recruitment behaviour** coupled to central-executive demand (a compensation that is helpful but incomplete — WM is improved, not normalised). The behavioural *attention* deficit during instruction is largely a **downstream consequence of the WM/executive-load failure**, not an independent primary defect of "attention".

## What must remain speculative
Any direct transfer to ME/CFS. Every included paper studies ADHD or ADHD-symptom samples; **none studies ME/CFS.** The ME/CFS connection is a *cross-disease analogy* between an ADHD "recruitment-side" energetic problem and an ME/CFS "supply-side" energy problem, and must be written as cross-disease inference, not shared-cohort evidence.

## What the evidence does NOT support
- It does **not** support "movement always helps cognition." Antrop 2005 found activity also rises during *low*-demand waiting situations, and Abikoff 1996's auditory-stimulation benefit appeared only when stimulation was the first condition (order interaction) — so the compensation account has boundary conditions and at least one order-sensitive result.
- It does **not** establish arousal as the sole cause: Sergeant 2000 (activation/effort pools), Metin 2016 (state-regulation deficit), Drescher 2021 (state-regulation difficulties with *no* group pupil-size difference), and Sonuga-Barke 2003 (delay aversion as a motivational-style alternative) provide genuinely competing explanatory layers. Drescher's null pupil effects are a direct caution against over-literal "hypoarousal" claims.
- It does **not** support causality from association: these are cross-sectional/within-subject task studies; the compensation account is inferred from covariation of activity with demand, not from a manipulation that isolates arousal.
