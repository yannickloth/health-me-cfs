## 2. New Papers Added (4 entries)

### 2.1 Louveau et al. 2015 — CNS Meningeal Lymphatic Vessel Discovery
**Bib key:** `@Louveau2015meningealLymphatics`
**Certainty:** 0.85

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Journal | Nature |
| PMID | 26030524 |
| DOI | 10.1038/nature14432 |

**Key finding:** First discovery of functional lymphatic vessels lining the dural sinuses in the CNS, capable of carrying fluid, immune cells, and macromolecules to deep cervical lymph nodes.

**ME/CFS relevance:** Foundational anatomy. Without these vessels, glymphatic clearance to peripheral circulation and skull-border B cell tolerance surveillance (@Chayama2026BrainClearance) could not function. Establishes the anatomic possibility of compartmentalized brain waste clearance and immune surveillance defects.

### 2.2 Aspelund et al. 2015 — Dural Lymphatic Vascular System
**Bib key:** `@Aspelund2015duralLymphatics`
**Certainty:** 0.80

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Journal | Journal of Experimental Medicine |
| PMID | 26077718 |
| DOI | 10.1084/jem.20142290 |

**Key finding:** Independent discovery confirming dural lymphatic vasculature. Transgenic mice with complete dural lymphatic aplasia showed attenuated macromolecule clearance and abrogated cervical lymph node transport. Complements Louveau 2015 with functional ablation evidence.

**ME/CFS relevance:** Demonstrates that disruption of dural lymphatics directly impairs brain waste clearance. If chronic neuroinflammation in ME/CFS damages or constricts these vessels (perivascular fibrosis), it creates the anatomical substrate for glymphatic failure.

### 2.3 Da Mesquita et al. 2018 — Meningeal Lymphatics in Ageing and Alzheimer's
**Bib key:** `@DaMesquita2018meningealLymphaticsAging`
**Certainty:** 0.80

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Journal | Nature |
| PMID | 30046111 |
| DOI | 10.1038/s41586-018-0368-8 |

**Key finding:** Meningeal lymphatic dysfunction impairs brain waste clearance and induces cognitive impairment. VEGF-C treatment in aged mice restored meningeal lymphatic drainage, improved brain perfusion, and rescued learning/memory. In AD models, lymphatic disruption aggravated amyloid-beta deposition.

**ME/CFS relevance:** ME/CFS involves chronic neuroinflammation that may functionally mimic aged lymphatic dysfunction. Cognitive impairment (brain fog) is a core symptom. If VEGF-C treatment can reverse cognitive impairment from meningeal lymphatic failure in mice, lymphatic-supporting interventions may merit investigation in ME/CFS.

### 2.4 Jiang-Xie et al. 2024 — Neuronal Dynamics Direct CSF Perfusion
**Bib key:** `@JiangXie2024neuronalCSFDynamics`
**Certainty:** 0.78

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Journal | Nature |
| PMID | 38418877 |
| DOI | 10.1038/s41586-024-07108-6 |

**Key finding:** Neuronal networks synchronize individual action potentials to create large-amplitude rhythmic ionic waves in brain ISF. These waves are the mechanistically plausible driver of glymphatic flow. Chemogenetic silencing of waves impeded CSF infiltration; optogenetic wave synthesis potentiated perfusion. Neurons are master organizers of brain clearance.

**ME/CFS relevance:** ME/CFS involves sleep fragmentation, alpha-delta sleep (wake-like EEG intrusion into NREM), and dysautonomia — conditions that disrupt the rhythmic neural synchrony this paper identifies as the clearance pump. This creates a plausible biological pathway: sleep fragmentation → disrupted neural oscillations → impaired ionic wave generation → reduced CSF perfusion → metabolic waste accumulation → cognitive symptoms + PEM amplification.
