## Key Findings by Paper

### 1. Pinggal2026localsleepADHD (CERT=0.80)
- ADHD adults (n=32) vs controls (n=31): higher waking slow wave density over parieto-temporal electrodes
- More commission errors, mind wandering, mind blanking, theta oscillations
- SW density mediated ADHD-related attentional difficulties
- SW density correlated with omission errors, slower RT, greater RT variability, elevated sleepiness
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Same behavioral attentional profile (RT slowing, variability, lapses) documented in ME/CFS cognitive literature

### 2. Pinggal2022pharmaSlowWaves (CERT=0.78)
- Double-blind crossover RCT (n=32): methylphenidate, atomoxetine, citalopram, placebo
- Citalopram (↑serotonin) increased waking slow waves and misses
- Methylphenidate (↑dopamine/noradrenaline) reduced misses
- Slow waves predicted both misses (sluggishness) and faster responses (impulsivity) in region-specific pattern
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Demonstrates catecholamines gate local sleep; ME/CFS has documented central noradrenergic deficiency

### 3. Gong2026slowWavesADHDSubtypes (CERT=0.65)
- N=120 children: healthy controls, ADHD-I, ADHD-C
- Waking SWA elevated in both ADHD subtypes, highest in combined type
- Prefrontal SWA distinguished ADHD-C from ADHD-I; correlated with executive control deficits
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Subtype-specific local sleep patterns; ME/CFS may show its own local sleep topography

### 4. Andrillon2023localSleepReview (CERT=0.82)
- Sleep is local, not purely global — local sleep occurs during both sleep and wakefulness
- Local sleep explains dreaming, parasomnias, intrasleep responsiveness, inattention, mind wandering
- Evidence across animals, humans, healthy and pathological brains
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Framework for understanding how brain fog could reflect waking local sleep intrusions

### 5. Deboer2026twoTypesLocalSleep (CERT=0.60)
- Type 1 local sleep: distinct vigilance states simultaneously (subcortical origin) — e.g. uni-hemispheric sleep
- Type 2 local sleep: use-dependent local neuronal changes (cortical origin) — e.g. SW after cognitive load
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Waking slow waves are Type 2 — cortically induced by prior use, consistent with PEM from cognitive effort

### 6. VanDongen2025localVsGlobalSleep (CERT=0.55)
- Local sleep is physics-based entropy-energy consequence of information processing; inevitable, no function
- Global sleep is biology-based evolutionary adaptation to manage local sleep pressure
- "Sleep may just be the unavoidable price we pay for cognition"
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** If ME/CFS has impaired energy metabolism, local sleep intrusions should occur at lower cognitive loads

### 7. Leemburg2025LPSvigilance (CERT=0.55)
- LPS-induced systemic inflammation → region-specific EEG disruption during wakefulness
- Certain cortical areas enter sleep-like states despite behavioral wakefulness
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Peripheral inflammation drives waking slow wave intrusions; ME/CFS has documented neuroinflammation/inflammatory markers

### 8. Alfonsa2023chlorideLocalSleep (CERT=0.78)
- Intracellular chloride accumulates in cortical neurons during wakefulness → drives local sleep pressure
- KCC2/NKCC1 chloride transporter manipulation alters slow wave expression
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Ion homeostasis disruption from mitochondrial dysfunction could accelerate chloride accumulation → more local sleep

### 9. ElGrawani2024BDNFlocalSleep (CERT=0.70)
- BDNF-TrkB signaling drives local sleep buildup in mouse cortex
- BDNF during wake increases subsequent local SWA; TrkB blockade prevents it
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** BDNF dysregulated in ME/CFS; could directly modulate local sleep pressure

### 10. Maness2019SEIDhypersomnolence (CERT=0.65)
- 21% of hypersomnolent sleep center patients (n=187) met SEID/ME/CFS criteria
- SEID patients had more profound fatigue, worse response to wake-promoting agents (88.6% vs 67.7% non-response)
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Suggests excessive daytime sleepiness co-occurs with ME/CFS; local sleep may be the shared mechanism

### 11. Neu2015swsMECFSinsomnia (CERT=0.72)
- CFS and primary insomnia both show decreased central ultra-slow power (0.3-0.79 Hz) during SWS
- CFS had increased SWS duration but impaired quality — quantitative compensation without qualitative restoration
- Ultra-slow power reduction correlated with fatigue severity
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Same frequency band as waking slow waves; suggests same oscillatory defect appears in sleep AND wake

### 12. Fatt2020parasympatheticSWS (CERT=0.72)
- CFS patients (n=24) had more SWS, more awakenings, reduced parasympathetic activity specifically during SWS
- Autonomic hypervigilance during deep sleep — patients are in SWS but not in restorative state
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Even when SWS is present, it lacks restorative quality; local sleep in wake would compound this deficit

### 13. Ruby2024sleepWakeTransition (CERT=0.65)
- Sleep inertia = persistent local sleep in specific brain regions after awakening
- Sleep-deprived individuals show longer, more intense sleep inertia
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Unrefreshing sleep may = failure of complete local sleep dissipation at awakening

### 14. Ortelli2022postcovidAttention (CERT=0.60)
- Post-COVID patients (n=65) showed global slowing and increased intra-individual variability in attention
- Same behavioral signature as waking slow waves in Pinggal2026
- **Relevance to ME/CFS:** Post-viral cognitive profile matches local sleep behavioral signature

  → Altered BDNF & ion homeostasis (ElGrawani2024, Alfonsa2023)
    → Impaired catecholamine tone (Walitt2024, Pinggal2022)
      → Reduced arousal system gatekeeping of sleep-wake boundary
        → Waking local sleep intrusions (Pinggal2026)
          → Attention lapses, RT variability, cognitive slowing
            → BRAIN FOG
