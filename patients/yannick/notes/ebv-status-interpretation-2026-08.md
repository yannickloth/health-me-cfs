## 1. Available serology (October 2025)

| Marker | Result | Meaning |
|--------|--------|---------|
| EBV VCA IgG | **>750 U/mL** | Past infection, robust antibody response |
| EBV VCA IgM | Negative | No acute/recent primary infection |

| Parvovirus B19 | 61.0 U/mL | Past infection |

**Not tested:** EBV EA-D IgG, EBNA-1 IgG, EBV DNA PCR, HHV-6, HSV-1/2, VZV.

**Interpretation limit:** VCA IgG is a *carriage* marker, not a *reactivation* marker. Meta-analytic evidence (Hwang 2023, 64 studies, ~5,000 ME/CFS vs ~9,200 controls) shows antibody titers do not distinguish ME/CFS patients from controls; reactivation markers (plasma EBV DNA, EA-D IgG) do (Shikova 2020: plasma EBV DNA 24.1% ME/CFS vs 4% controls). The >750 titer is compatible with reactivation but cannot demonstrate it.

## 2. Childhood history (reported 2026-08-04)

- **Tonsillectomy at age 5**
- **Constant/recurrent rhino-pharyngitis** in early childhood (the indication pattern for that tonsillectomy)
- Fatigue present since adolescence (documented in 2018 polysomnography report: "fatigue présente depuis l'adolescence")

**What this supports:** EBV primary infection before ~age 6 typically presents as recurrent nondescript rhino-pharyngitis, not mononucleosis. The oropharynx/tonsillar B cells are the EBV entry site. Tonsillectomy does not remove the reservoir — EBV latency resides in memory B cells body-wide. Childhood acquisition followed by fatigue onset in adolescence fits a **latency → recurrent reactivation** model (cumulative/repeated reactivation with progressive epigenetic erosion) rather than the classic adolescent-mono → post-IM conversion model (Jason & Katz 2025: ~11–13% conversion).

**Honest limits:** recurrent rhino-pharyngitis at age 5 is common and mostly not EBV; the history is compatible with childhood EBV but cannot prove it, and serology cannot date the infection retrospectively.

## 3. What the aripiprazole response does and does not say

**It does not say anything about EBV causation.** Aripiprazole 1 mg acts at the microglial D2 threshold — a downstream effector node. EBV-driven, HSAT2-driven, autoimmune, or metabolically-driven ME/CFS would all respond identically to raising that threshold. Drug response at a downstream node cannot discriminate between upstream causes.

**What it does establish:**
1. Microglia are *primed, not constitutively activated* — no systemic inflammation, yet 5h chainsaw work produced no PEM (2026-07-17). Consistent with a persistent sensitizing agent.
2. LDN−/LDA+ excludes TLR4 as the dominant microglial sensor; the pattern is consistent with PU.1/ETS-pathway sensitization (the HSAT2 route) — but "consistent with" constrains the pathway, not the viral trigger.
3. LDA compensates the defect without removing it; if EBV reactivation is ongoing, it presumably continues under LDA.

## 4. Tests that would answer the EBV question

In increasing order of directness:

1. **EBV reactivation panel:** EA-D IgG + EBNA-1 IgG + EBV DNA PCR (plasma). Cheap, routine; already listed as missing in the medical report's cimetidine section.
2. **Broader herpesvirus panel:** HHV-6 (incl. SITH-1 where available; cf. Oka 2026), HSV-1/2, VZV — none tested to date.
3. **EBV dUTPase antibodies** — reactivation-specific research marker (Liu 2023; Palomo 2026).
4. **EV HSAT2 RNA (ddPCR)** — the direct mechanistic test in this patient's own blood; cf. HSAT2 research program study A1.
5. **Cimetidine interventional probe** — already recommended in the medical report (07-recommandations). If titers fall and symptoms shift on cimetidine, that is interventional evidence for the viral arm (documented precedent: EBV IgG 596→514, EBNA 213→156 in the Ursula case).

## 5. Timing recommendation

EA-D IgG wanes between reactivation episodes — a single random draw can miss intermittent reactivation. If the first panel is negative but suspicion remains, **repeat the draw during a flare/crash** (or post-exertion). This pairs with the exertion-sampling design (CPET study H3) in the HSAT2 research notes: symptom flare + EA-D IgG + EV HSAT2 sampled together would connect trigger, marker, and symptom in one observation.
