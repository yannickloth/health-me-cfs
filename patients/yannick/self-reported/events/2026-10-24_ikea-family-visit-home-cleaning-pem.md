## Pattern Analysis

### Key Observations

1. **Two triggers, two PEM onsets, one merged episode.** The patient distinguishes the two PEMs. The record keeps them separate at the trigger level and merged at the episode level.
2. **The second load was taken during the first episode's recovery window.** This is the defining feature of the event. It is not a single large exertion; it is a second exertion before recovery closed.
3. **The merged episode is longer than either would be alone.** That is the patient's report. Its total length is not yet known.
4. **Driving is a repeated trigger class.** On 2026-09-12, 3 h of driving (2 × 1 h 30) at LDA 0.75 mg produced PEM with severe migraine. Here 3 h of driving at LDA 1 mg was followed by PEM, with a cleaning day added before recovery.

## Clinical Significance

- **Consecutive-day exertion is the pacing target.** The avoidable factor here is not the driving or the cleaning alone, but doing the second before the first recovery closed.
- A merged episode is longer than either component would be alone, so recovery time should be budgeted from the **last** exertion, not the first.
- Driving remains a non-trivial exertion class for this patient, and 3 h in two blocks is enough to sit inside a PEM-triggering load.
- The LDA 1 mg dose should not be re-evaluated on this episode alone, because two loads were stacked.
